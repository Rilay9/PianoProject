Accompaniment lab: mockups, take 2 (2026-10-13)

Built around what went wrong last time: you never knew what to press first, what each control did, what your part was, or what to play next. So here: one question first (Lead or Accompaniment), then a small step to try, a sentence saying exactly what you'll do and what the app plays, and while playing one instruction, the chord now and next, the beat, and the keys to use. Everything else (which band parts play, the mix) stays out of the way in ⚙.

(The staves here are sketches: the noteheads are placeholders, not the real tunes; the keyboards show F7 as F3 A3 C4 E♭4.)

Honest about where music comes from: melodies are never made up by the app. "Play the melody" uses a real song's lead sheet, and an "example to play" is one written into the lesson's content. What the app makes is the backing: the chords played by a style (a pattern per genre, written as content) and the simple beginner steps below, which are standard ways of starting out (roots, then chords on the beat; chord tones and a scale for improvising).

The beginner steps (what the app can honestly offer from a song's chords)

Accompaniment
  • 1 Roots: the chord's root, one note on beat 1 (left hand; the first thing anyone learns).
  • 2 Chords on beat 1: the whole chord, held for the bar (a simple voicing shown on the keys).
  • 3 The style's rhythm: the chord in the genre's pattern (shuffle: 2 and 4; rock: steady eighths; ballad: arpeggiated) — from the style file.
  • 4 Your own way: any voicing, any rhythm; judged on the chord's notes only.
Lead
  • With a song's lead sheet: hear it → melody with keys lit → melody alone → your own way (embellish; not judged).
  • With a chord chart (no melody): improvise — 1 roots in rhythm (one note, play with the drums) → 2 chord notes (the lit dark keys) → 3 the scale (the lesson names it, e.g. the blues scale) → free. Not marked; told plainly how it went.
  • "Show me an example": a lick or line written into the lesson's content, played and lit for you to copy.

The skeleton: the content it takes

Three kinds of file, so weekly lessons plug in without touching the app.

A song (lead sheet): MusicXML with the melody and chord symbols (<harmony>), as MuseScore and most lead-sheet sources export. In the song list with "kind": "lead-sheet", "style": "swing".
A chord chart (with its guides and examples): 
{ "title": "12-bar blues in C", "meter": "4/4", "tempo": 84,
  "style": "shuffle",
  "bars": ["C7","C7","C7","C7","F7","F7","C7","C7",
           "G7","F7","C7","G7"],
  "improvise": { "scale": "C blues" },
  "examples": { "lead": "blues-lick-1.musicxml" } }
A style (how a genre's band plays any chord): 
{ "name": "Shuffle", "meter": "4/4", "feel": "swing",
  "drums":  { "hat":   "x.x x.x x.x x.x",
              "snare": "... x.. ... x..",
              "kick":  "x.. ... x.. ..." },
  "bass":   [[1,"root"],[2,"fifth"],[3,"sixth"],[4,"fifth"]],
  "chords": [[2,"chord",0.5],[4,"chord",0.5]] }

Other things that would help (ideas, not decided)

  • Slow and loop by bars: start at 70%, loop the bars that went wrong; tempo up a step after a clean round (as Ladder does).
  • Hear your part first: "Show me" plays one round with your part in the band, lit, before you try.
  • Swap: one tap from Accompaniment to Lead on the same song, so you hear both sides of the band.
  • Drop-outs: the drums or the melody go quiet for a bar or two to see whether you keep the time and the form.
  • Call and answer (later): the band plays two bars, you answer two — the old app's "trading" done with the guides above.
