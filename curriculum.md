# The curriculum (step 1 outline, 2026-10-09)

What a learner learns at each level, from the first lesson to Grade 8, taken from published method books and graded syllabuses. The old curriculum is marked against it at the end. Rungs (step 2), pieces (step 3), exercises and modes (step 4) and lessons (step 5) come later and are not decided here.

Reading notes:
- **SR** = sight-reading. Trinity sets its sight-reading test "approximately two grades lower than the exam" (T p. 80), so its SR lines run behind ABRSM's.
- Exam syllabuses say almost nothing about reading range, dynamics or pedal in pieces; those lines come from the method books and from the sight-reading parameters (AB p. 16, T p. 81).
- Written theory away from the keyboard is out. Theory appears only as something played or recognised at the piano.

## Level P: first steps (F Primer, Al 1A)

- **Reading.** Black-key tunes and key names A–G before the staff (Al 1A pp. 6–26; F Primer p. 24). Then the staff from guide notes (Middle C, treble G, bass F) out to Bass C–Treble G, read by steps and skips (F Primer). Alfred reads by positions (Middle C, C, G) and intervals 2nd–5th (Al 1A pp. 27–58). *Both read by intervals; the app follows that.*
- **Rhythm.** Quarter, half, dotted half and whole notes; 4/4 and 3/4; quarter rest; tie (F Primer; Al 1A). Half and whole rests and incomplete measures (Al 1A).
- **Technique.** Posture, hand shape, finger numbers; the C five-finger pattern (F Primer). Legato and staccato (Al 1A pp. 33–58; Faber leaves them to Level 1).
- **Hands.** Hands separately and alternating; teacher duets (F Primer; Al 1A).
- **Expression.** p, f, mf; crescendo and diminuendo at the end of the level (Al 1A).
- **At the keyboard.** Steps and skips; melodic and harmonic 2nds–5ths; sharp and flat signs (F Primer; Al 1A).
- **Creative.** Improvising on black or white keys (F Primer).

## Level A (F Level 1, Al 1B, R Prep A)

- **Reading.** Whole grand staff by landmarks (treble lines and spaces; bass guide notes); intervals 2nd–5th; 8va (F L1; Al 1B p. 28). Exam SR: two four-note stepwise melodies, each hand alone (R Prep A p. 9).
- **Keys and positions.** C and G five-finger patterns (F L1); Middle C and Middle D positions (Al 1B). Pentascales in C, G, D major and A minor, legato and staccato; C major triad sequence (R Prep A p. 9).
- **Rhythm.** Quarter, half and whole rests (F L1); eighth notes, upbeat, 2/4 (Al 1B pp. 10, 21–22). RCM clap-back already uses an eighth pair (R Prep A).
- **Technique.** Legato and staccato touches (F L1; R Prep A); first damper pedal (Al 1B p. 26; F L1 "occasionally").
- **Hands.** Hands together with simple left-hand intervals under a right-hand melody (Al 1B pp. 7, 11, 29). RCM keeps all tests hands separate (R Prep A).
- **Expression.** Accent, fermata, ritardando, crescendo/diminuendo (F L1; Al 1B).
- **At the keyboard.** Half steps, sharps and flats; tonic and dominant; I and V7 (two-note) in C and G (F L1). Whole steps, tetrachords and the major-scale pattern (Al 1B pp. 36–41).
- **Aural.** Clap back a rhythm; tell a major from a minor triad; play back a four-note melody in C or G (R Prep A p. 9).
- **Creative.** Improvising over audio accompaniments (F L1).
- *Differences.* Eighth notes come here in Alfred (1B) and at the next level in Faber (2A); the full major scale and key signature come here in Alfred (1B pp. 40–44) and at Level B in Faber (2B), RCM (Prep B) and the exams (Initial). **Choice:** eighth notes here (Alfred, RCM clap-back); full scales at Level B (the majority).

## Level B (F 2A–2B, Al 2, R Prep B, AB Initial, T Initial)

- **Reading.** All notes on both staves; the five C's to two ledger lines; 6ths, 7ths, octaves (F 2B; Al 2 pp. 8–26). Exam SR: four to six bars, five-finger position, hands separately, C major and D minor, legato, staccato, f and p (AB Initial); a short melody divided between the hands (R Prep B); C major in 2/4 (T Initial).
- **Keys.** Major and minor five-finger patterns in C, D, G, A (F 2A). One-octave scales: C, G, F major (F 2B); C, G, D major (Al 2); C, G major and A natural minor, hands separately, plus C major contrary motion hands together (R Prep B); C major and D minor (AB Initial); C major and A minor (T Initial). First arpeggios across a fifth (AB Initial) or cross-hand on I–V–I (F 2B).
- **Rhythm.** Eighth-note patterns (F 2A); eighth rest and dotted quarter (F 2B; Al 2 pp. 4–6); changing time signatures (Al 2 p. 11); uneven (swung) eighths in blues pieces (Al 2 p. 35).
- **Technique.** Thumb under and finger crossings for scales (F 2B; Al 2 p. 24); finger substitution (Al 2 p. 7); broken tonic triads in all positions (R Prep B).
- **Hands.** Hands together with block chords, ostinato and boogie bass, waltz bass (F 2A–2B); contrary-motion scale hands together (R Prep B; AB Initial).
- **Expression.** Phrases, crescendo/diminuendo, pp (F 2A–2B); connected (legato) pedalling (F 2B Unit 6; Al 2).
- **At the keyboard.** W-W-H-W; minor five-finger patterns and minor chords; first lead sheet (F 2A pp. 56–59). Triads; I–IV–V7 in C, G, F (F 2B) or C, G, D (Al 2), blocked and broken; transposing a piece to another key (F 2A Unit 2; Al 2 pp. 46–47); AB and ABA form (F 2B).
- **Aural.** Clap the pulse; clap back rhythms; sing back short phrases (tonic to mediant); say loud/quiet and smooth/detached (AB Initial). Major or minor; play back in C, G, A minor (R Prep B). Highest/lowest note (T Initial).
- **Creative.** Improvising with five-finger and full major scales (F 2A–2B); four bars over I and V in C (T Initial).
- *Differences.* The method books start connected pedalling here (F 2B, Al 1B–2); the exams put pedal in sight-reading only at T G5 and AB G6. **Choice:** pedal in learned pieces from here; sight-reading without pedal until Grade 5.

## Grade 1 (F 3A, Al 3, R L1, AB G1, T G1)

- **Reading and SR.** Ledger lines and the 7th (F 3A). SR: any five-finger position, hands separately; G, F major, A minor added; 3/4; occasional accidentals in minor keys; slurs, accents, mf, mp, hairpins (AB G1). RCM: a four-measure melody divided between the hands in C, G, F major or A minor (R L1). Trinity: G major, A minor, 4/4 (T G1).
- **Technique.** Scales one to two octaves, mostly hands separately: C hands together one octave; G, F major and A, D minor two octaves (AB G1); C, G, F major and A, E, D minor natural and harmonic, two octaves (R L1); F, G major and D, E minor one octave (T G1); C, G, F one octave hands together (F 3A). Contrary-motion scale hands together (AB, R, T). Chromatic scale begins (R L1; T G1; F 3A).
- **Arpeggios.** One octave, hands separately, in G major and A minor (AB G1; F 3A one-octave arpeggios). RCM has none until L4; Trinity none until G2. **Choice:** one-octave arpeggios here (two sources).
- **Rhythm.** Dotted half, four beamed eighths, half and quarter rests (AB G1 SR). The method books bring in 3/8, 6/8, triplets, swing and cut time in pieces (F 3A; Al 3); the exams leave compound time in sight-reading to AB G4 and T G5. **Choice:** learned pieces may use compound time from Grade 1–2; sight-reading stays in simple time until Grade 4.
- **Accompaniment patterns.** Alberti bass, waltz bass, ostinato, ragtime bass (F 3A).
- **Expression.** Pedalling by ear (F 3A); overlapping pedal (Al 3).
- **At the keyboard.** Binary and ternary form; primary chords in D (F 3A); i–iv–V7 in A minor (Al 3).
- **Aural.** Pulse in two or three time; sing back phrases; where a pitch change happens; dynamics and articulation (AB G1). Minor and major 3rds; major/minor triad; play back five notes (R L1). Higher or lower; where a change happens (T G1).
- **Creative.** Four bars improvised twice over I and V in F or G (T G1).
- **Pieces.** Three, one from each of three lists: AB A (agile), B (lyrical), C (varied styles); R A (Baroque–Classical), B (Romantic–21st c.), C (Inventions); T one list (AB G1; R L1; T G1).

## Grade 2 (F 3B–4, Al 3–4, R L2, AB G2, T G2)

- **Technique.** Scales two octaves, now hands together for the Grade 1 keys: G, F major and A, D minor hands together, D, A major and E, G minor hands separately (AB G2); G, F, B-flat major and E, D, G minor, harmonic and melodic (R L2); B-flat, D major and G, B minor hands together (T G2). Two-octave arpeggios hands separately (AB G2; T G2). Natural and harmonic A and D minor; octaves (F 3B).
- **SR.** Hands together; D major, E and G minor; whole note, dotted quarter–eighth, ties, pp (AB G2). Beyond five-finger position (R L2; ABRSM waits until G3). A minor with G sharp, 3/4, dotted minim, ties (T G2).
- **Rhythm.** Sixteenth-note patterns, syncopation, swing in pieces (F 3B); triplets, sixteenths and dotted eighths (Al 4).
- **Expression.** Voicing the melody, singing tone (F 3B).
- **At the keyboard.** The 12 major and minor triads; inversions; motive and sequence; 12-bar blues; grace notes (F 3B). Seventh chords and triad inversions (Al 4).
- **Aural.** Sing back to the dominant; identify a pitch or rhythm change; tempo (AB G2). Minor 3rd, major 3rd, perfect 5th; play back five notes (R L2). Major or minor; describe the dynamics (T G2).
- **Creative.** Eight bars over I, IV, V in A minor; a motivic response of six to eight bars (T G2).

## Grade 3 (F 4–5, Al 4–5, R L3, AB G3, T G3)

- **Technique.** D, A major and E, G minor hands together; B-flat, E-flat major and B, C minor hands separately; minors harmonic or melodic; E major contrary motion; chromatic contrary motion (AB G3). D, F, B-flat major and B, D, G minor hands together; formula pattern in D; two études (R L3). E-flat, A major and C, F sharp minor hands together (T G3). Two-octave scales and Power Scales in sixteenths, sharp key signatures (F 4); flat keys F, B-flat, E-flat, three forms of minor, two-octave arpeggios, circle of fifths, Hanon (F 5). Arpeggios hands together for the first set of keys (AB G3).
- **SR.** Up to 8 bars; 3/8; A, B-flat, E-flat major, B minor; outside five-finger position; two-note chords; dotted eighth–sixteenth and simple semiquavers (AB G3). Four measures hands together (R L3). D minor, rests, mp, slurs (T G3).
- **Rhythm.** Sixteenths in 6/8 (F 4); 12/8 and changing time signatures in pieces (F 5). Sight-reading meets 12/8 only at AB G8.
- **Expression.** Sforzando, trill (F 4); appoggiatura, trill, mordent (Al 5).
- **At the keyboard.** Roman-numeral chord analysis; four-note V7 (F 4). I–IV–I–V–I in many keys; perfect, major and minor intervals by name; cadences (F 5).
- **Aural.** Two, three or four time; sing back within an octave; major or minor (AB G3). Intervals m3, M3, P4, P5; root, third or fifth of a triad (R L3). Interval numbers 2nd–6th; locate a change on a printed copy (T G3).
- **Creative.** A waltz or allegretto over I, ii, IV, V (T G3).

## Grade 4 (Al 5–6, R L4, AB G4, T G4)

- **Technique.** B-flat, E-flat major and B, C minor hands together; B, F sharp, A-flat major and F sharp, F minor hands separately; contrary motion in E-flat and C harmonic minor; chromatic hands together two octaves; arpeggios in the same keys (AB G4). D, A, B-flat, E-flat major and B, G, C minor hands together; formula pattern in C minor; tonic arpeggios hands separately (R L4). A-flat, E major and F, C sharp minor, legato or staccato (T G4).
- **SR.** About 8 bars; 6/8; anacrusis; chromatic notes; pause; tenuto (AB G4). Four measures hands together in 3/4 or 4/4 (R L4). Hairpins, staccato, accents (T G4).
- **At the keyboard.** Diminished and augmented triads; the diminished 7th; thirty-second and dotted-sixteenth notes (Al 6).
- **Aural.** Sing or play back a melody from memory; sing five notes from a score; character; clap a rhythm and name the time (AB G4). Intervals to the octave; play back six to eight notes (R L4). Perfect or imperfect cadence; intervals by quality (T G4).
- **Creative.** Twelve bars in 2/4; a syncopated motivic response (T G4).

## Grade 5 (R L5–6, AB G5, T G5)

- **Technique.** All legato scales hands together, two octaves; staccato scales; diminished 7th from B (AB G5). Dominant 7th chords and tonic arpeggios (R L5); leading-tone diminished 7ths, chromatic hands together (R L6). D-flat, B major and B-flat, G sharp minor; arpeggios hands together; diminished 7th on B (T G5). *All three reach the diminished 7th here (R at L6); the dominant 7th comes here in RCM and at Grade 6 in ABRSM and Trinity.*
- **SR.** 8–12 bars; four-part chords (at most two notes per hand); simple syncopation; slowing at the end; ff (AB G5). A passage like Level 2 pieces, up to two sharps or flats, eight measures, or **a lead sheet realised with an accompaniment** (R L5–6). Modulation to the dominant; 6/8; tempo terms; simple pedalling (T G5).
- **Aural.** Style and period (AB G5). Intervals to the major 6th; dominant 7th quality; I–IV–I and I–V–I; **play back or improvise an answer phrase** (R L5). Time signature; changing tonality; perfect, plagal, imperfect, interrupted cadences (T G5).
- **Creative.** Twelve bars in 6/8 with up to two chords per bar (T G5).

## Grade 6 (R L7–8, AB G6, T G6)

- **Technique.** Four-octave scales, legato or staccato, hands together; dominant and diminished 7th arpeggios four octaves (AB G6; T G6; R L8). Scale in thirds, C major (T G6).
- **SR.** 12–16 bars; 9/8, 5/8, 5/4; triplets; clef changes; right pedal (AB G6). Pedalling required but not always marked (T G6). A passage like Level 4 pieces, 8–12 measures, or a lead sheet (R L7).
- **Aural.** Upper part of a two-part phrase from memory; sing with accompaniment; perfect or imperfect cadence; texture and structure (AB G6). All intervals within the octave; augmented triad; I–IV–V progressions (R L7). Modulation (T G6).
- **Creative.** Sixteen bars in 12/8 with 7ths (T G6).
- **Pieces.** Two groups from Grade 6 at Trinity (technique / expression); Bach Inventions appear (AB G6; R L7).

## Grade 7 (R L9, AB G7, T G7)

- **Technique.** Scales a third apart; scales in thirds hands separately; arpeggios in first inversion (AB G7). Scales in octaves; arpeggios in all inversions (R L9). E major in thirds (T G7).
- **SR.** 16–20 bars; 7/8, 7/4; tempo changes; 8va; una corda (AB G7). Any common terms; pedalling essential; modulation to any related key; 9/8 (T G7). Up to 16 measures, five sharps or flats (R L9).
- **Aural.** Cadences including interrupted; name chords I, IV, V, V7, vi; modulations (AB G7). Four-chord progressions (R L9). Modulation (T G7).
- **Creative.** Sixteen bars in 9/8 with all diatonic chords and 7ths (T G7).

## Grade 8 (R L10, AB G8, T G8)

- **Technique.** Scales a sixth apart; thirds and sixths hands separately; whole-tone scales; chromatic a sixth apart (AB G8). Scales separated by a 3rd or a 6th; chromatic in octaves (R L10). B major and C harmonic minor in thirds (T G8).
- **SR.** About a page; 12/8; three-part chords per hand; spread chords; simple ornaments; accelerando (AB G8). Double sharps and flats; changing time signatures; duplets; different dynamics in each hand (T G8).
- **Aural.** Lowest part of a three-part phrase; plagal cadence; chords with their positions; modulations; describe a piece (AB G8). Intervals to the 9th; 7th-chord qualities; five-chord progressions; harmonise a melody with I, IV, V (R L10).
- **Creative.** Sixteen bars in 5/4 with 7ths, 9ths and suspensions (T G8).
- **Pieces.** Three (AB, T) or five (R L10), including a Bach prelude and fugue (AB G8 and R L10 examples).

## Style lines

A style line stays where a published syllabus gives it substance, and only as high as that syllabus goes.

### Jazz (with blues): Grades 1–8

- **G1–5 (J Practical Grades).** Three tunes per grade from lists Blues, Standards, Contemporary Jazz, each with a notated head and at least one improvised solo; feel straight 8s or swing. Scales from memory in straight or swing: Dorian and Mixolydian (G1), blues scale (G2), Lydian (G3); minor and major pentatonics throughout. Broken chords on 7th chords (C7, G7, Am7, Gm7 at G4; D7, F7, B♭7, Em7, Bm7, Dm7 at G5). Quick study: improvise a two-bar (G1–2) then four-bar (G3–5) continuation over chord symbols. Aural: improvised answering phrases over a groove; identify swing, rock or Latin grooves (G4–5).
- **G6–8 (J Performance Grades).** Standards from lead sheets: head, improvised solo over the changes "using suitable jazz vocabulary and comping voicing", head; with a bass-and-drums backing; solos of 16 to 100 bars.
- **Voicings and modes alongside (RK).** 7th-chord voicings (G2) up to 9ths (G4), 11ths (G5), 13ths (G6), altered dominants (G7–8); Dorian and Mixolydian (G4), Lydian and Phrygian (G5), Lydian dominant and Locrian (G6), altered (G7), diminished (G8).
- **Blues inside this line.** Blues scales J G2–5, RK G2, AZ G4 (minor blues); 12-bar blues (F 3B); blues tunes on every J list to G5; boogie bass from F 2A, a boogie-woogie improvising style at TR G7. No separate blues syllabus was found.

### Popular keyboard (chords, pop, rock): Initial/Debut–Grade 8

- **Chord symbols and lead sheets** start in the beginner levels: first lead sheet (F 2A), chords for both hands from p. 18 of the adult course (Al Adult Level 1).
- **RSL Keys.** Chord voicings every grade (triads at Debut–G1, 7ths at G2, the harmonised scale at G3, extended and altered chords above); pentatonics from Debut; C blues scale at G2; swung feel from G3; harmonic recall of I, IV, V from G4. Improvising over a chord progression and backing track every grade: single-note melodies (Debut–G3), right-hand melody over left-hand chords (G4–5), melody and comping freely (G6–8).
- **Trinity Rock & Pop.** Improvising over a looped chord chart, with a set style per grade: simple rock and pop over I, IV, V (Initial); ballad, heavy rock (G1); country (G2); swung blues with 7th chords (G3); reggae, R&B with maj7 and m7 (G4); funk, shuffle, disco with sus4 (G5); Latin, metal with power, added-6th and slash chords (G6); jazz, boogie-woogie with 9ths, diminished and augmented chords (G7); any common or hybrid style (G8). Improvised solos and fills inside the songs from G4.
- **ANZCA.** Accompaniment from chord symbols (G2–3), melodic embellishment of a lead sheet (G4–8), a lead-sheet solo at the Associate diploma.

### Improvisation: a strand through every level, not a separate line

It is in the method books from the first lessons (F Primer–5), in Trinity's improvisation option from Initial to Grade 8 (stylistic, motivic, harmonic), in RCM's improvised answer phrase from Level 5, and in every grade of the jazz and popular lines above.

### Not kept as lines

- **Ragtime:** no graded syllabus found. Ragtime pieces exist as a repertoire category (AZ, from G1), and the ragtime bass is a pattern at F 3A. Placed as pieces in step 3.
- **Latin:** no dedicated syllabus. It appears as a repertoire category (AZ, from G3), an improvising style (TR G6) and Latin Real Book tunes (J G6–8). Placed as pieces in step 3.
- **Gospel and hymns:** no graded syllabus found (styles-found.md).
- **Rock and metal, holiday, jam:** rock and metal sit inside Popular keyboard. Holiday is a kind of piece, not a line. Jam (playing over a backing track) is an app mode, decided in step 4.
