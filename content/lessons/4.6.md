The end of the core path. Nothing new is introduced; two habits are made
permanent.

**Reading ahead.** By now your eyes should be a bar ahead of your hands, not a
beat. The way to train it is uncomfortable and quick: cover the bar you are
playing with a card, or set the app's window to hide it, so you are forced to
have already taken it in. Ten minutes of that is worth a week of ordinary
reading.

**Keeping going.** In performance a wrong note that you play through is nearly
invisible; a wrong note you stop and fix is the only thing anyone remembers.
Practise recovering: deliberately drop a note and carry on in time. The skill is
to keep the pulse while your hands catch up, and it is a separate skill from
playing correctly.

**Phrase shaping.** A phrase is a musical sentence, usually two or four bars,
and it needs a shape — generally growing towards its high point and easing at
the end. Decide where the peak of each phrase is and mark it. A piece played at
one dynamic from start to finish is a piece nobody wants to hear twice, however
accurate.

**A dynamics plan.** Write it on the score: where it is soft, where it grows,
where the loudest bar is. One plan for the whole piece, decided in advance, is
worth more than reacting bar by bar.

**Memorising a short piece.** Learn it in four-bar chunks, hands separately, and
test yourself by starting from the middle of a chunk rather than the beginning.
If you can only start from bar one you have memorised a sequence, not the music.

**Performance mode** scores you with no stopping and no second attempts, which
is the only honest measure of whether a piece is finished.

**What to do at the piano.** Choose two pieces by different composers — the
Petzold *Minuet in G* and one of the easy *Für Elise* settings works well.

**Common mistake.** Choosing two pieces that are too hard, and spending three
months on neither. Choose ones you can already nearly play.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Both pieces in Performance mode at 90 %
accuracy at 90 % tempo.
