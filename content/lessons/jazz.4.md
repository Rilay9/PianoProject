Jazz starts here with two things and no new chords: the rhythm, and where to put
a chord you already know.

**Swung eighths.** Two eighth notes in swing are not equal. The first is long and
the second short — roughly two thirds of the beat and one third. The
shuffle-eighths exercise is four bars of nothing else: clap it, then play it on
one note, until the long-short is automatic and you have stopped counting it.

**Comping is where, not what.** Accompanying a tune from its chord symbols, a
jazz pianist plays short chords in the gaps rather than on every beat. At this
rung the chords are plain triads. The three comping exercises are the same three
chords — C, F and G minor, in the key of F — in three rhythms:

- **The Charleston:** beat one and the "and" of two, then nothing.
- **Off-beats:** only on the "ands".
- **Four to the bar:** a short, light chord on every beat. The exercise calls it
  four on the floor.

Play each one through all four bars before trying the next, and swing the "ands".

**Reading the symbols as triads.** The songs' chord symbols have sevenths and
sixths in them — C7, Cm7, B♭m6. Here, play the triad: the letter, and minor if
it says so, and nothing else. C7 is a C chord; B♭m6 is B flat minor. The
sevenths are what the next rung's shell voicings are for.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Three options, all lead sheets from 1920 — a tune
with chord symbols printed over it. *Avalon* is the shortest and is in two;
*Whispering* is in E flat. *Avalon* is in F, the key the
comping exercises are in, and *Margie* has the same one flat. Play the tune with
the right hand and comp one pattern with the left from the symbols, then change
the pattern.

**Tools for this rung.** The accompaniment lab's *Jazz — two five one* preset
walks a bass line under a two-five-one. Set its key to F and comp the Charleston
over it: the bass keeps the time, so your chords only have to find the gaps.

**Common mistake.** Rushing the "and". The short note of the swing is late, not
early — it lands just before the next beat. Stabbing it early turns swing into a
march with a limp.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play one song's tune while the left
hand comps the Charleston from its symbols, without looking at the left hand, and
the "ands" sound lazy rather than hurried.
