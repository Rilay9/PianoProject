---
title: "The clave, and a tune to hear it under"
stage: 3
unit: "latin.3.1"
readingTime: 3
---

Almost everything in this music is measured against one two-bar rhythm. Learn it
now, before any of the left-hand patterns, because the patterns are all built to
fit around it and none of them makes sense on its own.

**A clave is two bars, not two rhythms.** One bar has three strokes and the
other has two, and which comes first is what the names mean: son 3-2 puts the
three-side first, son 2-3 the other way round. The commonest mistake is to count
the two bars separately. It is one unit; lose that and the pattern flips, which
sounds like the band turning over.

**Clap it before you play it.** The exercises here are written on a single line
with nothing to read but the rhythm, which is the point — there are no notes in
the way. Clap along until you stop counting, then keep clapping while you say
the beat numbers out loud, then stop saying them and see if it survives.

**Then put it against a pulse.** The version over a quarter-note pulse is two
parts at once and it is a real step up: one hand syncopated against one hand
that is not. That is the coordination everything else on this track needs.

**The tresillo is the clave's first half, and it is everywhere.** Three, three,
two — eight eighth notes grouped unevenly. Once you have heard it you will find
it under habaneras, tangos, reggaeton and most of what gets called a latin beat.

**Repertoire.** Three options, and they are chosen to be singable rather than
hard. *Guantanamera* is the tune to carry while your other hand claps, which
is the whole exercise: if the clave survives you singing, it is in your body
rather than in your counting. *Cielito Lindo* is in three and a clave is in
four, so play it for the tune rather than clapping under it. *Só Danço Samba* is
the first bossa on this track and a gentler rhythm than its name suggests — the
syncopation goes quiet and the chords do the work.

**Tools for this rung.** *Play it as a duet* lets the app take one hand while you
hold the other, which is the easiest way into the two-part clave without doing
both at once on day one.

**Common mistake.** Speeding up on the three-side. It has more strokes so it
feels busier, and busier feels faster. A metronome on beats two and four catches
it immediately.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can clap a son clave through a whole tune
while singing the melody, start on either side, and say which side you are on
without counting back to the beginning.
