Improvising over a blues is improvising over one chord that moves. Improvising
over changes is different, and this is where it starts.

**Guide tones first, notes later.** Play only the third and seventh of each
chord, in time. It sounds like nothing and it is the most useful twenty minutes
on this rung: those two notes are where the harmony lives, and a solo that
touches them at the right moments sounds like it knows the tune whatever else it
does.

**Modes are the note pool, not the melody.** Knowing that D dorian fits Dm7 tells
you which notes are safe. It does not tell you what to play, and a solo made of
scales run up and down is what happens when you stop there.

**Play the rhythm first.** Choose a rhythm — the comping patterns work — and
improvise the pitches into it. Most weak solos are weak rhythmically, not
harmonically.

**What to practise.** Guide tones through a ii–V–I in three keys. Then the same
progression with three notes a bar, chosen freely.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Not required; the changes are the material.

**Common mistake.** Playing continuously. Leave a bar empty and see what you play
in the next one.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play through a ii–V–I and land on a
chord tone at the start of each bar without planning it.
