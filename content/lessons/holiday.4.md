title: "Three cheap devices that sound expensive"

The first carol rung was about playing the tunes. This one is about making one
sound finished — the difference between someone practising and someone playing.
None of the three devices is difficult, and all of them are things you can
already do; what is new is choosing where to use them.

**The three devices.**

1. **Move the melody up an octave for the last verse.** The same tune, brighter
   and further from the left hand. Nothing else changes, and everyone hears it.
2. **Add an octave under the bass on the strong beats.** Play the bass note and
   the same note an octave lower together — on beat one, and in 4/4 on beat three
   as well. The piano gets a floor.
3. **Break the chords where it is quiet, block them where it is loud.** A broken
   chord moves and leaves room; a blocked one lands.

**The exercises are the moves under the devices.** The two broken-chord
exercises, in C and in F, are the chord spread out one note at a time, in the
two keys of the carols here. The oom-pah bass in F is a low bass note on the
beat, then the left hand back up to the chord — the jump you need when one hand
plays both the low bass and the chord.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Four options, each with its left hand written out,
and each asking for a different device first.

- *Silent Night*, in C, is a fuller setting of the tune from the first carol
  rung, in 3/4 this time. Its left hand is a broken chord in nearly every bar.
  Play it twice, and the second time block the chords and move the tune up an
  octave.
- *We Wish You a Merry Christmas*, in F, holds two-note chords in the left
  hand for most of it. Break them the first time through, and hold them the
  second.
- *Deck the Halls*, in C, has mostly a bass line in the left hand, not chords.
  Add the octave under its strong beats and it stops sounding like an exercise.
- *Away in a Manger*, in F, is set in four parts, like a hymn: two voices in each
  hand. Leave the parts alone and use device two gently — the low octave on beat
  one only.

Get one carol sounding finished rather than four sounding started.

**Tools for this rung.** *Accompaniment lab* opens the *Ballad — broken chords*
preset on *Hold the chords*: four chords spread out underneath and no tune at
all, which is the part you are adding. The key is free, so set it to F or C and
hear how much room a broken chord leaves above it; at the end of a time round
it says how many of your notes were in the scale, and keeps none of it. *Free
play* is for trying a device on a carol you know without anything marking the
notes you changed.

**Common mistake.** Using all three at once, all the way through. A device is
heard because of what is around it; an octave bass in every bar is just a
louder left hand.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play one of these through twice — the
second time with the devices in — and someone listening can tell which was
which.
