So far every note lasted one beat. Now they last longer, and the shape of the
note head tells you how long.

- **Quarter note** — filled head, stem: 1 beat.
- **Half note** — hollow head, stem: 2 beats.
- **Whole note** — hollow head, no stem: 4 beats.

Each is twice the one below it, which is the whole rhythmic system in one
sentence. Almost every value you will ever read is a doubling or a halving.

**Rests** are silences with the same values: a quarter rest (the squiggle), a
half rest (a small block sitting *on* the middle line), a whole rest (hanging
*under* the line above). The trick to reading rests is to count them exactly as
loudly as you count notes. A rest is not a pause — it is a beat that happens to
be silent, and letting it run long is the most common rhythm error there is.

**Holding a note is work.** A half note is not "play and wait"; it is play, then
keep the key down and keep counting, and release exactly on the next beat. If
you take your finger off early the note stops early, and the app scores that as
a missed duration.

**Phrasing.** Melodies come in phrases, the musical equivalent of sentences.
*Lightly Row* and *Twinkle* both fall into four-bar phrases ending on a long
note; that long note is where you breathe. Playing a tune without breathing at
the phrase ends is what makes a beginner sound like a metronome with fingers.

**What to do at the piano.** Play the rhythm-reading drill on a single pitch
first, so the only problem is time. Then *Lightly Row*, *Jingle Bells*,
*Twinkle Twinkle* and *Frère Jacques*.

Twinkle needs one extra thing: an A, one key above your finger 5. Move the whole
hand up a step for it and come back — the fingering marks both moves.

**Common mistake.** Cutting whole notes short to get to the next bar. Count to
four with the key still down.

**How you'll know you've got it.** One of these tunes at 90 % in Tempo mode with
no early releases.
