Before any notes: the piano is played with the arm, not the fingers alone, and
everything below is about getting the arm into a position where the fingers can
do their job.

**Sit so your forearms are level.** Bench height is right when a line from your
elbow to your knuckles is roughly horizontal, and your elbows sit slightly in
front of your body rather than pinned to your ribs. Too low and you push up into
the keys; too high and you press down with a locked wrist.

**Sit far enough back.** Many teachers start you on the front half of the
bench, both feet flat on the floor (on a box if they do not reach), roughly a
forearm's length from the keys. You should be able to lean towards the high and
low notes from the hips without your arms locking straight.

**Curved fingers.** Let your hand hang by your side and notice its natural
shape — slightly curled, thumb relaxed. That shape is the starting point on the keys; it changes as you play, and it should. The
usual image is *holding a bubble*: enough curve to keep the bubble whole,
not enough to pop it. Play on the pads of the fingers, with the nail joint
firm so it does not collapse backwards.

**Shoulders down, wrist level.** If your shoulders creep towards your ears you
are holding tension somewhere. Drop them and start again.

**Pain means stop.** If your hand, wrist, forearm or shoulder hurts while you
play, stop for the day. Pain is never something to play through; *When to stop*
in the practice track says more.

**Finger numbers.** Both hands: thumb is 1, index 2, middle 3, ring 4, little
finger 5. This is mirror-imaged, so finger 1 of each hand points at the other
hand. Wherever sheet music in this app prints finger numbers, these are the
numbers it uses; many pieces print none.

**Your HP-130.** Three controls matter for now: volume, the headphone socket,
and MIDI OUT (this is what lets PianoPath see what you play). Everything else
can wait.

**Common mistake.** Fingers that lock straight, or a hand that collapses at the
knuckles. If that happens, stop, let the hand hang by your side again, and start
from that shape.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can sit down, put a hand on the keys and
play a slow five-note walk with your shoulders down, your forearm level and your
wrist neither dropped nor raised — and nothing aches. That is yours to check:
the app cannot see how you sit.
