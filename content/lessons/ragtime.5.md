Ragtime is written-out music, not improvised, and it is built from one idea: a
strictly regular left hand under a right hand that refuses to line up with it.

**The oom-pah left hand.** Bass note on beats 1 and 3, chord on beats 2 and 4,
in 2/4 or 4/4. The bass note is usually the root or the fifth, an octave or a
tenth below the chord, so the hand leaps back and forth every half bar. Those
leaps are the technical problem of the whole style, and the solution is to
practise the left hand alone, looking at the *chord*, letting the arm travel
early and the eye lead it.

**Syncopation in the right hand.** The right hand's accents fall between the
left hand's beats. The commonest figure is short–long–short (a sixteenth, an
eighth, a sixteenth) across a beat, so the middle note is accented and lands on
an off-beat. It is not swung: **ragtime is played straight**, with even
subdivisions. Swinging it turns it into something else.

**"Not fast."** Joplin printed the instruction *"It is never right to play
ragtime fast"* on his covers, and he meant it. The syncopation only registers if
the pulse is steady and moderate; at speed everything blurs into a scramble.

**Form.** Rags are strings of sixteen-bar strains — AABBACCDD is common — each
its own tune, often with a key change at the trio. Learn them as separate
pieces; there is no development to worry about.

**How to practise.** Left hand alone until the leaps are silent and accurate.
Right hand alone, counting sixteenths out loud. Hands together at half tempo,
four bars at a time. This is a style where slow practice is not optional.

**Repertoire for this rung.** *The Entertainer* (easy arrangement), in either
edition in the library.

**Common mistake.** Rushing into hands together. The left hand must be
automatic first — you cannot think about a leap and a syncopation at once.

**How you'll know you've got it.** One strain hands together at a moderate,
unhurried tempo, with the left hand dead even.
