Everything on this track has been a piece of a tune. This rung is the tune.

**One standard, four ways.** Comp it. Walk the bass and comp on top. Stride it.
Then play the melody with your own harmony underneath. The same 32 bars each
time; the point is that the form becomes something you are inside rather than
something you are reading.

**Learning it by ear.** The ear-tune drill gives you eight bars and takes them
back a phrase at a time. That is how a standard is actually learned — not from
the page, and not all at once.

**Roman numerals are the memory.** A tune you know as chord symbols is a tune in
one key. A tune you know as numerals is a tune in twelve. The secondary-dominant
drill is here because standards are full of them, and `V7/vi` is four characters
instead of a chord you would otherwise have to memorise separately in every key.

**What to practise.** One tune, twenty minutes at a time, in the four ways
above. Then the same tune a fourth higher.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Whichever standard you have been carrying since
Stage 7. Changing it now would restart the clock.

**Common mistake.** Learning six tunes badly instead of one properly. The second
tune takes a fifth of the time the first one did, and only if the first one is
finished.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can start the tune in a key you have not
practised it in, and get through the form.
