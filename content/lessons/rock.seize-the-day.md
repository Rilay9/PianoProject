**Not bundled.** This song is in copyright. The catalog entry carries an import
hint and no file; bring your own MusicXML through the import screen.

**What the piano part has to do.** A slow minor-key ballad built on a repeating
arpeggiated figure over a bass that changes rarely. The interest is in the
harmony moving underneath an almost unchanging right-hand pattern, and in the
long build from quiet to full.

**The techniques.**

- **Arpeggio over a pedal bass.** A left-hand bass note held (or re-struck) while
  the right hand cycles a broken chord. The pedal changes with the bass, not
  with the arpeggio.
- **Voicing the top note.** In a repeated arpeggio the melody is usually the
  highest note of each group; it has to sing above the others, which means
  finger 5 plays slightly louder than 1–2–3. This is a specific skill and it is
  worth ten minutes on its own.
- **Dynamic architecture.** The part exists to grow. Decide the softest bar and
  the loudest bar in advance and plan the route between them.

**Public-domain vehicles.** Beethoven *Moonlight Sonata* first movement — the
same texture, the same pedal problem, and enough of it to build stamina. Chopin
*Prélude* op. 28 no. 4 for slow minor harmony moving under a static right hand.

**What to listen for in the recording.** Where the bass note actually changes,
and whether the piano is doubling the guitar's arpeggio or filling underneath
it. Count the bars between changes — most of the part is the same two or four
bars repeated.

**Common mistake.** Pedalling per arpeggio instead of per harmony, which turns
the texture to mud within two bars.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play the *Moonlight* opening with the
top notes clearly above the inner ones and the pedal clean, and you can hear
that same shape in the song.
