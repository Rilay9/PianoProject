PianoPath watches what you play over MIDI and scores it. Knowing which mode to
use is most of the difference between practice that works and practice that
merely passes time.

**Wait mode** holds the score still until you play the right note. Nothing moves
without you. Use it the first time you meet a piece: it turns reading into a
puzzle you cannot fail, only take time over.

**Tempo mode** plays a click and moves on whether you are with it or not, and
this is the mode that scores you. Accuracy is the fraction of notes you got
right and on time; tempo % is how fast you ran relative to the piece's target.
A *pass* is 90 % accuracy at 80 % of target tempo; *mastered* is 97 % at full
tempo, twice, on different days.

**Hand focus** silences one staff so you can play hands separately, which is how
almost every piece should start.

**Tempo %** slows the click. Slow practice is not a lesser version of fast
practice — it is the only kind that changes what your hands do. Play at a speed
where you make no mistakes, then raise it a notch.

**Loops and bars-per-window.** A loop repeats a few bars until they are right;
the window controls how much music is on screen. Two bars in the window is a
magnifying glass; eight is a reading exercise.

**The review queue** brings a passed item back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days. This
spacing is not arbitrary: material re-met just as it starts to fade is what
sticks. Do the review items first in a session, not last.

**The 30-minute template**: 5 minutes warm-up, 10 new material, 10 review, 5
free playing. The last five minutes are not optional — playing something you
already enjoy is what makes you come back tomorrow.

**Common mistake.** Practising in Tempo mode from the start, at full speed,
making the same mistake forty times. You are rehearsing the mistake.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You have run *Hot Cross Buns* in Wait mode,
then in Tempo mode at 60 %, and you can say what your accuracy was and what you
would change next time.
