Four things that turn correct notes into music.

**A tie** is a curved line joining two notes *of the same pitch*: play the first,
hold through the second, do not re-strike. It exists because note values are all
doublings, so a duration like three-and-a-half beats often cannot be written any
other way — and because a note can be held across a bar line.

**A slur** is the same curve joining notes of *different* pitch, and it means
something completely different: play them smoothly, connected, without a gap.
Same shape, opposite instruction; the only way to tell is whether the pitches
match.

**The dotted quarter.** A dot adds half the note's value, so a dotted quarter is
one and a half beats and is almost always followed by an eighth note to fill out
the second beat. Count "1 and 2 and": the dotted quarter covers "1 and 2", the
eighth lands on "and". Do not learn it as "long, short" by feel — place the
short note on the "and" and it is right every time.

**Dynamics** are how loud: *p* (piano, soft), *mp*, *mf*, *f* (forte, loud), with
hairpins for *crescendo* (getting louder) and *diminuendo*. On a piano this is
entirely about how fast the key goes down — your HP-130 sends that as MIDI
velocity, and the app shows it on a meter. Loud is not "hit harder"; it is
"drop faster from a relaxed arm".

**Tempo words**: Andante (walking pace), Moderato (moderate), Allegro (quick),
*ritardando* (gradually slowing, usually at the end).

**What to do at the piano.** Dotted-rhythm reading, the dynamics drill (play a
phrase *p*, then *f*, and watch the meter), then *Greensleeves* — a minor-key
tune built almost entirely out of the dotted-quarter-plus-eighth figure.

**Common mistake.** Rushing the eighth after a dotted quarter so it arrives
early. Count the "and" out loud until the figure is automatic.

**How you'll know you've got it.** One song played through, plus a dynamics
contrast where your average *f* velocity is at least 1.6 times your average *p*.
