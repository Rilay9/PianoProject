Four octaves, in sixteenths, at a quarter-note pulse of 120. That is sixteen
notes a second in each hand, and it is the standard this stage is measured
against.

The exercises here are not new material. They are the scales you have played
since Stage 2, over a wider span and much faster, and the only thing that has
changed is that there is no longer room to think between notes.

**How to get there.** Not by playing them fast. Set the tempo where the passage
is genuinely clean — including the thumb, including the turn at the top — and
move it up a few beats at a time, dropping back the moment evenness goes. A
scale practised fast and uneven is a scale learnt uneven: the hand does not know
you meant to fix it later.

**Use Wait mode first.** It will not move on until the notes are right, which is
the only reliable way to discover that the F sharp you have been playing for a
year is actually an F. Then Tempo mode at eighty per cent, then at full.

**What endurance means here.** Four octaves up and down is roughly twice the
work of two, and the first thing to fail is not accuracy but the wrist. If it
aches, stop. There is no version of this rung worth an injury, and every piece
it prepares you for will still be there next week.
