**What the piano part has to do.** A slow ballad that carries the melody as much
as the harmony. On a solo piano this is the arrangement type where you play the
tune in the right hand, in octaves when it needs weight, over sustained chords
and a moving bass.

**The techniques.**

- **Melody in octaves.** The same note in both the little finger and the thumb of
  the right hand, with the inner notes of the chord filled in by the remaining
  fingers. It doubles the volume of the tune without playing harder. Practise the
  octave shape as a fixed frame that the hand moves as one unit.
- **Sustained chords with a moving bass.** The left hand plays a bass line that
  walks between chord roots while the harmony above is held. This is what stops a
  slow ballad standing still.
- **Long-range dynamics.** Verse quiet, chorus full, last chorus fuller. Write the
  plan on the score.

**Public-domain vehicles.** Chopin *Prélude* op. 28 no. 20 for chordal weight
and a dynamic arc in thirteen bars; *Greensleeves* with block chords for melody
over held harmony; the Stage 4.3 inversion work for the moving bass.

**What to listen for in the recording.** Where the arrangement thins out. Ballad
arrangements almost always drop to near-nothing before the last chorus, and
reproducing that drop matters more than reproducing the loud part.

**Common mistake.** Playing octaves from the fingers. The hand shape stays fixed
and the arm moves; otherwise you will be tired and out of tune with yourself
after a page.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play a melody in octaves over held
left-hand chords for sixteen bars, evenly, at a soft dynamic.
