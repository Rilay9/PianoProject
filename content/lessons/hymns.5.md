title: "Walk-ups, passing chords, and the chord that is not in the key"
stage: 5

readingTime: 3

The rung below gave you four voices with every note printed. This one takes
most of them away: the songs here are lead sheets and thin piano parts with the
chords named above the stave, and the point is what you add in the gaps.

**Walk to the next chord instead of waiting for it.** Where a hymn holds one
chord for two beats, put the bass on the move. Inside the key first: from the
root you are on, step up or down to the root of the next chord. Then through
the note between, which is not in the key and does not need to be — it passes,
it lands, and nobody hears it as wrong. The walk-up study is those two versions
of the same four bars, one after the other.

**A passing chord is the same idea with the whole hand.** Take the chord you
are going to, move all of it up a half step, play that, and let it fall. The
half-step study does it twice, in its two middle bars: an E flat minor seventh
sliding into D minor seventh, an A flat seven into G seven. Nothing is held.
The slash-chord study is the other half — in its first and third bars the right
hand holds one triad while the bass steps down underneath it.

**The chord that is not in the key is usually the dominant of the next one.**
One trick rather than many: any chord can be preceded by its own five chord,
borrowed from outside the key. Three of the songs print one, and reading the
symbol is enough to find them.

**Repertoire.** Five, easiest first. *What a Friend We Have in Jesus* is in D
on one stave and has all three devices printed: a G sharp diminished between
the G chord and the D, an E major whose G sharp is not in the key, and a D
seventh pulling to the G. *Down by the Riverside* is a bare tune in F,
thirty-three bars, with only F, B flat and C seven above it — three chords and
an empty left hand, the cheapest place to start. *This Little Light of Mine* is
in B flat, and its fourth bar is a B flat seventh: a seventh on the home chord,
whose A flat is not in the key, taking you to the four chord in the bar after.
*Just a Closer Walk with Thee* has a diminished chord and a bass that walks
down under a held seventh. *As the Deer* is the fullest, with an E major in its
twelfth bar pulling into the A minor that follows.

**Tools for this rung.** *Free play* names what you are holding: put three or
more notes down and the screen prints the chord. Build an approach chord by
ear, a half step above where you are going, and let the screen tell you what
you found. That is a faster way to learn the sound of one than reading its
name first.

**Common mistake.** Decorating before the harmony is right. Both devices are
ornaments on a sequence that already works, and a walk-up into the wrong chord
is a louder mistake. Play it plain until it is boring, then move the bass.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play one of these twice: once
exactly as printed, then with the bass walking into two of the changes and one
half-step approach somewhere — and the second pass does not sound busier, it
sounds like the same tune with more under it.
