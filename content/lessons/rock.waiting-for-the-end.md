**Not bundled.** In copyright; import your own MusicXML.

**What the piano part has to do.** A groove-based song where the keyboard's
contribution is rhythmic placement more than harmony: chords struck off the beat
over a half-time feel, leaving the downbeats to the drums.

**The techniques.**

- **Half-time feel.** The pulse is counted at half speed, so a bar contains twice
  as much space and the backbeat lands on beat three instead of beats two and
  four. Count it slowly and deliberately; the mistake is to keep hearing the
  fast pulse and crowd the bar.
- **Syncopated chord stabs.** A chord on the "and" of two, tied over beat three,
  is a completely different instruction from a chord on beat three — and it is
  the one that grooves. Stage 4.5's syncopation work is the prerequisite.
- **Open voicings across two hands.** Root and fifth low, third and ninth high,
  nothing in the middle. The gap is what makes it sound like a record rather
  than a hymn.
- **Playing with a click.** This whole style is written to a grid. Practise with
  the metronome on beats two and four only; it is much harder and it is the
  standard.

**Public-domain vehicles.** The Stage 4.5 syncopation and 6/8 material; the
twelve-bar blues comping exercises for off-beat chord placement; Satie
*Gymnopédie no. 1* for spacing chords wide and letting them ring.

**What to listen for in the recording.** Count where each chord actually lands
relative to the beat. Write it down before you play it — syncopation is nearly
impossible to copy by feel.

**Common mistake.** Straightening the rhythm onto the beat because it is easier.
That removes the entire point of the part.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Eight bars of syncopated chords with the
metronome clicking only on two and four, no drifting.
