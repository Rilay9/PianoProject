Up to now you have read notes by name: "that's an E, play E." That is slow, and
it does not scale. From here you read by **interval** — by how far the next note
is from the one you are on.

**A step** is a 2nd: the next letter up or down, and on the page it moves from a
line to the neighbouring space, or a space to the neighbouring line.

**A skip** is a 3rd: it misses a letter out, and on the page it goes line to
line, or space to space.

That is the whole trick, and it is visual. Line-to-space means step. Line-to-
line means skip. You do not need to know which note either of them is; your hand
already knows where it is, so all it needs is the distance.

**Looking ahead.** While you play a note, your eyes should be on the next one.
This feels wrong and is essential — reading music is like reading text aloud,
where you are always a few words ahead of your voice. Start by looking ahead one
beat. That is enough to stop the stumbling.

**The sight-reading generator** makes a new four-bar melody every time you press
it. This is the single most valuable practice tool in the app, because you can
only sight-read music you have never seen, and everything else you own you have
already seen. Two or three fresh melodies a day, at a tempo slow enough that you
never stop, does more for reading than an hour on a piece you know.

**What to do at the piano.** Sight-reading level 1, the interval ear drill (2nd
versus 3rd, played back), and *Steps and Skips in C*, a study where every
interval is a 2nd or a 3rd and the hand never leaves C position.

**Common mistake.** Stopping to fix a wrong note while sight-reading. Do not.
Keep going, keep the pulse, and take the loss — the skill being trained is
continuity, and stopping trains the opposite.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Five generated melodies at 90 % or better on
the first attempt, with no stops.
