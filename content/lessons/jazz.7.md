Shells say the chord in two notes. This rung is about the four-note voicings
that say it in colour, and the substitution that changes the bass without
changing the sound.

**Rootless A and B.** Drop the root — a bass player has it, and on your own the
left hand can put it down. What is left is third, fifth, seventh and ninth,
arranged two ways so that the hand barely moves through a ii–V–I. Learn one key
in A, then the same key in B, and notice that the top note is different: that is
the reason both exist. Whichever puts the melody note on top is the one you want.

**Quartal voicings** are stacked fourths. From D: D, G, C, F. Read from the root
that is a D minor eleventh, which is what the chart calls it, but the sound is
not a chord with a name — it is the sound of modal jazz and of most film music
written since 1960. They are worth practising precisely because you cannot find
them by thinking in thirds.

**The tritone substitution.** G7 and D♭7 share their third and seventh — B and F,
F and C♭, the same two notes spelled differently. So D♭7 resolves to C exactly as
well as G7 does, and the bass walks down a semitone instead of leaping a fourth.
Play ii–V–I and ii–subV–I back to back until the second stops sounding like a
mistake.

**Stride** is on this rung because the left hand needs somewhere to go when it is
not walking: bass, chord, tenth, chord. The tenth on beat three is what makes it
stride, and it is the leap you will miss.

**What to practise.** One ii–V–I in rootless A and again in rootless B; the same
progression with the tritone substitution; a quartal voicing moved through three
roots without renaming it each time.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Import a standard from 1920 or earlier and
reharmonise its dominants with the substitution. *Avalon* takes it well.

**Common mistake.** Reaching for rootless voicings before the shells are
automatic. The shells are what you fall back on when the tempo goes up.

**How you'll know you've got it.** A ii–V–I in four keys with rootless voicings,
and one chorus where you substitute every dominant without stopping.
