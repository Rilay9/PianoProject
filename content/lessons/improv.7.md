This rung is about what a piece sounds like rather than what it does.

**Quartal voicings are a texture you can own.** Stacked fourths have no
third-based identity, so they float: the same shape sits over several chords, and
moving it in parallel is a sound rather than a progression. Play one shape up a
scale and listen — that is thirty seconds of film music, and you did not choose
a single chord.

**Extensions are colour choices.** A ninth is bright, a flat ninth is tense, a
sharp eleventh is the sound of something unresolved on purpose. Practise them as
sounds, not as spellings.

**Constraint makes a style.** Pick three notes and one rhythm and improvise for
two minutes. What comes out is more like a piece than anything you would get from
the whole keyboard, because a style is a set of things you have decided not to
do.

**What to practise.** One quartal shape moved through a mode. One extension a day
in five keys. One two-minute improvisation under a constraint you wrote down
first.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Your own. Record the constraint improvisations and
keep one a week.

**Tools for this rung.** One button, *Free play*: no piece, no marking and
nothing kept, which is the only screen in the app that matches what this rung
asks you to do. It names a chord from three or more notes held down together,
so a quartal shape you like can be held and named — and when it names nothing,
that is the answer too, because a stack of fourths is not a chord with a name.
There is no other tool here on purpose: the two-minute improvisation under a
constraint is not something the app can mark.

**Common mistake.** Adding colour before there is anything to colour. A good
melody in triads beats a weak one in thirteenths.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Someone could recognise two of your
improvisations as being by the same person.
