A **triad** is three notes stacked in thirds — take a letter, skip one, take the
next, skip one, take the next. Start on C: C, (skip D), E, (skip F), G. That is
the C major chord. On the keyboard it is every-other-white-key, which is why a
triad in root position always looks like a snowman on the staff: three note
heads all on lines, or all on spaces.

The three chords of this unit:

- **C** = C–E–G
- **F** = F–A–C
- **G** = G–B–D

**Roman numerals.** Counting the notes of the C major scale, C is the 1st degree,
F the 4th and G the 5th, so these three are called **I, IV and V**. Nearly every
folk, hymn and pop song you know is mostly these three chords, in some key. That
is not a coincidence — I, IV and V between them contain all seven notes of the
scale, so any melody note has a home.

**Chord symbols** are the letters printed above the staff: "C", "F", "G". They
say what harmony is sounding, not which notes to play or in what order. Where a piece
on this rung writes the left hand out as blocks, as *Happy Birthday (simple)*
and *Jingle Bells* do, it plays the symbol as a block — all three notes at
once — with fingers 5, 3 and 1. Four songs print only the tune and its symbols.

**Moving between them.** In root position the left hand drops to reach both: C
to F is a **fifth** down, C to G a **fourth** down. (Going up instead they are
the other way round — a fourth up to F, a fifth up to G — which is the usual
reason this gets remembered backwards.) Both are big jumps, and both land on a
long note, so there is time. Look where you are going *before* you leave.

**What to do at the piano.** The chord drill (the app names a chord, you play
all three notes and MIDI checks them; it accepts them one after another too,
so keep them together), C–F–G changes at 60 bpm, then
*Happy Birthday (simple)*, whose blocks are C, F and G (its G7 symbol sits over
a plain G triad), and *Jingle Bells*, which is in G, so its blocks are G, C and
D7.

**Common mistake.** Playing the three notes as a rolled ripple instead of
together. The app does not catch a roll, so listen for
it; the fix is to place all three fingers on the keys silently first, then
press.

**One more tune.** *Skip to My Lou* is on this rung: two chords, D and A,
over a tune you already know, printed as one staff with the symbols above it.
D holds for two bars, then A for two and D for two; after that A and D take
one bar each. It is the simplest
possible song to practise a chord change in, which is why it is here.

**Tools for this rung.** The chord drill has two ways out when F will not
come. *Show me* lights all three notes and prints the chord as a whole note
under the words; *Hear it* plays it. Both give up the mark for that card, so
a set you listened your way through cannot pass, which is the point of
having them.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Twenty chord changes at 95 % (two sets of ten), and one song
hands together with the chords landing clean on the beat.
