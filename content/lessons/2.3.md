A **triad** is three notes stacked in thirds — take a letter, skip one, take the
next, skip one, take the next. Start on C: C, (skip D), E, (skip F), G. That is
the C major chord. On the keyboard it is every-other-white-key, which is why a
root-position triad looks like a snowman on the staff: three note heads all on
lines, or all on spaces.

The three chords of this unit:

- **C** = C–E–G
- **F** = F–A–C
- **G** = G–B–D

**Roman numerals.** Counting the C major scale, C is the 1st degree, F the 4th
and G the 5th, so these three are called **I, IV and V**. Nearly every folk,
hymn and pop song is mostly these three, in some key. That
is no coincidence: between them they hold all seven notes of the scale, so any
melody note has a home.

**Chord symbols** are the letters printed above the staff: "C", "F", "G". They
say what harmony is sounding, not which notes to play or in what order. Where a
piece writes the left hand out as blocks, as *Happy Birthday (simple)* and
*Jingle Bells* do, it plays the symbol all three notes at once, fingers 5, 3
and 1. Four songs print only the tune and its symbols.

**Moving between them.** In root position the left hand drops to reach both: C
to F is a **fifth** down, C to G a **fourth** down. (Going up they swap — a fourth to F, a fifth to
G — which is why this gets remembered backwards.) Both land on a long note, so
there is time. Look where you are going *before* you leave.

**What to do at the piano.** The chord drill (the app names a chord, you play
all three notes; it accepts them one after another too, so keep them
together), C–F–G changes at 60 bpm, then
*Happy Birthday (simple)*, whose blocks are C, F and G (its G7 symbol sits over
a plain G triad), and *Jingle Bells*, which is in G, so its blocks are G, C and
D7.

**Common mistake.** Playing the three notes as a rolled ripple instead of
together. The app does not catch a roll, so listen for it;
place all three fingers on the keys silently first, then press.

**One more tune.** *Skip to My Lou* is on this rung: two chords, D and A,
over a tune you already know, on one staff with the symbols above it. D holds
two bars, then A two and D two; after that a bar each. It is the simplest song
there is to practise a chord change in.

**Tools for this rung.** The chord drill has two ways out when F will not
come. *Show me* lights all three notes and prints the chord as a whole note
under the words; *Hear it* plays it. Both give up the mark for that card, so
a set you listened your way through cannot pass, which is the point of
having them. *Accompaniment lab* opens *Primary chords — one four five* with
the app playing the tune, so these three underneath it are yours to land on
the beat; it starts in D and the key is free, so set it to C. *Free play*
names any chord you hold down, so a shape you are unsure of can be checked
with nothing marking it.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Twenty chord changes at 95 % (two sets of ten), and one song
hands together with the chords landing clean on the beat.
