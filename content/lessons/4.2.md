**The flat keys** go the other way round the circle: F (B♭), B flat (B♭ E♭),
E flat (B♭ E♭ A♭). The flats always arrive in the order B, E, A, D, G — and the
last flat in the signature is always the *fourth* degree of the key, which is a
quick way to read a signature you do not recognise: look at the last flat and go
down a fourth.

**Fingering changes here**, and this is the part beginners get wrong. F major's
right hand is **1-2-3-4-1-2-3-4** — the thumb passes after the fourth finger, not
the third, because B flat is a black key and the thumb should not land on it. B
flat major starts the right hand on finger 2. The governing principle for every
scale: **the thumb avoids black keys**. If you can remember that, you can
reconstruct most fingerings you have forgotten.

**Minor scales, two forms.**

- **Harmonic minor** raises the seventh, up and down. A B C D E F G♯ A. The gap
  between F and G sharp is a step and a half, which is what gives the scale its
  eastern colour — and makes it awkward under the hand.
- **Melodic minor** raises the sixth *and* seventh going up, and cancels both
  coming down, so the descending form is plain natural minor. It exists because
  singers found that step-and-a-half unsingable, and the ascending form smooths
  it out.

Both are used. Harmonic minor is where the chords come from; melodic minor is
what melodies actually do.

**What to do at the piano.** F, B flat and E flat major hands together; A, E and
D harmonic and melodic minor. One to two octaves, contrary motion first as
before. Then a Grade 1 piece in a flat key or a minor key — *Bella Ciao* or the
beginner *Für Elise*.

**Common mistake.** Using C major fingering in F major and landing the thumb on
B flat. It works at slow tempo and collapses at speed.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Three flat-key scales and three minor scales
hands together at 80 bpm at 95 %.
