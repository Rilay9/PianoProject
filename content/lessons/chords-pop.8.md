The most useful thing a chord-player can do, and the thing that separates
someone who plays songs from someone who accompanies people.

**Transposing is a numeral problem.** A song in G that has to be in E flat is not
twelve chords to relearn; it is the same numerals over a different tonic. The
transposition drill on this rung prints four bars and asks for them a stated
interval away, and it judges you against the printed music moved — so there is
no separate answer key that can be subtly wrong.

**Do it by ear as well as by sight.** Play the song in the new key without
writing anything down. Slower, worse, and much more useful than reading a
transposed copy.

**Secondary dominants survive transposition too.** `V7/vi` is `V7/vi` in every
key, which is the entire argument for learning the numerals rather than the
letters.

**What to practise.** Four bars up a tone, then a minor third, then down a
fourth. Then one whole song, one key away, without notation.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Anything with a singer in it.

**Common mistake.** Transposing the shapes rather than the harmony. It works in
the white keys and falls apart in the flat ones.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Someone says "a bit lower" and you do it in
the next bar.
