Five fingers cover five keys. Music does not stop after five keys, so the hand
has to move — and there are exactly two ways it does so.

**A position shift** is the crude one: lift, move, land. Fine when there is a
long note or a rest to do it in. The printed fingering is what tells you a shift
is happening — a number that does not follow on from the last one means "your
hand is somewhere else now". *Ode to Joy (full theme)* uses one in bar 12, where
the tune drops to the G below middle C.

**Thumb under** is the smooth one, and it is how scales work. Right hand going
up C major: fingers 1-2-3 on C-D-E, then while finger 3 is still holding E the
thumb travels *underneath* the hand to F, and 1-2-3-4-5 finishes on F-G-A-B-C.
Coming down, finger 3 crosses *over* the thumb. Left hand going up is the mirror
image: 5-4-3-2-1 on C-D-E-F-G, then finger 3 crosses over the thumb onto A.

Two rules make it work. The thumb moves *early*, sliding under while the other
fingers are still playing, so it arrives before it is needed. And the elbow
stays quiet — if your wrist twists or your elbow swings out, the thumb is
arriving late and the hand is compensating.

**What to do at the piano.** The *Position shift* exercises first (the
position-shift drill plays the five-finger walk in the home position and then again from the fifth), then the C major scale
one octave, hands separately, slowly, watching only the thumb. Then *Ode to Joy (full theme)*, the
piece this rung is for: its one move is the bar-12 shift, not a thumb-under,
so the scale is where the thumb gets its practice. The *Ode to Joy (easy
variation)* on this rung is in G already.

**The same tune, a fifth higher.** Once the theme goes in C, play its right hand
again with your thumb on G instead of C: every note five letters higher, so E
becomes B, F becomes C and the bar-12 drop to the lower G lands on the lower D.
Most of the tune sits in the G position (G A B C D under your five fingers), and
there is no black key. Where the C version reaches down to its lower G, reach
down, or shift your hand, to the D below the position. Work it out with your ear
and the fingering you already have, and do not look it up.
Then check it: *Check your G version, page hidden* opens *Ode to Joy (in G
major)* with the notation hidden from the start. Choose R, turn the keys guide
off (Settings, *Keys guide*, *Off*; or *Keys* to nothing in the ⋯ controls),
and play your version. That printed edition is the C theme moved up a fifth note
for note, so the app marks your notes against exactly what you were aiming for.
What it cannot know is how you found them: it does not record that the page was
hidden, and it cannot tell transposing from reading, so do not open that edition
with the page showing until you have played it this way. If *Today* offers it to
you before then, leave it for another day.

**Common mistake.** A bump on the thumb note. It is louder than its neighbours
because the whole arm shifted to get it there. Play the scale listening for that
one note and make it the same as the others — that is the entire exercise.

**The reading drill.** *Sight-read, right hand* is here as it was three rungs
ago, the same drill: its phrases already reach up to the C above middle C,
beyond C position, so the thumb-under from this lesson gets read at sight rather than rehearsed.

**Tools for this rung.** *Play it blind* is the *Check your G version, page hidden*
button above.

**How you'll know you've got it.** C major scale, hands separately, one octave
up and down in eighth notes at 60 bpm, 95 % accuracy, with no audible bump where the
thumb passes. And the theme's right hand played in G from your own working-out,
checked with the page hidden; the app marks the notes, and that you worked them
out rather than read them is yours to know.
