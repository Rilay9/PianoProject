## Why bother

Because you cannot listen to yourself while you are reading. The eyes take
almost everything, and the moment the page is gone you start hearing the
balance between your hands, the shape of a phrase, the note you have been
fudging for a fortnight. Memorising is not a party trick — it is the fastest
way to find out what you are actually playing.

It is also the only way to play for somebody without a music stand between you
and them.

## How to do it

Not by repetition. Playing a piece forty times until your fingers know it
produces a memory that fails under pressure, because it is one long chain and a
chain breaks at its weakest link.

Instead:

- **In pieces.** Take four bars. Play them looking at the page twice, then
  close it and play them again. Four bars is enough; a page is not.
- **From several starting points.** Be able to begin at bar 9, at bar 17, at
  the double bar. A memory you can only enter from the beginning is a memory
  that ends the run when it fails.
- **Away from the piano.** Hear the four bars in your head, in the right key,
  with the fingering. If you cannot, you have not memorised them — your hands
  have.
- **Slowly, at least once.** Speed hides gaps. Half tempo shows you exactly
  where you are guessing.

## What the app does here

Pick a piece you already play well. Play it twice with the score, cleanly —
that is the app checking you have the notes before it takes them away. Then
choose **Blind**: the score disappears, the keyboard strip and the cursor stay,
and everything else about the run is exactly the same. It is judged the same
way too, which is the point — the blind number and the sighted number are
comparable, and the rung wants 90 % of what you managed with the page.

## When you have got it

You can start anywhere, at half tempo, without the page — and the run scores
within a tenth of your sighted one. When it does, the piece is yours in a way
it was not last week.
