**Why bother.** Because you cannot listen to yourself while you are reading.
The eyes take almost everything, and the moment the page is gone you start
hearing the balance between your hands, the shape of a phrase, the note you
have been fudging for a fortnight. Memorising is not a party trick — it is the
fastest way to find out what you are actually playing.

It is also the only way to play for somebody without a music stand between you
and them.

**How to do it.** Not by repetition. Playing a piece forty times until your
fingers know it produces a memory that fails under pressure, because it is one
long chain and a chain breaks at its weakest link.

Instead:

- **In pieces.** Take four bars. Play them looking at the page twice, then
  close it and play them again. Four bars is enough; a page is not.
- **From several starting points.** Be able to begin at bar 9, at bar 17, at
  the double bar. A memory you can only enter from the beginning is a memory
  that ends the run when it fails.
- **Away from the piano.** Hear the four bars in your head, in the right key,
  with the fingering. If you cannot, you have not memorised them — your hands
  have.
- **Slowly, at least once.** Speed hides gaps. Half tempo shows you exactly
  where you are guessing.

**What the app does here.** Pick a piece you already play well. Play it twice
with the score, cleanly — that is you checking you have the notes before you
take them away; the app does not check. Then choose **Blind**: the score and
its cursor disappear, the keyboard strip, the count-in and the beat dot stay,
and everything else about the run is exactly the same. It is
judged the same way too, which is the point — the blind number and the sighted
number are comparable, so aim for 90 % of what you managed with the page. The
app does not make that comparison for you; the rung counts a pass.

**The exercises are forms, not drills.** A twelve-bar blues is three four-bar
pieces, with natural places to start at bars 5 and 9; the oom-pah is two. Learn
one in pieces, then start it from each of those bars with **Blind** on.

**Tools for this rung.** *Play it blind* is all of that in one tap: it opens
the easy *Für Elise*, the first of this rung's three pieces, with the notation
already hidden and everything else about the run unchanged. The other two, and
the twelve-bar forms above, open the same way from the ⋯ controls on their own row.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can start anywhere, at half tempo,
without the page — and the run scores within a tenth of your sighted one. When
it does, the piece is yours in a way it was not last week.
