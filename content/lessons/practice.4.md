Piano injuries are real and they are almost always the result of practising
through a warning rather than of one dramatic event. The warning is usually
boredom or fatigue first, tension second, and pain third — and by the third you
have missed two.

## Warming up

Five minutes, slow, something easy, before anything hard. Cold hands are less
accurate and less able to stretch, and the first five minutes of a session are
where most people are least careful.

Your hands warm up faster than your attention does. That is why the warm-up is
also a chance to decide what this session is for.

## Tension

Check three things, in this order: are your shoulders up around your ears; is
your forearm hard when you touch it with the other hand; are you holding your
breath. Each of those is common and each is fixable in a second, once noticed.

The fix for tension is almost never "relax". It is: play more slowly, use arm
weight rather than finger pressure, and let go of each key after it has sounded.

## Pain

**Stop.** Not "finish this passage" — stop. Any pain in the wrist, forearm,
thumb or shoulder means the session is over and tomorrow's is shorter.

Pain that persists for more than a couple of days, or any numbness or tingling,
is a doctor's problem and not a practice problem. Nothing about the piano is
worth an injury that takes months, and hands do not have an unlimited number of
warnings.

**Common mistake.** Long sessions at weekends after a week of none. Four
twenty-minute sessions beat one eighty-minute one, both for learning and for
your hands.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You notice tension before it becomes
discomfort, and you have stopped at least once because you were tired.
