Every rung so far handed you a method for taking a rag apart: strain by strain
at Stage 6, the left hand alone at Stage 7, figure by figure at Stage 8. This
one puts the pieces back together and takes the page away.

**Why memory is the last step.** All four of these are built from sixteen-bar
strains, and the strains change key under you — three different key signatures
in three of them. Reading holds that together with your eyes. From memory, your
ear and your hands have to know which strain comes next and what key it is in,
which is the same knowledge held somewhere more useful.

**How to do it.** One strain at a time, memorised three ways: the sound of it,
the shape your hands make, and the harmony underneath. Any one will fail on its
own under pressure; all three will not. Test a strain by starting in the middle
of it — if you can only begin at the first bar, you have memorised a sequence
and not a piece.

**The left hand gives way first.** You cannot look at the page and should not be
looking at your hands either, so the leaps have to be automatic first. The
oom-pah study puts the chord a tenth above the bass note, which is the reach
these rags ask for; the stride study makes that hand travel four times a bar
instead of twice. Eight bars of the one, or four of the other, with your eyes
shut, beat another read-through.

**And the figure that will not sit on the beat.** The secondary-rag study is
three sixteenths repeating against a beat of four, and its own instruction is
the whole lesson: count the beat, not the figure.

**Repertoire for this rung.** Four options, all Joplin, all in two-four, none of
them printing a single chord symbol. *Original Rags* is the longest at a hundred
and nine bars and the widest-ranging: five repeated sections across three key
signatures. *A Breeze from Alabama* has three as well, and its middle strain sits
in four flats after an opening with none, which is the furthest any of these
travels in one step. *The Chrysanthemum* is a hundred and four bars in three key
signatures and opens with both hands on the same line an octave apart.
*Search-Light Rag* is the short one at ninety bars and the only one in two keys
rather than three, but it has much the heaviest left hand: a bass doubled at the
octave in eighty-four of those ninety bars, against thirty-three at most in the
others. It is also the only one of the four with a tempo written on it in words,
and those words are *Slow March Tempo*. The rags of James Scott and Joseph Lamb
belong here too, and this Library holds one of them; the rest are in no edition
it could reach.

**Tools for this rung.** *Play it blind* hides the notation so you play from
memory, and the app still follows you and still marks what you play — one strain
at a time, then two, then the lot. When a strain survives that, set *Perform* on
the score screen: one pass start to finish, no restarts and no loop, kept as a
performance rather than as practice.

**Common mistake.** Memorising at full tempo. Memory laid down fast has the
errors in it, and those never come out.

**How you'll know you've got it.** One whole rag, every strain, no page, and you
can start from the top of any strain somebody names.
