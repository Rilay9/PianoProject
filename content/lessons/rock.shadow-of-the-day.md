**Not bundled.** In copyright; import your own MusicXML.

**What the piano part has to do.** A slow four-chord loop that repeats for the
whole song, growing from bare to enormous and back. Harmonically it is the
simplest thing in this module; everything it does is done with texture,
register and dynamics.

**The techniques.**

- **A four-chord loop, played a hundred times.** The skill is variation without
  change: same chords, different density. Verse as single notes, pre-chorus as
  broken chords, chorus as full voicings with octaves in the bass.
- **Register as a dynamic.** Moving the same figure up an octave is heard as
  brighter and louder even at the same volume; moving the bass down an octave
  adds weight without effort. Use register before you use force.
- **Build and release.** The arrangement's whole architecture. Map it: which bar
  starts the build, which bar is the peak, and where it drops away. Mark it on
  the score.
- **Sustain across the loop.** One pedal change per chord, no more.

**Public-domain vehicles.** The chords-and-pop Stage 4 four-chord progression
work (I–V–vi–IV in three keys); Grieg *In the Hall of the Mountain King* for a
single idea building by register and density; Beethoven *Moonlight* I for
arpeggio texture with pedal.

**What to listen for in the recording.** The exact bar where the drums enter and
the exact bar where everything stops. Those two moments are what the arrangement
is for.

**Common mistake.** Playing every repetition the same way. Four chords played
identically forty times is not a song; it is a loop.

**How you'll know you've got it.** The same four-chord loop played four times
through at four clearly different densities, without changing the chords.
