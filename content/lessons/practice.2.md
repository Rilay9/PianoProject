Everyone is told to practise slowly and almost nobody does it slowly enough.
The test is simple: **if you can play it faster than you are practising it, you
are not practising slowly.** Slow means slow enough that you have time to see
the next note, place the hand, and notice what you are doing — usually about
half the speed that feels sensible.

**Why it works.** At speed you cannot correct anything; the note is gone
before you know it was wrong. Slow practice is the only tempo at which you can
be accurate on purpose rather than by luck, and accuracy is the thing that has
to be repeated.

It is also the only way to notice tension. At tempo everything feels urgent; at
half speed a tight forearm is obvious.

**The ladder.** Set the metronome where you can play it perfectly. Three clean
repetitions, then up one notch — about 5 %, not 20. One mistake and you go
back a notch, and stay there until it is clean again.

This is slower than it sounds and much faster than the alternative, because you
never spend a week grinding at a tempo you cannot hold.

**Common mistake.** Practising at your target tempo because it is more
satisfying, and treating the wreckage as the learning. It is not; it is the
wreckage.

**Tools for this rung.** The score screen will run the ladder for you. Set a
loop, switch the *Ladder* on in the ⋯ menu, and a clean pass takes the tempo
up a notch while a pass with a mistake takes it down. That is the rule above
made quicker: it moves after every pass rather than every three, its notch is
ten points of the written tempo, and it stops climbing at full tempo unless you
had already set it higher. The counting is done for you and the tempo figure is
underlined so you can watch it move.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play it at three tempos on request —
half, three-quarters and full — and the slow one is genuinely clean rather than
merely slow.
