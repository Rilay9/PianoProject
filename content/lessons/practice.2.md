Everyone is told to practise slowly and almost nobody does it slowly enough.
The test is simple: **if you can play it faster than you are practising it, you
are not practising slowly.** Slow means slow enough that you have time to see
the next note, place the hand, and notice what you are doing — about half the
speed that feels sensible is a common starting point.

**Why it works.** At speed a wrong note is often gone before you know it was
wrong. Slowly, there is time to be accurate on purpose rather than by luck, and
accuracy is what you want to repeat.

Slow practice also makes tension easier to notice. At tempo everything feels
urgent; at half speed a tight forearm is obvious.

**The ladder.** Set the metronome where you can play it perfectly. One way to
climb from there: after a few clean repetitions, say three, go up one small
notch, around 5 % rather than 20; after a mistake, go back a notch and stay
there until it is clean again.

It can feel slow; the point is not to spend a week grinding at a tempo you
cannot hold.

**Common mistake.** Practising at your target tempo because it is more
satisfying, and treating the wreckage as the learning. It is not; it is the
wreckage.

**Tools for this rung.** The score screen will run the ladder for you. Set a
loop, switch the *Ladder* on in the ⋯ controls, and a clean pass takes the tempo
up a notch while a pass with a mistake takes it down. That is the ladder above
made quicker: it moves after every pass rather than every three, its notch is
a tenth of the written tempo, and it stops climbing at full tempo unless you
had already set it higher. The counting is done for you and the tempo figure is
underlined so you can watch it move.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play it at three tempos on request —
half, three-quarters and full — and the slow one is genuinely clean rather than
merely slow.
