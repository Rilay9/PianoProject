Everyone is told to practise slowly and almost nobody does it slowly enough.
The test is simple: **if you can play it faster than you are practising it, you
are not practising slowly.** Slow means slow enough that you have time to see
the next note, place the hand, and notice what you are doing — usually about
half the speed that feels sensible.

## Why it works

At speed you cannot correct anything; the note is gone before you know it was
wrong. Slow practice is the only tempo at which you can be accurate on purpose
rather than by luck, and accuracy is the thing that has to be repeated.

It is also the only way to notice tension. At tempo everything feels urgent; at
half speed a tight forearm is obvious.

## The ladder

Set the metronome where you can play it perfectly. Three clean repetitions, then
up one notch — about 5 %, not 20. One mistake and you go back a notch, and stay
there until it is clean again.

This is slower than it sounds and much faster than the alternative, because you
never spend a week grinding at a tempo you cannot hold.

**Common mistake.** Practising at your target tempo because it is more
satisfying, and treating the wreckage as the learning. It is not; it is the
wreckage.

**How you'll know you've got it.** You can play it at three tempos on request —
half, three-quarters and full — and the slow one is genuinely clean rather than
merely slow.
