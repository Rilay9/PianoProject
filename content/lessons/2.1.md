Two hands at once is the first thing that feels genuinely impossible, and the
way past it is to make one hand trivial.

**The left hand holds; the right hand moves.** The left plays whole notes and
half notes — one note a bar, or two — while the right plays the tune. There is
almost nothing to think about in the left hand, so all your attention can go on
the one thing that is new: making the two hands agree about *when*.

**"The left hand is the floor."** The left-hand note starts on beat one and does
not move. That gives the right hand something to land on. If you ever lose your
place, the left hand is where you find it again.

**Vertical alignment.** Notes printed in the same column sound together. In this
unit that only happens on beat one of each bar (and on beat three when the
left hand changes at the half bar). Look for those columns and mark them: they
are the moments where both hands must be exactly together, and everything
between them is free.

**How to build it.** Right hand alone until it is automatic. Left hand alone
until it is boring. Then hands together at half tempo, counting out loud, in
two-bar chunks with a loop. Do not go on to bar three until bars one and two are
right, and do not raise the tempo until they are right three times running.

**What to do at the piano.** The five-finger pattern drill (the app plays a
right-hand walk up and down and you play it back, with the left hand holding
the low C under it — the card asks for the hold, and only your ear judges
whether it stayed down), then *Ode to Joy*, *Twinkle Twinkle*,
*Mary Had a Little Lamb* and *Jingle Bells* in their hands-together settings.
The left hand stays in its C position throughout: 5 on C3, 2 on F3, 1 on G3.

**Common mistake.** Speeding up the right hand and leaving the left behind,
usually in the second half of a bar. If the left-hand note wobbles, you are
playing too fast. Slow down until it does not.

**One more tune.** *Simple Gifts*, the Shaker melody, is on this rung as a
right-hand tune on one staff, with no left hand written: mostly steps and
thirds with the odd leap of a fifth, in eighths and quarters, a few of the
quarters dotted. It is the
right hand's half of this lesson; the hands-together work is in the four tunes
above.

**Tools for this rung.** *Duet* will hold the floor for you. *Play it as a
duet* opens a hands-together tune with the right hand chosen and Duet already
on, and the left hand's long notes come from the app while you play the tune, so you hear what landing together sounds
like before you have to do both at once. Then turn it off and match what
you heard.

**How you'll know you've got it.** Hands together at 90 % in Keep tempo, at 80 %
or more of the piece's own tempo, with the left-hand change landing exactly on the beat every time.
