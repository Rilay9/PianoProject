An **eighth note** lasts half a beat: two of them fill one quarter-note beat.
On its own it has a flag on the stem; in pairs or fours they are joined by a
**beam**, a thick horizontal bar. The beaming is a kindness — it groups the
notes into beats so your eye can see where beat two starts without counting.

**Count "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and".** Say it out loud, every beat, whether or not
there is an eighth note there. This is **subdivision**: keeping a faster pulse
running underneath the beat so that when a half-beat note arrives you already
know where it goes. Almost every rhythm problem a beginner has is a
subdivision problem, and almost every fix is to count the "ands" out loud.

**Put the metronome on.** Not because a machine is musical, but because your
sense of pulse is worse than you think and you cannot hear yourself rushing.
Set it slow enough that the eighth notes feel unhurried. If the click seems to
move, it is you.

**Where the "and" falls.** An eighth note on the beat and an eighth note on the
"and" are physically identical; only the timing differs. Practise the pair
slowly and deliberately, so the second note is *placed*, not just squeezed in
before the next beat.

**What to do at the piano.** Rhythm reading with eighths on one pitch first,
then right-hand melodies with eighths, then *London Bridge* — which has the
tune's eighth-note pair right in the first bar and a marked hand position with
the thumb on D so the A sits under finger 5 — and *Sakura*, whose eighth pairs
mostly sit on the second beat of a bar.

**Common mistake.** Playing eighth notes as "quick, quick" and then waiting. Two
eighths take exactly one beat between them: if the second one is early, the
beat sags. Count the "and".

**Two more things on this rung.** *Alouette* has eighth notes too, but in 6/8,
where the eighth is the counting unit and they come three to a beat: the
"gentille Alouette" figure is quarter, eighth, quarter, eighth, a long–short
lilt rather than four quick notes. Count that one "1 2 3 4 5 6" rather than
"1 and 2 and" — an "and" splits a beat in two, and here a beat holds three. And
the *Sight-read, right hand* drill is here: a fresh four-bar phrase every time,
generated for this level, and eighths can turn up in the very first one.

**Tools for this rung.** *Rhythm only*, in the ⋯ controls in Keep tempo, stops
the app asking which key: tap *London Bridge* out on any note at all and
only the placing of the eighths is judged. One tap per written note, and the
beats drawn over the count-in are the ones to put them on.

**How you'll know you've got it.** One tune hands together at 72 bpm with the
metronome on, at 90 % accuracy, and you can still say "1 and 2 and" out loud
while you play it.
