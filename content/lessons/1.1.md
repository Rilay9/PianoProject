Put your right thumb on middle C and let the next four fingers fall on the four
white keys above it: D, E, F, G. That is **C position** — one finger per key, no
finger moves, no thumb passing under. Every tune on this rung but one lives inside it.

**The staff** is five lines and the four spaces between them. The curly
**treble clef** at the left marks the second line up as G — the G your finger 5
is on. Middle C sits one short *ledger line* below the staff: a line drawn just
for that note, because the staff ran out.

**A quarter note** — a filled note head with a stem — lasts one beat. **4/4**
at the start means four beats in a bar, and a **bar line** is just a fence
every four beats to help your eye. Count "1 2 3 4" out loud while you play. Out
loud, not in your head: counting silently is how beginners drift.

**What to do at the piano.** Play the five-finger walk up and down at 60 bpm,
one note per click, saying the letter names. Then play *Hot Cross Buns*, which
uses only E, D and C — three fingers, three notes, and the whole point is that
your hand does not move. Then *Mary Had a Little Lamb*, which
adds G, and *Ode to Joy*, which adds F and G.

Keep the fingers that are not playing resting lightly on their keys. They should
stay in contact with the keyboard, not fly up.

**Common mistake.** Reaching for a note with the wrong finger because it is
nearer. In C position each key has exactly one finger and no other finger is
allowed to visit it. Where fingering is printed above the notes it is not a
suggestion; it is the reason the hand stays still.

**One more tune.** *Kum Ba Yah* is on this rung too: eight bars, right hand
only, but it sits higher than C position, from the G above middle C up to
the E a sixth above it: six notes, one more than five fingers hold, and no
fingering printed, so work yours out before you play it.

**How you'll know you've got it.** One of these tunes in Wait for me at 95 %
accuracy, then in Keep tempo at 90 % accuracy with the tempo at 80 % or more of
the written — played while looking at the screen, not at your hand.
