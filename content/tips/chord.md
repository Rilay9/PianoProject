## What it's for

Reading a chord symbol as a *shape* rather than as a spelling exercise. C, Am, F,
G7 have to arrive under the hand at the speed a song moves, and that only happens
when the symbol stops being decoded and starts being recognised.

## How to practise it

Learn the shape, not the letters. A major triad is the same hand shape in every
key — root, skip, skip — and the fingers can learn it faster than the head can
spell it. Play each one three times before moving on.

Say the chord name out loud as you play it. It sounds silly and it welds the
symbol to the shape faster than anything else.

## Common mistake

Spelling every chord from the root upwards under your breath. That is how you
learn what a chord *is*; it is not how you play one in time. Once you know the
theory, stop reciting it.

## How you'll know you've got it

The common chords land without thought and the awkward ones — B flat, F sharp
minor — take a beat rather than a bar.
