## What it's for

Recognising the same chord with a different note at the bottom. C, E, G and
G, C, E are one chord, and hearing and seeing them as one is what stops your left
hand leaping around the keyboard between changes.

## How to practise it

Play the chord in root position, then move the bottom note up an octave, then
again. Say which inversion you are in each time. Three chords through all their
inversions is a better five minutes than twelve chords in root position.

Then play a progression twice: once in root position, once choosing whichever
inversion is nearest. Listen to how much smoother the second one is — that is
the whole reason inversions exist.

## Common mistake

Learning inversions as three separate shapes to memorise. They are one shape
rotated, and treating them as three triples the work and hides the point.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can name the inversion from the sound of the bass note, and you reach for
the nearest one without being told to.
