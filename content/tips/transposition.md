## What it's for

Playing something in a different key from the one it is written in. Singers need
it, guitarists need it, and it is the fastest way to prove you understand a
piece rather than having memorised where your fingers go.

## How to practise it

Think in intervals and scale degrees, not in letters. "Up a third" and "the
fifth degree" survive a key change; "then an F sharp" does not.

Start with the bass line only, then add the melody, then the harmony. Doing all
three at once is why transposition feels impossible at first.

## Common mistake

Translating note by note as you play. That works at a quarter of the tempo and
falls apart at speed, because you are reading in one key and playing in another
with a conversion step in between.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can move a four-bar phrase into a neighbouring key at tempo, and into a
distant one slowly, without writing anything down.
