## What it's for

Knowing which notes fit the chord that is sounding now. It is what turns "the
right scale for the key" into "the right notes for this bar", which is the
difference between noodling and playing over changes.

## How to practise it

Start from the chord tones and add the rest. Root, third, fifth, seventh always
work; the scale is those four plus the notes that connect them. Play the chord,
then the scale, then the chord again.

Work one progression at a time rather than one scale at a time. The skill is
switching between them at the speed the chords move.

## Common mistake

Learning a scale per chord and then running up and down it. The scale is a
supply of notes, not a shape to play.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can improvise over a ii–V–I and land on a chord tone at each change without
planning it.
