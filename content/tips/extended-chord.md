## What it's for

Ninths, elevenths and thirteenths — the notes that make a chord sound modern
rather than merely correct. They are also where most self-taught players stall,
because the spelling gets long.

## How to practise it

Drop the fifth, and often the root. A five- or six-note chord under one hand is
unplayable and sounds thick; the third, seventh and the extension carry the
sound, and the bass has the root anyway.

Learn them as alterations of shapes you know. A ninth chord is the seventh you
already play with one more note a step above the root — not a new chord to
memorise.

## Common mistake

Playing every note in the symbol. C13 does not mean thirteen notes, and a
literal reading sounds worse than the shell it was built from.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can voice any of them in four notes or fewer, and choose which to leave out
without stopping.
