## What it's for

Turning "third line up, so that must be… B" into simply *seeing* B. Naming a
note by counting from a landmark is a two-step process, and at speed two steps
is one too many. This drill removes the counting by making it too slow to use.

## How to practise it

Short and often beats long and occasional — ninety seconds a day for a fortnight
will do more than an hour on Sunday. Answer at the speed you *can*, not the
speed you want; guessing to keep up teaches you to guess.

When a note keeps catching you out, stop the drill and find that note on the
keyboard, on both staves, in three different octaves. One minute of that is
worth twenty repetitions of getting it wrong.

## Common mistake

Counting up from middle C every time and getting the right answer anyway. The
score says you are fine and the reading is still slow, because the reflex you
want has not been built. If you catch yourself counting, slow the drill down
rather than speeding your counting up.

## How you'll know you've got it

The answer arrives before you have decided to answer, and it stays that way on
ledger lines. Around 90 % at the fastest speed you can hold for a full set, with
no note you dread.
