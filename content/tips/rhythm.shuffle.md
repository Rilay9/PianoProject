## What it's for

The long-short eighth of blues and jazz. It is written as two equal notes and
played as something closer to a triplet, and no notation tells you exactly how
far to go — that is a matter of feel and of style.

## How to practise it

Count "one-and-a two-and-a" and play on the *one* and the *a*. That is a hard
triplet shuffle. Real playing usually sits somewhere between that and straight,
and drifts with the tempo — faster tends to be straighter.

Listen to the ride cymbal in your head. If nothing you play locks to it, slow
down and exaggerate the long-short until it is unmistakable.

## Common mistake

Playing dotted eighths and sixteenths. That is sharper than a shuffle and it
sounds like a march; the difference is audible and it matters.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can switch between straight and shuffled on request at the same tempo, and
the shuffle stays even at speed rather than collapsing towards straight.
