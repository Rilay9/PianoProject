## What it's for

Naming a chord by its job rather than by its letter. I–vi–ii–V is one pattern in
every key; C–Am–Dm–G is that pattern in exactly one key, and remembering it that
way means learning it twelve times.

## How to practise it

Say the numeral and the letter together until the numeral comes first. "Five —
G". Then drop the letter.

Do it to music you already know. Take a song you can play and label its chords;
you will find the same three or four progressions turning up everywhere, which
is the insight the whole system exists to give you.

## Common mistake

Getting the case wrong and not noticing. Upper case is major, lower case is
minor, and ii is a very different chord from II — the second is a secondary
dominant and belongs to a different sound entirely.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can label a progression in any key at the speed the chords go past, and you
hear "four chords" rather than "C, Am, Dm, G".
