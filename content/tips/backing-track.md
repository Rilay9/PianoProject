## What it's for

Playing with something that does not wait for you. A metronome tells you when
you are wrong; a backing track makes you keep going, which is a different and
harder skill.

## How to practise it

Do not stop. If you lose your place, leave a bar out and come back in on the
next downbeat — that is what you would have to do with other people, and
practising the recovery is more useful than practising the passage again.

Listen more than you play. Play half as many notes as you want to and spend the
attention on staying with the track.

## Common mistake

Playing along at a tempo you cannot actually hold, and treating the wreckage as
a bad day. Take the tempo down until it is comfortable and put it up from there.

## How you'll know you've got it

You get to the end without stopping, you know where the form turned round, and
you could hold a conversation while doing it.
