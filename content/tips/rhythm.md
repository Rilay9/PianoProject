## What it's for

Putting notes in the right place in the bar. Rhythm is the thing an audience
notices first and the thing most self-taught players are weakest at, because
it is the one part you cannot fix by looking harder at the page.

## How to practise it

Count out loud. Not in your head — out loud, "1 and 2 and", the whole way
through. The mouth keeps time far better than the intention to keep time does.

Subdivide. If a bar keeps collapsing, count the eighths under it even where
nothing plays. The gaps are where rhythm goes wrong, and counting them makes
them visible.

Slow down until it is correct, then speed up in small steps. A rhythm played
fast and wrong fifty times is fifty repetitions of the wrong thing.

## Common mistake

Rushing the rests and the long notes. Almost nobody plays a two-beat note for
two full beats when they are nervous; the drill will show you exactly that.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can tap the pattern back at three different tempos without counting on your
fingers, and the long notes get their full value.
