## What it's for

Hearing where a chord sequence is going. Once you can hear I–V–vi–IV you can
play a very large number of songs without ever seeing the music.

## How to practise it

Sing the bass line. The bass carries the progression, and most people who "can't
hear chords" can hear a bass line perfectly well. Sing it, then work out what
each note is doing relative to the key.

Listen for the pull home. The V chord wants to resolve; when you hear that
wanting, you have found the dominant, and everything else is positioned from
there.

## Common mistake

Trying to name each chord in isolation as it goes past. Progressions are heard
as motion, not as a list — what matters is where a chord sits relative to the
one before it and to home.

## How you'll know you've got it

You can name a four-chord loop after one hearing, and you notice when a song on
the radio does something other than the obvious.
