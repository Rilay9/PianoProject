**The mechanisms, and the hypotheses tested.**

*The object (items 1, 3, 4).* `tools/content/excerpts.py` cuts the parent's **built** file by printed position (`score.measures(from − 1, to, indicesNotNumbers=True)`, as `bisect_render.py` cuts), carries the clef, key, time and tempo in force into the first bar, keeps a pickup when the row starts at bar 1, severs a tie into the first bar to a plain note and drops one out of the last, neutralises a repeat sign at either edge, silences the unselected staff of a one-hand cut and lets `convert.normalise` drop it (one staff), and writes the normalised header: the excerpt's id as the work title, no credits, no encoding date, and the archive's inner entry named after the excerpt. That last was a found fault: the first cut's bytes depended on the output path, because music21 names the inner file after it; renaming the entry and its `container.xml` made two cuts of one definition byte-identical wherever written. The id is `excerpt.<parent id less "song.">.b<from>-<to>[.rh|.lh]`; the key is sha256 over `parentSha256|from|to|selection|cutVersion`; the cut file's sha256 is D2's identity and what a run writes as `material`. The step runs after the parents' files exist and before notation, demands and provenance, which read the cut as any file; a row the cutter refuses stops the build, named. A strict build refuses the cut where it refuses the parent, because the row carries the parent's `source` and licence tags (`TheLicence`, two mutants).

*Positions without cutting (item 5; the brief's second hypothesis).* The bridge (`demandsOfFiles.test.ts` through `demands.py`) now writes, beside each demand's count, its located places per printed bar and staff (each printed note once, however many passes the repeats make), cached in `build/positions-cache.json` on the counts' fingerprint and kept out of the catalogue. **Test:** the proposer's window counts against each cut's own measurement. **Result — the hypothesis fails for three kinds of detector, each named and handled:** `leftHandPattern` and `walkingBass` locate nothing unless every bar of the whole model qualifies, so their places cannot tell a window: the bridge asks each of them of every printed bar alone, the detector itself on a one-bar slice of the model (`everyBar`); `beyondPosition` widens a running span from the start of the piece, so its places do not compose: the bridge writes each hand's lowest and highest pitch per printed bar instead (`hands`); and the key-relative demands (`key.signature`, `pitch.chromatic`) in a window whose key differs from the piece's first key are judged against the first key, so the proposer scores such a window 0 and says only the cut can tell. And a count read in the parent's context can exceed the cut's at the edges: Hark! 25–28 had 7 syncopations by position and measures 5 on the cut, where the tie from bar 24 is severed. Both are above the window minimum; the build's measurement of the cut is what the catalogue carries.

*Phrase boundaries (the brief's first hypothesis).* **Test:** the top window of ten parents against the editions' own marks (double bars, repeat signs, rehearsal marks, slurs, fermatas, breath marks; `propose --edition-check`, `runs/edition-check-final.txt`). **Result:** 5 of 10 starts and 6 of 10 ends agree with a mark. The disagreements are of two kinds: an internal phrase the edition does not mark (Anh. 113's bar 17, after the long note of bar 16), and a phrase that starts on an upbeat in mid-bar (Für Elise's bar 16, Swan Lake's 20, Greensleeves' 9), which a cut of whole bars cannot start at. So the signals were changed before any weight was added: a mid-bar upbeat does not meet `phraseStart` (0.5, with the reason), a long note is a whole bar or the strictly longest note of at least half a bar, a long note or rest that opens a phrase the edition marks is not a phrase end (0.4), a bass change alone marks nothing in this repertoire (0.3), and a cadence is each tonic with its own dominant (C to D minor in F is deceptive). The top-scoring window of a parent may still leave a signal unmet (three of the ten above do): approval asks every signal, not the top score. Each change has its case and its mutant.

*The window rule (item 9).* `perBar` reached and at least `minInWindow` located, in place of the whole-piece `min`; two where the file states none. The values are spliced into `opportunity-density.json` beside each rule's `min`, each with `windowWhy` and `hypothesis: true`; the two every-bar rules keep `hypothesis: false` (their detector is already an every-bar rule). The build writes an excerpt's `measurement.window` — what the window rule establishes that the whole-piece rule does not — and the app's reading in `eligibility.test.ts` and the build rows' case in `test_measured_truth.py` both include it.

*The evidence (item 7).* The Score screen hashes the bytes it loaded for an excerpt (`cutIdentity`) and the run's `material` is that `{ kind: 'file', sha256 }`, carried through the observation into the evidence context beside `itemId` and `seed` and kept on the stored self-assessment; nobody reads it yet.
