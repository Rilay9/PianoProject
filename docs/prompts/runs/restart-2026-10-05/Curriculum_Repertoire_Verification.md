# Curriculum Repertoire Verification

Direction: audit from the last curriculum rung backward, five repertoire items at a time.

## Working rules
- Judge the musical job, not a rigid checklist. A useful score in the wrong place is usually MOVE, not REJECT.
- Separate contains from teaches; match introduction, practice, transfer, and integration roles.
- Consider the whole burden: pitch, rhythm, coordination, hand movement, key, tempo, articulation, density, accompaniment, and unfamiliar concepts.
- Use curriculum context: previously taught demands are different from hidden prerequisites.
- Prefer authentic usefulness over artificial purity, but do not force difficult repertoire downward.
- Treat solo piano, lead sheet, accompaniment task, duet, ensemble reduction, and melody-only scores as different asset types.
- Verify what the pianist actually plays; title/genre/metadata are weak evidence.
- A coherent excerpt may be useful even when the whole piece is too difficult.
- Reuse is fine when the pedagogical purpose changes.
- Compare against alternatives; acceptable is not automatically best.
- Detectors are evidence, not authority.
- Separate source/data faults from pedagogical judgments.
- Keep borderline calls explicit rather than pretending to false precision.
- Optimize for learner progression and musical experience, not preserving the existing curriculum.

## Batch 1 — from the end backward

1. ragtime.9 — Original Rags — KEEP. Genuine advanced two-staff piano rag in 2/4, multi-strain/repeated form, dense syncopation and sixteenths; appropriate as a Stage 9 memory/performance project. Source carries ARR Charles N. Daniels, so edition/arrangement attribution should remain visible.
2. ragtime.9 — A Breeze from Alabama — KEEP. Genuine piano, 2/4, 'Not fast', demanding sixteenth-note RH against characteristic ragtime LH bass/chord motion; strong advanced memory/integration project.
3. ragtime.9 — The Chrysanthemum — KEEP. Genuine piano, 2/4, Slow March Tempo; opens with hands in parallel an octave apart, then moves into characteristic ragtime accompaniment and dense RH figures. Appropriate advanced project.
4. ragtime.9 — Search-Light Rag — KEEP WITH EDITION CHECK. The actual Joplin source is genuine two-staff piano in 2/4 with very active octave/bass LH and syncopated RH. Curriculum uses a PDMX edition; exact MXL bytes still need direct edition-level inspection before calling that specific file fully verified.
5. chords-pop.9 — Apex of the World — KEEP FOR ARRANGEMENT-STUDY ROLE, but curriculum finder is inconsistent. The lesson deliberately uses fully written arrangements with zero printed chord symbols so the learner works chords out and builds a new arrangement. The stage-9 finder instead says 'chord chart or lead sheet only' and 'avoid fully written-out arrangements'; that finder contract needs correction rather than removing the piece merely to satisfy the stale finder wording.
