**Finding.** Three bars-9-10 forms are attested: V7 then iv7 (the minor twelve-bar written like the major shuffle form; blues and guitar instruction); ♭VI7 then V7 (the jazz minor blues of "Mr. P.C." and "Equinox"; Wikipedia's table, PianoGroove, LearnJazzStandards); and iiø7 then V7 (Open Music Theory's "typical", JazzGuitar.be's "most common", whose ♭VI7 it names as the II's tritone substitute). The only open textbook read (Open Music Theory) prefers neither of the two forms in dispute over its ii-V. No source makes either disputed form *the* minor blues. **Both are standard variants**, and the product may teach them only as named variants (the ruling, `docs/review/responses/a7a-drafts.md` section 3).

**How a lesson would say it** (a draft of the claim, not lesson text): the generated walking items play the minor blues as many jazz players do, with ♭VI7 to V7 in bars 9-10 (Coltrane's "Mr. P.C."); a blues band's minor blues often keeps the major form's V7 to iv7 there instead, and the Lab's minor twelve-bar is that one. Which one is playing is said by name, each time.

## 2. The walking bass

| Source | Claim | Quote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wikipedia, "Bassline", section Walking bass | one note a beat | "usually quarter notes (known in jazz as a 'four feel')" |
| LearnJazzStandards, "Play a Walking Bass Line Like a Pro in 4 Easy Steps" (learnjazzstandards.com/blog/learning-jazz/bass/write-walking-bass-line/) | the root where each chord starts; the last beat a half step below or above the next root, or its fifth | "write in the root on the first beat the chord is introduced" |
| same | several chords a bar | "you'll only have room to fill in the root and the linking tone" |
| JazzGuitar.be, "Walking Bass Lines for Jazz Guitar (Jazz Blues in F)" (jazzguitar.be/blog/walking-bass-lines/) | the approach note above or below | "a half-step (one fret) above or below the root of the next chord" |
| Luke McIntosh, "How To Walk Through 2 Chords Per Bar", Become A Bassist, 2020-04-04 | two chords a bar | "you only have 2 notes to outline entire chords" |

**Finding.** One note a beat, the root where each symbol starts, the approach a half step below or above (jam.6's "above or below" is sourced; the generated items use below only). A bar of three chords (St James bars 1, 3, 9, 11) has no worked example in what was read; the rule above, applied, leaves root, approach, root, root (HYPOTHESIS). These are teaching sites, not a published text; no method book was read.
