## Beyer Op. 101 as the scan prints it (the Peters numbering)

**The contents page (p. 3)** gives the order:
- two-hand exercises in the five-finger compass (p. 24);
- duets up to quavers (p. 37);
- "Exercises on quavers for 2 hands" (p. 38);
- the bass clef (p. 40).

**Two things to know before using any of it:**
- The book notes that up to this point "the hand is at rest in most of the exercises".
- **Until p. 40 the left hand is written in the treble clef,** around middle C, with the right hand around C5. A transcription for the app keeps the notes as printed and says so, or rewrites the left hand in the bass clef at the same pitch. That is an edition choice to record, not a change to the music.

| Peters No. (page) | What the hands do | For |
|---|---|---|
| 12 (p. 24) | The right hand walks C–G–C in quarters, five-finger. The left hand holds whole notes, C then G | **N1, the purest case** |
| 13, 14 (p. 24) | The reverse: the right hand holds and the left hand walks | N1's mirror |
| 15 (p. 24) | The right hand moves in quarters over left-hand whole notes, with a few left-hand quarters | N1 |
| 16 (p. 25, 2/4) | The left hand moves in half and quarter notes under the tune | N1, the "changes" step |
| 17, 20, 21 (pp. 25–26) | The left hand moves in quarters under long right-hand notes | The next step: the left hand moving under the right |
| 18 (p. 25, 3/4) | The left hand holds two-note chords, dotted halves | N1, and an early step toward N3 |
| 23 (p. 27) | The right hand moves in quarters over left-hand whole notes, stepping C, D, E, then down | N1 |
| 29 (p. 29) | As 23, with ties | N1 with ties (taught at 2.4) |
| 35, 36 (p. 32) | The tune in quarters over left-hand whole notes C, B, C: the I–V7–I sound with the bass alone | **N1, and the step before N3** |
| "Exercise in quavers" (p. 38, unnumbered) | Both hands in eighths, stepwise | N2 drill |
| 45 (p. 38) | Right-hand eighths over left-hand whole notes, then left-hand eighths | **N2 with N1** |
| 46 (p. 38) | Right-hand eighths over the left hand alternating C–E–G and B–F–G in quarters: I and V7 in close position | N2; **the close-position I/V7 shape methods teach for N3** |
| 47 *Kinderlied*, "Winter ade" (p. 39) | The tune in quarters over left-hand eighths | Eighths in the left hand |
| 49 *Volkslied*, "Bald gras' i am Neckar" (p. 39) | A folk tune with an eighth pickup and eighth pairs | **N2 transfer, a real folk song** |

**N3 point.** Beyer No. 46, and Faber Level 1's "I and V7 chords in C and G", use close position: B–F–G for V7, so the hand barely moves. Rung 2.3's *Happy Birthday (simple)* uses root position, with leaps between chords. The rung already offers both through the cadence family (root position and voice-led, CF1's check). **Which shape the rung leads with is a curation decision.**

## The PDMX files, read

- **"Beyer Nro 8", "Nro 9", "Nro 10"** (cc-zero; archive paths in `RESULT.md`).
  - The right hand moves in quarters around C5 while the left hand holds G4, written in the treble clef. 4/4 and 3/4, 24–32 bars.
  - The texture is exactly N1's.
  - **The numbering is not the Peters edition's** (Peters Nos. 1–11 are three-hand pieces), and the uploader names no edition. So the musical shape is confirmed and the attribution to a specific Beyer number is not.
- **"Reinagle Allegro"** (`…5Ec6MyZsWBHB9hkY6q7cEPnRSH.mxl`, cc-zero). The file's own title is "Renaissance Dance", in 2/4: right-hand quarters and eighths over left-hand half notes C, C, B, B, C, A, G, C.
  - The attribution to Reinagle (and to ABRSM's Op. 1 No. 9) is not supported by the file. **Not proposed.**

## What this changes in `RESULT.md`'s proposals

1. **N1 has a public-domain source better than any held file:** Beyer Nos. 12, 15, 23, 35 and 36. No verified symbolic edition of them was found.
   - The PDMX "Nro" files have the right shape and no edition.
   - **Proposal:** transcribe Nos. 12, 23 and 35 from the Peters scan into the authored ABC format already used for the hands-together pieces, citing plate 8033 and the page.
   - Anyone who can read the page can check them against it. That is a copy of a published piece, not an arrangement.
2. **N2 gains Beyer No. 45** (eighths over a held left hand) **and No. 49** (a folk song with eighths), by the same route.
3. **N3 gains Beyer No. 46** as the close-position I/V7 source. Its leading shape is left to curation.
4. **The import request to the owner's machine for Beyer Nos. 8–10 is no longer needed.** The files were read here, and their shape is above.
