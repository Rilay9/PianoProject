## 0. What the app teaches, in order (summary of `content/curriculum/stage-*.json`, `concepts.json`)

`concepts.json` holds 286 concepts. Stages carry an `abrsmGradeApprox` field (quoted, not assessed).

- **Stage 0** (pre-grade): posture, finger numbers, keyboard geography, placement.
- **Stage 1** (pre-grade): 1.1 RH C position, quarter notes, treble clef, 4/4, steps · 1.2 half/whole notes, rests · 1.3 LH C position, bass clef · 1.4 grand staff, alternating hands, 3/4, anacrusis · 1.5 steps and skips, sight-reading (introduces leap). Practice track practice.1–5.
- **Stage 2** (pre-grade/initial): 2.1 hands together, held LH · 2.2 eighth notes · 2.3 triads, I-IV-V, block chords · 2.4 tie, slur, dotted quarter, dynamics · 2.5 position shift, thumb under, C major scale.
- **Stage 3** (initial/grade 1): 3.1 sharps, flats, major-scale formula, key signature · 3.2 I-IV-V7 in G and F · 3.3 relative minor, harmonic minor, minor triads · 3.4 ledger lines, 8va · 3.5 damper pedal · 3.6 broken chords, Alberti bass, waltz bass; plus track rungs (classical.3 Baroque/Classical dances, blues, latin, jazz swing, theory.3 intervals/key signatures …).
- **Stage 4** (grade 1): 4.1 scales HT C G D A, contrary motion · 4.2 F, B♭, E♭ major, harmonic/melodic minor · 4.3 inversions, arpeggios · 4.4 Hanon, sixteenth notes · 4.5 6/8, triplets, swing, syncopation · 4.6 sight-reading capstone · 4.7 memorising; classical.4 staccato/legato/ornaments; technique.4 scales, arpeggios, chromatic, staccato/legato.
- **Stage 5** (grade 2–3): tracks take over (classical.5 sonatina form, Alberti bass HT, trills, pedal; ragtime oom-pah; jazz shells; technique.5 repeated notes, 2:1, mordent, 5/4 …).
- **Stages 6–9** (grade 4–diploma): voicing, rubato, polyrhythm, double notes, octaves, advanced jazz/blues/ragtime/theory tracks, projects.

## 1. Published teaching evidence

### 1a. Concept-by-level table

How to read it. **FABER** is where the Faber chart lists the item under a Piano Adventures level. The chart's correlation columns map *levels*, not concepts, so "Alfred/Bastien via FABER" is the level the chart says corresponds to that Faber level. It is **not** a statement that Alfred or Bastien introduce the concept there (marked "corr."). **ABRSM** cites the 2025–26 syllabus: SR = sight-reading parameters table (p. 16, cumulative: "once introduced they apply for all later grades"); SA = scales and arpeggios requirements (pp. 20, 23, 26, 29). **RCM** cites the 2022 syllabus Technical Tests and Sight Reading tables (Prep A p. 9, Prep B p. 14, L1 pp. 19–20, L2 pp. 25–26, L3 pp. 31–32). "Not stated" means the item does not appear in the requirement tables read (Initial–G3 / Prep A–L3); it may still occur in the repertoire.

### 2d. MIDI-derived observations (35 files from 33 Mutopia entries; Clementi Op. 36/1 is a zip of 3 movement files)

**What MIDI can and cannot show.** The facts below come from Mutopia's LilyPond-generated .mid files, read with music21 10.5.0 (`converter.parse(..., quantizePost=True)`). MIDI carries pitch numbers, times, tracks and meta events. It does **not** carry note spelling: the ranges below use music21's default spelling, e.g. "B-3" (B♭3) may be A♯ in the edition. It also lacks voices, slurs, articulation, dynamics, fingering and repeat signs. LilyPond writes one track per staff, and voices within a staff are merged. "Bars" is total MIDI length ÷ bar length, so it counts unfolded repeats if the edition unfolded them, and grace notes and ornaments realised in MIDI (e.g. trills) can appear as short values or tuplets ("other(1/3)"). Key signature = the MIDI key-signature meta events (sharps positive, flats negative). **Texture** is a heuristic of my own script (`analyse.py`) applied per bar to the lower track: *held* = one onset or only long notes; *block chords* = simultaneities at half or more of the onsets; *alberti* = a low–high–mid–high 4-note pattern of three distinct pitches; *stepwise* = 60% or more of intervals ≤2 semitones; *broken/leaping* = 60% or more ≥3 semitones; else *mixed*. These are counts of bars, not judgements, and the heuristic was not validated against a score.

### 2e. Conversion route and its limits (from `tools/content/import_mutopia.py` docstring; read, not run)

- python-ly's MusicXML writer (`convert.parse_lilypond`) was tried and refused for the Joplin rag. It does not implement `\alternative` (both volta endings are written in turn), and music21 rejected the output (a bar closing early; a chord split across a barline). The finding is recorded in `content/sources/mutopia.json` `checkedAndRefused`.
- The project route therefore converts the **Mutopia .mid** with `tools/midi-cleanup/midi_to_musicxml.py`, which refuses a file that gained or lost a note or has a bar that does not add up. From the **.ly**, read with python-ly (`rel2abs`, `rhythm_explicit`, repeats unfolded), the importer then takes each note's spelling, aligned by pitch class; an unspellable note refuses the item. Key changes come from the MIDI key-signature events.
- Stated residual limits: repeats are written out; two voices in one hand are merged into chords; the result is never called Mutopia's notation. Files are pinned by sha256 (.ly from the GitHub mirror `MutopiaProject/MutopiaProject` at a pinned revision, .mid from mutopiaproject.org), and the licence is re-read from the .ly header.
- Present state: `content/sources/mutopia.json` has 1 item (Joplin *Pine Apple Rag*). `build/ct1/build.log` shows `import [MUTO] imported 0 score(s), 1 placeholder(s)` because the .mid was not fetched in that build.
- Consequences for the files sampled here (inferred, not tested): items whose Mutopia `.ly` is a zip (`-lys.zip`, e.g. Czerny Op. 840, Clementi Op. 36) or whose MIDI is a multi-movement zip (`-mids.zip`) need unpacking that the docstring does not describe. The duet entries (Diabelli Op. 149/163) carry two players' parts in one file.
