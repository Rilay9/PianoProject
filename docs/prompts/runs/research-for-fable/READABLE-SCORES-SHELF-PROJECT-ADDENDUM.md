# Readable-score review: shelf and project addendum

Research/review only. Authority: `claude/readable-scores@c1556d70`.

This pass is deliberately lighter than the acquisition-rung review. A repertoire shelf/project does not need to isolate one newly taught mechanical fact. It needs to be a legitimate score, plausibly placed for the project, and useful for the learner action named by the rung.

The main result is reassuring: most of these areas do **not** need the kind of surgery required by `rock.4`, `jazz.4`, `blues.4`, `chords-pop.7`, etc.

---

## `rock.7` — building it: register, density and knowing when

### `In the Hall of the Mountain King`
Direct read: 87 bars, two staves, estimated level 8.4, 47 dynamics, 156 pedal elements, 909 articulations. The score text explicitly contains repeated `cresc.`, `cresc. poco a poco`, and `e sempre cresc.` indications while the texture/register develops.

**Verdict: KEEP — excellent conceptual reference / stretch project.** The written score really does embody gradual build through register, density and dynamics. It is difficult, so use an excerpt/reference rather than pretending it is Stage-7 acquisition repertoire.

### Rachmaninoff Piano Concerto No. 2 / Moonlight III
These are obviously large stretch works rather than controlled exercises. That is acceptable on this rung because the point is observing/using large-scale build and density, not isolating one finger pattern.

**Rung conclusion:** keep the shelf/project framing; use excerpts for analysis/application. No detector redesign needed.

---

## `latin.7` — the showpiece and the hand nobody practises

Representative direct reads:

### `El Choclo`
49 bars, two staves, estimated level 7.57, real key changes (2 sharps → 1 flat → 2 sharps), dense tango accompaniment and active LH.

### `Asturias`
199 bars, two staves, 63 dynamics, 50 pedal elements, 1,522 articulations, very dense two-hand writing.

### `Malagueña`
141 bars, two staves, 601 tuplet elements, 683 articulations, substantial two-hand writing.

These are genuinely **showpiece** scores with demanding accompaniment/left-hand work. They are not mislabeled as easy teaching examples.

**Verdict: KEEP — STRETCH/PROJECT.** The rung title itself signals that this is a destination/showpiece shelf. Use smaller exercises separately to prepare the neglected hand; do not simplify these scores just to make them resemble drills.

---

## `jam.7` — trading fours and a set list both players know

This is a learner/social task, not a written-technique claim.

### `Jazz Me Blues`
Direct read: 41-bar one-staff lead sheet, 41 chord symbols, and score text explicitly says `Break 1 Bar` multiple times and **`Solos at "C"`**.

**Verdict: KEEP — PRIMARY JAM SUBSTRATE.** A lead sheet is exactly appropriate here because the learner/player is meant to supply accompaniment, take solos and interact rather than reproduce a fixed two-hand arrangement.

`After You've Gone` and the other shared standards can serve the same set-list function even when they are one-staff lead sheets. Here that format is a feature, unlike on a rung that claims to teach a written comping texture.

---

## `classical.6+`, `classical.9`, later ragtime shelves

These are repertoire/project rungs rather than narrow acquisition claims. The direct reads already sampled across the research packet show authentic full works rather than title-only placeholders (Clementi, Burgmüller, Schumann, Chopin, Beethoven, Joplin, etc.).

For these shelves Fable should focus on:
- file/source correctness;
- sensible difficulty/order;
- whether the whole work or an excerpt is the intended project;
- edition quality and markings.

Do **not** apply the early-rung purity test to a long-form repertoire shelf. A Ballade or full rag is supposed to contain many demands.

---

## Holiday shelves

The holiday branches are primarily repertoire/context shelves. They do not need every score to mechanically demonstrate one named new technique. Verify the score and difficulty, then keep useful variety.

The earlier review still applies where a holiday score is cross-listed onto a narrow acquisition rung: cross-listing does not exempt it from that rung's specific purpose.

---

# Final distinction for the implementation/content pass

Use three different review standards, not one giant classifier:

1. **Acquisition rung** — at least one score/excerpt must literally and cleanly demonstrate the named new thing.
2. **Transfer rung/song** — can include already-taught complexity, but must actually give the learner a chance to use the target.
3. **Shelf/project/jam** — may be complex by design; verify authenticity, file correctness, difficulty and suitability for the learner task rather than demanding mechanical purity.

This distinction explains why `Greensleeves (with chords)` is unacceptable proof of a power chord on `rock.4`, while an advanced Grieg/Joplin/Latin showpiece can be completely appropriate on a later project shelf.
