## 1. Promised endpoint

**Track description** (`00-tracks.json:48`): "A drill and a short text at every stage; runs the whole way." **Design** (`02-curriculum.md` D6, lines 577-617): stage-by-stage list ending "Stage 7: extended chords (9/11/13), chord-scale relationships, form analysis (binary/ternary/rondo/sonata). Stage 8-9: chromatic harmony, counterpoint species, jazz reharmonisation, composition." **Last rung's lesson** (`theory.9.md:12-13`): "The last theory rung is not more theory. It is using all of it at once on something the length of a piece of music"; its test (`theory.9.md:43-44`): "You can hear eight bars three times and write them down."

**What a competent developing pianist can do in this domain** (RCM Ear Tests and Berklee Ear Training 1 as evidence of coverage): clap or tap back a rhythm; sing or hum an interval and a scale degree; name intervals ascending and descending, triad and seventh qualities, cadences and short progressions in major and minor keys; play back a short tonal melody given the key and the first note; take a heard progression to the keyboard; transcribe a short melody and a bass line; connect sound to notation and to function (numerals).

**Reasonable PianoProject endpoint:** a learner who can play back a heard tonal phrase and a heard progression at the keyboard in several keys, name the function of what they hear (cadences, V/x, a key change), and write an eight-bar tune plus chords from hearing it.

**Is the track narrower than its name and its design?** Yes, in two ways. (a) The lessons deliver ear work plus numerals/secondary dominants/modulation; the design's figured bass, extended-chord teaching, form analysis (binary/ternary/rondo/sonata), chromatic harmony, species counterpoint and composition are not taught in these seven lessons (section 3 gives the searches). Some of those live in other tracks (counterpoint and form in `classical.4.shelf`/`classical.7`-`9`, reharmonisation in `improv.8`/`jazz.7`); none is cross-referenced from `theory.*`. (b) It is a **keyboard-echo** ear track: every drill row I read asks the learner to play something at the keyboard. None asks them to pick a name from a list, to sing into the app, or to write notation.

## 5. Correctness concerns

**No wrong note name and no invented concept found in the seven lessons.** Every objective statement was checked; each of these is correct against the sources:
- Interval counting and quality (`theory.3.md:12-22`): inclusive count, 4 semitones major 3rd, perfect 4th/5th/octave have no major/minor form, ratios 4:3, 3:2, 2:1 (just intonation; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_(music)). Twelve listed distances inside the octave is the correct count (m2, M2, m3, M3, P4, tritone, P5, m6, M6, m7, M7, P8).
- Key signatures (`theory.3.md:30-33`): "a half step above the last sharp"; "second-to-last flat"; one flat is F major (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_signature). Sharp order F C G and flat order B E A correct.
- Circle (`theory.4.md:15-22`): order of keys, relative minor a minor third below, neighbours share all but one note.
- Cadences (`theory.4.md:30-36`): V-I authentic, anything-V half, IV-I plagal "amen", V-vi deceptive (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadence).
- Seventh qualities and the ii of a minor key (`theory.5.md:12-19`); I-vi-IV-V as the 1950s progression; bass drops a third in I-vi and leaps a fourth in ii-V.
- Modes (`theory.5.md:29-42`): D Dorian = minor with a raised 6th, G Mixolydian = major with flat 7, A Aeolian, C Ionian; the leading-tone remark is correct and explicitly hedged ("a colour, not a law"). Mode step tables in `theory.ts:198-206` are correct for all seven.
- Roman numerals (`theory.6.md:15-18`): G is V in C, B-flat is V in E-flat; F aeolian prints four flats (`:50-51`) (F aeolian = A-flat major, four flats).
- Secondary dominant (`theory.7.md:15-20`): V/V in C is D major, F-sharp marks the departure (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_chord). - Chord-scale (`theory.7.md:30-39`) is correct and hedged (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord-scale_system): mixolydian/dorian/locrian as first choices, the four chord tones fixed, lydian-dominant/altered/aeolian/phrygian as alternatives, "a way of finding notes, not a law of harmony", and it states that the drill marks only its own scale (matches `CHORD_SCALES`, `theory.ts:223-245`).

## 7. Measurement limits

**What the app can verify from MIDI** (read from code, not run):
- Intervals, chord qualities, cadences, progressions, seventh qualities, modes, numerals, chord-scale, melodic dictation, tune playback: whether the learner played the expected pitches in order, any octave (`anyOctave`), via `PromptDrill`; chain length for Simon; chord segmentation for harmonic dictation (120 ms silence or next-chord note, `theory.6.md:25-29` says so correctly); rhythm tap-back timing within 150 ms of the pattern relative to the first tap.
- It cannot tell a learner who *recognised* the interval from one who found the notes by trial on the keyboard, because the answer is pitches and the name is revealed afterwards (`types.ts:116-120`). Whether the learner could *name* the interval, cadence or quality is never asked; `theory.3/4/5 "How you'll know"` lines speak as if it were.

**What is self-checked and the lesson does not say so:**

- I-IV-V by ear: honestly labelled ("Nothing drills it yet", `theory.3.md:39-40`).
- "Two progressions recognised in unfamiliar music" (`theory.5.md:65`) and "one tune transposed into three keys on the spot" (`theory.5.md:66`): no row on the rung supplies unfamiliar music or the transposition.
- Mode, pivot-chord and key-change *naming* (`theory.8.md:49-50`, "You can say which key a passage moved to and which chord was the door"): the dictation drill checks the chords played, not the spoken name of the pivot or key.
