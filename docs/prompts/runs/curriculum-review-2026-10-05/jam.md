## 1. Promised endpoint

Track description, `content/curriculum/00-tracks.json:90`: "Mini-module: playing with a guitarist, in guitar keys." Design, `docs/02-curriculum.md:735-742`: "The friend plays guitar, so the jam keys are E, A, G, D (and C)"; content named there is the head and chorus, counting off, twelve-bar blues in those keys, comping behind a soloist, walking bass when there is no bass player, trading fours, following a lead sheet and the form tracker, "endings and turnarounds", a shared set list, and "recording a jam in Free mode". Last rung's own test, `jam.7.md:61-63`: "Eight trades in a row at a steady tempo, in on the beat every time, with at least one of them being a single idea rather than a run."

What a competent developing pianist can do here: hold a role (comp, bass, soloist) in a two-to-four-piece group; keep the form while listening; leave space; recover when lost and find the form again; follow cues and breaks; adjust dynamics to who is soloing; simplify under pressure; swap roles; count off at the tempo the tune needs; end together. The dossier sets the endpoint as "can function with another musician", not "can play a chord chart alone" (`dossier:1411-1412`). ABRSM treats playing with a rhythm section as a late skill (Grades 6-8; Grades 1-5 are unaccompanied), which supports the track starting at stage 4 as an extra module rather than as core.

Reasonable PianoProject endpoint: the learner can sit in on a twelve-bar blues and on a lead-sheet tune with one other player, in a key that player can use, in either role, and can notice and fix being lost and end when the other player ends. The track is intentionally narrower than its name in three ways: the partner is a guitarist (keys E, A, G, D, C), the form is mainly the twelve-bar blues until `jam.7`, and nobody else is ever modelled (no singer, no horn). Today the shipped endpoint is "comp and walk a blues form and trade phrases over a generated bed", which is a part of the one above; the missing parts are in section 3.

## 3. Missing or weak abilities

Search scope for every absence below: the four lesson files (`jam.md`, `jam.5.md`, `jam.6.md`, `jam.7.md`, read in full), plus `grep -n -i` over `content/lessons/*.md` (109 files) for the terms named in each item. "Absent" means those two scopes, nothing wider; the app code was grepped where stated.

1. **Recovering when lost and finding the form again. Foundational bridge for the stated endpoint.** The sentence "Knowing where you are is the thing beginners lose first" (`jam.md:52`) is the only acknowledgement. Over `jam*.md` the terms `lost|get back in|rejoin|re-enter|come back in|find the form|find your place` give only the two "without losing the form" test sentences (`jam.md:65`, `jam.5.md:48`). Elsewhere: `improv.5.md:26` asserts "The form gives you landmarks, so you are never lost" (a claim, not a drill); `4.6.md:23-26` teaches keeping going and "deliberately drop a note and carry on in time" for a solo piece; `holiday.6.md:35` is about performance. Nothing teaches what to do when the learner has lost bar 5 or the chorus count while the bed continues. The mechanism for the dossier's drill exists in part, see section 7 and recommendation 1. Why it matters: it is the difference between "plays along" and "functions with another musician". External: the dossier (`:1397-1411`) and ABRSM Grades 6-8 (the candidate must follow the chord sequence through the solo section with a rhythm section that does not stop). Severity: foundational bridge.
2. **Ending together. Foundational bridge for ensemble use, depth for the module.** The design promised "endings and turnarounds" (`docs/02:741`). The lessons give `jam.7.md:28-30` ("at the end a tag or a turnaround to send it round again") and nothing else; over `jam*.md` the pattern `ending|ritard|rit\.|fermata|end together|final chord|last chord|outro` hits only that line, and over `blues.*.md` and `jazz.*.md` the word `ending` appears only at `blues.5.md:16` (a left-hand figure ending on the V7). A learner is never asked to find or follow them as an ending. Severity: foundational bridge for the endpoint; the repertoire (MODEL) is already shipped.
3. **Listening while playing, as a task. Depth.** Two sentences (`jam.md:44-45`, `jam.7.md:19-20`). The lab deliberately does not judge the answer against the call (`tradingFours.ts` header: "a mode that scored imitation would teach the opposite"). Call-and-response answered "same rhythm, different ending" is on the improv ladder (`improv.3.md:29`, `improv.4.md:66`), not in this track. Nobody in this process can judge whether a learner answered what they heard; it stays self-checked.
4. **Counting off at several tempos; counting off at all. Depth.** One paragraph (`jam.md:30-32`). The tools could carry the practice (chart count-off, lab tempo 40-240, Settings count-in bars 0-4, all above) but the lesson never asks for it. 5. **Adjusting dynamics, simplifying under pressure, following live cues. Enrichment.** One line each, no control and no check. Serenade Magazine (above) names dynamics and non-verbal cues as ensemble basics; a general-audience source, so recorded as supporting only.
6. **Role switching and trading eights. Enrichment.** The lab supports both roles; lessons never ask for one chorus comping and the next soloing. Fours and twos are enough for the stated endpoint; eights add little.
7. **Recording a jam in Free mode. The sentence "that is where the horns played them in 1920" gives one date for five charts (only After You've Gone carries a year in its title, 1918, per the dump) and a cause no file in the repo documents; it is a heuristic stated as a reason, low severity.
- "the one you have already met on the jazz ladder": the dump lists `rungs: jam.7, jazz.5` for After You've Gone. True.
Role fit: single stave, chords printed, a repeating chorus or solo section, printed cues. Correct substrate for a trading and form task. The audit's GREEN stands. Edition note: `song.pop.after-you-ve-gone.pdmx` is the 36-bar edition; `docs/genre-plans/jam.md` lists a different After You've Gone CID (20 bars, `QmWUXq...`) as IN ARCHIVE, so that plan line is a different edition and says nothing about the shipped one.

Concerns at HEAD:
1. **`jam.7.md:28-30`: "a tag or a turnaround to send it round again".** A tag is the closing phrase added to finish a tune, a turnaround is what sends the form round again. The shipped chart that has a tag, Storyville, shows both: bar 53 is the "back to B" instruction and bar 54 the `rit.` and `Tag` that ends it with a final barline at 56. Calling the tag a way to "send it round again" is wrong for that chart. Minor, easy to fix, and it is the only line in the track about endings. I did not fetch a source defining "tag"; this rests on the chart's own bars and standard usage, so confidence is medium.
2. **`jam.6.md:44-48`, the lab verdict for a walking bass.** The text sends the learner to *Jam it* and says "At the end of each time round it says how many of your notes were in the blues scale". The code counts every note the learner plays against the blues scale (`LabScreen.ts:993`, `judgeLabPass`, `currentTradeScale()` giving the blues scale for the `blues` progression, `tradingFours.ts:tradeScale`). A walking line written by this rung's own exercise, `exercise.walking-bass.e.blues.intro` bar 1, is E G# B D#, and the E blues scale is E G A B♭ B D. G# (the third) and D# (the chromatic approach the lesson calls "the whole trick", `jam.6.md:23-26`) are out of the scale by construction. So a correct walking bass is reported as roughly half "in the blues scale" with no warning. This is misleading feedback for the rung's own task, the sort of measurement that teaches the wrong lesson. The mechanism (`bed === 'hold'` reports scale membership) is documented as honest only "under Hold the chords the learner is playing a line" (`LabScreen.ts:535-541`); a bass line is a line that is not a blues-scale line.
3. **`jam.5.md:34-36`: "a bass and drums that keep the form without keeping time *for* you".** The bed's drums are a kick on 1 and 3, a snare on 2 and 4 and a hat on every beat (`backingLoop.ts:119-165`); they keep time. What the sentence presumably means is "do not lead you". Wording only, but it contradicts the machine. Likewise `jam.md:57-59` says the bed is "a bass line and a kick"; it also has snare and hat.
4. **`jam.md:30-32`, the count-in.** "Whoever starts says the tempo by counting two bars out loud: 'one, two, one two three four'." Two bars of 4/4 are eight beats; the quoted count is six. The usual jazz count-off is "one, two" at half the tempo then "one, two, three, four" at tempo, which equals two bars; the lesson does not say the first two are at half tempo, so a learner reading it literally counts six beats at tempo, a bar and a half. Whether the half-tempo reading is meant cannot be decided from the text. ABRSM's wording (a "two-bar count-in") matches the two-bar claim. Unverified as music; flagged because it is the one counting rule the track gives.
5. **`jam.5.md:28-31` "anticipated" and the generator.** The generated `anticipated` figure is beat 1 plus the "and" of 3 (`generate_exercises.py:4111`, offsets 0.0 and 2.5; confirmed in `exercise.comping.c.anticipated.intro` as a chord at 0 and 2.5, rest to the end of the bar). Anticipation in the comping sense is a chord played an eighth early, normally the "and" of 4 into the next bar's change. The "and" of 3 anticipates beat 4, where nothing in the 4-bar studies changes. I did not fetch a published definition of "anticipation" to settle it, so this is a lead for the synthesis and not a defect I assert; it sits in the Part D4 comping table (`docs/02`) and is shared with `jazz.4`/`jazz.5`.
6. `jam.md:15-18`: "Guitars are built around open strings in E, A, D and G, so those keys, and C, are usually comfortable". Stated as "usually", as a heuristic, and the design says the same (`docs/02:735`). Not a law. (The sentence omits that jazz guitarists play in flat keys without difficulty; the track is about a friend jam, which is its stated scope.)

No unsafe statements found in the four lesson files.

## 7. Measurement limits

What the app can verify from MIDI in this track today:
- Trade runs: whether the first note lands inside the learner's window and the offset in ms, and how many notes fall in or outside that window and in scale (`tradingFours.ts` `judgeTrade`: `cameIn`, `entryOffsetMs`, `notesInWindow`, `notesOutside`, `notesInScale`). Said on a quiet line; "nothing is recorded and nothing here can be passed or failed" (`04` lines 1296-1301; `jam.7.md:54-55`).
- A time round under *Hold the chords*: how many notes were in the scale; under *Play the tune*: how many were chord tones of the bar they fell in (`LabScreen.ts:545-562`). As section 5 shows, the first is the wrong measure for a walking bass.
- Chart view: which bar is sounding, and whether what is held matches the bar's chord (`ChordChartScreen.ts:204-215`, `data-match` yes/no/idle).
- Tempo mode on the Score screen: the clock advances regardless of input and notes are matched to steps within a tolerance (`docs/05` §3, line 126 onward); the one existing mechanism where the music continues while the learner may fall off it.

The lab has no mechanism to remove its own bass (`backingLoop.ts:140-155`: the bass event is pushed whenever a root exists; no setting in `LabScreen.ts` turns it off, `grep -n -i bass` finds one comment) and no way to hide the form tracker, drop the bed for a bar, or demand a re-entry at an unannounced bar. No "sit out N bars" setting exists (`grep -n -i "mute|sit out|rest bars|drop out" app/src/ui/screens/LabScreen.ts` finds no such control; I did not grep the whole of `app/src`).

Self-checked, no actor can decide in this process: whether the learner listened, whether the answer related to the call, whether space was musical, whether comping was supportive or intrusive, whether the dynamics were right, whether a count-off was the right tempo, and any question of how the music sounded (unverified as music). Space and dynamics are measurable in principle from MIDI (onsets per bar, velocities), but nothing in this track measures them, and I do not propose a detector.

The dossier's recovery drill: nothing like it exists. Searched: `jam*.md` for the terms in section 3 item 1; `content/curriculum/stage-*.json` track `jam` options (form-tracker drill is free play, nothing judged); `special.ts` `BackingTrackDrill` (counts notes played only); lab trade code. Closest mechanisms, grounded in code: (1) the lab's bed keeps going, shows "Bar n of N · pass n" and has no first-note latch (`04` lines 1280-1283: "no first-note latch anywhere in this mode"); (2) trading already asks the learner to re-enter at a window boundary and measures the entry offset; (3) Tempo mode on the Score screen runs on, whatever the learner plays. A learner can "drop out" on their own today (stop playing for a chorus and re-enter where they think the form is) and read the bar counter afterward; nothing tells them to, and nothing checks the re-entry bar. "Muting" the learner is not something the app can do to a MIDI keyboard played through the learner's own speakers; the oracle for "re-entered at the right bar" would be the existing bar-at-key-down record in the lab (`passNotes`, `04` line 1340 "the bar a note counts against is the bar the loop was on when the key went down").

## 8. Recommended changes

Ranked. None is a design for code; each is the smallest change that names the learner problem.

1. **Add the lost-and-find task to `jam.5` or `jam.6`, as prose plus a self-run protocol on the existing lab. Smallest change: in `jam.5.md` (comping), a paragraph and a three-step protocol: start *Jam it* on the twelve-bar bed, play one chorus, stop playing for two bars while the bed runs (the lab does not stop), come back in at the next four-bar boundary (bar 5 or bar 9) using only what you hear and the bar counter, then do it again with the chart covered (the same card the core path uses, `4.6.md:12-17`).
