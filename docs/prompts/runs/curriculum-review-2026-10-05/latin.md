## 1. Promised endpoint

Track description (`content/curriculum/00-tracks.json:76`): "Mini-module: clave, tumbao and montuno." Last rung (`latin.7.md:12-14`): "Three concert pieces, each written to be played through in front of people rather than to accompany anything." Last rung's own test (`latin.7.md:59-61`): one showpiece played through once in Perform mode, the unpractised hand as even at the end as at the start.

A competent developing pianist in this domain (Berklee outline: 12/8 bell pattern; Cuban styles; clave, cascara, campana; guajeo/montuno; tumbao; Brazilian maxixe/choro/samba/bossa; Argentine/Uruguayan tango, milonga, candombe; odd meters; transcription; creating original accompaniments) can hold a clave, play a bass tumbao and a guajeo locked to it, comp in at least two or three distinct traditions, and take parts by ear. Berklee frames "performing stylistically accurate Latin piano parts in solo and rhythm-section settings" and "creating original accompaniments" as the objectives.

Reasonable PianoProject endpoint: the track is intentionally narrower than its name. It is a **mini-module** in the track file, but the shipped rungs go beyond that description: latin.6 is Argentine tango plus a Jelly Roll Morton tresillo piece, and latin.7 is Spanish/Cuban concert repertoire (El Choclo, Asturias, Malagueña) taught as a left-hand and endurance problem, not as a Latin style. So the as-built endpoint is: (a) hold and feel a clave, (b) play the tresillo/habanera/tumbao/montuno figures with the stated order of build, (c) play printed tango and tresillo left hands under a tune, (d) play a showpiece with an ostinato hand. It does not reach comping from a chart in a Latin style, varying a groove, or learning a pattern by ear. The track description undersells rungs 6 and 7 and oversells nothing; the name "Latin" oversells the breadth (Brazilian is three one-staff melody files and one lesson sentence).

The one video per lesson is a how-to, not a listening example. The brief asks each rung to name a listening/transcription example: none does.
3. **No station where the learner chooses, varies or improvises a Latin pattern, or comps from a chart.** Severity: depth. The latin.5 concept list includes `comping` and `bossa-nova` (stage-5.json), but the lesson text contains neither a comping task nor a bossa pattern (grep: "comp" appears nowhere in the four lessons; `docs/02-curriculum.md:1004` records that "latin no longer names the walking bass"). The packet and dossier say to treat styles distinctly (packet section 8 "Latin"). Berklee separates Cuban, Brazilian and South American weeks. Severity: depth; enrichment if the owner keeps the track narrow.
5. **The montuno exercises are the clave's rhythm played as chords, not a guajeo.** Generator: `write_montuno` puts a chord on each of the five clave strokes and nothing else (`generate_exercises.py:5077-5101`). External: a guajeo is an arpeggiated chord-tone ostinato, typically octave-doubled, aligned to the clave pattern, not necessarily struck on each clave stroke (Wikipedia Guajeo, Montuno; Mauleon *101 Montunos* cited, not read). The lesson's own words are "a repeating syncopated figure built from the chord's notes, usually in octaves or thirds, locked to the clave" (`latin.md:28-31`), and `latin.6.md:35-41` says "every note is a clave stroke". As a first acquisition drill that isolates the rhythmic lock this is defensible; but the rung never later asks for a real arpeggiated guajeo, so the learner finishes with the rhythm skeleton. Severity: depth. Not an error; a thin step.
6. **Rumba clave and bossa clave are in the exercise list but not defined in any lesson.** `latin.3.md:16-20` defines only son 3-2/2-3. The exercises `exercise.clave.rumba-3-2` and `.bossa` (stage-3.json:1113-1114) are single-line rhythms with no lesson sentence saying how they differ (the one-stroke delay: son's third stroke on beat 4, rumba's on the "and" of 4; bossa delays the last stroke by an eighth). 7. **Cuban and Argentine traditions share a rung without saying so** (`latin.6.md:15-45`; the dossier asks that this be clarified). Severity: depth. ## 5. Correctness concerns

Checked against sources and notation. Items are heuristics or imprecisions; none is a definition error.

1. **Definitions verified correct.** Son clave 3-2 on beat 1, "and" of 2, beat 4, then beats 2 and 3 (`latin.md:15-18`); 2-3 as the same pattern with bars swapped; clave as one two-bar unit (`latin.3.md:16-20`); tresillo as the clave's three-side, 3+3+2 over eight eighths (`latin.3.md:31-33`); tumbao as the bass on the "and" of 2 and beat 4 with the downbeat empty (`latin.md:23-27`; Wikipedia Tumbao: bombo/ponche, "downbeat left empty", last note often held over the next downbeat, which matches the generator tying the beat-4 note across the barline). Generator clave table (`generate_exercises.py:4870-4876`): son 3-2 = (0, 1.5, 3.0, 5.0, 6.0) and 2-3 = (1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 5.5, 7.0) correct; rumba and bossa rows correct (Wikipedia Clave).
2. **"Neither is on the beat" is false as written** (`latin.md:33-34`: "Tumbao and montuno are independent and neither is on the beat"; also the groove exercise's on-score text). The generator's tumbao strikes beat 4 (offset 3.0) and the montuno strikes beats 1, 4, 2 and 3 of the clave's five strokes (`CLAVE_PATTERNS`); what is empty is the **downbeat (beat 1) of the tumbao**, and the two parts are interlocking (they coincide on offsets 1.5 and 3.0 of the three-side), not "independent". Severity: wording; it is a heuristic stated as a fact. Source: the generator tables themselves.
3. **"usually in octaves or thirds"** (`latin.md:28-31`) for a montuno: Wikipedia Guajeo says piano guajeos are typically in octaves (single notes in both hands or octave-doubled right hand) and are arpeggiated; the exercises voice third+fifth (a third) or third, fifth and octave. Not wrong for the drill, but "arpeggiated" is the missing word. Severity: wording.
4. **Malagueña description loose** (`latin.7.md:29-35`): the lesson says the opening pages' left hand is "two positions ... alternating in eighths, three of each to the bar", and the ostinato is "difficult twenty bars running". Notation (`song.classical.lecuona-malaguena-by-ernesto-lecuona.pdmx`): bars 1-8 are quarter-note fifths (C#2+G#2); the six-eighths-per-bar alternation (dyad above, C#2 below, three of each) occurs in only the even bars 10-20 and bars 104, 106, 108 (music21 onset scan of the left hand); the odd bars 9-19 are a dotted-quarter / eighth / quarter version. A left-hand repeated-pattern run of about twenty bars exists (bars 1-20), but it is not uniformly the figure described. Severity: imprecision in a claim that depends on the notation.
5. **The other notation claims checked and true:** Por Una Cabeza 66 bars, two staves, four-event left hand (bars 1-14 unbroken; 56/66 bars exact); The Crave 53 bars, D minor, LH 3+3+2 (27 exact, 32 with flourish); La Cumparsita B 16 bars, two flats, LH G, G flat, F then E, E flat, D; El Choclo 49 bars, 2/4, two sharps; Asturias 199 bars, 3/4, G minor, right hand D4 on alternate sixteenths with the left hand's melody between (bars 1-12); Malagueña 141 bars, 3/4, C# minor.
6. **Guantanamera under a son clave** (`latin.3.md:35-38`): guajira-son is described as riding a son clave (Wikipedia Guajira (music), via search; secondary). Medium confidence; I did not obtain a primary source.
