## 1. Promised endpoint

Track description (`content/curriculum/00-tracks.json`): "The twelve-bar form, shuffle feel and boogie left hand." Design (`docs/02-curriculum.md` D3): stage 3 blue notes; 4 form, shuffle, boogie bass; 5 turnarounds, tremolo thirds, call and response, walking bass introduced; 6 boogie left hands, "slow blues 12/8, minor blues", walking line under a right hand; 7 "improvising full choruses, tritone subs in blues, gospel-blues, New Orleans", leaping left hand; 8 form in twelve keys, ninths, comping over own walking bass; 9 improvise over the form, "fast boogie, stride-blues hybrids, transcription projects".

Last rung's lesson (`blues.9.md:12`, `:57-58`): "Nothing new is added here. This rung is the point of the other six." Exit: three different choruses, none running out of ideas in bar five.

A competent developing blues pianist (Berklee outline, URL in section 0) plays authentic accompaniment across shuffle, straight rock-and-roll, slow blues and New Orleans grooves; plays blues phrases, calls and the vocabulary of intros, turnarounds and endings; improvises single-line solos; keeps a left-hand pattern going; and adapts for solo piano and ensemble.

Reasonable PianoProject endpoint: a learner who can play a twelve-bar blues in a handful of keys with a shuffle feel, a boogie or walking left hand, a turnaround and a simple intro/ending, and can improvise a few choruses with the left hand running on its own, from a small vocabulary of licks. The track is intentionally narrower than its name (the description names form, shuffle and boogie only). The shipped stages 7-9 widen it toward stride and ninths (lessons) and toward early-jazz/ragtime repertoire (Chevy Chase, Stumbling, Black Bottom Stomp, Handful of Keys), while D3's slow 12/8, minor blues, gospel-blues and New Orleans never reached any lesson (section 3).

## 3. Missing or weak abilities

1. **A scaffold for "right hand improvises, left hand keeps the groove on its own" (foundational bridge for the endpoint).** Why it matters: this is the defining skill of blues and boogie piano and what blues.9 asks for unprompted ("over your own left hand", `blues.9.md:26-29`). Evidence a curriculum includes it: Berklee weeks 8-10 "solo blues piano left-hand patterns and improvisation" (URL in section 0). Where it is built: nowhere. Boogie exercises put held right-hand shells over the left-hand figure (`make_boogie`, `fingered_chord` per bar); Jam it and Trading fours supply the app's own bass, so the learner's left hand is silent; Duet gives the learner the right hand with the app on the left. Scope: blues.3-9 lessons, their `exerciseOptions` and `tools`. I did not inspect the lab's settings for a bass-off option (unverified whether one exists).
2. **Blues vocabulary / licks and transcribing them by ear (depth, close to foundational at blues.9).** `blues.9.md:17` and `:53` dismiss "a solo built from licks" and "the lick you always use" but no blues lesson teaches a lick (grep `\blick` over `blues.*.md`: only those two lines plus a video label). Ear production: `drill.ear.simon-blues-c` (stage 3) and `drill.ear.tune-long` at stage 9, which `earTuneDrill` builds as a random diatonic major-scale tune in G, straight eighths, no blue notes or shuffle (`harmony.ts`, scale `[0,2,4,5,7,9,11]`). So the only ear-production drill on the last rung contains none of the idiom. Evidence: Berklee weeks 11-12 "single-line lick development"; dossier Track 6 "transcribe one short historical lick and transform it". Scope: `grep -n -i transcrib content/lessons/*.md` hits only theory.4, classical.4.shelf, rock.overview.
3. **Intros and endings (depth).** Berklee names "intros, turnarounds, endings". In `blues.3-9` grep for `intro|ending|tag|coda|stop-time|break` finds only "ending on the V7" inside the turnaround description (`blues.5.md:16`). Outside the track, `jam.7.md:28` mentions "a tag or a turnaround" (not read in full by me). A one-line ending is the thing a learner needs to finish a chorus.
4. **Blues-idiom turnarounds are described but not exercised (depth).** `blues.5.md:14-17` describes the chromatic descent from tonic to fifth ending on V7; the exercise taught is I-vi-ii-V, whose generated chords are Cmaj7-Am7-Dm7-G7 (`TURNAROUNDS` in `generate_exercises.py`), a jazz turnaround; blues.7's variant is iii-VI7-ii-V. I-IV-I-V and the chromatic descent have no exercise. Evidence: PianoGroove and the turnaround sources above treat the V7 ending and chromatic line as the stock move. Scope: stage-5/6/7 `exerciseOptions`.
5. **Form variants and the other feels: quick IV, minor blues, slow 12/8 blues, straight rock-and-roll (depth).** D3 names "slow blues 12/8, minor blues" at stage 6; no lesson mentions them (`grep -i -E 'minor blues|slow blues|12/8|quick change|quick-change'` over all `content/lessons/*.md`: no hit in any blues lesson). Straight-feel blues is only framed as a mistake (`blues.4.md:51`). Evidence: Berklee ("straight versus swing feels", "slow blues"), PianoGroove (quick IV).
6. **Rhythmic phrasing variety (depth).** blues.5 and blues.9 vary note choice and space; the app's Trading fours feedback reports "inside your own bars" and "how many of your notes were in the blues scale" (`blues.5.md:56-59`), a pitch-class measure. No lesson asks for a rhythmic idea to be reused, displaced or varied. Evidence: Berklee "blues phrasing, calls".
