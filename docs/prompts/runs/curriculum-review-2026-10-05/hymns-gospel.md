## 1. Promised endpoint

- **Track name and description.** `content/curriculum/00-tracks.json:61-62`: title "Hymns & gospel"; description "Mini-module: four-part texture, passing chords and walk-ups." Design D8 (`docs/02-curriculum.md:705-708`): "Amazing Grace, Be Thou My Vision, It Is Well, Great Is Thy Faithfulness (1923), Holy Holy Holy, Come Thou Fount, What a Friend, Swing Low, Wade in the Water, Go Tell It on the Mountain, This Little Light of Mine, Down by the Riverside; gospel passing chords and 'walk-ups'." So the design itself defines the "gospel" half as "gospel passing chords and walk-ups" plus spirituals; the track description names only the devices and says nothing about call-and-response, choir, singer or shout material.
- **Last rung** (`hymns.6.md:12-15, 59-62`): "These are piano pieces made out of hymn tunes, with every note of the accompaniment written ... You can play the melody of one of these on its own from memory, and you can say in one sentence what its left hand is doing — and when you play the whole thing, a listener hears the hymn rather than the accompaniment." So the last rung's endpoint is playing a written hymn arrangement and describing its left hand, not making one and not playing gospel style.
- **Where the word "gospel" occurs in lesson prose.** `grep -n -i gospel content/lessons/hymns*.md` over the five files finds the word in prose once: `hymns.md:28` ("**Gospel passing chords.** Where a hymn holds one chord for two beats, gospel fills the gap"), plus the video label at `hymns.md:6`; elsewhere it is only the unit id string. Over all 109 files of `content/lessons/*.md`, `grep -l -i` finds no file containing "spiritual", "shout", "choir", "congregation", "testimony" or "preacher". The curriculum JSON (not lesson text) carries one more use of the word: the `hymns.5` finder constraint "a gospel or spiritual tune" (stage-5.json) and `levelWords` "gospel hymn with chord symbols".
- **What a competent developing pianist in this domain can do.** Two different domains share the name. (1) Hymn and spiritual accompaniment: put chords under a tune, read and play SATB hymn settings on two staves with the tune audible, add simple decoration, play a written hymn arrangement. (2) Gospel keyboard as Berklee outlines it (https://online.berklee.edu/courses/gospel-music-for-keyboard): history and spiritual/blues roots, call-and-response in testimony and service music, traditional hymns/cadences/chants, gospel evolution since the 1970s, praise-and-worship, choir music and rehearsal practice, talk and shout music, accompanying preachers and singers, blending gospel with secular influences. Berklee lists "Piano Scales 101", "Piano Chords 101", "Jazz Piano" and "Blues and Rock Keyboard Techniques" as prerequisites or equivalent knowledge (same page), so it treats gospel as a later, specialised study.
- **Reasonable PianoProject endpoint.** The track delivers domain (1) plus two gospel devices (bass walk-up, half-step passing chord) and spirituals as repertoire. It does not teach domain (2). The track is narrower than its name: the name "Hymns & gospel" invites domain (2) and the description does not.

1. **`hymns.md:43-45`: false statement about an edition.** The text says "*Abide with Me*, *Jesus Loves Me* and *Rock of Ages* ... are in four parts with no symbols". For `song.classical.jesus-loves-me.pdmx` the dump (bars 1-18) shows one melody line in the right hand over a left hand of repeating eighth-note broken chords (bar 1: C3 G3 E4 G3 D4 G3 C4 G3), 0 chord symbols, 2 staves, 4/4, C major; it is not a four-voice setting. Corrects: the one id `song.classical.jesus-loves-me.pdmx` at Stage 3. The 425 audit's `KEEP` for `hymns / song.classical.jesus-loves-me.pdmx` reflects a placement review, not this claim check.
2. **`hymns.2` Joyful, Joyful edition has the tune's third lowered.** `song.classical.beethoven-ludwig-van-beethoven-joyful-joyful-we-adore-thee.pdmx` (hymns.2 only), read with music21 with grace notes removed, bars 1-4 read F F G A | A G F E | D D E F | F E E; bars 9-12 E E F D | E F G F D | E F G F E | D E A; no key signature (`fifths` 0), catalog tempo 40, 72 grace notes (`hymns.2.md:36-39` states "on D with every F natural, a grace note before nearly every note and a tempo of 40"). The hymn tune HYMN TO JOY is the scale-degree sequence 3 3 4 5 5 4 3 2 1 1 2 3 3 2 2 in a major key (https://hymnary.org/tune/hymn_to_joy_beethoven: incipit "33455 43211 23322", key G major). With tonic D the third degree is F sharp, so this edition turns the major tune into one with a minor third. The hymns.4 edition (`song.classical.beethoven-joyful-joyful-we-adore-thee.pdmx`, one sharp, soprano B B C D | D C B A | G G A B) is correct. Why it matters for the lesson's job: the lesson asks the learner to put chords under the tune (`hymns.2.md:29-32`); the standard D, G and A major chords clash with an F natural. The lesson discloses the F naturals but frames them as a score quirk ("so read what is written rather than playing it from memory"), not as a wrong transcription. Not a wrong claim, but this edition teaches a different melody than the hymn it is named for; whether the other three archive copies of the title in `docs/genre-plans/hymns.md` (stage 3 list: four copies, one of them this CID `QmY8XeRQK9...`) carry the same lowered third was not inspected. Classify as: lesson discloses; edition content is wrong against the hymn; `KEEP` verdict in the 425 CSV is contradicted by this evidence.
3. **Overstated generalisations about how many chords hymns use.** `hymns.2.md:12-15`: "Almost all of them use three or four chords"; `hymns.2.md:22-23`: "Three chords will get you through most of a hymnbook". No source is given and this track's own material is counter-evidence: What a Friend prints seven distinct symbols (A, A7, D, D7, E, G, G#dim), Just a Closer Walk seven, As the Deer six (dump headers). A crude Roman-numeral count of the beat-sampled chords of the four-part scores I ran through music21 (`chordify`, strong-beat onsets, passing tones not removed, so an overcount) gave Amazing Grace 4 distinct numerals, Rock of Ages about 5, Abide with Me 11, the Joyful SATB edition 10, O Sacred Head 17 (key detection for O Sacred Head was wrong; its count is not meaningful). I did not find a published corpus count; I record the claim as unsourced rather than false. The three-chord claim is true of the Stage 2 options, which are chosen to fit it (Swing Low: C, D7, G).
4. **`hymns.md:12-13`: "every chord is spelled out and there are four of them a bar".** Read literally false: Amazing Grace SATB bars 5, 9, 12, 18 hold one chord for the bar or more, and Abide with Me bar 4 is one whole-note chord. Probably meant four notes per chord or a chord per beat at most. Misleading, minor.
5. **Heuristics stated close to laws.** `hymns.md:35-36`: "A half-step approach from below works nearly everywhere"; `hymns.5.md:30-31`: "any chord can be preceded by its own five chord". Standard theory excludes at least the diminished vii; "nearly everywhere" is a style claim with no source. Minor.
6. **Passing chord and walk-up definitions differ across published sources, and the lessons also differ internally.** PianoGroove defines gospel passing chords as stepping-stone harmonies, with the chromatic type being dominant-7 shapes approaching I, IV or V (https://www.pianogroove.com/blues-piano-lessons/gospel-passing-chords-substitutions/), and a walk-up as "multiple chromatic passing chords before a chord change" (https://www.pianogroove.com/blues-piano-lessons/gospel-walk-ups/). Piano With Jonny describes the walking bass as a line connecting changes, strong chord tones on beats 1 and 3, chromatic approach tones between (https://pianowithjonny.com/piano-lessons/gospel-piano-chords-and-walking-bass/). The lessons use a third version: walk-up = bass line only (`hymns.md:31-33`, `hymns.5.md:16-21`); passing chord = the target chord's own shape moved a half step, "up or down" at `hymns.md:34-36` ("from below works nearly everywhere") but only from above at `hymns.5.md:23-26` and in the study (Ebm7 into Dm7, Ab7 into G7), whose approach chords are the same quality as the target (generator `PASSING_TARGETS`, `generate_exercises.py:5424-5427`; `exercise.passing-chord.c` progression Imaj7, bIIIm7, iim7, bVI7, V7, Imaj7). These are legitimate named variants, not false statements; the gap is that the lessons teach one narrow version and call it "gospel passing chords" with no mention that other sources define the device as a chain of dominant-7 chords. Source disagreement recorded; no change decided here.
7. **Four-part voice-leading rules stated as laws: none found.** Scope: all five lesson files read in full. They contain no rule against parallel fifths, no spacing or doubling rule; the voice-motion claims are hedged ("mostly by step", `hymns.md:17-19`; "mostly by a half or a whole tone ... Where they jump, something has happened", `hymns.4.md:24-27`). Wikipedia frames the parallel-fifths/octaves and voice-crossing rules as common-practice convention with style-dependent importance (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_leading). Clean.
8. **Hymnody history: two wording issues, no false history found.** Scope: `grep` of the five files for composers, dates and attributions. (a) `hymns.4.md:45-46` "*O Sacred Head* is Bach": the tune is Hans Leo Hassler's (c. 1600, a secular love song); Bach arranged it for the St Matthew Passion (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Sacred_Head,_Now_Wounded). The shipped title reads "Johann Sebastian Bach on a tune by Hans Leo Hassler", so the lesson's shorthand is imprecise, not false. (b) `hymns.6.md:14-15` "piano pieces made out of hymn tunes": `song.folk.10000-reasons-matt-redman.pdmx` is a contemporary worship song (composer field "Matt Redman and Jonas Myrin"), not a hymn tune. The "amen" claim at `hymns.md:25-26` ("IV-I, the 'amen'") is correct (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagal_cadence, "Amen cadence" because of its use at hymn endings).
9. **Stage 3 song option not described by the lesson.** `song.classical.simple-gifts-2-part-round.pdmx` is on the `hymns` list (stage-3.json) but the lesson never mentions it. The dump (17 bars, 2 staves, no chord symbols) shows two interleaved melodic lines with the same material offset, i.e. a round, not four-part texture nor a lead sheet to walk up on.
