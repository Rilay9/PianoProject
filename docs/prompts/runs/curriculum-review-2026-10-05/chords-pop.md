## 1. Promised endpoint

**Track description** (`00-tracks.json:20`): "Playing from chord symbols: accompany, transpose, arrange." **Last rung** (`chords-pop.9.md:12-13`): "A chart gives you the harmony and nothing else. Everything that makes it sound like a record is a decision you make." Success line (`chords-pop.9.md:54-55`): "Two arrangements of the same song that sound like different pieces of music." Design D2 stage 9 (`02-curriculum.md` line 509 region): "intro/outro, modulation, countermelody, solo piano covers — a chart turned into an arrangement of your own."

**Competent developing pianist in this domain** (Berklee Keyboard Method and Pop/Rock Keyboard outlines above): reads a lead sheet and realises it with voice-led triads and sevenths; knows slash chords; chooses an accompaniment pattern and register to suit the song and melody; comps rhythmically; builds a bass line; transposes for a singer; finds a song by ear; and assembles intro, sections and ending into an arrangement.

**Reasonable PianoProject endpoint:** the learner can take a chord chart or a finished arrangement, decide the four things the lesson names (left hand, melody position, accompaniment rhythm, texture change), and produce two audibly different settings, in a key a singer chooses, with sevenths and colour chords used with restraint.

**Narrower than its name?** Yes, and intentionally so: the name says "Chords & pop"; the track teaches chord-symbol playing, triad and seventh vocabulary, the four-chord loop, slash bass, sus/add9 colour, transposition and arranging. It does not teach pop *rhythm styles* (eighth-note pop comping, syncopated stabs, funk pocket, gospel walkdowns), playing by ear from a recording, or bass-line patterns beyond one descending slash line. Whether that narrowing is acceptable is addressed in sections 3 and 10; I do not consider it a name/promise redefinition forcing an owner choice (section 10).

**Design intent versus lessons (the lesson ships):** D2 promised at stage 6 "reharmonisation basics, secondary dominants, borrowed iv, pedal points, riffs/hooks, ballad style"; at stage 7 "playing by ear, transposing songs, gospel passing chords, rhythmic comping, syncopated LH octaves"; at stage 9 "intro/outro, modulation, countermelody". The shipped lessons substitute: stage 6 = the loop in twelve keys plus descending slash bass; stage 7 = sus/add9/ninth colour; stage 8 = transposition; stage 9 = arranging. D2 stage 7 and 8 (by ear; transposing) are in `chords-pop.8`; D2 stage 5's sevenths are in `chords-pop.5`. D2 items with no `chords-pop` lesson home: reharmonisation, borrowed iv, pedal points, riffs/hooks, gospel passing chords, rhythmic comping, syncopated LH octaves, modulation, countermelody, outro (scope: the seven `chords-pop.*.md` read in full plus `grep -il` over `content/lessons/*.md` for "reharmon", "borrow", "pedal point/pedal bass", "passing chord", "anticipat", "syncopat", "countermelod", "modulat", "outro"; they exist elsewhere: reharmonisation in `jazz.7`, `improv.8`, `hymns.6`; pedal in `rock.6`; passing chords in `hymns.5`; modulation in `jazz.8`, `theory.8`; none is cross-referenced from `chords-pop`).

## 3. Missing or weak abilities

Each: why it matters; external evidence; scope searched; severity. "Scope" for all absence claims below: `content/lessons/chords-pop.3.md` through `chords-pop.9.md` read in full, plus `grep -n -i` over `content/lessons/chords-pop.*.md` for the terms stated, and `grep -il` over `content/lessons/*.md` where I say "elsewhere".

**3.1 Voice leading as a sound goal.** *Mostly present.* `chords-pop.6.md:15-19` states it. `3.2.md:25-29` (core) states it as efficiency ("a fifth less travel"). Missing: an exercise or lesson step where the learner *hears* a smooth versus a jumping progression and chooses; the loop exercises compare root position and inversions only by file, not by task. Berklee: voice-led triad changes are a named module (https://online.berklee.edu/courses/berklee-keyboard-method and https://online.berklee.edu/courses/pop-rock-keyboard). Severity: depth; the foundation is taught.

**3.2 Slash chords.** Present: `.4.md:31-34`, `.6.md:21-25`, `exercise.slash-bass.*`. Weak point: slash chords are taught only as *one descending-stepwise line* (C, C/B, Am, C/G, F, F/E, Dm, G). A slash chord that is an inversion (`C/E`, chord tone in bass), a slash chord that adds a non-chord bass note (`F/G`, `C/D`), and a rising or pedal bass are not distinguished. Severity: depth. See section 5 on the exact `.4` example.

**3.3 Choosing texture by melody density and register.** Absent as a taught decision. `.5.md:34-37` offers two textures; `.3.md:26-29` offers three LH rhythms; `.9.md:15-18` names "where is the melody" as a decision. Nothing tells the learner *when* a busy left hand collides with a busy melody, or where to put the chord in register. Berklee Pop/Rock Keyboard lists arpeggiated textures, eighth-note patterns and ballad voicings as choices tied to context. Searched: the seven lessons for "register", "density", "melody" in a decision sense: `.9.md:16` (melody position) and `.9.md:33` ("an arrangement has to leave something out to stay playable") only. Severity: depth, but it is the heart of "arrange" (see 8.3).

**3.4 Bass-line construction.** Weak. Only the descending slash line is built (`.6`) plus root-then-chord (`.3`). No lesson builds a bass line from root, fifth, octave, approach or passing notes, or from the voice that connects two chords; `.9.md:15` lists "root, walking, or a pattern" as options, but walking bass is taught only in `blues.5.md` and `jazz.5.md` (neither is a prerequisite; `blues-boogie` and `jazz` are `defaultActive: false`, `00-tracks.json:29, 36`). Berklee Keyboard Method lists "bass lines" within lead-sheet performance. Severity: depth (borderline foundational bridge for the arranging endpoint: a learner is told to choose "walking" without having it in this track).

**3.5 Rhythmic comping (pop idiom).** Absent from lesson text. `grep -n -i "comp"` over `chords-pop.*.md`: no match for "comp " or "comping"; the concept `comping` is only a tag on `.9` (`stage-9.json:263-270`) and the only exercise is `exercise.comping.c.anticipated` (level 6.3; pattern offsets `[0.0, 2.5]` over a ii-V-I with shell voicings; `generate_exercises.py:4108-4113, 4180-4270`; tracks `jazz`, `chords-pop`). D2 stage 7 promised "rhythmic comping, syncopated LH octaves"; Berklee Pop/Rock Keyboard: "quarter-note and eighth-note accompaniment patterns", "funky groove patterns with syncopated pocket playing". Severity: depth. The only comping a `chords-pop` learner meets is a jazz-swing study placed on the last rung with no words about it.

**3.6 Playing by ear from a recording (tonic, bass, progression).** The dossier's "important probable omission". ## 5. Correctness concerns

- The other hit for "chord chart or lead sheet only" outside `content/` is the audit note `docs/prompts/runs/restart-2026-10-05/Curriculum_Repertoire_Verification.md:27`, which describes this defect; `docs/prompts/runs/X1/mutant-session-is-evidence.txt` also contains the phrase (a mutation fixture, not a source of truth).

## 7. Measurement limits

**What the app can verify from MIDI:**
- The lab reports, per time round, "how many of your notes were chord tones of the bar" (`.4.md:55-57`) or "in the scale" (`.6.md:51-53`, `.7.md:53-55`); *Free play* names three or more held notes and the bass note (`.3.md:60-62`, `.4.md:58-60`).
- The transposition drill judges notes against the printed music moved (`.8.md:17-19`).
- Accuracy and tempo thresholds on generated exercises (mastery fields in the stage files: 0.9 accuracy, 0.8-0.9 tempo).

**Self-checked by design:** playing while singing (`.5.md:39-41`); "keep the half of each that worked" (`.9.md:26-27`); whether two arrangements "sound like different pieces" (`.9.md:54-55`); hearing the transposed key agree with the chart (`.8.md:48-50`); playing by ear in a new key (`.8.md:21-23`).

**What the app cannot verify and no actor in this process can decide:** whether a voicing, texture or arrangement is musically good; whether a chord-colour choice is tasteful; whether a transcription in a PDMX arrangement is accurate as music (*unverified as music*); whether the arrangement the learner hands in ("written down as a chart", `.9.md:35-36`) differs audibly from the first. The `.9` pass criterion is one exercise run, so the rung's real objective is entirely unmeasured. I record that as a measurement limit, not as a curriculum omission.

**A voice-leading, inversion-choice, or "chord changes smoothly" measure from MIDI is possible in principle (common-tone count between successive chords) but I do not propose a detector.**
