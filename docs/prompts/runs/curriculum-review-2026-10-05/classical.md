## 1. Promised endpoint

Track description (`content/curriculum/00-tracks.json`): "Baroque to Romantic repertoire, from dances to sonatinas and beyond." Last rung (`classical.9.md:12-14`): "There is no rung above this one ... pieces to live with — minutes of music, several months of work." Success line (`classical.9.md:58-60`): the piece played "from four different starting points, at a tempo you chose ... sounds like a decision rather than an accident." Design D1 (`docs/02-curriculum.md:478-496`) adds "fugue, virtuoso Romantic, Impressionism" to stage 8.

A competent developing pianist in this domain plays Baroque, Classical and Romantic repertoire to about ABRSM Grades 6-8, with articulation chosen by context, shaped phrases and cadences, ornaments realised from a source, balance between hands and between voices, controlled pedal, rubato over a steady pulse, form-aware learning and memory, and the ability to plan and perform a long piece. ABRSM states the shape of this: control of "pitch, time, tone, shape" applied to "a musical and stylistic" interpretation; editorial fingering, metronome marks and "realisation of ornaments" need not be observed strictly; pedalling is judged by "overall musical outcome" (ABRSM 2023-24 syllabus p.13). RCM pairs repertoire with a separate etude requirement at every level 1-10 and memory marks to Level 8 (RCM syllabus, Memorization and Etudes sections).

Reasonable PianoProject endpoint, and what the track delivers: a repertoire-and-interpretation track, whose controlled practice mostly lives on other tracks (technique, practice), whose repertoire is judged strong, and which reaches a Grade 7-8 project piece. It is **intentionally narrower than "Classical"** in two ways it does not state: no fugue (D1 lists it; no lesson or rung option has one; section 3) and Impressionism appears only as two Debussy options with no lesson text about its idiom. The shipped track description ("Baroque to Romantic") promises neither, so this is a mismatch with D1 more than with the shipped promise.

## 5. Correctness concerns

**Verified true at HEAD against a source or the notation:**
- `classical.3.md:33-40` mordent sign: line through means lower neighbour, plain means upper. Matches the modern convention (Wikipedia Mordent: "the upper mordent is indicated by a short squiggle; the lower mordent is the same with a short vertical line through it"). The lesson says editions differ and gives a default as an instruction, not a law. Historical note for learners later: in the Baroque "mordent" meant the lower one and the plain squiggle was the Pralltriller (same article).
- `classical.4.md:33-39` trill alternates with the note above and "usually" starts on the upper note in Mozart's time: hedged and consistent with the common Classical-period convention (not re-sourced online).
- `classical.6.md:26-33` rubato: Leopold Mozart "the accompaniment should remain strictly in tempo" and the Chopin account match Wikipedia Rubato (see finding 2 below).
- `classical.7.md:54-56` Op.9 No.1 Larghetto, No.2 Andante; 7:47-48 mazurka accent on beat 2 or 3: correct.
- `classical.8.md:16-22`: Op.25 No.9 is octave work switching detached and connected (Wikipedia Op.25 No.9); Op.10 No.6 has a chromatic inner voice (Wikipedia Op.10 No.6); Op.25 No.1 melody over an arpeggio wash (standard).
- `classical.3.md:19-20` Petzold has 32 bars with phrase endings at 8, 16, 24, 32: confirmed in the dump.
- `classical.5.md:42-45` "Neither ... First Loss nor ... Old French Song marks pedal": MusicXML pedal count 0 for both.
- `classical.4.md:41-48`: K.1e file carries K.1f as Trio (text "Trio", key change G to C, 36 bars, 52 slurs); Bach March has staccato in both hands; Gurlitt carries 14 slurs.
- **No millisecond or fixed-duration articulation claim exists in the classical lessons** (grep `millisecond| ms` over `classical.*.md`: 0 hits). Numeric heuristics present, with their hedges: staccato "roughly half the written value, shorter in fast music, longer in slow" (`classical.4.md:19-21`, relative and hedged); Alberti "perhaps a third of the volume" (`classical.5.md:30-31`, hedged); "a common starting point" for melody over accompaniment (`classical.6.md:19`, hedged). **Findings:**
1. **Heuristic stated as law (medium).** `classical.8.md:24-29`: "The method that works: ... five times. Then raise the metronome by four clicks. When it fails, drop back two and stay there for a day. What does *not* work is playing the whole piece a little faster each time." A practitioner recipe presented as the proven method; the same lesson (8:54-58) then describes the app's Ladder with a different step (a tenth of written speed). "Clicks" is unit-ambiguous on a digital metronome. I found no published source for these numbers in the lesson or the dossier (Chaffin and Imreh in the dossier concerns structure, not tempo steps).
2. **Rubato absolutism (low-medium).** `classical.6.md:30-31`: "If your bars are getting longer, that is not rubato, that is hesitation." Wikipedia Rubato records two kinds (earlier rhythmic freedom; later melodic rubato over a steady accompaniment). The lesson scopes itself ("the test for this kind of rubato", 6:31) but the previous sentence reads as the definition. The pulse-stable teaching is the right choice and matches the dossier; say "this kind" in both places.
3. **Absolute about counting in cross-rhythm (medium).** `classical.8.md:39-42`: "do not try to work out where the notes coincide ... Counting will not save you, and it is not supposed to." Counting a shared subdivision is the usual first step in polyrhythm teaching; the app's own `technique.7.md:31` teaches two-against-three as a drill. Learning each hand then letting them disagree is a valid second stage. I did not fetch an authoritative source for this in this run: *standard practice, not re-sourced here*.
4. **Attribution without caveat (low).** `classical.5.md:24` "Beethoven's Sonatina in G, Anh. 5": the Anh. prefix marks a work outside the numbered canon; Wikipedia: "Modern scholars now consider Beethoven's authorship of the work unlikely"; the RCM syllabus (Level 3, List B) prints it as "attr. Beethoven". The lesson footnotes Petzold's attribution (3:22-23) but not this.
5. **K.331 theme is a recast (low).** `classical.3.md:46-47` "set here in C major in 3/4, a minuet-length tune". Notation: 16 bars, C major, 3/4, no slurs, no dynamics. The source movement is the Andante grazioso of the Sonata in A, K.331 (ABRSM lists it as "Andante grazioso and Vars. 1-6 (1st movt from ... Sonata in A, K. 331)", Grade 8 list); its 6/8 metre is general knowledge, not fetched. The lesson says "set here", which is honest; a learner meeting the real work later is not told the metre changed.
6. **Contested absolute (low).** `classical.9.md:20-23`: learn from the score and listen only after making your own decisions. Offered with "By all means listen", so defensible as one approach; ABRSM p.13 takes no side.
7. **Legato definition (low).** `classical.4.md:15-16` "no overlap and no gap" is a teaching definition; the app's legato scorer allows overlap up to one step (`Scoring.ts:566-568`). The lesson and the measurement differ by design; not unsafe.

- **The app can verify from MIDI:** notes and timing, and the Ladder (`classical.5.md:60-63`, `classical.8.md:54-58`); sustain-pedal timing and half-pedal value (`special.ts:225-260`; core `3.5` explains the 120 ms overlap window); staccato/legato held fraction (`Scoring.ts:563-568`) and voicing ratio of at least 1.4 (`Scoring.ts:631`, described as a rule of thumb on `technique.6`), but only on the exercises that carry those scorers, not on the classical repertoire.
- **Self-checked, and no actor in this process can decide:** rubato quality, phrase shape, tone, whether a melody "sings", whether an ornament "sounds like one gesture" (`classical.7.md:66-68`), balance in a real piece, interpretive choices in a Chopin or Bach piece. All *unverified as music*. The lessons phrase these as listening tests ("listen for the old chord still ringing", 6:37-38) and do not claim the app grades them.
- **What a score cannot say:** whether printed pedal in the rung 6 and 8 editions is the composer's, an editor's or an arranger's. The Chopin first-edition encodings print none (Prelude 7, Waltz 64/2, Mazurka Op.7/1, Fantaisie-impromptu: 0 pedal elements).
- The measured articulation exercises are right hand only (`exercise.articulation.*.right`), so left-hand articulation, which `classical.4.md:27-31` asks for, has no scored exercise.
