# Brief: familiar-song extraction pass from the local PDMX archive (lightweight, 2026-10-05)

Decision rationale (§10b): the owner wants readable MusicXML plus event summaries for sixteen familiar songs so the outside reviewer can pick a few that are enjoyable and useful; this is not a curriculum audit and places nothing. Alternatives: a full quarry lane with style verification (too heavy for the question); the owner browsing the archive by hand (what the tooling exists to avoid). What would reverse it: nothing; the output is raw material for a human read. Standards for the loose note: *fun* (recognisable, playable, not obviously bad notation), *useful* (clearly practises an existing skill, could sit as an optional piece near that rung), *really educational* (fills a missing station or is unusually good); genre is secondary to what playing the song teaches.

The agent writes only under `docs/review/familiar-song-pass-2026-10-05/`; no other file changes; no commit; no git checkout, stash or reset; never names an AI model. The archive is local only: `PDMX.csv` and `mxl.tar.gz` in `C:\Users\yalir\repos\Piano Stuff\` (set `PIANOPATH_PDMX_DIR` to that folder, per `tools/content/pdmx/README.md`); stream the tarball once for all chosen CIDs, never unpack it. Use the existing machinery: `tools/content/pdmx/quarry_core.py`, `quarry_lanes.py` and `summarise_xml.py` (read their docstrings and the README first; `py -3.11`, `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8`), and the index in `PDMX.csv` for title search. Do not write a new search tool; a short driver script that calls the existing functions is fine and goes in the output folder as `run_pass.py`.

**Titles (first batch):** Mad World; Let It Be; Stand By Me; Viva La Vida; Someone Like You; Your Song; A Thousand Miles; Chasing Cars; Iris; Creep; Karma Police; Take Me Home, Country Roads; Jolene; Ring of Fire; I Walk the Line; Tennessee Whiskey.

**Per title:** search the index; choose the best one or two piano-useful editions, preferring a two-staff piano score, then a lead sheet with melody and chord symbols, then a real keyboard part inside a mixed score; reject drum-only, percussion-only or unrelated same-title results; keep two editions only when they do different jobs (lead sheet versus full arrangement); resolve obvious identity cases by hand where the strict classifier is fooled by arranger, transcriber or blank creator fields; do not prove every metadata field. Where the archive has no usable edition, say so in the index.

**Output per retained edition:** `xml/<slug>-<CID>.musicxml` (the inner MusicXML, byte-for-byte) and `summary/<slug>-<CID>.txt` with at least: CID; title; artist or composer; part and instrument names; staff count; metres; bar count; chord-symbol count; tempo marks; key signatures; the event summary of the opening 16 bars; event summaries around each obvious section or texture change; section or rehearsal labels if present; enough later bars to show whether the opening accompaniment pattern persists or changes. Short scores: summarise the whole score.

**`INDEX.md`:** one row per retained edition: title | CID | shape | bars | chords | why this edition was retained | loose note. The loose note is one of: accompaniment candidate; by-ear candidate; arrangement or reduction candidate; repeating-pattern candidate; full-piece or fun repertoire candidate; and a flag where earned: **HIGH-VALUE** (unusually useful for learning, not merely famous) or **NOT WORTH FURTHER REVIEW** (mediocre on reading). Also list titles with no usable edition. No curriculum placement beyond the loose note; the reviewer reads the notation and judges.

**Effort boundary:** lightweight. No source research, no copyright analysis, no curriculum rewrite, no verification campaign. Reply in at most ten lines: the folder; titles found n/16 with editions retained; the HIGH-VALUE and NOT-WORTH flags; anything you could not do.
