## Rows checked

### Core (section 2.1)

**C1. "Teach repeat signs, first and second endings, D.C., fermata, the natural sign, staccato, once, where pieces first print them." Evidence cell: "ABRSM sight-reading parameters list staccato and ties from Grade 1 `[D]`."**
- Source: ABRSM Piano 2025 & 2026, "Sight-reading parameters", printed p.16 (PDF 17).
- Table cells: Initial: "legato phrases, staccato", "f and p". Grade 1: "slurs, accents", "mf and mp", "cresc. and dim. hairpins", "occasional accidentals (within minor keys only)". Grade 2: "tied notes", "pp". Grade 4: "pause signs", "tenuto". The table says parameters are "cumulative". No repeat sign, volta, D.C. or natural sign appears anywhere in the table.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION.** Staccato is introduced at the Initial grade, not Grade 1; tied notes at Grade 2, not Grade 1; Grade 1 adds slurs, accents, mf/mp and hairpins. The table is silent on repeats, endings, D.C. and the natural sign.
- Supplementary, Faber Level 1 things-to-know: "Articulation with legato, slur, and staccato"; "Accent mark, octave sign, fermata, ritardando, upbeat"; "Sharps and flats". Faber 2B: "1st and 2nd endings, a tempo, D.C. al Fine, eighth rest, accidentals, and pianissimo". Faber Primer, Top 5 things to do: "Circle elements in the score: clefs, dynamics, repeating measures, etc."
- The specification may take: staccato is an Initial-level reading feature, slur and accent Grade 1, tie Grade 2, pause sign Grade 4 (ABRSM); Faber puts fermata and staccato at Level 1 and endings and D.C. at Level 2B. Nothing here dates the natural sign or the repeat sign.

**C2. "Reading rows: key signatures and 3/4." Evidence cell: "ABRSM Grade 1 includes G, F, 3/4 `[D]`."**
- Source: ABRSM Piano 2025 & 2026, "Sight-reading parameters", printed p.16 (PDF 17).
- Table cells: Initial: time "4/4" (a second line reads "6" bars and "2/4"), keys "C major, D minor", hand position "Each hand: playing separately, in 5-finger position (tonic to dominant)". Grade 1: time "3/4", keys "G, F majors, A minor", hand position "any 5-finger position". Grade 2: keys "D major, E, G minors", "playing together". Grade 3: "up to 8" bars, "3/8", "A, B flat, E flat majors, B minor", "outside 5-finger position". Grade 4: "6/8".
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED.** G major, F major, A minor and 3/4 are first listed at Grade 1. Extra detail the row does not carry: at Grade 1 each hand still plays separately in a 5-finger position (hands together first appears at Grade 2). The Initial row's "6 / 2/4" line is split from the Grade 1 label in the table; it was read as belonging to the Initial row.
- Supplementary, RCM 2022: Level 1 sight-reading "Playing" (printed p.20): "C, G, F major, A minor", four measures, hands on the grand staff. Level 2 (p.26): "C, G, F major, A, D minor", "Melodies may move beyond the five-finger position." Level 5 (p.46): "major and minor keys, up to two sharps or flats".
- The specification may take: ABRSM Grade 1 reading is three keys plus A minor with 3/4, hands separate, 5-finger; RCM Level 1 is the same three major keys and A minor. Hands-together and key signatures beyond one sharp or flat come later in both (ABRSM Grade 2 and 3; RCM Level 5 for two sharps or flats).

**C3. "Transposition as a core task at 1.2 and 2.5/3.2." Evidence cell: "Faber transposes at Level 4 and the dossier from Stage 1 `[D]`."**
- Sources: Faber Level 4 things-to-know; Level 1 things-to-know and Q&A (item 6); Level 2A and 2B things-to-know.
- Level 4, Top 5 things to do: "The exercises may also be transposed to other keys. Consider letting the student decide the key!" (sight-reading book exercises).
- Level 1 things-to-know: "'Firely,' written in the C 5-finger scale, introduces transposing to the G 5-finger scale." (spelling as printed). Level 1 Q&A item 6: "For the Level 1 student, this means placing the hands across the keys, usually to another 5-finger scale."
- Level 2A: "Transposition of 5-finger pieces"; "Transposing requires reading by interval." Level 2B: "Level 2B transposition broadens outside a 5-finger position with the full major scale."
- ABRSM and RCM PDFs: no occurrence of "transpos" in the extracted text of either file (scope: text extraction of both whole documents).
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION.** Faber does not first transpose at Level 4: it introduces transposition at Level 1 (C 5-finger to G 5-finger), continues it in 2A (5-finger pieces) and 2B (full major scale), and Level 4 transposes sight-reading exercises. "The dossier from Stage 1" is the dossier's own claim, not a primary source, and was not checked. Neither syllabus requires transposition.
- The specification may take: a Faber-style first transposition is a 5-finger-position shift to a second key, framed as reading by interval, from the first level after the primer. This supports the existing row text (options exist) at 1.2; it does not support a transposition requirement from ABRSM or RCM.

**C4. "Clap the pulse, clap an echo, two-or-three-time listening, labelled self-checked." Evidence cell: "ABRSM Grade 1 aural `[D]`."**
- Source: ABRSM Piano 2025 & 2026, "Aural tests", printed p.46 (PDF 47), Initial Grade and Grade 1.
- Grade 1 A: "To clap the pulse of a piece played by the examiner, and to identify whether it is in two time or three time." The candidate claps "a louder clap on the strong beats" and "is not required to state the time signature."
- Grade 1 B: "To sing as `echoes' three phrases played by the examiner." Two bars each, major key, "within the range of tonic-mediant."
- Initial Grade B: "To clap as `echoes' the rhythm of two phrases played by the examiner", two bars, "three or four time", "melody line only".
- Grade 2 (p.47) repeats A (two or three time) and B (sung echoes, tonic-dominant); Grade 3 A adds four time.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION.** The clap-the-pulse and two-or-three-time test is Grade 1 A. The clap-back echo of a rhythm is at the Initial Grade (test B), not Grade 1; the Grade 1 echo is sung, in pitch. Page: p.46, not p.41.
- Supplementary, RCM 2022: Preparatory A (p.9) Clapback: "Students will choose to clap, tap, or sing the rhythm of a short melody after the examiner has played it twice. The examiner will identify the time signature and count one measure before beginning." Same wording in Preparatory B (p.14), Level 1 (p.20) and Level 2 (p.26). Level 1 and 2 intervals: "Students may choose to sing or hum" minor 3rd, major 3rd (Level 2 adds perfect 5th).
- The specification may take: clapped pulse with a two-or-three-time judgement is a Grade 1 listening task, a clapped rhythmic echo is an Initial-level task; RCM accepts clap, tap or sing for the rhythm. Both syllabi assess these by an examiner's ear; neither source says how a self-checked version would be judged.

**C5. "Memory before 4.6." Evidence cell: "Faber expects a memorised item each unit `[D]`."**
- Source: Faber Level 4 things-to-know, Top 5 things to do: "Memorize a piece, a section of a piece, or an exercise in every unit."
- Same pages: Primer, "Ask students to analyze and memorize a piece weekly." Level 1: "Analyze the patterns and memorize one piece from each unit." Level 2A: "Memorize one piece from each unit."
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED.** The memory expectation is present from the Primer (weekly) and is per unit from Level 1, so it is earlier than Level 4.
- Supplementary: ABRSM p.13 "All requirements must be played from memory" (scales and arpeggios only); RCM Level 1 (p.20): "Two memory marks are awarded for each repertoire selection that is performed by memory."
- The specification may take: a memorised item per unit is a Faber expectation from Level 1; it does not by itself set where in core memory first appears.

**C6. "Score-study routine (owner)", evidence cell "single lines only `[R]`; dossier 2.5". The earlier record cites ABRSM for this claim.**
- Source: ABRSM Piano 2025 & 2026, "Sight-reading", printed p.15 (PDF 16).
- Quote: "They will be given half a minute to look through and, if they want to, try out any or all of the test before they are asked to play it."
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED** for the narrow fact (a timed look-through with permission to try parts). The syllabus says nothing about a checklist or routine for the look-through; "dossier 2.5" was not checked.
- The specification may take: a thirty-second look-through, with trying allowed, is how ABRSM frames unseen reading.

### Theory-ear (section 2.9)

**T1. "A key or tonic before melodic dictation and the tune drill." Evidence cell: "RCM names the key `[D]`."**
- Source: RCM Piano Syllabus 2022, Ear Tests, Playback, Preparatory A (p.9).
- Quote: "The examiner will identify the key, play the tonic triad once, and play the melody twice." Keys "C, G major", beginning note "tonic, mediant", "four notes". The same sentence form recurs at Level 1 (p.20, "first five notes of a major or minor scale", tonic or dominant start) and Level 2 (p.26, keys "G, F major, D minor"). From Level 5 (p.46) the examiner names "the key and time signature" and plays "the tonic chord once".
- Also ABRSM p.46 (Grade 1 B, C): "First the examiner will play the key-chord and the starting note (the tonic) and then count in two bars."
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED** in both syllabi: the key is named and the tonic sounded before every melody the candidate must reproduce, at the lowest levels.
- The specification may take: key announced plus tonic chord first, a short melody within a five-note span, replayed (RCM: twice at the lowest levels, three times from Level 5).

**T2. "Minor-key progressions and numerals by ear; keys other than C." Evidence cell: "all major, all C `[R]`; RCM minor progressions `[D]`."**
- Source: RCM Piano Syllabus 2022, Chord Progressions: Level 5 (p.46), Level 6 (p.54), Level 7 (p.62), Level 8 (p.71).
- Level 5: "identify chord progressions in major keys as I-IV-I or I-V-I after the examiner has played the progression twice." Minor is not listed. Level 6: "in major or minor keys", Major column I-IV-I and I-V-I, Minor column i-iv-i and i-V-i. Level 7 adds I-IV-V and i-iv-V. Level 8 (p.71): "each chord in a four-chord progression in a major or minor key", with i-VI-iv-V and i-VI-iv-i among the minor entries (quoted from the table text). All say "The progression will be played in keyboard style, and the bass line will ascend from the tonic."
- Levels below 5 have no progression test; the Levels 1 and 2 chord test is major or minor triad quality only (pp.20, 26).
- Minor keys elsewhere in RCM ear tests: playback in A minor at Preparatory B (p.14) and Level 1; D minor at Level 2 (p.26); E minor at Levels 5 and 6.
- ABRSM p.47 (Grade 3 B, C): "in a major or minor key"; no progression test at Grades 1 to 3.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION.** RCM progressions are major only at Level 5; minor first appears at Level 6 (i-iv-i, i-V-i), not at the first progression test. Keys other than C appear in RCM melody playback from Preparatory A (G major) and in progression tests only by the examiner's choice (the syllabus gives no key list for progressions).
- The specification may take: major-only progressions first (I-IV-I, I-V-I), then minor with i-iv-i and i-V-i; the sequence and the keyboard-style ascending-bass presentation.

**T3. "Singing with scale degrees (movable do) as a self-checked habit." Evidence cell: "Berklee ear training `[D]`."**
- Source required: Berklee course pages. The brief names only ABRSM, RCM and Faber, so Berklee was not fetched.
- Verdict: **NOT REACHABLE under this brief** (a source outside its list, not a failed fetch). The row stays gated.
- Supplementary from the sources read: RCM Levels 1 and 2 let the candidate "sing or hum" intervals; ABRSM Grade 1 B is a sung echo. Neither uses scale-degree or solfege vocabulary (no occurrence of "solfege", "movable" or "scale degree" in the extracted text of the ABRSM PDF; the RCM PDF was not searched for these terms).

The dotted quarter is not named in any Faber page read (the Level 2B and Level 4 rhythm patterns are images with no text), so a dotted-quarter introduction level is not established. The Level 1 Q&A also mentions "occasional 8th notes" in PlayTime Piano. Faber Level 4: "Sharp key signatures and writing the sharp pattern"; "Pieces in D, A, and E major".
- **RCM Preparatory to Level 2, sight reading** (pp.9, 14, 20, 26): Rhythm: "Tap a steady beat with their hand or foot for one measure" and "Continue tapping while speaking, tapping, or clapping the given rhythm", two measures at Preparatory and Level 1, two to four at Level 2. Playing: Preparatory A "two four-note melodies" starting on given notes with given fingers; Level 1 "a four-measure melody"; Level 2 adds "Melodies may move beyond the five-finger position."
