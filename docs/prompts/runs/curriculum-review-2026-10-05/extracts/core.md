## 3. Missing or weak abilities

Scope for every absence below: the 27 core lesson files read in full, plus `grep -i` over `content/lessons/*.md` (all 105 lesson files) and `content/curriculum/*.json`, run 2026-10-05. The term and scope are stated per item.

1. **Notation marks the shipped core repertoire already uses are never taught (foundational bridge for score literacy).** Terms searched over all of `content/lessons/*.md`: "repeat sign", "repeat bar", "first ending", "volta", "D.C.", "da capo", "coda", "fermata", "natural sign", "tenuto": no match in any lesson. "Staccato" occurs only in `technique.4.md` and `classical.4.md` (other tracks, stage 4). Where core scores print them (tag counts from the built `.mxl`: `<repeat>`, `<ending>`, `<fermata>`, `<staccato>`, `<accidental>natural`): Petzold Minuet `song.classical.petzold-minuet-g-bwv-anh114` has 3 repeat and 12 staccato tags (rungs 3.4, 4.4, 4.6, 4.7); the Schumann Chorale (`song.classical.schumann-schumann-album-for-the-young-op-68-no-4-a-hymn-tune-choral.pdmx`) has 18 fermata tags (3.5); easy Fur Elise has 4 naturals (3.4, 4.1, 4.2, 4.6, 4.7); Bella Ciao has 6 repeat tags, 12 ending tags and 4 naturals (4.2); Canon in D easy has 2 repeat tags (4.6, 4.7); Carol of the Bells easy has 2 fermatas (4.6). `3.1.md:16-34` teaches "accidental" as sharp or flat only; the natural sign that cancels one is not mentioned. Why it matters: a learner who cannot read a repeat, a first and second ending, a fermata or a natural cannot play the capstone pieces from the page. External evidence that a competent syllabus includes such marks: ABRSM's sight-reading parameter table lists staccato, slurs, accents and tied notes among the features introduced from Grade 1 up (file above, p.16). Whether the app plays repeats as written was not examined. Severity: foundational bridge (small fix).
2. **Transposition is never a core task.** `grep -i "transpos"` over the 27 core lessons gives only `4.4.md:39-51` (Hanon "transpose them", with the note that the app prints C only). `drill.reading.transposition` is level 6.3 and `-hard` 8.3 (catalog). `3.2.md:12-14` says "a song you know in C becomes a song you can play in any key", but the tasks are fixed alternate-key scores (Ode in G, Twinkle in F, Saints in F) and cadence drills in C, G, F, never "play this again a step higher". Faber asks for transposing sight-reading exercises at Level 4 (Level 4 URL above); dossier 2.2 asks for tiny five-finger transposition from Stage 1-2. The catalog does offer `exercise.five-finger.g-major.right/left` on 1.1 and 1.3, but no lesson line frames it as transposition. Severity: depth.
3. **Ear work for Stages 0-2 is thin and its production side is absent.** In core: interval ear drill (2nd versus 3rd, `1.5.md:41-43`), Simon (`1.5.md:49-57`), major/minor ear drill (`3.3.md:36-37`). Search "clap", "echo", "sing", "hum", "by ear" over the 27 core lessons: the only hit is the placement list's "Clap a rhythm back" (`0.4.md:15`), which no lesson teaches. ABRSM Grade 1 aural tests include clapping the pulse and saying whether it is in two or three time, singing two-bar echoes, and spotting a pitch change (file above, p.41). The `theory-ear` track starts at stage 3 (`00-tracks.json`), so Stages 0-2 depend on core for ear. Singing and clapping cannot be MIDI-verified; the lessons neither ask for them nor say they would be self-checked. Severity: depth (dossier ability D; playback through Simon is partly present).
4. **Memory as structure plus retrieval starts only at 4.6 and 4.7.** Search "memor" over core lessons: 1.3 (a passing line), 4.3, 4.6, 4.7. Faber expects a memorised piece, section or exercise in every unit (Level 4 URL above). 4.7 does teach several starting points, hearing without the piano, slow checking and forms as units (`4.7.md:21-49`), which is most of the dossier's C list; it does not ask the learner to name the harmonic or formal landmarks of a repertoire piece (only the blues and oom-pah forms are named). Severity: depth.
5. **Score study before playing is only a one-line habit.** "Before you play, find every F" (`3.1.md:44-45`), "work yours out before you play it" (`1.1.md:40-43`), "count the missing beat" (`1.4.md:35`). Search "look through", "preview", "scan", "score study" over core lessons: none. ABRSM gives half a minute to look through an unseen test (file above, p.15). The dossier's "Before you play" checklist (key, metre, repeats, hardest passage, recurring pattern, starting points) does not exist in core. Severity: depth.
6. **Sixteenth-note reading is not taught before the capstone pieces that use it.** Sixteenths appear only as a counting paragraph in the Hanon lesson (`4.4.md:43-45`). Canon in D easy (4.6, 4.7) has 96 sixteenth notes by music21 count, in 49 bars with two sharps. The generator source agrees: its `sixteenths` control comment says no rung teaches reading them (`app/src/engine/sightReading.ts`). Severity: depth for the capstone pool, not for the spine.
7. **Pain and tension guidance is not in Stage 0.** `0.1.md:31-32` has one tension line; the explicit "never play through pain" first appears at `4.4.md:32-33`; the full treatment is `practice.4.md:31-35` in the `practice` track (prerequisite 1.1; nothing in core stands on it). Dossier TRACK 1 Stage 0 lists a pain/tension warning. Severity: depth.
8. **Cadence awareness is named but not taught.** "Cadence" occurs in core only at `3.3.md:34`; cadence drills exist (`exercise.cadence.*`) but no core lesson defines the term or its types. The `theory-ear` track may carry it (not read for this record). Severity: enrichment.
