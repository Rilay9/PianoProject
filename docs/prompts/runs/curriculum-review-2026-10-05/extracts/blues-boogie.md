## 3. Missing or weak abilities

1. **A scaffold for "right hand improvises, left hand keeps the groove on its own" (foundational bridge for the endpoint).** Why it matters: this is the defining skill of blues and boogie piano and what blues.9 asks for unprompted ("over your own left hand", `blues.9.md:26-29`). Evidence a curriculum includes it: Berklee weeks 8-10 "solo blues piano left-hand patterns and improvisation" (URL in section 0). Where it is built: nowhere. Boogie exercises put held right-hand shells over the left-hand figure (`make_boogie`, `fingered_chord` per bar); Jam it and Trading fours supply the app's own bass, so the learner's left hand is silent; Duet gives the learner the right hand with the app on the left. Scope: blues.3-9 lessons, their `exerciseOptions` and `tools`. I did not inspect the lab's settings for a bass-off option (unverified whether one exists).
2. **Blues vocabulary / licks and transcribing them by ear (depth, close to foundational at blues.9).** `blues.9.md:17` and `:53` dismiss "a solo built from licks" and "the lick you always use" but no blues lesson teaches a lick (grep `\blick` over `blues.*.md`: only those two lines plus a video label). Ear production: `drill.ear.simon-blues-c` (stage 3) and `drill.ear.tune-long` at stage 9, which `earTuneDrill` builds as a random diatonic major-scale tune in G, straight eighths, no blue notes or shuffle (`harmony.ts`, scale `[0,2,4,5,7,9,11]`). So the only ear-production drill on the last rung contains none of the idiom. Evidence: Berklee weeks 11-12 "single-line lick development"; dossier Track 6 "transcribe one short historical lick and transform it". Scope: `grep -n -i transcrib content/lessons/*.md` hits only theory.4, classical.4.shelf, rock.overview.
3. **Intros and endings (depth).** Berklee names "intros, turnarounds, endings". In `blues.3-9` grep for `intro|ending|tag|coda|stop-time|break` finds only "ending on the V7" inside the turnaround description (`blues.5.md:16`). Outside the track, `jam.7.md:28` mentions "a tag or a turnaround" (not read in full by me). A one-line ending is the thing a learner needs to finish a chorus.
4. **Blues-idiom turnarounds are described but not exercised (depth).** `blues.5.md:14-17` describes the chromatic descent from tonic to fifth ending on V7; the exercise taught is I-vi-ii-V, whose generated chords are Cmaj7-Am7-Dm7-G7 (`TURNAROUNDS` in `generate_exercises.py`), a jazz turnaround; blues.7's variant is iii-VI7-ii-V. I-IV-I-V and the chromatic descent have no exercise. Evidence: PianoGroove and the turnaround sources above treat the V7 ending and chromatic line as the stock move. Scope: stage-5/6/7 `exerciseOptions`.
5. **Form variants and the other feels: quick IV, minor blues, slow 12/8 blues, straight rock-and-roll (depth).** D3 names "slow blues 12/8, minor blues" at stage 6; no lesson mentions them (`grep -i -E 'minor blues|slow blues|12/8|quick change|quick-change'` over all `content/lessons/*.md`: no hit in any blues lesson). The generator makes minor-blues walking and boogie exercises (`generate_exercises.py` near `make_boogie(k, pattern, form="minor-blues")` in the minor-blues loop) but none appears in any blues rung's `exerciseOptions` (`grep minor-blues stage-*.json`: no hit). `blues_forms.py` comments say the quick change is deliberately left out "because no lesson introduces it", yet `song.classical.st-louis-blues.pdmx` bar 2 is A7 (a quick IV) in the dump. Straight-feel blues is only framed as a mistake (`blues.4.md:51`). Evidence: Berklee ("straight versus swing feels", "slow blues"), PianoGroove (quick IV).
6. **Rhythmic phrasing variety (depth).** blues.5 and blues.9 vary note choice and space; the app's Trading fours feedback reports "inside your own bars" and "how many of your notes were in the blues scale" (`blues.5.md:56-59`), a pitch-class measure. No lesson asks for a rhythmic idea to be reused, displaced or varied. Evidence: Berklee "blues phrasing, calls".
7. **Whole-chorus form memory (depth).** blues.4 exit is "twice round without losing your place" and blues.8 uses Play it blind on Pinetop's Boogie Woogie (97 bars, not a twelve-bar form: `blues.8.md:47-50`, dump `bars: 97`). No lesson names the landmarks (bar 5 IV, bar 9 V, bar 11-12 turnaround) as retrieval cues, and the tool that lights "the bar you are in" (`blues.5.md:52-55`) is removed in the stage where memory is tested.
8. **Simplifying while preserving groove (enrichment).** Not taught anywhere in blues.3-9 (no grep hit for simplif*); `chords-pop.9` covers thinning in another track.
9. **D3 stage 7 items: tritone substitutions, gospel-blues, New Orleans (enrichment, scoped out by the lessons).** Not in `blues.7.md`; blues.7 as shipped is stride plus turnaround. Design and lesson disagree; the lesson is what ships.
10. **Twelve-bar blues repertoire at stages 8-9 (material, depth).** The stage-8 and stage-9 pieces are Pinetop's (97 bars), Chevy Chase (107 bars, key change at bar 70), Black Bottom Stomp (101 bars), Stumbling (105), Handful of Keys (176): none is a twelve-bar blues chorus. ## 4. Sequencing concerns

- **blues.4 does not require blues.3, and blues.3 says it is the next rung.** Both list `prerequisites: ["3.2"]` (`stage-3.json`, `stage-4.json` blues units); `blues.3.md:47-49` says "The form is the next rung". Core 3.2 teaches the dominant seventh, so the I7 chord is covered; the ordering of blue notes before form is design intent only.
- **The shuffle (`blues.4.md:27-30`, triplet outer notes) relies on triplets, which core 4.5 teaches** ("Compound time, triplets and syncopation", `stage-4.json`). blues.4 does not require 4.5.
- **The hard part of blues.6 is the shift between chords** (`blues.6.md:22-23`), but the written boogie exercises sit on one chord for four bars. The figure through the changes appears only in Boogie (easy) (bars 1-8 C C F F C G C, and the 12-bar quarter-note version in bars 9-20). The authored 12-bar shuffles shift root-5-6-5, not the climbing figure.
- **Stride is practised at stage 7 and modelled at stage 8** (Chevy Chase, `blues.8.md:36-37`), and the stage-7 repertoire has no stride (Boogie easy is a boogie, Rhythm and Boogie is chord riffs, Boogie en Sol is bass-note/chord alternation; section 5). Prior audit already noted this (`Fable_Curriculum_Interpretation_Audit.md`, blues.7).
- **blues.8 depends on theory.6 (roman numerals, `theory.6.md`)** by name (`blues.8.md:15-18`) without a declared prerequisite (blues.8 prerequisites `["blues.7"]`). It works because `theory-ear` is defaultActive; the dependency is invisible to the unit graph.
- **blues.9 is a destination with no ramp.** blues.5 asks for three choruses with a different right-hand idea each time and four silent bars; blues.9 asks for three different choruses over your own left hand. Between them sit stages 6-8 (left-hand figures, stride, ninths) with no improvisation task, and the Trading fours tool's last appearance is blues.7's tools paragraph.
- **A topic list more than a development after stage 6.** Stage 7 stride, stage 8 keys and ninths, stage 9 improvisation each introduce a separate skill; the track's first-promised skill (blue notes, `blues.3`) is not asked for again as a choice after blues.5.
