## 3. Missing or weak abilities

Each: why it matters; external evidence; scope searched; severity. "Scope" for all absence claims below: `content/lessons/chords-pop.3.md` through `chords-pop.9.md` read in full, plus `grep -n -i` over `content/lessons/chords-pop.*.md` for the terms stated, and `grep -il` over `content/lessons/*.md` where I say "elsewhere".

**3.1 Voice leading as a sound goal.** *Mostly present.* `chords-pop.6.md:15-19` states it. `3.2.md:25-29` (core) states it as efficiency ("a fifth less travel"). Missing: an exercise or lesson step where the learner *hears* a smooth versus a jumping progression and chooses; the loop exercises compare root position and inversions only by file, not by task. Berklee: voice-led triad changes are a named module (https://online.berklee.edu/courses/berklee-keyboard-method and https://online.berklee.edu/courses/pop-rock-keyboard). Severity: depth; the foundation is taught.

**3.2 Slash chords.** Present: `.4.md:31-34`, `.6.md:21-25`, `exercise.slash-bass.*`. Weak point: slash chords are taught only as *one descending-stepwise line* (C, C/B, Am, C/G, F, F/E, Dm, G). A slash chord that is an inversion (`C/E`, chord tone in bass), a slash chord that adds a non-chord bass note (`F/G`, `C/D`), and a rising or pedal bass are not distinguished. Severity: depth. See section 5 on the exact `.4` example.

**3.3 Choosing texture by melody density and register.** Absent as a taught decision. `.5.md:34-37` offers two textures; `.3.md:26-29` offers three LH rhythms; `.9.md:15-18` names "where is the melody" as a decision. Nothing tells the learner *when* a busy left hand collides with a busy melody, or where to put the chord in register. Berklee Pop/Rock Keyboard lists arpeggiated textures, eighth-note patterns and ballad voicings as choices tied to context. Searched: the seven lessons for "register", "density", "melody" in a decision sense: `.9.md:16` (melody position) and `.9.md:33` ("an arrangement has to leave something out to stay playable") only. Severity: depth, but it is the heart of "arrange" (see 8.3).

**3.4 Bass-line construction.** Weak. Only the descending slash line is built (`.6`) plus root-then-chord (`.3`). No lesson builds a bass line from root, fifth, octave, approach or passing notes, or from the voice that connects two chords; `.9.md:15` lists "root, walking, or a pattern" as options, but walking bass is taught only in `blues.5.md` and `jazz.5.md` (neither is a prerequisite; `blues-boogie` and `jazz` are `defaultActive: false`, `00-tracks.json:29, 36`). Berklee Keyboard Method lists "bass lines" within lead-sheet performance. Severity: depth (borderline foundational bridge for the arranging endpoint: a learner is told to choose "walking" without having it in this track).

**3.5 Rhythmic comping (pop idiom).** Absent from lesson text. `grep -n -i "comp"` over `chords-pop.*.md`: no match for "comp " or "comping"; the concept `comping` is only a tag on `.9` (`stage-9.json:263-270`) and the only exercise is `exercise.comping.c.anticipated` (level 6.3; pattern offsets `[0.0, 2.5]` over a ii-V-I with shell voicings; `generate_exercises.py:4108-4113, 4180-4270`; tracks `jazz`, `chords-pop`). D2 stage 7 promised "rhythmic comping, syncopated LH octaves"; Berklee Pop/Rock Keyboard: "quarter-note and eighth-note accompaniment patterns", "funky groove patterns with syncopated pocket playing". Severity: depth. The only comping a `chords-pop` learner meets is a jazz-swing study placed on the last rung with no words about it.

**3.6 Playing by ear from a recording (tonic, bass, progression).** The dossier's "important probable omission". Searched `chords-pop.*.md`: ear is a *transposition* technique (`.8.md:21-23`, "Play the song in the new key without writing anything down") and "learning by ear is part of arranging" (`.9.md:23-24`); the in-app ear exercise attached to `.9` is `drill.ear.tune-long`, titled "Play back a tune — eight bars", level 8.4 (built catalog), i.e. melody playback. Nothing in the track asks the learner to find a tonic, find the bass notes, infer a progression, reproduce the chord rhythm. Elsewhere: `theory.9.md` ("Take down eight bars. Melody and harmony, by ear") and `theory.6.md:35, 40` (bass by ear), both in `theory-ear` (`defaultActive: true`), not referenced from `chords-pop`. Berklee Ear Training and RCM both include playback and harmonic hearing (dossier section 2.4 URLs). Severity: depth for the arranging endpoint (the stated reason for the drill, `.9.md:23-24`, does not match what the drill trains: it trains melody, while the claim is that "the arrangement you can hear is the one you can copy").

**3.7 Intros, endings, countermelody, build/release, record-listen-revise.** `.9.md:20-21` "Steal the intro from the last eight bars" is the only intro/ending advice (an intro heuristic that implies the ending). No ending types (tag, ritard, fade, repeat last phrase), no countermelody, no modulation, no build/release (design D2 stage 9 promised intro/outro, modulation, countermelody). Record-listen-revise: `grep -n -i "record"` over `chords-pop.*.md` finds only "recording" meaning a commercial recording (`.6.md:18`), "sound like a record" (`.7.md:58`, `.9.md:13`) and "Nothing is recorded" (`.7.md:55`, a lab statement). `.9.md:26-27` "Play them back to back and keep the half of each that worked" is a self-evaluation without a recording step. Berklee Pop/Rock Keyboard assigns recorded performances and "combining multiple techniques in full arrangements". Severity: depth (intro/ending/build), enrichment (countermelody, modulation).

**3.8 Secondary dominants used before taught in this track.** `.8.md:25-27` and the `.8` concept list (`stage-8.json`, `secondary-dominants`) and exercise `drill.theory.roman-numerals-secondary` presuppose `theory.7.md:15-22` (secondary dominants, taught in the `theory-ear` track at stage 7). `chords-pop.8` has prerequisite `["chords-pop.7"]` only (stage-8.json prerequisites). `chords-pop.4.md:44-45` gestures at it ("borrowing a D major chord from another key"; the notation, bar 13 D then D7 to G in C, is V/V), which is a different name for the same thing. Severity: sequencing, see section 4.

**3.9 Pop idiom items promised by D2 and absent.** Reharmonisation, borrowed iv, pedal points, riffs/hooks, gospel passing chords, syncopated LH octaves (scope in section 1). Severity: enrichment for the track as now defined; they are D2 stage-6/7 promises and the reason the design table and the lessons no longer agree. Spec-serves-the-code applies: either the design row or the lessons should move; recorded in section 8, not decided here.

**3.10 Performance and recovery.** `grep -n -i "perform"` over `chords-pop.*.md`: no match. Severity: enrichment (cross-track F).

4. **`.9` asks for the walking left hand without the lesson having taught it** (`.9.md:15`; see 3.4).
5. **D2 stage 6/7/9 list-versus-lesson drift** (section 1). Not a learner-facing sequencing fault by itself; the lessons are internally coherent as a development: chart reading (3), vocabulary and loop (4), sevenths and texture (5), loop in every key and slash bass (6), colour (7), transposition (8), arranging (9).

Lead, not a finding (I did not read the Isabella notation bar by bar).
