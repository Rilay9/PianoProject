## 3. Missing or weak abilities

1. **The habanera is printed twice in the catalog but never taught or named.** Severity: foundational bridge (named in the genre plan as its own rung; named in the latin.6 finder constraints at stage-6.json "a tango or habanera with the left hand written out"). Evidence: grep over `content/lessons/` (all lesson files) returns "habanera" only in `latin.3.md:33` and `ragtime.7.md:25`. The latin.6 lesson (`latin.6.md:15-21`) describes Por Una Cabeza's left hand but never says it is the habanera; the tresillo exercise is the only control item and has the three-note root only. External: the habanera is the tresillo plus the second main beat (Wikipedia Tresillo; Habanera (music)); my notation read shows both forms are already in the app (The Crave: tresillo; Por Una Cabeza: habanera).
2. **No listening, transcription, or by-ear station anywhere in the track.** Severity: depth (Berklee: "emphasizes transcription skills"; packet 7D). Scope searched: grep over `content/lessons/latin*.md` for listen/transcri/by ear/improvis/vary: no hits (the four lesson files read in full). The one video per lesson is a how-to, not a listening example. The brief asks each rung to name a listening/transcription example: none does.
3. **No station where the learner chooses, varies or improvises a Latin pattern, or comps from a chart.** Severity: depth. The latin.5 concept list includes `comping` and `bossa-nova` (stage-5.json), but the lesson text contains neither a comping task nor a bossa pattern (grep: "comp" appears nowhere in the four lessons; `docs/02-curriculum.md:1004` records that "latin no longer names the walking bass"). Two of the six latin.5 songs are bossa lead-sheet melodies with no left hand: the learner is never shown or asked to produce a bossa accompaniment.
4. **Brazilian styles are three one-staff melodies and one lesson sentence** (Só Danço Samba, Insensatez, Tico-Tico; `latin.md:39-41`). The packet and dossier say to treat styles distinctly (packet section 8 "Latin"). Berklee separates Cuban, Brazilian and South American weeks. Severity: depth; enrichment if the owner keeps the track narrow.
5. **The montuno exercises are the clave's rhythm played as chords, not a guajeo.** Generator: `write_montuno` puts a chord on each of the five clave strokes and nothing else (`generate_exercises.py:5077-5101`). External: a guajeo is an arpeggiated chord-tone ostinato, typically octave-doubled, aligned to the clave pattern, not necessarily struck on each clave stroke (Wikipedia Guajeo, Montuno; Mauleon *101 Montunos* cited, not read). The lesson's own words are "a repeating syncopated figure built from the chord's notes, usually in octaves or thirds, locked to the clave" (`latin.md:28-31`), and `latin.6.md:35-41` says "every note is a clave stroke". As a first acquisition drill that isolates the rhythmic lock this is defensible; but the rung never later asks for a real arpeggiated guajeo, so the learner finishes with the rhythm skeleton. Severity: depth. Not an error; a thin step.
6. **Rumba clave and bossa clave are in the exercise list but not defined in any lesson.** `latin.3.md:16-20` defines only son 3-2/2-3. The exercises `exercise.clave.rumba-3-2` and `.bossa` (stage-3.json:1113-1114) are single-line rhythms with no lesson sentence saying how they differ (the one-stroke delay: son's third stroke on beat 4, rumba's on the "and" of 4; bossa delays the last stroke by an eighth). Severity: depth. Generator rows verified correct against Wikipedia Clave (rumba 3-2 = 0, 1.5, 3.5, 5.0, 6.0; bossa = 0, 1.5, 3.0, 5.0, 6.5).
7. **Cuban and Argentine traditions share a rung without saying so** (`latin.6.md:15-45`; the dossier asks that this be clarified). Severity: depth. The lesson does not say that the montuno is Cuban and the tango left hand Argentine, nor that the tango's pattern descends from the habanera (Wikipedia Habanera (music): "The Argentine milonga and tango makes use of the habanera rhythm").
8. **Modern tango (`latin.8`) and the habanera repertoire rung (`latin.4`) are not built.** See Section 8 for the candidate decisions. The absence claim is scoped to: the four stage JSON units and `docs/generated/ladder.md` "Latin" (4 rungs).
