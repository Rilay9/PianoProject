## 3. Missing or weak abilities

Search scope for every absence below: the four lesson files (`jam.md`, `jam.5.md`, `jam.6.md`, `jam.7.md`, read in full), plus `grep -n -i` over `content/lessons/*.md` (109 files) for the terms named in each item. "Absent" means those two scopes, nothing wider; the app code was grepped where stated.

1. **Recovering when lost and finding the form again. Foundational bridge for the stated endpoint.** The sentence "Knowing where you are is the thing beginners lose first" (`jam.md:52`) is the only acknowledgement. Over `jam*.md` the terms `lost|get back in|rejoin|re-enter|come back in|find the form|find your place` give only the two "without losing the form" test sentences (`jam.md:65`, `jam.5.md:48`). Elsewhere: `improv.5.md:26` asserts "The form gives you landmarks, so you are never lost" (a claim, not a drill); `4.6.md:23-26` teaches keeping going and "deliberately drop a note and carry on in time" for a solo piece; `holiday.6.md:35` is about performance. Nothing teaches what to do when the learner has lost bar 5 or the chorus count while the bed continues. The mechanism for the dossier's drill exists in part, see section 7 and recommendation 1. Why it matters: it is the difference between "plays along" and "functions with another musician". External: the dossier (`:1397-1411`) and ABRSM Grades 6-8 (the candidate must follow the chord sequence through the solo section with a rhythm section that does not stop). Severity: foundational bridge.
2. **Ending together. Foundational bridge for ensemble use, depth for the module.** The design promised "endings and turnarounds" (`docs/02:741`). The lessons give `jam.7.md:28-30` ("at the end a tag or a turnaround to send it round again") and nothing else; over `jam*.md` the pattern `ending|ritard|rit\.|fermata|end together|final chord|last chord|outro` hits only that line, and over `blues.*.md` and `jazz.*.md` the word `ending` appears only at `blues.5.md:16` (a left-hand figure ending on the V7). Yet the shipped charts print the cues: `song.blues.storyville-blues` bars 53 `Back to "B" for Solos`, 54 `rit.` and `Tag`, final barline bar 56; `song.blues.riverside-blues` bar 36 `Play 2 bar unison on out-chorus`. A learner is never asked to find or follow them as an ending. Severity: foundational bridge for the endpoint; the repertoire (MODEL) is already shipped.
3. **Listening while playing, as a task. Depth.** Two sentences (`jam.md:44-45`, `jam.7.md:19-20`). The lab deliberately does not judge the answer against the call (`tradingFours.ts` header: "a mode that scored imitation would teach the opposite"). Call-and-response answered "same rhythm, different ending" is on the improv ladder (`improv.3.md:29`, `improv.4.md:66`), not in this track. Nobody in this process can judge whether a learner answered what they heard; it stays self-checked.
4. **Counting off at several tempos; counting off at all. Depth.** One paragraph (`jam.md:30-32`). The tools could carry the practice (chart count-off, lab tempo 40-240, Settings count-in bars 0-4, all above) but the lesson never asks for it. External: ABRSM's aural tests (grades 1-5 syllabus) use "a two-bar count-in", which confirms the two-bar convention `jam.md:30` states. See section 5 for a wording problem in that paragraph.
5. **Adjusting dynamics, simplifying under pressure, following live cues. Enrichment.** One line each, no control and no check. Serenade Magazine (above) names dynamics and non-verbal cues as ensemble basics; a general-audience source, so recorded as supporting only.
6. **Role switching and trading eights. Enrichment.** The lab supports both roles; lessons never ask for one chorus comping and the next soloing. Fours and twos are enough for the stated endpoint; eights add little.
7. **Recording a jam in Free mode. Scoped-out design item.** `docs/02:742` promised it; nothing in `jam*.md` mentions it (`record|free mode|listen back` over `jam*.md` finds only "behind a recording" and "Nothing is recorded", `jam.5.md:47`, `jam.7.md:54`). `improv.3.md:35-38` describes *Listen back* for free play. Not building it is defensible; record it as a design promise not kept.
8. **No real tune to comp behind or walk under at `jam.5` and `jam.6`. Depth.** Ladder: both rungs have 0 songs (`ladder.md:175-176`, "song-optional"). The only transfer material for comping is the lab bed, which plays a generated line; `genre-plans/jam.md` lists catalog pieces for both stages (Bill Bailey, Some of These Days, Darktown Strutters for stage 5; Tiger Rag, Royal Garden Blues, Muskrat Ramble, Jazz Me Blues for stage 6) but none is placed here at HEAD. Under the repertoire-first principle this is a gap, a modest one: the chart view (§3b) already opens any chord-symbol piece.
9. **Transposing a lead sheet is asked for but not taught or tooled.** See sequencing, section 4.

## 4. Sequencing concerns

1. `jam.7` asks the learner to "move the chart to a guitar key by numerals" (`jam.7.md:32-37`) on charts of 36 to 56 bars with up to 75 chord changes (`song.blues.storyville-blues`: 56 bars, four flats, 75 chord symbols per the dump). What the track has taught about transposition is one sentence about the twelve-bar shape (`jam.md:19-22`). The track's prerequisite chain is `blues.4` then `jam`, `jam.5`, `jam.6` (`stage-*.json` `prerequisites`). Transposition by numerals is taught in `chords-pop.3` and `chords-pop.8` (stage 8, later than `jam.7`) and elsewhere (`grep -c -i transpos` over lessons: `4.4, blues.5, blues.8, chords-pop.3/4/6/8, holiday.3, jam.md, practice.5, ragtime.6, theory.5/8`). The chord-chart view has no transposition control: `grep -n -i transpos app/src/ui/screens/ChordChartScreen.ts` finds nothing, and `docs/02:743-748` itself says the guitar-friendly variant of the flat-key charts "nobody has made". So the "try both" task is real but unsupported. Abrupt jump: a twelve-bar shape to a 56-bar chart with modulating chords. Weary Blues also changes key mid-chart (see section 5), which makes "by numerals" harder, not easier.
2. `jam.4.1` lists `trading-fours` among its concepts (`stage-4.json` concepts for lesson `jam`) and defines trading and walking bass in its text (`jam.md:40-46`) three and four rungs before either is practised (`jam.6`, `jam.7`). Read as a glossary this is fine; as a promise on the rung it is a topic list. No `jam.4.1` option trades or walks.
3. `jam.4.1` exerciseOptions include `exercise.comping.c.anticipated.intro` (an anticipated figure in C) while the lesson `jam.md` teaches no rhythm figure; the Charleston and anticipation arrive at `jam.5` (`jam.5.md:22-31`, concepts `charleston`, `anticipation`). Also, three of the four exercises are not in a guitar key at all: `drill.blues.lh-patterns` has keys C, F, G (`catalog.json`), the comping exercise is in C, the rhythm exercise has no key; only the form-tracker drill (E, A, G, D, C) and the E/A/G shuffles match the keys `jam.md:15-18` insists on. `docs/02:735-736` allows C, so this is not an error, only a mismatch between emphasis and material.
4. `jam.6` asks for the walk "under the guitarist" but the app's bed always has its own bass, see section 7. The learner is walking against an app bass that plays root and fifth (`backingLoop.ts:122-155`), not replacing a missing bass player.
5. `jam.7` offers `exercise.blues-scale.e-flat.1oct.both`, `.b-flat.`, and `exercise.turnaround.e-flat.i-vi-ii-v`, `.b-flat.` (levels 5.2 and 6.4 in `catalog.json`). The lesson text names none of them and none trains trading, breaks or charts. The rung's level band 3.09-6.4 is wide because of them. They fit the flat-key charts (B♭ and E♭), which is defensible, but nothing connects them to the lesson.
6. The track has four rungs spread over stages 4, 5, 6, 7 with `estimatedDays` 21, 14, 18, 21 (`stage-*.json`), a development: form and keys, comping, walking bass, trading and set list. The ordering is defensible as a development of the rhythm-section roles. The missing step is a rung or paragraph where the learner uses the two roles together without a prompt (see section 8).

## 6. Practice and material sufficiency

- **CONTROL for comping is enough in count but all four-bar studies.** Four comping exercises (`jam.5` exerciseOptions: E Charleston, A Charleston, G off-beats, D anticipated), each 4 bars of ii-V-I-I; the rung's requirement is two runs (`stage-5.json` `requirements`: exercises count 2). `jam.5.md:38-40` asks for "eight choruses" in the lab, which is where the real practice is, and it is unmarked by design.
- **CONTROL for walking bass is thin in the combined case.** Left-hand-only exercises exist in E, A, D (level 4.5); the combined walk-plus-comp exists only in A (`exercise.walking-bass.a.blues`, level 6.2). The rung's own test (`jam.6.md:54-56`) is "twelve bars of walking bass in E ... then the same twelve bars with chords on top"; the E combined version does not exist in the options. Thin generated family, recorded only.
- **Revisited:** the form tracker recurs in `jam`, `jam.5`, `jam.7` (`stage-4/5/7.json` exerciseOptions). Space recurs in `jam`, `jam.5`, `jam.7`. Counting in, dynamics and cues do not recur (section 3).
- **Transfer:** only `jam.7` has songs, the five charts; they are used as substrate (the interpretation audit's role, KEEP / TASK_SUBSTRATE for all five rows). `jam.4.1`'s five "songs" are the twelve-bar shuffles; `exercise.blues.twelve-bar-shuffle.e` has a continuous boogie bass and a chord on beats 1 and 3 in every bar (read with music21): it demonstrates the twelve-bar form in the guitar key but not "keep the voicings small, leave whole bars empty" (`jam.md:34-36`), so the rung's comping rules have no written model and are practised only in the lab. The lesson's job for those files is the form and the key, so under demonstrate-versus-apply this is acceptable; the comping rules are an apply task.
- **A gap in the materials the lessons imply:** `exercise.blues.twelve-bar-shuffle.d` exists (catalog, tracks `blues-boogie`, `improv-compose`, on core `4.7`, `stage-4.json:568`) and is not offered on `jam.4.1`, while `jam.md:19-20` tells the learner to move the C shuffle up to D by hand. This reads as a deliberate transposition task, so it is a note, not a defect.
- **Mastery on this track measures written-part playing.** `jam.4.1` has `minAccuracy 0.9, minTempoPct 0.85` over the shuffle exercise and song runs; `jam.7` requires a run of one exercise and one chart at 0.85 and 0.8 (`stage-4.json`, `stage-7.json`). The ensemble tasks themselves (space, entries, trades, cues) are not what the mastery gate sees. See section 7.
- **Repertoire lead check:** dossier archive leads (St James Infirmary `Qmdyj1...`, Sweet Georgia Brown `QmSpTe...`, After You've Gone `QmWUXq...`, Indiana `Qmcy1D...`): not inspected. St James Infirmary is already on `blues.3` (`ladder.md:91`); After You've Gone is shipped as another edition; none is needed to fix a gap named here.

## 8. Recommended changes

Ranked. None is a design for code; each is the smallest change that names the learner problem.

1. **Add the lost-and-find task to `jam.5` or `jam.6`, as prose plus a self-run protocol on the existing lab. A fix (new text), no new unit.** Problem: the endpoint's central skill (recovering) is never practised and the lesson only says it is the first thing beginners lose. Smallest change: in `jam.5.md` (comping), a paragraph and a three-step protocol: start *Jam it* on the twelve-bar bed, play one chorus, stop playing for two bars while the bed runs (the lab does not stop), come back in at the next four-bar boundary (bar 5 or bar 9) using only what you hear and the bar counter, then do it again with the chart covered (the same card the core path uses, `4.6.md:12-17`). The bar numbers of the IV and V (bars 5 and 9) are themselves the audible landmarks. Evidence: the mechanism exists (section 7); `4.6.md:23-26` is the precedent for a deliberate-drop drill; dossier `:1397-1411`. What it would delete: the claim "you are never lost" (`improv.5.md:26`) could be softened to point here. A measured version (a lab setting that sits out bars and reads the re-entry bar from the key-down record) would be a new capability and is scoped out here; the prose version needs no code and is the smallest honest step. Unverified as music: nobody here can say whether the learner's re-entry sounded right; only the bar number is checkable.
2. **Teach and use the ending cues in `jam.7`, and correct the tag sentence. A fix.** Problem: `jam.7.md:28-30` has one wrong sentence and no ending task; the charts already carry the cues. Smallest change: correct "tag" versus "turnaround" (section 5, item 1) and add a line sending the learner to Storyville bars 53-56 and Riverside bar 36 to find who ends and how, with "rit." and "Tag" named as the band's agreed ending. Evidence: dumps cited in sections 3 and 5. Severity foundational for ensemble use because without it the track ends on a jam that cannot stop.
3. **Fix the jam.6 lab-verdict mismatch. A fix.** Problem: the verdict mis-describes a correct walking line. Smallest change: remove the scale-count sentence from `jam.6.md:47-48` (the walking bass is a chord-and-approach line, not a scale line), or say in the lesson that approach notes and thirds fall outside it. Do not alter the lab (a code change belongs outside this review). What it deletes: a sentence.
4. **Support or narrow the transposition ask in `jam.7`. A fix, small.** Problem: "by numerals" on 36-to-56-bar charts, with no teaching, no tool, and the key-change in Weary Blues. Smallest change: either (a) work one chart's first eight bars through in the lesson text as a numeral example (After You've Gone, 8 bars of B♭ chords, `song.pop.after-you-ve-gone.pdmx` bars 1-8) or (b) say plainly that the learner may leave the chart where it is and let the other player work, which `jam.7.md:36-37` already allows. Note that a transposition control on the chart view is a capability, not part of this fix.
5. **Counting in as a practised skill, three lines. A fix.** Problem: one paragraph, no task, and a count the text does not make unambiguous (section 5, item 4). Smallest change: say whether the first two beats are at half tempo; add "count in the same tune at a slow, a medium and a quick tempo, aloud, and then set the chart's Count off to the same tempo and compare" (chart count-off and tempo box exist). Evidence: the tools above; ABRSM's two-bar count-in.
6. **Wording corrections, no new content. Fixes.** `jam.5.md:34-36` and `jam.md:57-59` (the bed's drums), the "1920" sentence (`jam.7.md:32-33`), and the comparable "figures below" at `jam.5.md:26` (there are no figures below in the page text, only the exercise list).
7. **Missing materials the dossier lists, scoped out:** a real tune to comp behind at `jam.5`/`jam.6` (section 3, item 8); the combined walk-plus-comp in E (section 6). Neither is needed for the endpoint. If a unit is added later, a `jam.8` "trade eights, end on cue, whole tune from memory" would be the new-unit shape; I do not recommend it before the fixes above because the lessons already carry more topics than practice.
8. Leave alone: the five `jam.7` charts (audit verdict confirmed), the lab's trade measure, the form-tracker drill, and the "Nothing here can be passed or failed" contract.
