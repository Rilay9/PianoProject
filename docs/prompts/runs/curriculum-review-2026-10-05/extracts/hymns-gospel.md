## 3. Missing or weak abilities

Each absence is scoped to: the five hymns lesson files read in full plus `grep -l -i` over all 109 `content/lessons/*.md` on the stated terms; unit data in `content/curriculum/stage-*.json`.

1. **Gospel as a style beyond walk-ups and one passing-chord idea (call-and-response, gospel cadences/chants, choir and singer accompaniment, shout/talk textures, gospel-style reharmonisation, live adaptation).** Why it matters: it is what the name promises (Berklee outline above; dossier TRACK 11). Absence scope: none of "shout", "choir", "congregation", "testimony", "preacher", "spiritual" occurs in any of 109 lesson files; "call-and-response" occurs in `1.4.md`, `blues.5.md:33,49`, `blues.9.md:15`, `improv.3.md:30` and never in hymns; "reharmon" occurs in `hymns.6.md`, `improv.8.md`, `jazz.7.md`. Severity: depth if the name is narrowed, foundational bridge only if the name stays. This is the owner decision in section 10.
2. **Accompanying people who sing.** A hymn exists to be sung with; no lesson in this track teaches choosing a key for a singer or congregation, a count-in or intro, keeping a steady tempo for voices, a last-verse lift or ending. Scope: `grep -n -i 'intro\|last verse\|key change\|modulat\|transpos\|singer'` over `hymns*.md` finds `hymns.4.md:12` ("four singers" as a metaphor) and no instruction. For contrast `holiday.md:43,54-58` states a singers' tempo constraint, and the Stage 2 `holiday` unit description "how to play for people who are singing" (00-tracks.json). Why it matters: the dossier's Option A endpoint list says "transpose/accompany" (TRACK 11), but the track never teaches either. Severity: depth (a real gap against the endpoint the dossier itself ascribes to the narrowed track).
3. **Transposition (cross-track B).** Not taught or asked in any hymns lesson (`grep -n -i transpos content/lessons/hymns*.md`: no match). The Lab is described as "in whatever key you set" (`hymns.md:59`, `hymns.6.md:48`), which is a setting, not a task. Hymns are the repertoire where key-for-the-singer matters most. Severity: depth.
4. **Choosing chords for an unharmonised tune.** `hymns.2` asks the learner to put chords under three tunes with no symbols but no lesson in the track teaches the method (which chords harmonise which melody notes). The learner has only Free play. Scope as above (`harmonis|harmoniz` grep). Severity: depth; the unit text is honest that Swing Low is "where you stop guessing and start reading" (`hymns.2.md:39-40`).

6. **Reharmonisation as taught content.** Concept id on `hymns.6` with no rule in the prose (item 7 above). Severity: enrichment if the track stays hymn accompaniment; it is part of Berklee's gospel list.

## 4. Sequencing concerns

1. **Stage 3 teaches two devices with no drill for either on the rung.** `hymns.md:28-36` introduces the bass walk-up and the chromatic passing chord and `hymns.md:64-65` asks for "one verse with a bass walk-up into each new chord", but the Stage 3 unit's `exerciseOptions` hold neither `exercise.walkup.c` (4.6) nor `exercise.passing-chord.c` (5.4); they appear only in the Stage 5 unit. The generator docstring says the same of `walkup`: "Reachable through `passing-chords` ... until a rung names this item" (`generate_exercises.py:5358-5363`). So the CONTROL station for both devices is two stages after the first use. Evidence: stage-3.json `hymns-gospel.3.1.exerciseOptions` and stage-5.json `hymns-gospel.5.1.exerciseOptions`. This is a spiral by design (docs/02 lines 89-95) rather than a defect, but the Stage 3 exit has no isolating study.
2. **Repeated repertoire across rungs.** The Stage 3 `songOptions` list contains three of the five Stage 4 songs (`amazing-grace-satb.pdmx`, `abide-with-me-william-henry-monk.pdmx`, `rock-of-ages-cleft-for-me.pdmx`) and three of the five Stage 5 songs (`what-a-friend-we-have-in-jesus.pdmx`, `just-a-closer-walk-with-thee-easy-piano.pdmx`, `martin-j-nystrom-as-the-deer-piano.pdmx`); each rung requires one song run (`requirements`: runs from songs count 1). A learner can satisfy Stage 4 with a song already played at Stage 3. `docs/02-curriculum.md:89-95` records this as intended ("the list doing its job"); I note only that the rung requirement cannot tell a revisit from new material.
3. **Level bands overlap heavily and out of order.** `hymns` band 2.6-5.7 at Stage 3 contains four-part scores at 4.6-5.3 (ladder.md line 81), while the Stage 2 unit's top option is 2.5 and Stage 4's band starts at 3.2. This is a topic-before-level ordering (four voices before decoration) that is defensible musically; recorded as the audit's finding to the synthesis, not as an error.
4. **Hymns.4 refers to the learner's history.** `hymns.4.md:43-44`: "*Joyful, Joyful* is the tune you played from a single line at Stage 2". The Stage 2 edition is a different file in a different key (see section 5, item 2), and Stage 2 offers four options so the learner may not have played it.
