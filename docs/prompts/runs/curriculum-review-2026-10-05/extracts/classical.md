## 4. Sequencing concerns

1. **Sonata form is taught on rung 5 with a Beethoven edition that does not contain it.** `classical.5.md:14-26` defines an exposition (second theme in the dominant), "a short development" and a recapitulation of both themes, then says "Before you play a note of one, mark the three sections on the page." Notation of `song.classical.beethoven-sonatina-in-g-major-ahn-5.pdmx` (34 bars): bars 1-8 are a theme in G; bars 9-16 are a sequential passage (C naturals, F#; dominant area) ending bar 16; bars 17-24 repeat bars 1-8 note for note; bars 25-34 are a coda over D then G pedal figures. That reads as A-B-A plus coda, with no development and no recapitulated second theme. Only `song.classical.clementi-sonatina-no-1-muzio-clementi.pdmx` has the three-part layout (exposition 1-15, development 16-23, recapitulation 24-38). Under demonstrate-versus-apply the task is "mark them", so the Beethoven edition can only show that sonatinas vary, and the lesson should say so. Medium confidence: the labels are my reading of bars, and the score is a short edition.
2. **Ornaments: apply before control.** The mordent drill is on `technique.5` and the measured trill on `technique.6`, yet `classical.3` (stage 3) tells the learner to add a mordent on repeats (`classical.3.md:33-40`) and `classical.4` (stage 4) teaches the trill. Petzold prints no ornament (0 trill/mordent). Nothing breaks, because it is optional, but the order is apply, then control.
3. **Cross-reference attribution.** `classical.9.md:25` calls "Sections, backwards, out of order" "the Stage 8 habit"; "work backwards" is taught at Stage 6 (`classical.6.md:42-46`) and Stage 8 teaches "out of order" and a written plan (`classical.8.md:35-37`).
