## 1. Promised endpoint

Track description (`content/curriculum/00-tracks.json:97`): "Scales, arpeggios, double notes, octaves and the rest of the technique syllabus (docs/02 Part E). P11 recorded this as a label rather than a track because it had 434 items and no ladder; P12a gave it one, so it is a module you can follow." It starts at stage 4. Design (`docs/02-curriculum.md:412-428`): one rung per stage, generated exercises plus three studies each. Part E's table (`02-curriculum.md:780-789`) promises at stage 8 "all scales 4 oct at quarter = 120 in 16ths; scales in 3rds/6ths all keys".

Last rung's own words (`technique.8.md:12-14`, `:42-46`): "Four octaves, in sixteenths, at a quarter-note pulse of 120 ... the standard this stage is measured against", and "Four octaves hands together, sixteenths at 120, with the turn at the top sounding like the rest of the run ... a wrist that feels the same at the end as it did at the start." What ships at stage 8 is 12 exercises, all four-octave major scales, hands together, similar motion, and no repertoire (`docs/generated/ladder.md` line 189: 12 exercises, 0 songs).

What a competent developing pianist in this domain can do: scales and arpeggios in all keys, major and minor, at several tempi and in several touches; chromatic, third and sixth scales; dominant and diminished sevenths; octaves and repeated chords; a controlled leap; even repeated notes; tone control (balance within a hand, a shaped line); and, throughout, a way of producing sound that can be sustained without strain. RCM Level 10 and ABRSM Grade 8 are the published public ladders for this (Section 3 lists what they contain). Neither is a target PianoProject must match; the point is only that the competent-pianist content is broader than a major-keys-only stage 8.

Reasonable PianoProject endpoint: the physical vocabulary that stage 4-8 repertoire (Classical, Romantic, jazz piano, ragtime stride) leans on: even scales and arpeggios in the keys the repertoire uses, repeated notes, hand independence, double notes, octaves, a sounding top note, a pedal that is not a switch, and a habit of stopping when the body complains. The track name "Technique" promises more than that, so the track is intentionally narrower than its name (no leaps as a skill, no repeated chords, no minor-key speed work, no tone or relaxation teaching of its own). That narrowing is stated in neither the track description nor any lesson; Section 8 item 6 says where to say it.

## 4. Sequencing concerns

1. **Two lessons describe different starts for the same exercise.** Core `4.1.md:29-31` says "Start both thumbs on the same C and move outwards: now the hands are exact mirrors", and lists `exercise.scale.c-major.2oct.contrary.both.2` as a 4.1 option. `technique.4.md:26-30` calls that same exercise "the one to start on: the left hand begins on the C above the right hand's, so in the first bar they pass through each other." (G4,F4), so the left hand begins to the right of the right hand and the hands pass at beat 4). The one-octave form `exercise.scale.c-major.1oct.contrary.both.2` starts both hands on C4 and diverges, as 4.1 describes. All twelve two-octave contrary files have the same construction; mechanism: `tools/content/generate_exercises.py:1085-1089, 1101-1102`, where the left hand's start is its own low octave and the contrary run reads from the top of that range. ## 7. Measurement limits

What the app verifies from MIDI (family contracts, `tools/content/family_contracts.json`, each family's `assessment.judged`): written notes in order and onset timing within +/-150 ms in Keep tempo (every technique family); note length for articulation (staccato at most half the written value, legato at least 0.9); velocity range for shaping (30, mostly one way); top-note velocity ratio 1.4 for voicing; CC64 value share inside 32-96 for the half pedal (not the held-melody row: "nothing measures it", `pedal_variant`).

Self-checked, per the contracts' `unjudged` lists: evenness of tone and time across fingers (every scale, arpeggio, repeated-note, rotation, trill, double-note and Hanon family: "velocity and onsets are recorded and not measured for it (G8)"); which finger played (nothing tracks fingers, so a printed number is advice); the thumb's passage without a bump (tone and weight: "no input reports them"); forearm and wrist rotation (`rotation`, `tremolo_octaves`: "no input reports it"); legato between double-note pairs ("note-off is not measured for this family"); finger independence; the held melody surviving a pedal change; tone quality; the ring the half pedal leaves.

What no actor in this process can decide (nobody hears or watches a hand here): whether the voicing at ratio 1.4 sounds like a tune over an accompaniment; whether a crescendo reads as one line; whether the half pedal at 32-96 produces the ring the lesson describes on a given piano; whether a trill, mordent or rotation figure is physically even and relaxed; whether the drills are comfortable for small hands (stretch limits are bounded in the contracts' `physical` rows, `maxSpan` 12, but no instrument or hand is tested). These stay open, unverified as music.

One measurement-honesty gap: the app does not detect tension, so the only instrument for the track's central safety property is the learner's own report. That is why the stop-condition gap in Section 3 item 1 is a content gap rather than a detector gap.
