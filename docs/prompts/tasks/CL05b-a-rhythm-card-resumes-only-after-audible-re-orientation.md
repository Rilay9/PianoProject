**The reviewer's words, verbatim** (`docs/review/responses/8764c643.md`):

> **Required change — resume a rhythm card only after audible re-orientation**
>
> Use one rule for both questions:
>
> **A rhythm card returning from a hidden span stays held until sound is actually available, then gives the learner one count-in before the held judged grid resumes.**
>
> The count-in is not a new attempt and is not judged. When it finishes, continue from the same musical/judgement point the exact-span re-anchor preserved; do not restart the card from its beginning and do not skip hidden material.
>
> This means:
>
> 1. Do **not** resume the judged grid immediately when `visibilityState` becomes visible. The proof case's next judged onset 300 ms after return is too abrupt to be a fair temporal reference after an interruption.
> 2. Do **not** judge against an inaudible click while `AudioContext` is still unavailable/suspended. The grid remains held until `audioEngine.ensureStarted()` has actually produced a running/usable context.
> 3. If the platform requires a user gesture before audio can resume, that gesture must be a **non-judged resume action**. Do not consume the learner's first musical answer tap as both "wake the audio system" and an answer against a grid they have not heard. If the existing Drill controls cannot provide such a gesture without inventing a new learner-facing control/state, stop and return that small UI choice instead of guessing.
> 4. Once audio is available, play the existing count-in policy, then resume the same held grid point and its click together. Hidden time remains excluded from every timing channel.
>
> A discriminating case should cover at least: hide after the rhythm grid is established; return with the audio context still suspended; prove no judged-grid movement and no answer acceptance while sound is unavailable; resume audio; prove a count-in occurs before the next judged onset; then prove the grid continues from the pre-hide musical point rather than from bar 1 or from elapsed wall time.
>
> X43 remains separate: the pre-existing missing count-in downbeat click is a general count-in defect, not created by this resume rule. Do not widen CL05a into fixing all count-in audio. X44 also remains separate as recorded.

The four numbered points above are the specification; nothing here reopens them.

## Hypothesis and its refuting test

**Hypothesis.** Premises 1, 2 and 4 are the whole mechanism: `resumeDrill`/`resumeRhythmClick` treat "the page is visible again" and "`ensureStarted()`'s promise settled" as sufficient for a rhythm card to be live, without ever checking that sound is actually audible, and without a lead-in once a tap has already set the grid's origin (Branch A already has one; Branch B does not). There is no second gate elsewhere (a global input-suspend, a MIDI-source-level pause) that already protects Branch B.

**Refuting test, run before any edit (red first).** Extend the double (as above) so `ensureStarted()` can resolve while `audio.contextState` reads `'suspended'`. Mount a rhythm item, tap it once so the downbeat is known and `firstTapAt` is set — "the rhythm grid is established," the response's own phrase — hide the page, advance the fake clock past where the next onset would fall, set the double's `contextState` to `'suspended'`, call `setVisibility('visible')`, and, while still `'suspended'`, send a tap and advance the clock across that pending onset. Assert: no new click was scheduled (`audio.clicks` unchanged since the hide), the tap moved neither `drill.result().answered` nor `.correct`, and the onset that was next when the page hid is still unmatched. If all three already hold on the committed tree, the hypothesis is refuted and the real gap is elsewhere — look at whatever already guards this path before touching `resumeDrill`/`resumeRhythmClick`.

## What is decided

- The four numbered points, verbatim, are the specification. Ownership is unchanged: the drill still owns its grid and moments, the screen still only decides when (`screenLifecycle.ts:35–38`), the same boundary CL05a already established — this is a mechanical suspend/re-anchor extension, not a new state/ownership decision, so CL05a's own stop condition is not retriggered by points 1, 2 and 4.
- **The hold is scoped to the rhythm card, not to the shared `suspended` flag's timing.** Delaying when `suspended` itself clears would delay every other drill kind's resume for a reason that does not apply to them (none has an audible click to re-orient to). The mechanism — a second, rhythm-specific gate read alongside `suspended` in `onNote`/`onControl` (on the precedent of the existing `cardHeld() && MANUAL_ADVANCE.has(drill.kind)` kind-specific check at `:707`), cleared only once the resolved context genuinely reads `'running'` — is the builder's judgement; that it must not change what any non-rhythm kind experiences on resume is not.
- **A second re-anchor is required, not optional.** `excludeHidden`'s exact-span shift (CL05a, unchanged, "all four departures stand") fixes the origin to the moment the page became visible. Whatever further wall time the hold and the count-in themselves consume is additional hidden-equivalent time the learner still cannot act on; if the grid's origin is not moved again by that span before judging resumes, the onsets due during the hold are silently missed rather than reached — "count hidden time again" in a new place, which point 4's "hidden time remains excluded from every timing channel" forbids. The same mechanism CL05a already used — `RhythmDrill.startAt`, called again once the lead-in's last beat is scheduled, the same way the very first count-in fixes the origin to its own final click (`DrillScreen.ts:1297–1307`) — is available; whether to route it through `startAt` directly or a small helper is the builder's judgement, not a new ownership question.
- **The stop condition, verbatim** (point 3): if a real resume needs a gesture the automatic visibility-return call cannot supply, that gesture must be non-judged, and if no existing Drill control can serve as one without becoming a new learner-facing control or state, stop and bring that UI choice back rather than guess. Premise 6 found none of the rhythm card's existing controls fits: "Done" and "Skip" discard the current card, "🎤 Listen" is a different input channel, and "Show me"/"Hear it" never appear on a rhythm card at all. Point 3's UI question (which non-judged gesture, given that no existing rhythm-card control serves, premise 6) is put to the reviewer in parallel with this build; the orchestrator forwards the answer if it arrives while you build. Build points 1, 2 and 4 regardless — they hold whether or not a platform ever needs the gesture — and only stop on point 3's narrow question if the discriminating case (or a documented inability to close it without one) shows a real gesture requirement that the hold-and-count-in alone cannot satisfy.

**What a learner meets differently, for itemisation:** on a platform where sound comes back on its own once the phone unlocks, a rhythm card now gives one quiet-then-clicking bar before the grid is live again, instead of being judgeable the instant the screen lights up and possibly against a click that has not actually started; a tap sent in that gap does nothing rather than being scored or discarded. On a platform where sound needs a fresh tap to come back, this lane makes the mechanical parts correct and reports rather than guesses at what that tap should be. This needs no ear.
