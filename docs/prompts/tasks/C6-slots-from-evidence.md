## The goal, in the orchestrator's words

C4c made the reading slot answer the learner's reads; C5 made the rung answer the evidence. The other four slots on Today still answer the stage number and a seed: the warm-up is whichever exercise the seed lands on, the review is a calendar of items, "new" is the next thing in the rung's list, and every reason line is a fixed sentence. After C6 each slot is chosen from what the evidence supports and what the rung asks next, the reason line says so or claims nothing, an alternative is an alternative because it trains the same skill, and the fallback ladder is a stated order of weaker claims rather than a level window. The balance the reviewer set stands: not only remediation of the weakest skill — the rung's intent, retention, and well-rounded exposure choose too.

## What is decided

1. **Technique** offers, from the rung's exercise options, the exercise whose `targetSkills` the rung's unmet requirements name and the evidence has shown least (a skill not yet at the requirement's standard; among those, the one with the fewest recent supported records), not the seed's pick; when the rung's options are all met, the next rung's; when nothing is unmet, an exposure choice (L26) that says so. The reason line is drawn from the requirement ("2.2 asks for eighths in time; this one has them") or from the exposure rule ("keeping your scales warm"), never a fixed string (T19, I1).
2. **Review** is retention, not a calendar: the ladder's `notShownRecently` and `retained` over the learner's skills choose what is worth a return, and the item chosen is one that carries that skill's demand and was met before; the review queue by item and day (L17's "every ~30 days" and the mastered-piece-every-session habit) is deleted, not bypassed; a piece returns because a skill it carries has not been shown for the retention window, and the line says which.
3. **New** offers the rung's next unmet requirement's item, in the order the lesson teaches, with the requirement's words as the reason; when the rung is met, the next rung's first, said as such.
4. **Repertoire** offers a piece whose measured demands the learner's skill states support (met or proficient) and that carries at least one demand at the rung's edge, with the reason naming the demand; "a piece you know" stays for a mastered piece (L18); the deeper repertoire experience (R17–R20) is G's, not this task's.
5. **The fallback ladder** (L14): when a slot's first claim finds nothing, the next weaker claim is tried in a stated order — the rung's own option; an item declaring the same target skill; an item carrying the same demand; a prerequisite skill's item; an exposure choice — and the reason line names which claim was used; level windows (`Math.abs(item.level - level) <= 1`) are deleted from the slots. `fallbackOrder.test.ts` (the inventory's Q6) is this ladder's proof.
6. **Alternatives and the swap sheet** (L12's third reader, L36): `alternativesFor`'s third tier becomes "shares a target skill or a measured demand", the `repertoire` tag no longer matches anything, and the tier used is printed on the sheet ("same lesson", "trains the same skill", "carries the same demand"); the swap sheet reads the same tiers and the same reasons.
7. **The four Stage-N rules** (L35) are deleted or restated as predicates over the evidence; none remains as prose the code enforces.
8. **The warm-up never takes the rung's reading row** (L65); the reading slot stays the reader's.
9. **The reruns**: `firstThirtyDays` and `firstThirtyDaysOnTheLadder` extended to the other slots for the three learners, read as a teacher: every offer in thirty days has a reason drawn from evidence or the rung, no slot repeats an item the evidence says is met while an unmet one waits, and the exposure rule shows up as the balance the reviewer asked for.
