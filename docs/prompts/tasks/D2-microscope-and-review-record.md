## The goal, in the orchestrator's words

After D0 and E0 the repository can say what every item is for and what its notes provide, and it says honestly that no person has judged any of it: 14 music families marked unheard, 38 families the app cannot judge, 467 rung claims only a person can settle, and every review bit `null`. The tools that exist for looking are four command-line scripts and a static PDMX page. After D2 a person opens one builder-only screen in the app, walks a queue of items in the order that matters (the music families first), sees each one rendered by the app's own renderer, hears it through the app's own audio, reads beside it everything the contract and the measurement say, and records a judgement in the vocabulary the record needs: usable score or not, good teaching use or not, and whether that judgement rests on inspection, on the notation, or on hearing. The record is data in the repository; the build reads it into every item's provenance and the rung-claims report shows it. Nothing in D2 judges music: it puts a person in front of it and remembers what they said.

## What is decided

1. **The microscope is a builder-only route in the app** (`#/dev/microscope`, beside `#/dev/score`; never a tab, never in the navigation, never reachable from a learner screen), because "how does it render" and "how does it sound" must mean the app's renderer (`OsmdView`, `WindowRenderer`) and the app's audio, the same argument `render_check.py` makes. It reads the built catalogue and, per item: the notation as the score screen draws it; Hear it (the app's playback of the item's model at its tempo, hands together and each hand alone); the facts — family, version, seed and role, promise and `heard`, target skills or "not judged" with the candidate, measured demands with their located counts and the contract's verdict on each (required at density, incidental, forbidden, untaught), the physical flags, the provenance facts labelled as E0 labels them, the rungs listing the item and what each claims of it (from the rung-claims report's data), and any existing review of it. Generated items first, then authored, then imported sources; the item is addressed by id in the URL so a link in an entry opens it.
2. **The review record is data in the repository**: `content/review/decisions.jsonl` (one line per decision, append-only; the builder decides the exact fields, but every line carries: the item id and its identity — `drill.generator`'s family, version and seed for a generated item, the file hash for a notated one; the two decisions of R42 kept apart — `usableScore` and `goodTeachingUse`, each `yes`, `no` or `fix` with a reason and a category; `basis`: `inspected` (the facts only), `notation` (read the page), `heard` (played back in the app), the strongest that applies; the reviewer's name as given by the person, the date, and free text). A decision binds to the identity it was made on: a family whose version moves, or a file whose hash changes, makes every earlier decision on it stale, shown as such and counted as none.
3. **How a decision gets from the screen into the record.** The screen keeps its decisions in the app's storage and exports them as a file (the PDMX review page's pattern, which needs no server); `tools/content/review.py --merge <file>` appends them to the record with the identity check above and refuses a line that names an identity the catalogue does not have. `--check` lists what is queued and undecided, music families first.
4. **The build reads the record** (`build.py`, E0's provenance step): an item's `reviewed` facts are filled from its current, non-stale decisions — the two bits, the basis, the date; a family's `heard` in `family_contracts.json` stays a hand-maintained declaration and the contract test now holds it consistent with the record (a family marked `heard: true` has at least one `heard` decision on a current item; never the reverse). The rung-claims report's "review bit" column shows the teaching-use decision and its basis. A source `keep` (the PDMX quarry) is neither bit, as E0 left it.
5. **The queue is the order Part 15 §22 gives**: the 14 music families' canonical items (unheard, and promised as music) first; then the 38 not-judged families' canonical items; then every option a rung lists whose claim the notes cannot check (the 467); then the rest. The screen shows the queue with counts and lets the person jump.
6. **Triage is a line in the same record with `by: triage`, and it never counts.** A triage line (from any reader — a script, a person skimming, a model outside this repository) can flag an item and give a reason; the screen shows flags at the top of the queue; no triage line fills a review bit, sets `heard`, or changes a report. No model is called from this repository and no key is handled; the format is the whole provision.
7. **Not D2's:** the excerpt view and the review of a cut with context outside it (E1, which extends this screen); any learner screen or word; audio files written by the pipeline (the app plays; a file per item is not needed for a person to hear, and a synth outside the app would not be the app's sound); any change to a generator or contract from what a review says (that is the family owner's seam, from the record); the PDMX quarry's own review page (it stays for the tarball; the microscope links to its decision where one exists).

## Verification layers

- **Unit** (`app/tests/unit/reviewRecord.test.ts` or the builder's name): a decision on an old version is stale and counts as none; `by: triage` fills nothing; the strongest basis wins; a malformed line is refused with its line number.
- **Build-time** (`tools/content/tests/test_review_record.py`): the provenance's `reviewed` facts equal the record's current decisions for every item, red first on a fixture with one decision; a family marked `heard: true` without a `heard` decision fails; `--merge` refuses an unknown identity; the report's review column matches.
- **The screen** (`app/tests/e2e/microscope.spec.ts`, the dev route on port 4173, two workers): opens a generated item by id, shows the notation settled (`.score-view[data-settled]`), the facts named by item 1 each present with a test hook, Hear it enabled, a decision recorded and exported as a file whose contents `--merge` accepts; the learner navigation never shows the route (a check on the shell's tabs).
- **The orchestrator's read**: the screen rendered at the owner's width on one music-family item and one PDMX item (the pictures in the record), and one real review made by the orchestrator through the screen and merged, so the loop is proven end to end once — that decision stays in the record as the first line, with its basis honest (`notation` or `heard`, whichever was true).
- Not this brief's, named: Q41 case 14's review as music (a person's, through this screen, in D's later seams); the excerpt cases (E1).
