## The goal, in the orchestrator's words

The runtime sight-reading generator is structurally honest: key, metre, hands, tempo and promises reach it; it refuses impossible asks; a seed names one phrase; nothing below a rung's teaching appears. Its phrases are still legal rhythmic cells over a constrained melodic walk, and they stop rather than arrive: the last note lands anywhere, the contour oscillates or wanders, the right hand ignores the left hand's chord, and one seed can tie a note into the next bar and then leap a fifth past the level's cap. After D1 every phrase a learner reads was chosen from its seed's valid candidates for its shape — a convincing beginning, a rhythmic arrival at the end, one contour, local repetition with variation, rests that breathe, agreement with the harmony — by a scorer whose parts are each tested on constructed phrases; the tie's closing note obeys the leap cap and ties leave the beat only where syncopation is taught; and a distribution suite over hundreds of seeds per level and per row reports and bounds what the generator actually produces, so a level cannot pass every single-phrase invariant with a terrible distribution. The phrases stay unseen and reproducible; nothing is over-composed; the promise tests stay green throughout.

## What is decided

1. **Hard constraints and soft qualities are separated in the code as they are in Part 15 §11.** What exists stays the hard layer: promises required and excluded, taught material only, range, metre, key, the physical bounds, `unrealisable`. D1 adds a scorer over valid candidates: for a seed, the attempt sequence that already exists (`attemptSeed`) yields candidates; each valid one is scored; the best is the phrase. Deterministic from the seed; the number of candidates bounded (the builder sets it with the timing reason, and H0's budget rule applies to the tests).
2. **The scorer's parts, each a pure function on a phrase with its own unit cases, each weight stated with its reason:** a beginning on the tonic or a chord tone of bar 1's harmony; an arrival — the final note on the felt beat 1 of the last bar or a note at least a beat long there, and at level 5 and above a cadential approach (a step or a chord tone of the final harmony into the tonic or the fifth); one contour (an arch, a descent or an ascent with one turn, measured on the notes' pitches; two-note oscillation beyond a bound penalised; a random walk that changes direction on most steps penalised); local repetition with variation (a motif — a rhythmic cell or an interval shape — that recurs at least once, transformed, at levels 2 and above; exact repetition of a bar penalised beyond one); rest placement (a rest at a phrase boundary rewarded, a rest breaking a beam group or the arrival penalised); harmonic agreement where the left hand plays a chord (right-hand notes on strong beats are chord tones or steps between them; the existing `snapToChordTone` becomes a scored quality at 5–7 rather than a rewrite); leaps counted against the level's cap as a soft cost below it. No part uses a golden template; `A A' B A''` is one form the motif part can recognise, never the form it requires.
3. **S26 as a hard constraint:** the tie's closing note stays within the level's leap cap, and below 4.5 a tie starts on the beat only. Red first with seed 71282's reproduction from the row (A4 tied into bar 4 then E4).
4. **Identity moves with the music.** A reading row's phrase for a seed changes when the scorer lands, so the runtime drill's identity carries a generator version (where `fromCatalog.ts` builds it; the builder finds the right field, in the shape of D0's `drill.generator`), bumped in this change, so the learner's history never treats an old seed's phrase as the same encounter as the new one. Every promise and absence test still passes on the new phrases.
5. **The distribution suite** (`app/tests/unit/sightReadingDistribution.test.ts`): over a deterministic seed set per level and per shipped row (the nine), report and bound — key and metre as asked; interval classes; rhythmic-event, rest-density and repeated-note rates; contour diversity (the share of phrases whose contour is one arch, descent or ascent; the share oscillating); phrase-ending (the share arriving on the felt beat 1 or on a long note); opportunity density of each promised demand; accidentals; range; left-hand pattern mix; the rejection rate (attempts per accepted phrase); the candidate-score distribution (median, worst); near-duplicates (two seeds naming the same notes). The bounds are the builder's hypotheses with their reasons, set from the reported distribution and never so loose that the committed generator passes them (that is the red line: the suite red on the committed generator for phrase-ending and contour, green after). The table of every measure per level goes in the entry. The suite runs in CI with a local budget and a reason at the file (H0's rule; never a global timeout).
6. **The reader's read.** The builder renders three phrases per level (from the built rows, on the score screen, in a worktree preview or from the written MusicXML) and reads them as a teacher: which arrive, which still stop, what a first reader would trip on; the orchestrator renders three more on the merged checkout. Hearing stays unverified until D2's microscope lets a person hear them; the entry says "unverified as music" in those words.
7. **Not D1's:** the Python families' musical gate (a hook until D3); the study generator (D3); new rhythmic cells or levels; any change to what a rung asks of a row (`curriculum.json`); the lesson text about reading; the evidence a read earns.
