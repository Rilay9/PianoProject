## The goal, in the orchestrator's words

The app knows what a learner has met (G1) and what the evidence measured (the ladder). It does not know what the learner is doing with a piece: a song saved for later, a piece being learned, one being polished for a recital, one kept playable, one learned two years ago and brought back, one paused, one put away. Every surface guesses from `ProgressRow.status` — four words that C5 took out of rung truth and that say nothing of intent — so a piece learned long ago reads as new or as merely due, "learn this" has no home, and Stage 9's projects print as rungs with runs to count. R19's lifecycle is the missing layer: a state the learner sets, informed by history, never derived from one run, never a substitute for history or evidence.

## What is decided

1. **The store.** A `projects` object store (`DB_VERSION` 9; an upgrade that creates it and touches no other store; the fixture-database test of G1's kind) with one row per project: `{ id, material: Identity | { kind: 'id', itemId }, itemId, state, since, history: [{ state, at, why? }], goal?, problem?, sections? }`. The material is D4's identity where the item has one (`materialOfItem`; an import's stored bytes since G1); the id-only form where it has none, as G1 does. `since` is when the current state was entered; `history` every state the project has been in, appended and never rewritten.
2. **The states and their entries** (Part 27's list; exploring is the absence of a row): `saved` ("Save for later"), `learning` ("Learn this"), `polishing` ("Prepare it for performance"), `performance-ready` ("It is ready"), `maintaining` ("I performed it", with the date, or "Keep it playable"), `refreshing` ("Bring it back", from `paused`, `retired` or `maintaining`), `paused` ("Pause"), `retired` ("Put it away"). Every transition is the learner's action, from any state to any state the sheet offers for it; the app proposes nothing and moves nothing. A project can be created in any of the first three states before any run ("learn this piece" before success is one of the adversaries); one play creates no project; retiring or pausing deletes no encounter, evidence or history row; bringing back a retired piece keeps its whole history and every encounter (R47: relearning is a state entered while history truthfully says the material is familiar).
3. **Never the bridge, never the source.** `ProgressRow.status`, `learnedPieces`, the review's repertoire retention, Today's *A piece you know* and every evidence reader stay exactly as they are; a project's state is read by no evidence, skill or eligibility code. The encounter history is read here (`familiarity`, for the sheet's line "you last played it on …" or "you have never opened it") and never written. The orchestrator's hypothesis: the lifecycle can live beside `ProgressRow.status` with no consumer needing both. Its refuting test: a reader (the repertoire row, retention, `learnedPieces`, the Library's status filter) that must read the project state to stay truthful. If you find one, stop that reader and say which sentence it would print wrongly; do not fold the state into `ProgressRow`.
4. **R18's three facts** on the row, typed by the learner in the sheet, each optional and free text: `goal` (this week's goal), `problem` (the current problem), `sections` (bars from–to with a label, added one at a time; the piece's bar count bounds them). Nothing chooses from them yet — the session's use is X's.
5. **The sheet.** One module, `app/src/ui/projectSheet.ts`: the piece's title, its state with `since`, the history's last line, the encounter line from `familiarity`, the actions this state offers, the three R18 fields. Opened from the Score screen's finish sheet ("What next with this piece?") and from a project's row on Progress. The Library's row and filter gain the state after X3 lands (a follow-on row, not this seam).
6. **Progress** lists the projects under one heading, newest change first: title, state, since, the goal where one is typed; a passed or mastered piece with no project offers "Make it a project" there (the learner's action, never automatic). Nothing else on Progress changes.
7. **Stage 9 (L86).** The lesson page of a Stage 9 unit shows its song options as projects — each with its state, or "not started" — and never a requirement met or unmet: the unit's `requirements` rows in `stage-9.json` are left as data (F2a's file; L86's data half is a later F row) and the page reads the projects store instead of counting runs for units whose stage is 9. The sentence on the page: "A project: there is no rung to pass here." The rung state's reading of those requirements is unchanged (nothing else reads Stage 9 as a rung to pass; confirm at `rungState.ts` and `session.ts`, and say so).
8. **Backup and reset.** The backup round trip carries the store; *Reset progress* clears it; a row from before G1b is nothing — no project is manufactured from a passed piece.
9. **The adversaries, as tests:** save without opening (a project, no encounter); one play of an excerpt (no project); "learn this piece" before any success (a project in `learning`, no evidence); pausing (the project's history grows, no encounter or session row goes); retired two years and returned (`refreshing`, `familiarity` still says played, `learnedPieces` unchanged); a project on an import (the stored bytes' identity); the same piece under two ids (one project, by material).
10. **Not G1b's:** session composition from projects and their sections (X); the re-entry diagnostic after a long break (I21); Stage 9's data (`stage-9.json`, F); the Library's row (after X3); Today's repertoire block; the transfer policy (G2); any write to the encounter history; any change to `ProgressRow`; the run header (G1a).

## Approved 2026-09-29 (`responses/a96395d.md`, the second section of that file; read it whole before building)

The reviewer's rulings are part of this brief:

1. **Every lifecycle transition is learner-driven.** No app-driven drift: `maintaining` never becomes `paused` by elapsed time; `learning`, `polishing`, `refreshing` and `retired` are never inferred from runs or history. The app may later *suggest* an action from history; the stored lifecycle changes only when the learner chooses. `I performed it` → `maintaining` is a learner action; a date or reason it records is a learner-stated project fact and must not manufacture a `performed` encounter, a performance run, a competence result or an evidence event — **pinned by a test.**
2. **Stage 9 / L86.** The Stage 9 learner-facing page reads project lifecycle instead of counting runs; the Stage 9 curriculum data stays F's. `rungState` and session progression never treat project states as requirements. Acceptance proves: the page shows project state or *not started*; no "x of y met" or rung-completion claim there; changing a project state changes only that project's presentation; the same action changes no rung state, evidence, eligibility or skill state. If the page cannot be made truthful without changing `rungState`, stop that subpart.
3. **The finish sheet is the first door**; the Library integration is not held for; Progress is the second door. After X3 the Library may expose the same project state and open the same sheet, consuming the one `projectStore` truth.
4. **Constraints.** Identity migration fails conservatively (a project keyed by known material identity may unify renamed or duplicate catalogue ids; an id-only project is never guessed to be another piece later). Retire and pause never erase facts. Refreshing is not novelty (an old piece may enter `refreshing` while encounter history still says familiar). Project state is not session policy yet (goal, problem and sections choose nothing in G1b). Stage 9 presentation never mutates the rung or evidence model to suppress counts.
5. **Sequencing.** G1a and X3 closed, so G1b dispatches now; its post-build review is required before X or session composition consumes project lifecycle as an input; no unrelated lane waits on it.

**The invariant, in the reviewer's words:** encounter history says what was met; evidence says what was measured; skill and rung state say what the evidence supports; project lifecycle says what the learner intends to do with the music; session state says today's plan.
