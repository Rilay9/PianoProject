## The goal, in the orchestrator's words

The ladder's v0 says a skill transferred when a supporting run came on a different item id at first contact. That is neither necessary nor sufficient: a new seed of the same generator is a different id and not transfer; a neighbouring excerpt with the same pattern is not transfer; a learner proficient in C position who fails an unfamiliar excerpt with big leaps is not less proficient. D4 wrote the facts — the material's identity, the relationship to what established the skill as categorical differences, declared and measured — and G1 wrote what the learner met. Nothing reads them yet. G2 is the reader: one policy, skill-relative, that promotion and challenge protection both consume, that credits only the dimensions observed, and that says "unknown" for a row whose context cannot be reconstructed.

**The orchestrator's premise, tested before building:** D4's relationship facts, G1's contact and the skill's dimensions decide all fourteen adversaries with no new fact. Its refuting test: an adversary the three facts cannot tell apart — the likely one is "a substantially different section of the same composition", which needs G1's composition facet beside the material identity (entry-112 P2 says the query carries it). Write the fourteen as cases against a stub policy first; any case the facts cannot decide is a finding before a line of the policy is written.

## What is decided

1. **The module.** `app/src/evidence/transferPolicy.ts`: `transferReading(skill, established, run)` where `established` is the skill's establishing contexts (D4's `shownOnRecords`: material references, an unknown historical reference where the evidence is a legacy row), and `run` is the evidence context of one supporting full-standard record (its `material`, `intent`, `relationship`, `firstContact` since G1a, and the measured demands). It returns `{ verdict: 'demonstrated' | 'not-transfer' | 'unknown'; on: Dimension[]; why: string }`. Never a scalar distance. `unknown` where the run has no `relationship` (a legacy row, or D4's race rows, G68), where every establishing reference is unknown, or where `firstContact` is absent; `unknown` credits nothing.
2. **The three facts, in order.** *Contact:* `firstContact` false is `not-transfer` (first contact is a condition of a transfer demonstration, Part 26's "no prior contact that invalidates first contact"); heard-only and pruned contact count as contact through G1's `contact` (the summaries), never through a run scan alone. *Relationship:* the run's `relationship.differsOn` says which dimensions differ; the declared and the measured facts of a dimension are read separately, and a dimension counts as differing for evidence only where the measured fact says so (the reviewer's constraint); where measured and declared disagree, the reading records the disagreement in `why` and credits nothing on that dimension. *The claim:* the skill's own dimensions — those on which a change is transfer for this skill — from the skill's vocabulary entry (`transfer.dimensions`, item 5); `demonstrated` when at least one of the skill's dimensions differs by the measured fact and the run supported the skill at full standard; `on` is the intersection of the differing dimensions and the skill's.
3. **Two consumers, one policy.** *Promotion* (`ladderState`): `transfer` becomes true on a supporting full-standard record while proficient only where the policy says `demonstrated`; v0's `firstContact && !shownOn.has(itemId)` goes, and the module note says so. *Challenge protection* (`countsTowardsMovingDown`): a failure does not count against the skill where the run was first contact on material whose relationship differs on any of the skill's dimensions, or whose measured demands exceed the skill's (an appropriately harder attempt); thresholds may differ from promotion's, the facts are the same module's. A test holds that every constructed history the D4 test built for "D4 changes no ladder state" now changes only where the policy says `demonstrated`.
4. **The scope beneath the summary.** The ladder's reading gains `transferScope: { on: Dimension[]; since: string }[]` — where generalisation was shown, accumulated from `demonstrated` readings, never collapsed to one flag beyond the summary's `transfer`; read by no screen in G2 (the teacher model's; a later U row names the copy). One hard success credits its dimensions and nothing else.
5. **The skill's dimensions, as data.** Each skill in the vocabulary (`content/curriculum/vocabulary/`) may carry `transfer: { dimensions: Dimension[]; why: string }` — position reading: key and position; rhythm skills: rhythm and texture; hands-together skills: hands and texture; and so on, one line of reason each, filled by the builder from each skill's definition and checked by the validator (every dimension named exists in `transfer.ts`'s `DIMENSIONS`). A skill without the block reads `unknown` — no transfer credit until the data says which changes matter — and the build's report lists those skills, so nothing is credited by a default. The reviewer decides whether that conservatism is right (question 1).
6. **The offer's contact through the one adapter.** `transferOffer` reads contact from the session input's contact summaries (G1's `contact`, encounters and pruned-run summaries included) through one function, so a piece heard once or practised and pruned is `met` and never offered as new; `contactIn` over rows alone is not enough and is not used there. The session input's contact field and its test contract are G2's; X1 consumes them and computes no second novelty (the reviewer's seam). The offer's other rules are unchanged (D4 item 4; G64 and G69 stay recorded).
7. **The adversaries, as tests** (Part 26's fourteen): a new seed of one family (not transfer); a family's legitimately changed context by contract (may qualify, on the dimensions changed); a `role: transfer` item failing the target-opportunity gate (no evidence; the gate's, held); a neighbouring excerpt with the same pattern (not transfer); a different section of the same composition (recognised, not the same piece); a different arrangement (related); a duplicate or imported copy (no regained first contact, through G1); heard but never played (no first contact); an unfamiliar excerpt with appropriate demands at full standard (demonstrated, on its dimensions); an unfamiliar excerpt with untaught demands failed badly (no demotion); familiar material after 21 days (retention, not transfer); right-hand transfer not establishing hands-together (`on` excludes hands); a generator version change (a different identity, judged on the dimensions); historical evidence without provenance (kept, transfer unknown).
8. **Not G2's:** the offer's ranking and the repeated unplayed offer (G64, E2's or X's); the snapshot's material match (G69); the session's contact-assumption snapshot and recheck (L32, X); any screen copy (the Skills and Progress screens keep C7's words; a U row for the scope's words later); retention's rule; the lifecycle (G1b); the evidence job's shape; the run header (G1a).

## The fact path (the reviewer's required change, `responses/a96395d.md`, applied)

The ladder replays `Evidence[]` and nothing else; every fact the policy reads is on the attempt itself, and no helper the ladder calls may call the ladder back.

1. **Each attempt carries its own facts.** `EvidenceContext` gains `relationship?: Relationship` and `demands?: string[]` beside `material`, `intent` and `firstContact` (G1a's field). They are written once, at `recordRun`, for every run that has a material: the relationship by the same pure function the offer uses (`relationshipOf`, against the skill's establishing contexts as they stand at that moment, item 2), the demands as the run's measured list on the row; on a transfer-offer run the stored `RunHeader.relationship` is that same computation made at the offer and is kept as written. Absent means unknown — a legacy row, a D4 race row (G68), a row from before G2 — and unknown is never reconstructed from a current catalogue item, never guessed from `itemId`, and never filled by a later reader. `EvidenceContext.firstContact` keeps its name (the G1a review, `responses/5b14b7a.md`: one name for one fact) and, when G2 extends the evidence beyond phrase observations, is populated from the run header's `firstContact` — never from `unseen`; where the header's field is absent the context's stays absent, never rebuilt from `unseen`, the item's identity or the current encounter history; existing phrase evidence is compatible because its two values were equal when written. Every general-contact read in G2 reads `firstContact` directly and accepts `undefined` on rows from before G1a, without `isPhraseRun`.
2. **The establishing contexts come from the replay.** As `ladderState` replays, each supporting full-standard record before proficiency adds its `MaterialReference` (the record's `itemId` and `material`, unknown where the material is absent) to the established list; the reading exposes `established: MaterialReference[]`. `transfer.ts`'s `establishing`/`shownOnRecords`, which today call `ladderState` per row to find those records, read the ladder's `established` from one call instead; the ladder itself calls neither `relationshipOf` nor anything in `transfer.ts`.
3. **A non-offer supporting run's relationship** is therefore the one written at its `recordRun` (item 1) — measured then, against the contexts established then, from the stored rows — and the policy reads `measured[d].differs === true` per dimension; `differsOn` is not read; `declared` is read only to record a disagreement in `why`, never as evidence.
4. **One path, two thresholds.** Promotion and challenge protection both call `transferReading` over the attempt's own facts and the replay's `established`. Promotion: `demonstrated`. Protection: a failed full-standard attempt is not counted against the skill where `firstContact` is true and either a skill dimension measurably differs or the attempt's `demands` carry a demand no establishing record carried; where any of those facts is unknown the attempt counts as it does today — unknown facts are never guessed into a protection verdict. The unrelated retention rule stays.
5. **Five tests, red first:** an ordinary new-context success (a non-offer run on a piece in another key at full standard → `demonstrated` on `key` for a skill whose dimensions include it); a transfer-offer success (→ `demonstrated` on the offer's measured dimensions, `differsOn` ignored); a legacy row with no relationship (→ `unknown`, no credit, no protection); two failed unfamiliar attempts protected (first contact with a measurably different skill dimension; first contact with a demand the establishing records lacked), and one failed attempt with unknown facts not protected — each on constructed evidence with no helper call from inside the ladder (a test spies `ladderState`'s call count: one).
