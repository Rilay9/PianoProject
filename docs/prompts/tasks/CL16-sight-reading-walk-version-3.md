## Premises at the lines (HEAD `889afb94`)

1. **S34 — the walk draws a leap uniformly up to the level's cap, at every level, every time.** `sightReading.ts`:872: `const leap = Math.round((rng() * 2 - 1) * spec.maxLeap);` — a leap magnitude is drawn from a flat distribution over `[-maxLeap, maxLeap]`, so a leap of the full cap is exactly as likely as a step of one. This is unconditional: nothing here favours a smaller interval. Levels 5–7's harmony snap (`chordToneWithin`, :572–595, called from :823–824 only on strong beats, only where `spec.chordTones` is set) narrows the position *after* a leap is drawn, but does not change how the leap itself is drawn. `sightReadingDistribution.test.ts`'s own bounds (:468–469) already name the consequence: `unitsOne` (the share of four-bar units reading as one contour) is bounded at 0.18 for level 6 and 0.10 for level 7 — "modest, and say so" (:436–439): "the walk, not the candidate count, is the ceiling at 6–7 (three times the candidates moved level 7's own from 13% to 14% and level 6's from 22% to 26%)". Scoring harder does not move this; only the draw itself can.
2. **S35a — the generator's own rest draw is excluded from only the last note of the last bar, not from the positions the scorer itself calls "bad."** `sightReading.ts`:792–797: `isRest` is true (12% of the time) wherever `spec.allowRests`, there is already a note, the cell is not the syncopation-opening rest, and `!(isLastBar && i === barRhythm.length - 1)` — i.e. the *only* position excluded is the very last note slot of the very last bar. `sightReadingScore.ts`:581–601 (`restFacts`) separately names three categories of "bad" rest placement that the generator can still produce: `inLastBar` (any rest anywhere in the final bar, not just its last slot), `afterRest` (a rest immediately following another rest), and `breaksBeam` (a rest that splits the short notes of one beat). `rests()` (:610–613) scores these at −0.35 each inside a 0–1 part worth weight 1 of roughly 13.5 total (`WEIGHTS`, :698–706: `arrival` 5, `contour` 3, `beginning`/`rests` 1 each, `motif`/`harmony` 1.5 each, `leaps` 0.5) — a soft preference the scorer can and does override when the rest of a candidate's shape is strong enough, which is the generator's own stated mechanism (`chooseCandidate` keeps the earliest candidate within 0.05 of the best, never the one cleanest on any single part). The backlog's own example (`backlog-2026-09-25.md`:474: "a bar of G 𝄾 A B 𝄾 C a first reader trips on") is exactly a `breaksBeam` case the soft weight did not reject.
3. **S35b — no rhythm palette at levels 2–7 can write a bar-long note, so the phrase's last downbeat can never be an arrival-by-holding.** `sightReading.ts`'s `LEVELS` table: level 1 (:357) is the only level whose `rhythms` array includes `DIVISIONS * 4` (a whole note in simple 4/4); levels 2 (:370), 3 (:383), 4 (:396), 5 (:411), 6 (:425) and 7 (:440) all cap at `DIVISIONS * 2` (a half note). `pickRhythmPlaced` (:677–730, levels 1–4) and `pickRhythm` (:608–637, levels 5–7) both draw only from `spec.rhythms` (plus triplets/syncopation cells that are shorter, never longer). `arrives`/`arrivesOnStrongBeat` (`sightReadingScore.ts`:286, :304 — not read in full here, a claim to verify at those lines) score a phrase's ending; the backlog's own finding (`backlog-2026-09-25.md`:474) is "a quarter on beat 4 as the arrival at 9 of 21 read phrases is a weak ending — the last downbeat is unreachable at levels 2–7 in 4/4 because no bar-long note is in their palettes (a new rhythmic cell, out of D1's scope)."
4. **S35c — level 1's near-duplicate bound is a named, accepted cost, not an unmeasured gap; levels 2–7 already carry a tight one.** `sightReadingDistribution.test.ts`:462–479 (`BOUNDS`): every level and row already has a `nearDuplicatesAtMost` entry — 0.2 (20%) for level 1 and its two aliased rows (`sight-reading-1`, `sight-reading-1-left`), 0.02 (2%) for everything else. The comment at :453–457 states the level-1 number's origin directly: "two seeds writing the same page... version 1 repeats a page in 7% (13% on the left-hand row), version 2 in 17% and 13% — the cost of shaping so small a space, bounded so it cannot grow, and named in Entry 94." **Claim to verify, not yet checked by me:** whether a generator-local mechanism exists to lower level 1's actual duplicate rate without either (a) widening level 1's deliberately minimal five-note, three-length design (`rhKey: {low: 60, high: 67}`, :355; `rhythms: [DIVISIONS, DIVISIONS*2, DIVISIONS*4]`, :357) — which is a pedagogical choice about the very first reading exercise, not a bug — or (b) carrying state across independent `generateSightReading` calls (seeds are independent draws by design; a "two seeds wrote the same page" measurement is a population statistic over many independent calls, not a defect a single call can see). See Hypothesis 3 below.
5. **G37 — the alberti pattern's register sits roughly an octave below the exercise that first teaches it.** `sightReading.ts`:408–419 (level 5): `lhKey: { low: 36, high: 60 }` — 36 is MIDI C2 (the backlog's own gloss, `backlog-2026-09-25.md`:228, confirmed against this file's own convention where 60 = C4, the level-1 `rhKey.low`, :355). `buildLeftHand` (:924–968) builds its scale from exactly this `lhKey` range (:935) and roots the pattern at `tonicIndexIn(scale, fifths)` (:947) — the bottom of that range, for a level whose only `leftHand` setting is `'alberti'` (:417). The matching taught exercise, `make_accompaniment` (`tools/content/generate_exercises.py`:2169–2213, the alberti pattern named at :2160), writes its left-hand tones via `scale_pitches(k, 3, degrees)` (:2186) — octave 3, not octave 2 — at `level: float = 3.6` (:2170), which is G37's own rung. **Claim to verify, not yet checked by me:** the exact MIDI value `scale_pitches`'s octave argument "3" resolves to in this codebase's Python-side convention (I have not traced `scale_pitches`'s implementation), so the roughly-one-octave gap is read from the two call sites' octave/MIDI labels, not from a measured semitone difference.
6. **G37 — a "broken" or "walking" left-hand pattern's unit is a plain quarter regardless of the phrase's metre, including 6/8.** `LEFT_HAND_PATTERNS` (`sightReading.ts`:912–922): `alberti: { steps: [0,4,2,4], unit: 0.5 }`, `broken: { steps: [0,4,2,4], unit: 1 }`, `walking: { steps: [0,2,4,5], unit: 1 }` — `unit` is a fixed quarter-note count, read by `buildLeftHand`'s `step = DIVISIONS * pattern.unit` and `count = Math.max(1, Math.round(divisionsPerBar / step))` (:950–951), with no branch on `isCompoundTime(timeSig)` (the helper already exists, :658–660, used elsewhere for the right hand's rhythm, e.g. `pickRhythmPlaced`:685–696). In 6/8 the felt beat is a dotted quarter (`feltBeat`, :663–665), so a `broken`/`walking` pattern drawn in plain quarters crosses the compound beat rather than sitting on it.

## Constraints taken as given (each with its source and what it protects; the costliest has an alternative measured)

1. **The phrase is a random walk with a harmony snap**, shaped after the fact (`sightReading.ts`, the walk and `chordToneWithin`; D1/D1a's design). It protects the existing levels' validated shape and the version mechanism's guarantees. This is the costliest constraint: Hypothesis 1's refuting test measures whether biasing the draw is enough, and if `unitsOne` does not move at levels 6–7 the lane reports that the alternative, a walk that aims at a contour (`backlog-2026-09-25.md`:473), is the real fix, and stops rather than tuning inside the walk.
2. **A phrase is deterministic from its seed alone** (`sightReading.ts`:141–143). It protects every reader that regenerates a phrase from a stored seed (the session record, evidence, goldens). Not relaxed here: cross-call memory would break it, so S35c stops instead of adding state.
3. **Level 1 is five notes and three lengths** (docs/05, the very first reading exercise's design). It protects the first reading step's difficulty. Not relaxed here: S35c's duplicate rate is reported against that space's combinatorial floor, not fixed by widening it.
4. **One generator version per behaviour change, old versions byte-identical** (the v1/v2 precedent). It protects history's comparability. Kept.

## The real-music-first check (operating-procedure.md §12, "Real music before generated music")

This lane keeps generated material for a musical skill (unfamiliar-phrase sight-reading at seven graded levels), so the rule requires stating whether a score-verified, legally usable real excerpt teaches the same thing at these levels, and why generation wins if kept.

**It does not.** Sight-reading's defining pedagogical property is that the material is *unseen* at the moment of reading — a learner who has read a passage once, however briefly, is no longer sight-reading it. A fixed real excerpt, however well chosen, is exhausted after one or two sessions in a way a repertoire piece used for phrasing or style is not; this cluster's whole mechanism (one seed → one phrase, hundreds of seeds measured for their *distribution* of contour, arrival, rest placement and density, never one golden template, `sightReadingDistribution.test.ts`:1–13) exists because the skill needs graduated variation and exact, swept control over the parameters that make a page hard or easy (interval width, metre, key, rest placement, left-hand texture) — precisely the two conditions §12 names as generation's own ground ("exact constraints, graduated variation, transfer testing, availability/licensing, or the learner's measured need make control more valuable"). A real corpus could supply the *style* a learner eventually reads at sight (which this cluster does not touch — that is CL18's repertoire-supply question, not CL16's), but it cannot supply an inexhaustible, difficulty-swept, never-before-seen page on demand, which is the one thing this engine is for. Generation wins here on the rule's own terms; nothing in S34/S35/G37 adds a new generated *family* — it reshapes the existing one.
