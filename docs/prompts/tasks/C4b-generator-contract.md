## The goal, in the orchestrator's words

The diary's learner read cleanly for ten days on 2.5 and was told six times that the next step waits for a later lesson, because the rung's next rhythms (ties, dotted quarters) are taught at 2.4 and the generator only writes them at higher levels. The reviewer's rule: for every reading progression the curriculum can request, the generator must realise the requested next demand at that point while holding the unrelated dimensions stable, and where it cannot, it must say so explicitly, never hand back different material or a plateau. This is a contract between the taught-at table and the generator, proven by a test, not a fix for 2.5.

## What is decided

1. **Every reading demand has a control, and the map lives in app code, not in the vocabulary** (Part 8, the fourth message §4: the vocabulary is the ontology of musical demands; the generator's options are today's controls, which D may unbundle without redesigning the evidence). Each entry in `demands.json` gains only `dimension` (the musical dimension it belongs to: `interval`, `rhythm`, `hands`, `range`, `key`, `metre`, `texture`, `accidental` — a small closed list in the schema). A new module `app/src/engine/readingControls.ts` maps each demand id to the `SightReadingOptions` field and value that turn it on, or to `null` with a reason, and declares `unrealisableAt` (rung × demand the generator cannot honour within the promises, with the reason); a test asserts every reading demand in the vocabulary has an entry. New options where a taught demand has none: `ties`, `dottedQuarters`, `ledger` (range that reaches the ledger lines beyond middle C), `leftHand` (the pattern: `whole`, `chord`, `alberti`, `broken`, `walking`), `sixteenths`. With every option absent, the generator behaves exactly as today: the goldens and the nine rows' phrases are unchanged (assert it).
2. **The contract test** (vitest, over the real generator and the real detectors): for every core rung that offers a reading row, and every demand whose `taughtAt` is that rung or earlier in the curriculum's order, asking the rung's row recipe plus that demand's option yields, over a run of seeds, phrases in which the detector finds the demand present and the rung's promises still hold (reuse the promises test's bounds), and with the option off the demand is absent unless the row always carries it. Where the combination is impossible (the promises forbid it, or the level's grid cannot hold it), the generator says so through a new pure `unrealisable(options): string[]` (the reasons), and the test asserts that the set of impossible combinations is exactly `unrealisableAt` in `readingControls.ts` — never silent, never different material. The contract is the reviewer's chain in full: taught → selectable → generatable → the detector confirms the requested demand → demands taught later than the rung are absent (reuse the promises test's `unintended()`).
3. **The four rows the diary and C2 caught.** S25: ties and dotted quarters realisable at 2.4's level and from the reading rows 2.5 offers. S22: the row 3.4 offers writes ledger notes when asked (`ledger`). S16: on 2.2 the right-hand row stays in C position (the row's recipe says `position: false`; the move to `position: true` is 2.5's, and the contract test proves both). S23: no rung teaches reading sixteenths and row 7 writes them; under "never teach wrong" a demand no rung teaches is not written by a row a rung offers — decide whether row 7 drops them until F adds the teaching or whether a rung already implies it, and say which and why.
4. **Keys are not ordered.** `key.signature`'s opportunity is any key with a signature; the generator's `fifths` stays a set the seed chooses from; nothing in this task ranks keys. C4c writes the words.
5. **The promises test** keeps every assertion and gains the contract cases; the `KNOWN_EARLY` list shrinks where S16's fix removes the early demand.
