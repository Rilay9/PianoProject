## The goal, in the orchestrator's words

In F♯ major the seventh degree is E♯, and a learner reading a ii-V-I under six sharps sees F natural where the chord has E♯: the written pitch and the scale degree are wrong even though the key sounds right. The cause is one shared policy: `UNWRITTEN` names four white-key spellings and `_readable` simplifies every one of them enharmonically in every key, which is right for a tritone substitute in B♭ (nobody writes C♭7 there) and wrong for a note that is the key's own. After this task the policy keeps a chord tone's letter spelling whenever that spelling is the key's own or the chord's own, simplifies only what is genuinely unreadable (double accidentals, and the borrowed chord whose interval spelling lands on an unwritten root), and a generated-score regression proves it on the F♯ major families with a mutation that would catch F natural coming back. Domain-complete: the mechanism and every score it emits, never a patch in one family.

## What is decided

1. **The rule lives in `_readable` and `up` (one mechanism), and takes the key or the chord into account.** A pitch whose letter spelling is diatonic to the item's key (E♯ in F♯ major, C♭ in G♭ major, B♯ in C♯ major if any item is in it) is written as it is. A chord tone whose spelling follows from the chord's own root and quality by interval (F♭ as the seventh of G♭7; E♯ as the third of C♯7 in F♯ major) is written as it is. A double accidental is still simplified. A *borrowed* chord — one not built on a degree of the item's key — whose interval spelling puts its root on one of the four unwritten spellings is simplified as a whole (the tritone substitute in B♭ stays B7, not C♭7), which is the case the comment on `UNWRITTEN` was written for. State the rule in the docstring in these terms, with the two examples that go each way.
2. **Every emitter goes through the corrected policy.** Find every caller of `up` and `_readable` and every maker that spells chord tones another way (`make_five_finger` transposes by interval directly because of this fault; leave it unless unifying it is trivially safe and you show the diff is empty). Say per maker which path it takes.
3. **A generated-score regression, red first.** In `tools/content/tests/` (the D0 files' home; a new file or `test_measured_demands.py`'s neighbour, your call): build the F♯ major harmony items (the ii-V-I trio, the four-chord loops, the seventh voicings, any cadence) and read the written pitches from the built files — every E♯ present as E♯, no F natural under six sharps; the same for C♭ in the G♭ major items; a control that the tritone substitute in B♭ is still written B, and that no double accidental survives. A mutation (the committed `_readable` restored) turns the F♯ case red with the message naming the item, the bar and the pitch. Red captures kept.
4. **Identity moves with the music.** Every family whose notes change gets its `version` moved in `family_contracts.json` and its pin rewritten in `identity_pins.json`; the note-for-note diff of every changed item (before and after, as D0's `changed-music.txt`) goes beside the entry, and the entry's table says which families and how many items. No family's notes change without its version moving; no version moves without a note changing.
5. **The bridge fixture** is re-read by hand and rewritten only if one of its ten items' notes changed, with the reason in the entry; otherwise untouched and green.
