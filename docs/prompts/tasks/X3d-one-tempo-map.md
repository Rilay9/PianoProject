## The goal, in the reviewer's words

X3c's own probe establishes a live learner-facing contradiction: a half-note mark at 60 with `<sound tempo="120">` is truthfully shown on the sheet as 120 quarter notes per minute, but the Score model opens at 60; a dotted-quarter mark at 60 with `<sound tempo="90">` opens at 60; a later quarter-note mark can replace an explicit opening `<sound tempo>`; after the learner states 100 or 72 through E48, marked files can still play at 50 or 132. The tempo correction control does not reliably control the music, and the app can teach or practise at a tempo different from the one it states. The same defect affects bundled scores (*Row, Row, Row Your Boat*, dotted quarter = 54 with `<sound tempo="81">`, opens at 54; the easy *Canon in D*, half note = 50 with `<sound tempo="100">`, opens at 50; 41 bundled scores open with a mark that is not an undotted quarter, by X3c's count). Fix the canonical score-model tempo path: normalise metronome `beat-unit` and dots to quarter-note BPM, honour event position, define precedence with `<sound tempo>`, so the Score label, the playback clock, the count-in, measurement, the import sheet and the E48 statement consume one tempo map. Do not patch only imported XML; do not reproduce OSMD's misreading in the sheet.

The mechanism X3c read: OSMD turns a `<metronome>` into a tempo expression whose tempo is its `<per-minute>` with the `<beat-unit>` ignored, lets it overwrite the `<sound tempo>` in the same direction, makes the first tempo expression anywhere the start tempo, and reads a bar-level `<sound tempo>` only in the first bar. `extractScoreModel.ts` takes its tempo map from the iterator's `CurrentBpm`, so every consumer inherits that reading. The orchestrator's hypothesis is X3c's, and its refuting test is the harness: if the model built from the half-note file reads 120 already, the mechanism is elsewhere and this brief stops at that finding.

## What is decided

1. **The file is the source, through one reader.** A pure module under `app/src/score/` (a name you choose, say `tempoFromXml.ts`) reads a MusicXML string's tempo events in score order with their positions: each `<direction>`'s `<metronome>` (beat unit, beat-unit dots, per-minute; a "note = note" metric modulation or a mark with no per-minute is not a tempo and is skipped, said in the entry) and each `<sound tempo>` (a direction's, or a measure's own), at its measure and its offset within it. It normalises a mark to quarter notes a minute (per-minute × the beat unit's length in quarters; one dot × 1.5, two × 1.75). **Precedence:** where a mark and a `<sound tempo>` stand in the same direction, the `<sound tempo>` is the sounding fact and wins (MuseScore writes both and they agree when normalised; a disagreement is the file's, and the sound is what the file says it plays); a mark alone gives its normalised value; a sound alone gives its value; a file with no event opens at the model's default as today. The reader also answers the opening tempo: the first event at the first measure's start, else the default until the first event, which is then a tempo change at its own position (an opening `<sound tempo="100">` is never displaced by a mark that appears only in bar 2; that mark is a change at bar 2).
2. **The model's tempo map comes from that reader, placed on the unrolled timeline.** `extractScoreModel.ts` stops taking `CurrentBpm` from the iterator for the map and instead places each tempo event at the unrolled onset of its measure plus its offset, every time the walk enters that measure (a repeat plays the change again where the file has it); `TempoMapEntry` keeps `{ atBeat, bpm }` in quarter notes a minute so every consumer's contract is unchanged, and the entry says whether `bpmAt` needed any change (it should not). If placing an event within a measure by offset is ambiguous for a shape, place it at the measure's start and say so.
3. **Every consumer reads the map and nothing else.** `ScoreScreen`'s `writtenBpm`, `bpmNow` and the count-in (confirm the count-in clicks at `bpmNow`; if it reads anything else, make it read that), `evidence.ts`'s and `difficulty.ts`'s first-entry reads, and the dev screens' `writtenBpm` need no change if they already read the map; say so per consumer. **The import sheet consumes the same reader:** `importSheet.ts`'s private `openingTempo` and `writesTempo` are replaced by the shared module's opening-tempo and writes-tempo answers, so the sheet's X3c words and the player agree by construction; the words themselves stay as accepted (the printed mark's own beat unit beside the quarter-note number; a text-only mark said as the app's reading; a later change never the opening; the fractional policy: a file value kept to three meaningful places).
4. **E48's statement reaches the player.** `withOpeningTempo` writes a `<sound tempo>` beside each mark's direction and at the opening; with the reader's precedence the stated number wins on every marked shape. No change to the store's writer unless a probed shape shows its `<sound tempo>` is not where the reader looks; then the change is in the reader's positions, not in what the store writes, and the entry says which.
5. **The six adversaries, as the reviewer set them, each red first and then green through the real extraction** (X3c's harness pattern, `scripts-probe-player-tempo.test.ts`): (1) half note = 60 plus sound 120 opens, labels and plays at 120; (2) dotted quarter = 60 plus sound 90 opens at 90; (3) an opening sound 100 is not displaced by a mark appearing only in bar 2 (and that mark is a change at bar 2); (4) E48's stated 100 and 72 override those two marked shapes through `withOpeningTempo` and the real model; (5) a genuine later tempo change remains later, at its position; (6) an undotted quarter mark and a fractional value read as today (X3c's `90.00009000009` case unchanged). Add the two bundled pieces as cases: *Row, Row, Row Your Boat* opens at 81, the easy *Canon in D* at 100.
6. **What changes for the bundled catalogue is stated.** The app's own measurement (`evidence.ts`, the import path's demands) reads the map: say which measured values move for a marked import, and that the content build's measurement (Python, E0) is not this seam's. For the 41 bundled scores, say what the Score label showed and shows on the two probed ones; the others by the reader alone (a table under `runs/X3d/`), not looked at.
7. **Not X3d's:** OSMD's engraving of the mark on the page (it draws what the file prints; unchanged); the content build's Python measurement; the E32 text mark standing after the first bar (X3c follow-up 4) unless the reader's positions make it right for free, then say so; the sheet's glyph weight; the store's writer.
