## The mechanisms, and the tests that told them apart

**Stopping.** *Hypothesis:* phrases stop because the walk writes the last bar's rhythm with no rule about how it ends: the last note is whatever length the bar ran out on. *Alternative:* the levels' palettes cannot end well at all. *Test:* the arrival measures over each level's valid draws. Result: levels 2–7 can never put the final note on the last downbeat in 4/4 — no bar-long note is in their palettes — at either version (0 % "on the last downbeat" in both tables), but a valid draw ends on a note held a felt beat often enough that one is almost always among sixteen: arrival by the rule reaches 87–100 % by selection alone. So the brief's first inherited hypothesis holds for arrival by the rule, and no level needed arrival in the hard layer (none has valid draws that never arrive); it fails for the downbeat, which would need a closing cell (new cells are not D1's; Follow-ups).

**Wandering.** *Hypothesis:* scoring the attempt sequence's candidates reaches one contour at every level. *Alternative:* the walk itself — a leap drawn uniformly up to the cap, about half the cap on average — is the ceiling, and no count of candidates reaches it at levels 5–7. *Test:* the same seeds with 48 candidates instead of 16, on the final scorer. Result: level 7's one-contour units 13 % → 14 %, level 6's 22 % → 26 % (`runs/probe-candidates-16.txt`, `probe-candidates-48.txt`): the walk is the ceiling at 6–7. The hypothesis holds at levels 1–2, partly at 3–5, and fails at 6–7; the bounds there are modest and say why.

**The drift the suite caught.** The first scorer passed every single-phrase test and the suite found two failures no example showed: level 2 fell from about four notes a bar to three and a half (a sparser phrase is easier to shape and ends long more often), and level 1 collapsed onto a handful of five-note phrases that half the seeds wrote. *Mechanism:* the strict best among candidates is biased to sparse draws, and where the best-shaped phrases are few the strict best is the same few phrases. *Fix:* the choice keeps the level's density (only draws at or above the valid draws' lower quartile of notes are eligible; the median held density too but halved the pool) and takes the earliest draw within 0.05 of the best (level 1: 156 distinct in 200 seeds at the strict best, 182 with the tolerance, 192 at version 1).

**Sitting on a note.** The reader's read found version 2's level-2 seed 4242 writing eight of sixteen notes on C: the walk's floor at C4 clamps moves down into repeats, and nothing in the scorer asked otherwise, so it chose among C-heavy draws. *Fix:* one pitch struck more than three times running costs the contour (a factor, like rocking). Repeated notes went back to version 1's level (e.g. 2.2's row 30 % → 25 %, version 1 24 %), at a cost of a few points of contour at level 3 and more rocking on 2.2's five-note row (bounded, with its reason).

**S26.** *Mechanism:* the tie-closing pass set the next bar's first note to the tied pitch after the walk had moved on from where the tie started, so the note after it was chosen from elsewhere. *Test:* seed 71282 on the right-hand row with ties (the reader's move at 2.4–2.5), through `readingOptions`: version 1 writes C4 tied into bar 2 then G4 (four scale steps) and A4 tied into bar 4 then E4 (three), where the cap is two; version 2 holds the walk through the tie. The chord-tone snap at 5–7 was the other way past a cap (it moved a strong beat one step from wherever the walk put it); version 2 moves to a chord tone only within the cap of the note before — which keeps every strong beat on a chord tone at 5–7 (100 % at both versions), where scoring alone got about two in three (below, the builder's calls). ## The builder's calls, each with its reason

- **Version 2 behind an option, version 1 in force.** The brief: stop at the schema line, and do not ship the scorer behind the existing identity. - **The capped move to a chord tone at 5–7, not scoring alone** (a deviation from the brief's "the snap becomes a scored quality rather than a rewrite"). Scored only, the strong beats on chord tones fell from every strong beat to about two in three at 5–7 — selection among sixteen random walks cannot do better (S7 names "the right hand ignores the left hand's harmony" as the fault) — while a move to the nearest chord tone within the cap keeps both: 0 violations and 100 % agreement. - **Ties off the beat only where the phrase syncopates on purpose** — designed at levels 5–7, promised at 1–4 (4.5's row) — stricter than the brief's "below 4.5": T37's placement rule for levels 1–4 is every note where its length belongs, whatever the rung, and a tie from an off-beat is syncopation the row does not promise. - **Contour on the beats.** A four-bar unit's contour is read on the note sounding at each felt beat, with a turn counted once the line comes back a third: on every note, level 5's eighths "turned" on half their notes while the line rose. One contour per four bars (an arch, a valley, an ascent or a descent), so an eight-bar phrase may be two arches; no form is required.
- **The arrival grades and weight.** The rule is the reviewer's, stated in the tests; the score grades within it (downbeat 1, the half bar held 0.9, another beat 0.7, off the beat 0.5) because a quarter on beat 4 is weaker than a half from beat 3; arrival weighs 5 against contour's 3, because at 3 each one phrase in five still ended short at 5–7.
- **Candidates 16, window 96, tolerance 0.05, lower-quartile floor**: each with its measurement above. The window bounds rare recipes (row 4's accidental keeps about one draw in eight: it makes about a hundred draws a phrase, within the budget).
- **The suite reads the page.** It measures the MusicXML read back, not the generator's own model, so the same suite measures the committed generator's pages (the red tree) and confirms the page is the phrase the generator chose (the page's score equals the reported one). Page reading is by pattern (the writer emits one fixed shape); jsdom's parser cost tens of milliseconds a phrase.
- **Seeds**: 200 per level, 150 per row per rung (rows on two rungs get 300); every configuration's phrases are written once and every case reads them.
- **Felt beat in odd metres.** 6/8: the dotted quarter, held from the second beat counts, a whole-bar note lands on the downbeat (levels 3–4's 6/8 figure makes it possible, 11 % → 53 % on row 3). 5/4: a quarter, as a teacher counts. 7/8 and 5/8: `feltBeat` gives an eighth, which a teacher reads as a subdivision of 2+2+3; the arrival measure uses a quarter there.
