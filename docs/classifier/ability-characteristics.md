## 0. How to read a row

**Rows and parts.** One row per ability, or one row per part where an ability names things that different characteristics, or different kinds of one characteristic, show (abilities.md 8.4: "evidence for one part never certifies another"). A graded parameter of one act (which keys, which note values, which chord qualities) is a level parameter, not a part: its characteristic reports each kind separately, and an item fits only the kinds it shows. A part a characteristic cannot show is marked taught by the lesson on its own line.

**Detected by.** The characteristics that show an item trains or demands the ability, in backticks (characteristics-list.md ids), and how they combine:
- **all of**: each is required; **any of**: one suffices, and each alternative is reported as its own evidence; **not** / **Absent**: must not be present.
- **"A, with B"**: B is required too; **"with B where ..."**: B is required only in the stated case.
- A characteristic followed by a kind name (`texture.tango` síncopa, `metre.class` compound) means that kind of the characteristic, which the characteristic reports separately.
- **LH / RH**: the left or right hand. The hand is the staff unless `prereq.hand-assignment` says otherwise (characteristics-list.md conventions), which is why every hand-reading row carries the hand question on PDMX (Q-hand).
- **What the item is.** For playing abilities, the score played. For ear abilities (AB-117 to AB-143, the by-ear parts of AB-166 and AB-167), the item is the heard stimulus, the score the app plays, whose characteristics establish the right answer. For improvisation and accompaniment-making abilities, the item is the stimulus (what is given and the bars left to fill: `form.improvisation-space`), and, where a part names a vocabulary the learner uses (AB-151, AB-172), written model items show it. **Set-level** rows (AB-069, AB-072 (a), the twelve keys of AB-162) are computed over a set of items from each item's own rows.

**Verified (generated, PDMX).** Whether the item's fit is established, never whether the learner did it: no one in this process hears the learner (there is no listening model), so the playing itself is the lesson's. The marking is the weakest marking, on that pipeline, of every characteristic named in the row's detected and fit columns (notes excluded), in the order code < code (to be validated) < code + agent < agent < gap (the brief: "the weakest needed one decides"). In brackets: the rows that make it a gap or agent-only, the rows still to be validated, and every agent question the row depends on, by label (section 2 gives 1.M's wording for each label). An "any of" row is marked by the weakest alternative; where another alternative is better, the note says so.

**Fit.** "carried": the ability's level parameters, named by the characteristic that carries each (in the sources' order where they give one, "then" separating stages); no numbers or thresholds, which are Phase 2. "Absent": what must not be present: a demand beyond the level that this ability's own rows measure, or a conflicting feature. "None specific" means only the gates below apply.

**What every fit also needs, stated once.** For every ability and part: no demand beyond the level in the dimensions the row does not name (the difficulty rows); a correctly written score (`integrity.notation-sanity`: never teach a misspelt or mis-beamed note); and, for a musical item, `quality.coherence` and `quality.idiomatic`. These are the same for every ability, so they are not folded into the "verified" columns; their own markings are:

So, beyond each row's own marking, the level gates add the hand, chord and key questions (Q-hand, Q-chord, Q-key) on PDMX and the chord question (Q-chord-gen, through `difficulty.harmonic-load`) on generated items, and the quality gates add their checklist question ("the agent answers the checklist from the coded facts", 1.M) on both pipelines. A row marked code is therefore code for its own detection and fit rows only. `difficulty.level` is not used (fit uses each ability's own level parameters and the difficulty rows; the owner, 2026-10-08: "we don't need to worry about exact placement yet").

**Gap / notes.** GAP per pipeline with what is missing; generated rows whose characteristic is "absent by the family list" (no generator family writes it, so code finds no generated fit until one does); MISSING CHARACTERISTIC; and the reading behind a call. "Phase 2 work" marks a rule or a source still to be written or quoted (characteristics-list.md 1.M, F13), never an agent question.

**Taught by the lesson.** An ability (or part) is taught by the lesson when no characteristic separates an item that trains it from one that does not: the body (posture, tension), ways of playing or practising any item (memory, recovery, counting aloud, practice methods), or a source that is not a score (a recording, a poem, a live partner's response). Its row says why.

## 3. Taught by the lesson

**Whole abilities (21).** Nothing in the music separates an item that trains these from one that does not; the lesson teaches them on any item (or on the items its notes name).

- AB-001 (Sit at the piano at a height and distance that keeps the forearms level, shoulders relaxed...): posture (height, distance, forearms, feet) is the body at the instrument; no score property separates an item that trains it from one that does not.
- AB-002 (Keep a rounded, relaxed hand with curved fingers and firm fingertips while playing, free o...): hand shape and freedom from tension are the body; every item played trains or strains them alike, and nothing in the score shows which.
- AB-004 (Release tension in shoulders, arms and hands while playing): releasing tension while playing is the body; no score property marks an item that trains it (a long or fast item strains it, which `technique.endurance` and `technique.velocity` measure as demand, not as this ability).
- AB-008 (While reading, keep the eyes on the music, finding keys by touch within a position and gla...): where the eyes are is a way of reading; every reading item trains it, and its shifts and leaps (`technique.position-shift`, `technique.leap-size`) say only where a glance is allowed, not whether an item trains the ability.
- AB-038 (Count the beats aloud while playing): counting aloud is a way of practising any item; printed counts (`notation.reading-aids`) support it but do not separate an item that trains it from one that does not.
- AB-066 (Keep going through a piece and recover promptly from a slip without stopping): recovering from a slip is a way of performing; every item trains it alike.
- AB-067 (Play a piece, a pattern or a tune from memory, and keep a bank of tunes memorised): playing from memory and keeping tunes memorised are ways of playing any item; the item's memorisation load is read from `form.length` and `reading.redundancy`, which grade it but do not separate an item that trains the ability from one that does not.
- AB-096 (Recall and play any requested scale, arpeggio or chord pattern from memory, given only its...): recalling a requested pattern from memory is how the patterns of AB-081 to AB-105 are played; the request names key, hands and articulation, and the pattern items' own rows apply.
- AB-154 (Make up a piece or song of one's own in a style and perform it: a clear melodic line over ...): making up one's own piece starts from no item; what it must contain (range, devices, form) would be read from the learner's own result, which no one in this process hears and which is not written out (writing music on paper is out of scope).
- AB-208 (Practise a new passage slowly first, then raise the tempo in small steps (a metronome as g...): slow practice raised in steps is a method applied to any item; `difficulty.local-profile` locates the passage that needs it, which picks where to apply it, not an item that trains it.
- AB-209 (Learn a piece hands separately, then put the hands together (slower at first), when the co...): hands separately then together is a method applied to any two-hand item; `difficulty.coordination` shows where the coordination needs it, which picks where, not an item that trains it.
- AB-210 (Isolate a hard spot as a short segment, solve it, then rejoin it to its neighbours with an...): isolating a hard spot is a method; `difficulty.local-profile` locates the hard spot.
- AB-211 (Fix a mistake in practice where it happens: stop, find its cause and rework the passage un...): fixing a mistake where it happens is a method of practice, on any item.
- AB-212 (Practise a passage in variants: altered rhythms, different tempos, one hand alone, in othe...): practising in variants (rhythms, tempos, keys, blocked, outlined) is a method; the item's own rows (`texture.broken-chord` for blocking) say which variants apply.
- AB-213 (Record one's own playing and listen back to find what to fix): recording and listening back is a method; the source is the learner's recording, which no one in this process hears.
- AB-214 (Start, and restart, from any landmark in a piece (a section start, a key change, a chosen ...): restarting from landmarks is a method; the landmarks are the item's `form.sections` and `key.change`, which every sectioned item has.
- AB-215 (Play a prepared piece through as a performance: a mock performance alone at home or before...): a mock performance is a way of playing a prepared item, any item.
- AB-216 (Count oneself (and others) in: one or two bars aloud at the chosen tempo and metre before ...): counting oneself in happens before the first note; `mark.tempo-text` and `mark.anacrusis` give the tempo and whether an upbeat follows, which every item has or lacks without making it a fit.
