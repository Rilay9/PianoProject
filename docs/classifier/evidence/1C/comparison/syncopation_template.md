# rhythm.syncopation: the current detector against SynPy, AMADS and Beatsearch (handoff step 3), 2026-10-08

**What this is.** One characteristic of the bounded comparison of handoff step 3 (the pilot is `docs/classifier/evidence/1B/comparison/key.md`; its method is followed). Nothing is rewritten. The current `rhythm.syncopation` detector (the chunk-1 validators' `sync.py`, executed unchanged) is measured against the candidates of `docs/classifier/tools-survey.md` section 22.3, on the recipe declarations of the generated items, on a published rating set, and on the items the validation row names. Base commit c47f18c7. Every figure was produced by a script in this folder; nothing is copied from the validation pages (where one agrees with the validation row, this page says so).

**Labels.** *measured*: a script in this folder produced it on the data named. *reading*: from a page, a source file or the notated rhythm, not run. Nothing here has been heard.

**Applied from the outside review (relayed 2026-10-08, `docs/prompts/inputs-2026-10-08/chatgpt-review-step3.md`, row `rhythm.syncopation`; and `chatgpt-review-chunk1-rules.md` section 7).** The characteristic is reported as evidence types, not one "present" flag: held over a stronger beat (three kinds), off-beat attack, accent, rest, bass-then-held-chord. Section 3 says per type which method covers it. The ability being taught chooses which types count; an accompaniment's off-beat chords or a rest never certify tied syncopation. SynPy and AMADS are witnesses for the types they cover, not authorities for the characteristic. Agreement between methods is confidence, not proof: sections 5 and 8 list where methods agree and where a method that scores well answers a different musical question.

## 1. What was compared, and the ground truth

**Current detector.** `docs/classifier/evidence/1C/validation/sync.py` (`analyse`), unchanged, through `syn_common.current()`: the lines a listener follows (right hand top line, left hand bottom line), kinds *beat-level held*, *held*, *held at the subdivision*, *off-beat attack*, *accent*, *rest*, *bass-then-held-chord*, plus the reported-only *silent beat after a run*. Its "present" (the page's list) is any of the seven kinds; "presence without off-beat attack and bass-then-held-chord" is the validation row's second count. It reproduces the row's counts here: generated present 119, without 52; PDMX 328 of 520 that ran (the row has 329; one item, Stardust, died of MemoryError in the first run and was re-run: `rerun_failed.py`); rep 211 and 208 (`cur_run.py`, `cur_items.json`).

**Current detector with fix** (`cur_run.py`, text replacement at load time, `sync.py` untouched): (F1) the bass-then-held-chord test counts the previous onset as a single note when its pitches share one pitch class (an octave bass: the row's failure 2); (F2) that kind only in the left hand (it fired in Mazurka Op. 68 No. 4's right-hand melody); (F3) UNKNOWN when the file has no `<time>` element (the row's failure 1; the check is on the file, `has_time`). Section 7 says what each fix did and did not do.

**Candidates** (survey 22.3): SynPy (seven models); AMADS `syncopation` (weighted note-to-beat distance, WNBD) and `syncopation_span` (the "IOI span" measure); Beatsearch (monophonic Longuet-Higgins and Lee syncopation vector). GrooveToolbox: not run (section 9).

**Ground truth, three kinds, fixed before any candidate was scored.**
1. *Generated items* (1,211 `exercise.*` items): positive = the recipe declares syncopation (concepts name syncopation, or the drill kind is a syncopation drill): **46** items; negative = every other generated item: **1,165** (the validation row's counts: 44 by concept plus both `syncopation`-family drills; 1,165 declare none). This is a declaration, not a hearing: 73 of the negatives are figures the definition names (comping off-beats 46, the 4:3 waltz, the tresillo bass cell, half notes on beat 2 in riffs and studies, anticipated last notes in the shaping drills), so precision against it is a lower bound for every method.
2. *Song et al.'s syncopation dataset* (111 rhythm patterns with the ratings of 10 listeners each, 0 to 4; 27 monorhythms in 4/4, 36 in 6/8, 48 polyrhythms in 4/4): the only published syncopation dataset with ratings the survey names (SynPy paper section 4, survey 22.3). Fetched without form, login or terms from `github.com/electronstudio/synpy`, a fork of the original repository (`code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/projects/syncopation-dataset`, which refused the connection from this machine); files `Raw_rating_data/Ratings for entire stimuli.xls` and `Rhythm_stimuli_text_format/*.rhy`. All 111 stimuli have 10 ratings. Rules fixed beforehand (`song_metrics.py`): a method's value on a stimulus (below); Spearman rank correlation with the mean rating, per group; label "listeners heard syncopation" = mean rating of at least 1.0 (92 of the 111; 44 of the 63 monorhythms); presence = value above the method's own zero (above its value on a bar of plain beats for the two models that have none). The ratings ask a question about an isolated rhythm: they cover the held-over-the-beat and rest types only (no accompaniment, no accents, no ties as such).
3. *Named catalogue items* (`named_items.json`, 33 items): every item the validation row names as found, rejected, wrong or open, plus the rules page's examples, with the expected answer stated before any candidate ran: present, absent, UNKNOWN, or open (a musical judgement the row leaves open; not scored). The expected answers are *reading* (of the notated lines, `lines_items.json`, and of the row and the rules page); several come from the same pages the current detector was built from, so the current detector's agreement with them is not independent.

**Metrics** (the brief's): presence precision and recall against the recipe declarations (plus AUC, since the thresholds of the candidates are arbitrary); the named items, right or wrong per method; correlation with ratings; handling of a file with no time signature; runtime per piece.

**How the candidates were fed.** SynPy reads MIDI or its own `.rhy` text; its MIDI reader does not run in the available port (section 2), so bars are built directly through SynPy's own `Bar`/`BarList` objects from the onsets a MIDI export would carry (`cat_synpy.py`): two inputs. *texture*: every onset of either hand, one rhythm per piece (the survey's "export to MIDI" route); *lines*: the current detector's two lines, each its own rhythm. Both are onset-only, with no durations, ties, rests or accents, which is the single most important property for section 3. AMADS WNBD is run three ways: on the score file as its docstring says (partitura note array, beats of the denominator), and on texture and lines onsets in the metre's felt beat; `syncopation_span` on texture and lines onsets against a hierarchy that follows SynPy's subdivision table for the metre. For the Song stimuli each tool is given the stimulus itself: SynPy its `.rhy`, Beatsearch and AMADS the pattern bar as a binary vector, the current detector and AMADS-on-score a MusicXML built from the pattern (`song_scores.py`; five bars, each note lasting to the next onset; read back through the project's reader, 111 of 111 onset lists equal the intended ones, `song_scores_check.json`).

Licence: neither clone holds a licence file; the paper record on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.851079) says CC BY 4.0 and the paper footer CC BY 3.0 (the survey's note); the code licence is therefore **not confirmed**. Venv `build/venv-synpy` (numpy, scipy, xlrd), no install error. Two faults in the port, worked around without editing it (`synpy_adapter.py`): `TimeSignature.pkl` is a Python 2 text pickle that raised `UnpicklingError('the STRING opcode argument must be quoted')` on the first bar (cause: the Python 3 default encoding and CRLF line endings written by git's autocrlf on this machine; read back with CRLF undone and `encoding='latin1'`, and checked equal to the dict hard-coded in `parameter_setter.py`); and the MIDI reader cannot be imported (`midiparser.py` imports Python 2's `exceptions`; `readmidi.py` raises a `TabError`), so SynPy's MIDI route was not usable and bars were built from onsets. - **AMADS** (`amads` 1.4.0 from PyPI, MIT): `build/venv-amads`, pip install succeeded (a second `pip install` started by mistake in the same venv failed with WinError 32 while the first was still running; the first completed; no junction needed). `amads.time.meter.syncopation` and `syncopation_span` ran. `inner_metric_analysis` is a weights computation for other characteristics (hemiola), not a syncopation measure: not run.
- **Beatsearch** (`github.com/Tomasito665/Beatsearch` at 315114a129a87a5553217b27ab025862b63ca9c1, MIT): `pip install beatsearch` failed, exact error: `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'build_dependencies'` raised by the sdist's `setup.py` (`beatsearch-0.0.0.tar.gz`) in "Getting requirements to build wheel". It ran on the Song monorhythms and on the unmetred test only (it takes its own rhythm objects, not scores); not run on the catalogue.
- **GrooveToolbox** (`github.com/fredbru/GrooveToolbox`, Apache-2.0, d24af07d74ad0068827c60a02bb3ac43fddb55c7): **not run**. Reading of its README and `Groove.py`: its input is a 10-part drum-kit hit matrix with kick, snare and hi-hat syncopation functions weighted by kit part, and the README lists Python 3.5 and 2.7; there is no way to give it a piano line without pretending a part. The survey says the same.
- **Not usable here**: SynPy's polyrhythm handling (four models decline polyrhythms by design; section 4); the original SynPy repository; the code licences.
- **Reader failures** (the project's reader, already listed on the rules pages): Bach Invention No. 1 PDMX, Chopin Ballade No. 1, *O mio babbino caro*, Sakamoto: no result for every method that needs the reader (SynPy and the lines; AMADS on the score file ran on Ballade No. 1 and raised on the other three, `cat_amads.json`). ## 3. Which method covers which evidence type (reading, with the measured check beside it)

| evidence type | current detector | SynPy (LHL, TMC, SG, KTH) | SynPy PRS, TOB, WNBD; AMADS WNBD | AMADS span | Beatsearch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| held over a stronger beat, from a beat (*beat-level held*) | names it | covers the question (a weaker onset followed by silence at a stronger position) but cannot tell a held note from a rest: its input is onsets only | other quantities (complexity, off-beatness, distance of onsets to the beat) | same as LHL, onsets only | LHL-type, onsets only |
| held across the next beat from off the beat (*held*), held at the subdivision | names them | as above (the subdivision level only through a finer grid) | other quantities | as above | as above |
| off-beat attack (a stroke alone in its beat, silent beats around it) | names it | **flags it as syncopation without naming it** (measured: 71 of 71 generated items whose only kind is off-beat attack are present for LHL on the lines) | flags almost everything with an off-beat onset | flags it (71 of 71) | flags it |
| accent on a weak position | names it | **not covered** (no articulation in the input) | not covered | not covered | not covered |
| rest on a strong beat | names it (whole texture silent) | a rest and a tie are the same zero in its input: cannot tell | not covered | not covered | not covered |
| bass-then-held-chord | names it, with a documented split (section 6) | not covered (a chord is one onset) | not covered | not covered | not covered |

So: the candidates can serve at most as second witnesses for the first two rows, and only when they are given one line at a time. Section 5 puts numbers on each statement.

## 8. What the numbers say (each point names the table it reads)

**Per evidence type** (section 3 table, section 5 type and class tables). The current detector alone names the types. Over the 1,211 generated items the types occur in: beat-level held 22 items (13 declared positives, 9 declared negatives), held 35 (19, 16), held at the subdivision 7 (2, 5), off-beat attack 76 (30, 46), rest 6 (6, 0), bass-then-held-chord 6 (4, 2), accent 0; in real items beat-level held 306, held 371, subdivision 126, off-beat attack 90, accent 228, rest 69, bass-then-held-chord 75. Grouped by the current detector's kinds, the generated items in which it finds a tied kind number 46 (21 declared positives, 25 declared negatives), and 71 more have off-beat attack but no tied kind (25 declared positives). SynPy LHL on the lines flags 41 of the 46 tied-class items and **71 of the 71 off-beat-only items**, and AMADS WNBD flags 71 of 71 as well: a flag from these tools cannot tell tied syncopation from an accompaniment's off-beat stroke. On the 1,092 generated items in which the current detector finds nothing, LHL on the lines flags 44 (scales 24, broken octaves 10, double sixths 4 ...), SynPy PRS 714 of 1,075, AMADS WNBD 681: those tools count something else (an unplayed beat in a hand's own line; any off-beat onset).

**Against the recipe declarations** (section 5 first table). The current detector (page list) finds 46 of 46 declared positives and flags 73 of 1,165 declared negatives (precision 0.387, AUC 0.975); without off-beat attack and bass-then-held-chord, 27 of 46 and 25 negatives (precision 0.519, recall 0.587). The best candidates reach the same recall on the lines with more false positives: SynPy LHL on the lines 46 of 46 and 112 negatives (precision 0.291, 8 items with no result, AUC 0.983); AMADS span on the lines 46 of 46 and 65 negatives (0.414, AUC 0.980). On the texture, recall falls (LHL 37 of 46, span 36 of 46) because the other hand's onset fills the beat: the survey's route (export the score, hand it to SynPy) loses 9 of the 46 declared positives. PRS, TOB and WNBD flag 750 or more negatives or miss most positives: they are measures of complexity, off-beatness and onset distance, not syncopation detectors, and cannot be used for presence.

**Against listeners** (section 4). On the 63 monorhythms the current detector's Spearman rho is 0.67 (4/4: 0.81, 6/8: 0.64) with presence precision 0.82 and recall 0.82, against SynPy LHL 0.71, TMC 0.69, SG 0.79, PRS 0.78, KTH 0.79 (4/4 only), AMADS span 0.67 and AMADS WNBD on the pattern 0.66, Beatsearch 0.51. They are level to within what 63 rated rhythms can separate; none is clearly better than the current detector. The current detector's 0.41 on the 48 polyrhythms and -0.07 over all 111 is by rule (tuplets of a beat or longer are left out as a cross-rhythm, `texture.polyrhythm`); LHL, PRS, TMC and SG decline them; the three that measure them (KTH, TOB, WNBD) correlate -0.23 to -0.04. **A method that scores well and answers a different question**: PRS (rho 0.78) has no zero for "not syncopated" (its value on a bar of plain beats is 2) and flags 788 of the 1,165 declared negatives; AMADS WNBD on a score file is exactly 0 for every 6/8 stimulus (partitura counts eighths as beats), a rho of nan, and 0.05 on the monorhythms.

**Named items** (section 5, last tables; expected answers `named_items.json`, 29 scored). Right / wrong / no result: current detector (page list) 28 / 1 / 0; without off-beat attack and bass-then-held-chord 25 / 4 / 0; with fix 29 / 0 / 0; SynPy on the lines LHL 22 / 4 / 3, SG 21 / 5 / 3, TMC 21 / 5 / 3, PRS 19 / 7 / 3, KTH 16 / 5 / 8, TOB 11 / 17 / 1, WNBD 17 / 11 / 1; SynPy on the texture LHL 19 / 7 / 3 and the others as in the table; AMADS span on the lines 23 / 4 / 2, AMADS WNBD on the score file 16 / 13 / 0. SynPy on the lines is wrong where the line has a rest on the beat that the other hand fills: Bach's Prelude in C and BWV 999 (the right hand "rests" on each downbeat that the left hand plays: the case the project's whole-texture rest test was written for, so the lines input is not safe for a one-hand tool), the trill, and the Handel Sarabande (the project's rule rejects its equal-weight holds; why LHL flags it was not traced). The current detector's near-perfect score is partly circular: its rule was written from these items.

**Open items** (kum-ba-yah, Ode to Joy easy, Sakura, Mazurka Op. 17 No. 4): no expected answer can be stated from the score, and none is scored. SynPy models and AMADS WNBD say present on the Kum ba yah final, SynPy LHL on the lines says absent for Ode to Joy and Sakura, AMADS span says absent for all three finals; agreement or disagreement here is not evidence either way. The accent on beat 3 of 3/4: no candidate reads accents.

**Agreement is confidence, not proof.** The two Longuet-Higgins and Lee implementations disagree on 11 of 63 stimuli; the candidates share one blind spot (onsets only: a held note, a rest and a gap are the same zero); and where they agree with the current detector on "present" they agree about the question "is a weaker onset not followed by a stronger one", which is not the lesson's question when the stroke is an accompaniment's.

**Runtime** (measured on this machine, shared with other jobs; relationships, not general figures). One process, 31 catalogue items (`timing.py`, every 67th): the current detector median 0.025 s and mean 0.21 s per piece (read plus analyse; maximum 2.9 s); reading plus line extraction median 0.020 s; SynPy seven models, two inputs, median 0.028 s (maximum 0.49 s); AMADS WNBD on the score file median 0.010 s (maximum 0.20 s). Full runs of 2,016 items with 3 to 6 worker processes (section 5 last table) give medians 0.018 s (current), 0.060 s (SynPy), 0.089 s (AMADS on the file, which includes partitura's own load), and ninetieth percentiles of 3.0, 0.9 and 1.2 s: the cost is the reader, not the model. Song stimuli: SynPy, seven models on all 111 files, 1.34 s in all; Beatsearch and AMADS on a vector: milliseconds.

## 9. Not run, and why

- GrooveToolbox (drum kits only; section 2). Beatsearch on the catalogue (it takes its own rhythm objects; run on the Song monorhythms and the unmetred test). AMADS `inner_metric_analysis` (not a syncopation measure).
- SynPy's MIDI route (the port's reader does not import); Sioros and Guedes' velocity use (all velocities are 1 in this input); SynPy and AMADS on accents, rests as such and bass-then-chord (not in their inputs).
- No second published rating set: the survey names none besides Song's; Fitch and Rosenfeld (2007) was not read or fetched.
- Items that died of MemoryError in the first run (5) were re-run once, the machine having run out of memory while other jobs ran; one first attempt at the SynPy run was stopped by hand after a worker spent 7,000 s of CPU on one item and re-run with the grid guard of section 2.

## 10. Limits

- The generated-item truth is a declaration: it marks as negative 73 items whose figure the definition names, and it cannot tell the types apart. Precision is therefore a lower bound for every method; recall is cleaner.
- The Song ratings are of isolated rhythms, a rating of strength rather than a count of figures, with 10 listeners per pattern, and cover two of the seven types. The stimuli scores are built here (note lasts to next onset); the current detector sees one line.
- The named items' expected answers are reading, partly from the pages the current detector was built from. Open items are not scored.
- "Current detector" is the validators' re-implementation, not production code. The line extraction of `lines_extract.py` is a copy of its line selection, not an import.
- SynPy is the 2020 Python 3 port, run with a workaround for one fault and a guard against one non-terminating grid; its licence is unconfirmed; its published agreement with listeners is not reproduced here beyond section 4.
- Nothing here has been heard. One run of each; methods deterministic.
