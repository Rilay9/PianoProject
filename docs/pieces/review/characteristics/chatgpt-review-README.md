# Independent review of Claude's score-characteristic proposal

**Scope and source:** User-supplied `README.md`, `characteristics-easy.csv` (48 rows) and `chatgpt-detectability-213-with-claude.csv` (213 rows), reviewed 2026-10-10. All 261 rows retain their original fields and have a new `chatgpt_independent_review` cell. These were written and flushed to the reviewed CSV as the review progressed, in original order. The reviews judge the *proposal*; they are not claims that the proposed extractors have been executed.

## Overall decision

**Proceed with the genuinely direct extraction subset after correcting a few definitions; do not treat the full 48-row easy batch as a single all-or-nothing implementation gate.** Most basic MusicXML symbol readings are appropriate. A few rows silently replace a hard characteristic with a narrower raw fact. That may be an excellent implementation choice, but the narrower output must not certify the original musical or pedagogical claim. The most important corrections are E01, E13, E33, E44, D02, and the D03 classification as an easy task.

### Main corrections in source-order terms

- **E01 / item.format:** counting parts and staves is direct; limiting all subsequent analysis to one two-staff pitched part is a deliberately restrictive support policy, not a property of detectability. It discards beginner one-staff work and potentially useful lead-sheet and arrangement formats. Preserve basic layout facts for *every* score, and decide which advanced calculations truly need an unsupported-layout result.
- **E04 + E05 + E33:** preserve the distinction between the MusicXML `<pitch>` sounding pitch and the note's displayed position under `<octave-shift>`. MusicXML defines a conventional 8va using a `down` octave shift because the score is printed below the sounding pitch. Confirm the direction with synthetic source-to-output tests; avoid shifting the sounding pitch twice when calculating E33's compass. See https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/octave-shift/ .
- **E13 / triplets and tuplets:** the proposed group definition is `<notations><tuplet>`-centric and refers to a printed bracket. MusicXML separates rhythmic `<time-modification>` from graphical `<tuplet>`; the latter may specify `bracket="no"`, and not all durational modifications require a graphical notation element. This is a serious false-negative risk if the claim is “has triplets,” although a detector for *explicit tuplet signs* may intentionally be narrower. Count trusted rhythmic ratios and display notation as separate evidence, while rejecting ill-formed export artefacts. See https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/tuplet/ and https://www.w3.org/2021/06/musicxml40/musicxml-reference/elements/time-modification/ .
- **E44 / alternating staves:** the proposed positive test (one staff playing bars 1–2, the other bars 3–4) contradicts the given definition requiring both to play *within the same bar* without simultaneous sound. Correct the test or introduce a separately defined between-bar handoff output.
- **E36–E39 / chord, octave and leap descriptors:** note clusters across all voices on a staff are not necessarily single-hand chords; C–E–C counts as an octave-containing triad but not a pure octave dyad; the top edge of a staff is not always the actual melody. Retain useful raw descriptors but do not promote them to definitive physical or musical skill claims.
- **D02 / difficulty prediction:** “within one grade on 70% of leave-one-out predictions” is proposed without a baseline or a reason the criterion corresponds to useful curriculum placement. Do not make that number an acceptance gate. Verify the edition-specific grade labels, avoid leakage across related works/arrangements, use grade-distribution-aware baselines and withheld evaluation, and report mismatch alarms without inventing a published grade.
- **D03 / published CIPI model:** legitimately worth knowing about but explicitly **not easy**; it requires another environment, a different label scale (Henle 1–9), and careful comparison with simpler baselines. The authors' code does expose `compute_difficulty` and identifies AGPL-3.0; the published study describes a 652-piece dataset and approximately 39.5% **balanced accuracy**. The uploaded row's 87.3%-within-one claim comes from a summarizer; it has not been confirmed here against the paper. Sources: https://github.com/PRamoneda/difficulty-prediction-CIPI and https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417423022789 .
- **Original 213-characteristic mapping:** narrowed easy outcomes must not be mistaken for completion of the original rows: e.g., `pitch.chromatic` is not the same as printed accidental marks; `key.change` is not synonymous with printed key-signature change; `texture.hands-together` includes simultaneous sounding not just shared attacks; `harmony.chord-quality` from `<harmony><kind>` is not verified sounding chord quality; `meta.arrangement` cannot be established by a statistical grade discrepancy.

## Response to README's review questions

**1. Are any rows not actually easy or misleading?** Yes. Direct element extraction is feasible for most E rows, but the rows above need narrower naming or corrected interpretation. D02 is a modelling and evidence-validation experiment, and D03 is an external-model feasibility experiment, not ordinary score fact extraction. E09 meter grouping is sometimes interpretation, E10 pickup detection has corrupted/incomplete-short-bar counterexamples, and E13 can miss valid unbracketed tuplets.

**2. Is any easy and useful fact missing?** Nothing justifies a broad additional inventory. Two small useful distinctions should accompany the existing parsing outputs: per-staff **sounding overlap versus simultaneous onset** (needed for `texture.hands-together`, not supplied by E43 alone), and **same-voice repeated pitches versus equal-duration streams** (E40 currently combines different phenomena). Also retain actual score offsets and stable measure identifiers as shared source-evidence fields. These are small extensions/clarifications, not new classifier workstreams.

**3. Is D02's 70% within-one-level plus shuffled-label control sensible?** The shuffle is a useful *negative control*, but the 70% pass criterion has no demonstrated educational or statistical justification. Depending on the grade distribution, a trivial central-grade prediction might already exceed it. Use a baseline and grouping-safe evaluation; assess ordinal error, grade-specific behavior and confidence. If the system is only a mismatch flag, validate the usefulness of that flag (true problems found / false alarms) rather than its similarity to published grades alone.

**4. Should CIPI/D03 be tried before D02?** Treat D03 as a **later, optional benchmark**, not a prerequisite. First establish identity-clean evaluation examples and simple D02 descriptive/baseline results. If remaining level disagreements would materially benefit from external comparison, run a limited CPU-compatible CIPI inference experiment in its own environment and compare the exact same held-out examples. Do not replace the app's runtime or introduce GPU dependencies for the experiment.

## Comments on the README's conventions

- **Staff is not hand:** appropriate conservative rule for score features. Allow explicit generator hand assignments or verified selected-piece annotation to provide hand information, rather than permanently discarding hand knowledge. The cited 3.3% and 41.5% error rates are from an old limited sample and should not be treated as universal estimates.
- **Raw MusicXML as the reference reader:** appropriate for what the received XML *encodes*. Successful parsing of 2,020 files is not a guarantee of correctness in semantic interpretation or render fidelity. A second library using the same XML is a useful check, not an independent teacher judgment.
- **Encoded accidentals rather than rendered marks:** a sound simplification if the column clearly says **encoded**, and if a reading lesson is not falsely told that an expected printed symbol definitely appears in the app.
- **Printed bar numbers:** pair with a unique measure index (and part/repeat context) so repeated or suffixed printed numbers cannot misdirect an excerpt or review note.
- **UNKNOWN:** important. Retain it for unsupported layout, missing or disputed hand ownership, ambiguous metrical grouping, questionable tempo, conflicting harmonic readings, and malformed spanners.
- **Pass/fail/fool plus 30 real outputs per row:** focused adversarial examples are valuable. Exactly 30 real cases **for every row** is an arbitrary fixed workload, especially for rare features. Prefer enough independently checked positive/negative examples to expose the actual risk; disclose when there are fewer than 30 available positives, and do not claim a meaningful accuracy percentage from sparse cases. No additional bureaucracy is needed.

## Boundaries of this review

The review read the three files provided by the user in full structurally and inspected all 261 CSV rows individually. It did **not** execute the proposed parsers, rerun Claude's 842-file corpus counts, inspect `chosen.csv`/`candidates-passing.csv`, or read the separate `old-library-findings.md` and `new-library-research.md` reports (they were referenced in the attached README but not attached here). Repository and research claims repeated in Claude's rows are treated as reported evidence unless independently checked. MusicXML and CIPI source documentation were consulted to resolve the main technical disagreements.

This review is meant to help the primary builder select proportional corrections and decide the next code changes; it is not a parallel implementation plan or a mandate to implement all original 213 characteristics.
