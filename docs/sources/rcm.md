# RCM Piano Syllabus: Preparatory A, Preparatory B, Level 1, Level 2 (pilot extract)

- **Source title:** The Royal Conservatory of Music, *Piano Syllabus, 2022 Edition* (official PDF, 132 pages; PDF metadata created 27 Jan 2022, modified 11 Mar 2022)
- **Edition/year:** 2022 Edition. Checked together with the official *Piano Syllabus, 2022 Edition, Errata* dated 11 Jan 2024. The RCM Teacher Portal resources page lists no newer piano syllabus edition.
- **Exact URL of the document used:** https://teacherportal.rcmusic.com/getattachment/57f3734d-97e5-4777-b67e-4b1111ee31a3/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf
- **Errata URL:** https://teacherportal.rcmusic.com/getattachment/e006ae03-875a-4772-837b-cc0760149c49/rcm-piano-syllabus-2022-errata.pdf
- **Listing page (RCM Teacher Portal, an rcmusic.com site):** https://teacherportal.rcmusic.com/Resources/Syllabus-Piano
- **Date accessed:** 2026-10-09

**Citation convention.** "p. N" is the page number printed on the page, which equals the PDF page index in this file. Many requirements are given as music notation (note values, time signatures, tempo marks); those were read from the page images and are marked "(read from notation)". "(inferred)" marks anything not stated in words. "Not specified in the syllabus" means the syllabus says nothing on the point. The syllabus gives no reading-range, dynamics, pedal or hand-coordination criteria as such, so those headings are mostly "not specified". Metronome speeds are "a guideline for the minimum tempo" (p. 7).

**General rules that apply to all four levels:** technical tests (scales, chords, arpeggios) must be played from memory; the examiner chooses "a representative sampling of items" from the technical list; sight-reading is rhythm reading plus sight playing of short passages; the student taps one measure of beat first, then speaks, claps or taps the rhythm; ear tests are melody clapback, melody playback, and identification of intervals and chords (all p. 7). All patterns are played ascending and descending "with good tone and logical fingering, at a steady tempo", and "all scales are to be played legato unless otherwise indicated" (p. 120). Memory marks: 2 per repertoire piece performed from memory, 6 in total (p. 5).

## Preparatory A

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified as a range. Sight playing uses the grand staff: one four-note melody in treble clef (right hand alone) and one in bass clef (left hand alone) (p. 9).
- The staff example on p. 9 gives the possible starting notes: treble C4 (finger 1), G4 (finger 5), C5 (finger 1) (note names inferred from the staff example); bass clef: three starting notes with fingers 1, 5, 5. Melodies "move by step in one direction only and may contain a repeated note" (p. 9).

### 2. Keys
- Technical: pentascales in C, G, D major and A minor; triad sequence in C major (p. 9).
- Ear: playback in C and G major (p. 9). Sight playing: no key listed; melodies start from the notes shown (p. 9).
- Repertoire keys: not specified in the syllabus.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Ear clapback: 3/4 and 4/4; whole, dotted half, half, quarter notes and a pair of eighths; about two measures (read from notation, p. 9).
- Sight rhythm: 4/4; whole, half, quarter notes and a pair of eighths; two measures (read from notation, p. 9).
- Sight playing: 4/4; whole, half and quarter notes (read from notation, p. 9).
- Repertoire time signatures/rhythms: not specified in the syllabus.

### 4. Technique (p. 9; patterns shown on pp. 120-121)
- All patterns from memory; hands separate (HS) throughout.
- **Legato pentascales** and **staccato pentascales** (five-finger patterns): C, G, D major and A minor; HS; tonic to dominant, ascending and descending, "ending with solid/blocked root-position triad"; quarter notes at quarter = 100 (read from notation).
- **Triad sequence, C major:** HS, 1 octave, ascending. Broken: eighth-note triplets at quarter = 60. Solid/blocked: quarter note then rest at quarter = 72 (read from notation).
- No scale longer than five notes, no arpeggios and no etude are listed at this level (technical table p. 9; marks table p. 8).

### 5. Hand coordination
- Hands play separately in every technical test (p. 9) and in sight playing (right hand alone in treble, left hand alone in bass, p. 9). Hands together: not specified in the syllabus.

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Articulation: pentascales are tested both legato and staccato (p. 9). Solid/blocked triads are played with rests between (the Preparatory triad-sequence pattern is on p. 121).
- Dynamics and pedal: not specified in the syllabus.

### 7. Sight-reading (10 marks: rhythm 5, playing 5; p. 8)
- **Rhythm** (p. 9): tap a steady beat for one measure, then continue while speaking, tapping or clapping the given rhythm; "a steady pulse and metric accentuation are expected". 4/4, two measures (read from notation).
- **Playing** (p. 9): two four-note melodies on the grand staff, each starting on one of the given notes with fingering shown for the first note only; one for the right hand (treble), one for the left hand (bass); stepwise in one direction, a repeated note allowed; 4/4, whole/half/quarter notes (read from notation).
- Examples only: p. 128 (rhythm).

### 8. Aural (10 marks: clapback 4, chords 2, playback 4; p. 8)
- **Clapback** (p. 9): clap, tap or sing the rhythm of a short melody after the examiner plays it twice; the examiner names the time signature and counts one measure first; about two measures.
- **Chords** (p. 9): identify major or minor (root-position triad) after the examiner plays the first five notes of a major or minor scale and then the tonic triad once in solid/blocked form.
- **Playback** (p. 9): play back a four-note melody based on the first three notes of a major scale; beginning on the tonic or mediant; C or G major; the examiner names the key, plays the tonic triad once and the melody twice.
- Examples only: pp. 124-125.

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three contrasting selections from one "Syllabus List"; there are no separate lists A/B/C at this level (p. 8). 20 marks each; 2 memory marks per piece performed from memory, 6 in total (pp. 5, 8). Repertoire is 66 of 100 marks (p. 8).
- Substitution allowed: one piece from Preparatory B, or one Teacher's Choice piece (p. 5).
- Example titles (p. 8): *Allegro in C Major*, op. 1, no. 4 (Reinagle); *The Juggler* (Faber); *Ladybug Waltz* (Ogilvy).

## Preparatory B

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified as a range. Sight playing is a short grand-staff melody "divided between the hands" starting on any of the notes shown in the staff example (p. 14; wording per the errata, see heading 7).
- Staff example, p. 14: treble C4, G4, C5 each with finger 1 (note names inferred from the staff example); bass clef: three starting notes with fingers 1, 5, 5. Fingering is given for the first note of each hand only (p. 14).

### 2. Keys
- Technical: pentascales D, A, F major and E, D minor; one-octave scales C, G major and A minor (natural); contrary motion C major; tonic triads C, G major and A minor (p. 14).
- Ear: chords and playback use C, G major and A minor (p. 14). Sight playing: keys not listed (p. 14).
- Repertoire keys: not specified in the syllabus.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Ear clapback: 3/4 and 4/4; whole, dotted half, half, quarter notes and a pair of eighths; about two measures (read from notation, p. 14).
- Sight rhythm: 4/4; whole, half, quarter and eighth pair; two measures (read from notation, p. 14).
- Sight playing: 4/4; whole, half and quarter notes (read from notation, p. 14).
- Repertoire: not specified in the syllabus.

### 4. Technique (p. 14; patterns on pp. 120-121)
- All from memory.
- **Legato and staccato pentascales:** D, A, F major; E, D minor; HS; tonic to dominant, ascending and descending, ending with a solid/blocked root-position triad; eighth-note pairs at quarter = 60 (read from notation).
- **One-octave scales:** C, G major, A minor (natural); HS, 1 octave; eighth-note pairs at quarter = 60.
- **Contrary-motion scale:** C major; hands together (HT), 1 octave; quarter = 60.
- **Tonic triads, broken:** C, G major, A minor; HS; 1 octave, root position and inversions; eighth-note triplets at quarter = 50.
- No arpeggios and no etude are listed at this level (p. 14; marks table p. 13).

### 5. Hand coordination
- Hands separate for all technical items except the C major contrary-motion scale, which is hands together for one octave (p. 14). Sight playing is "divided between the hands" (p. 14).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Articulation: pentascales tested legato and staccato (p. 14); one-octave scales legato by the general rule (p. 120).
- Dynamics and pedal: not specified in the syllabus.

### 7. Sight-reading (10 marks: rhythm 5, playing 5; p. 13)
- **Rhythm** (p. 14): same procedure as Prep A (tap one measure, then speak/tap/clap); 4/4, two measures (read from notation). Examples only: p. 128.
- **Playing** (p. 14): a short melody on the grand staff, divided between the hands, starting on any note in the given positions. The errata (11 Jan 2024) says it should read "starting on any of these notes". Fingering for the first note of each hand only; 4/4, whole, half and quarter notes (read from notation). Length: "short melody", no measure count given.

### 8. Aural (10 marks: clapback 4, chords 2, playback 4; p. 13)
- **Clapback** (p. 14): as Prep A; about two measures; 3/4 or 4/4.
- **Chords** (p. 14): identify major or minor (root position) after the examiner plays the first five notes of a major or minor scale and the tonic triad once in solid/blocked form.
- **Playback** (p. 14): four-note melody based on the first three notes of a major or minor scale; begin on tonic or mediant; C, G major, A minor; key named, tonic triad once, melody twice.
- Examples only: pp. 124-125.

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three contrasting selections from one "Syllabus List" (no lists A/B/C), 20 marks each; 2 memory marks per piece performed from memory, 6 in total (pp. 5, 13). Repertoire is 66 of 100 marks (p. 13).
- Substitution allowed: one piece from Level 1 List A or List B, or one Teacher's Choice piece (p. 5).
- Example titles (p. 13): *The Calico Cat* (Marlais); *Minuet in F Major* (attr. L. Mozart); *Pumpkin Boogie* (Faber).

## Level 1

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified as a range. Sight playing: a four-measure melody "written on the grand staff and divided between the hands" (p. 20). Both treble and bass clefs are therefore read (inferred from "grand staff").

### 2. Keys
- Technical: two-octave scales C, G, F major and A, E, D minor (natural and harmonic); contrary-motion scale C major; chromatic scale starting on C; tonic triads C, G, F major and A, E, D minor (p. 19).
- Ear: playback C, G major and A minor (p. 20). Sight playing: C, G, F major and A minor (p. 20).
- Repertoire keys: not specified in the syllabus.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Ear clapback: 3/4 and 4/4; whole, dotted half, half, quarter, a pair of eighths, dotted quarter and single eighth; two to three measures (read from notation, p. 20).
- Sight rhythm: 4/4; whole, half, quarter and eighth pair; two measures (read from notation, p. 20).
- Sight playing: 4/4; whole, half, quarter notes (read from notation, p. 20).
- Repertoire: not specified in the syllabus.

### 4. Technique (p. 19; patterns on pp. 120-121)
- All patterns from memory; "All scales are to be played legato" (p. 19).
- **Two-octave scales:** C, G, F major; A, E, D minor (natural and harmonic); HS; 2 octaves; eighth-note pairs at quarter = 69.
- **Contrary-motion scale:** C major; HT; 2 octaves; quarter = 69.
- **Chromatic scale:** starting on C; HS; 1 octave; quarter = 69.
- **Tonic triads** in C, G, F major and A, E, D minor; HS; 1 octave, root position and inversions. Broken: eighth-note triplets at quarter = 50. Solid/blocked: quarter then rest at quarter = 100 (read from notation).
- **Etude:** one from the syllabus list (Celebration Series, Sixth Edition: Piano Etudes 1); a popular-selection substitute is allowed (pp. 5, 19). Etudes need not be memorized (p. 7). Marks: etude 12, technical tests 12 (p. 18).
- Arpeggios: none listed.

### 5. Hand coordination
- Scales and triads are hands separate; the C major contrary-motion scale is hands together, two octaves (p. 19). Sight playing is a four-measure melody divided between the hands (p. 20).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Articulation: "All scales are to be played legato" (p. 19). Solid/blocked triads are played with rests (pattern for Levels 1-4, p. 121).
- Dynamics and pedal: not specified in the syllabus.

### 7. Sight-reading (10 marks: rhythm 3, playing 7; p. 18)
- **Rhythm** (p. 20): tap a steady beat for one measure, then speak/tap/clap the given rhythm; 4/4, two measures (read from notation). Examples only: p. 128.
- **Playing** (p. 20): four-measure melody on the grand staff, divided between the hands; fingering for the first note of each hand only; keys C, G, F major and A minor; 4/4; whole, half and quarter notes (read from notation).

### 8. Aural (10 marks: clapback 2, intervals 2, chords 2, playback 4; p. 18; the errata, entry for p. 118, confirms clapback 2 and playback 4)
- **Clapback** (p. 20): rhythm of a short melody played twice; 3/4 or 4/4; two to three measures.
- **Intervals** (p. 20): identify minor 3rd and major 3rd, played melodically ascending and descending, once; OR sing or hum those intervals (examiner plays the first note once).
- **Chords** (p. 20): identify major or minor (root-position) triad played broken and then solid/blocked, once.
- **Playback** (p. 20): five-note melody based on the first five notes of a major or minor scale; begins on tonic or dominant; C, G major or A minor; key named, tonic triad once, melody twice.
- Examples only: pp. 124-125.

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three contrasting selections, one each from List A (Baroque and Classical), List B (Romantic, 20th- and 21st-century) and List C (Inventions) (pp. 4, 18, 20). Marks: List A 16, List B 18, List C 16; memory 2 per piece, 6 in total; repertoire 56 marks (p. 18).
- Substitution: one piece or the etude from the level above, or a Teacher's Choice piece for List B only (pp. 5-6).
- Example titles (p. 18): *Minuet in C Major*, op. 38, no. 4 (Hässler), List A; *A Simple Waltz* (Burge), List B; *Cranky Cat* (Richert), List C.

## Level 5

### 1. Reading range and clefs

- **Dominant 7th (major keys) and leading-tone diminished 7th (minor keys) chords:** HT; 2 octaves, root position and inversions. Broken alternate-note pattern at quarter = 96; solid/blocked quarters at quarter = 120.
- **Arpeggios:** tonic, dominant 7th, leading-tone diminished 7th; HT; 4 octaves, root position and inversions, individually or in sequence starting with root position or any inversion; sixteenth groups at quarter = 92.
- **Etudes:** two technically contrasting etudes from the syllabus list (Piano Etudes 10) (pp. 89-90).

### 5. Hand coordination
- All technical items are hands together (p. 91). Playback: the left hand plays solid/blocked I, IV, V chords under the melody (the melody hand is not named; right hand inferred) (p. 92). Hands in sight playing: not stated (p. 93).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Articulation: scales legato (p. 120); octave scales tested solid/blocked staccato or broken legato (p. 91). Dynamics and pedal: not specified in the syllabus.

### 7. Sight-reading (pp. 92-93)
- **Rhythm** (p. 92): tap one measure of beat, then speak/tap/clap the rhythm of a given melody; 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 or 6/8; four to six measures (read from notation).
- **Playing** (p. 93): a passage comparable to Level 7 repertoire; keys up to five sharps or flats; any time signature; up to sixteen measures. OR read a lead sheet (vocabulary aligned with Level 10 Harmony) and realize an accompaniment; keys up to four sharps or flats; any time signature; up to sixteen measures.

### 8. Aural (p. 92)
- **Intervals:** as Level 9 plus minor 9th and major 9th; played melodic or harmonic (one form, once); OR sing or hum.
- **Chords:** after a solid/blocked close-position chord, once: major and minor four-note chords (root position, 1st inversion); augmented triad, dominant 7th, diminished 7th, major-major 7th, minor-minor 7th (root position).
- **Chord progressions:** identify each chord of a five-chord progression beginning on the tonic chord, played twice, keyboard style, as I, IV, V, vi (major) or i, iv, V, VI (minor), root position only, or cadential 6/4.
- **Playback:** play back a diatonic melody and harmonize it with I, IV, V in solid/blocked chords in the left hand; begins on tonic, mediant, dominant or upper tonic; any key up to four sharps or flats; 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 or 6/8; four measures. OR play back a two-measure opening, complete the antecedent and improvise a consequent to make an eight-measure contrasting period; keys up to three sharps or flats.
