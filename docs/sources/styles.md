# 1. ABRSM Jazz Piano

## 1a. Practical Grades, Grades 1-5 (1a document)

**General rules for all five grades (pp. 33-34).** Six elements: three Tunes (one each from Blues, Standards, Contemporary Jazz; 30 marks each), Scales and Arpeggios/Broken Chords (21), Quick Study (21), Aural Tests (18) (p. 33). Tunes are unaccompanied, in the printed arrangements; each has a fully notated head, "an indication of the feel (straight 8s or swing)", a tempo indication (the minimum exam speed) and "at least one section for improvisation (solo)" (p. 33). Scales are played "from memory in either straight 8s or swing, as directed by the examiner"; the range of scales "reflects the more common roots, keys and modes found in jazz" and "scales have been integrated with the keys/modes of the tunes, so that improvising arises naturally from scale practice"; pedalling must not be used in scales (p. 33). Pedalling in tunes is welcome but "not expected until Grade 5" (p. 33). Assessment mentions "inventive and stylish improvisation" (p. 33).

### Grade 1
- **Improvisation.** Tunes: at least one improvised solo section each (p. 33). Quick Study (p. 40): play, at sight or by ear, a two-bar passage (one hand only for pianists) in 4/4 within the range of a 4th with chord symbols given, then improvise a two-bar continuation "based on the scale indicated", which is one of those set for this grade, in strict time without a pause; guideline pitches are given for the response. By ear: the examiner plays the two bars three times, states the scale, plays an initial chord and names the starting note. Aural C (p. 43): improvise answering phrases to four two-bar phrases (range of a 4th; answers need not be so limited) in a major or minor key or mode; the examiner names and sounds the key chord, gives the pulse, plays a four-bar introductory groove and continues the groove throughout.
- **Chord work.** Chord symbols are provided in the Quick Study (p. 40). Arpeggios: common chords of G major and D minor, root position only, each hand separately, one octave (p. 35). Voicings or comping patterns: not specified in the syllabus (the Tunes' arrangements carry them; the book introduction was not read).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Tune feel is straight 8s or swing (p. 33); scales in straight 8s or swing as directed (p. 35). Aural A1-A3 (p. 42-43): clap the pulse of a passage in 3 or 4 time, clap on the last beat of each bar, clap the rhythm of a short extract.
- **Style-specific technique.** Dorian on D; Mixolydian on G; C major (two octaves); major pentatonic on C; b3 pentatonic on G (five notes) (p. 35). Hands separately. (The "b3 pentatonic" and the five-note pentatonics are marked "Jazz Piano only", p. 52.)
- **Style-specific aural.** Aural B: sing as an echo four two-bar phrases within a 3rd, in a major or minor key or mode (p. 43). Aural C as above.
- **Kinds of tunes (p. 35).** Three lists of five: Blues, Standards, Contemporary Jazz. Examples: *Bags' Groove* (Milt Jackson, arr. Richard Michael; Blues); *Perdido* (Tizol, arr. Richard Michael; Standards).

### Grade 2
- **Improvisation.** Quick Study (p. 40-41): as Grade 1 but range of a 5th, and the scale may be any set up to and including this grade. Aural C (p. 43): answering phrases to four two-bar phrases, range of a 6th (answers not so limited), with the examiner's groove.
- **Chord work.** Arpeggios of D major and G minor, root position, hands separately, two octaves (p. 36). Chord symbols provided in the Quick Study (p. 40). Voicings: not specified.
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight 8s or swing as directed (p. 36). Aural A2 (p. 43): clap on the second or last beat of each bar, as directed.
- **Style-specific technique (p. 36).** Hands together one octave apart and hands separately: Dorian on A; Mixolydian on D; G major (two octaves); major pentatonic on F; b3 pentatonic on C; minor pentatonic on A (five notes); **blues scale on D (one octave)**; chromatic scale from D, hands separately (one octave).
- **Style-specific aural.** Aural B (echo, 5th) and C as above (p. 43).
- **Kinds of tunes (p. 36).** Examples: *C-Jam Blues* (Ellington, arr. Richard Michael); *Moanin'* (Bobby Timmons, arr. Pete Churchill).

### Grade 3
- **Improvisation.** Quick Study (p. 41): a four-bar passage (one hand only) in 4/4 within a 6th, chord symbols given, plus an improvised four-bar continuation based on a set scale. Aural C (p. 43-44): answering phrases to four two-bar phrases, no range limit stated, key chord named and sounded, four-bar groove then continuing groove.
- **Chord work.** Arpeggios of F and Bb major, A and C minor, root position, hands separately, two octaves (p. 37). Voicings: not specified.
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight 8s or swing (p. 37). Aural A1 (p. 43): pulse in 2, 3 or 4 time; A2: clap a set beat chosen by the examiner.
- **Style-specific technique (p. 37).** Dorian on G; Mixolydian on C; **Lydian on Bb**; F major (two octaves); minor pentatonic on D and C (two octaves); **blues scales on C and F (one octave)**; chromatic scales from Ab, B and C, hands separately (two octaves).
- **Style-specific aural.** B: echo within a 6th (p. 43-44); C: improvised answers (p. 44).
- **Kinds of tunes (p. 37).** Examples: *Fly Me to the Moon* (Bart Howard, arr. Pete Churchill; Standards); *Birks Works* (Gillespie, arr. Pete Churchill; Blues).

### Grade 4
- **Improvisation.** Quick Study (p. 41-42): four-bar passage in 3 or 4 time, "both hands for pianists, the left hand playing a very simple bass line", RH range an octave, chord symbols given, then an improvised four-bar continuation based on a set scale; by ear, both hands' starting notes are named and the bass line is played as an introductory groove (p. 41-42). Aural B1 (p. 44): improvised answering phrases (as before).
- **Chord work.** Arpeggios of A and Eb major, root position, similar motion and hands separately, two octaves (p. 38). **Broken chords formed from C7, G7, Am7 and Gm7**, hands separately (p. 38). Voicings/comping: not specified.
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight 8s or swing (p. 38). Aural A1 (p. 44): state the time of a passage (2, 3 or 4) and **identify the groove as swing, rock or Latin**; A2: clap on the fourth or last eighth note of each bar.
- **Style-specific technique (p. 38).** Dorian on E and C; Mixolydian on A and F; Lydian on G and Eb; D and Bb majors (two octaves); major pentatonic on G and Eb; minor pentatonic on E (two octaves); blues scales on Bb and E (one octave); chromatic scales from any black key named.
- **Style-specific aural.** A1 groove identification; B2 (p. 44): sing and identify two melodic intervals (major 2nd to perfect 5th) from the answering phrases.
- **Kinds of tunes (p. 38).** Examples: *Blue Monk* (Thelonious Monk, arr. Richard Michael; Blues); *Lazy River* (Carmichael & Arodin, arr. John Myhill; Standards).

### Grade 5
- **Improvisation.** Quick Study (p. 42): four-bar passage in 3 or 4 time, both hands (LH simple bass line, RH may include two-note chords), RH range a 10th, chord symbols given, plus an improvised four-bar continuation based on a set scale. Aural B1 (p. 44): improvised answering phrases.
- **Chord work.** Arpeggios of E and Ab major, F# and F minor, root position, hands together one octave apart and separately, two octaves (p. 39). **Broken chords formed from D7, F7, Bb7, Em7, Bm7 and Dm7**, hands separately (p. 39). Voicings: not specified.
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight 8s or swing (p. 39). Aural A1 (p. 44): time in 2, 3, 4 or 5 and groove (swing, rock or Latin); A2: clap a set eighth note of each bar.
- **Style-specific technique (p. 39).** Dorian on B and F; Mixolydian on E and Bb; Lydian on F, C, D and Ab; A and Eb majors (two octaves); major pentatonic on D and Bb; minor pentatonic on B and G (two octaves); blues scales on A, F# and G (one octave); chromatic from any note named. Pedalling may be used in tunes (not expected before this grade, p. 33).
- **Style-specific aural.** B2 (p. 44): sing and identify two melodic intervals (major and minor 2nd to major and minor 6th).
- **Kinds of tunes (p. 39).** Examples: *All Blues* (Miles Davis, arr. Richard Michael; Blues); *Take the 'A' Train* (Strayhorn, arr. Eddie Harvey & Nikki Iles; Standards).

## 1b. Performance Grades from 2026, Grades 1-8 (1b document)

**General (pp. 3-7).** Four tunes in one continuous video, at least one from each of three lists (the fourth from any list); maximum programme time 9 to 28 minutes from Grade 1 to Grade 8 (p. 5). No scales, no aural tests, no quick study: "Jazz Performance Grades are accessible exams given their sole focus on performance, without the assessment of any supporting tests" (p. 3). "No changes have been made to Grades 1 to 5" (p. 4); the Grade 1-5 lists are the same as the Practical Grades (pp. 6, 9-13).

### Grades 1-5 (pp. 9-13)
- **Improvisation.** "All repeats must be followed, including the improvisation/solo sections" (p. 6). How long the solos are and over what: not specified here ("refer to specific guidance in the publications", p. 7).
- **Chord work, feel and rhythm, style-specific technique, aural.** Not specified in this document (none of these is tested separately); the Practical Grades extract above states them.
- **Kinds of tunes.** Lists Blues, Standards, Contemporary Jazz, the same titles as the Practical Grades (p. 3: "drawing on the same repertoire"). Examples per grade: Grade 1 *Bags' Groove*, *Perdido* (p. 9); Grade 2 *C-Jam Blues*, *Moanin'* (p. 10); Grade 3 *Fly Me to the Moon*, *Birks Works* (p. 11); Grade 4 *Blue Monk*, *Lazy River* (p. 12); Grade 5 *All Blues*, *Take the 'A' Train* (p. 13).

### Grades 6-8 (pp. 14-19)
General for these grades: lists A and B are standards from lead sheets (Real Book and similar); list C is arranged solo piano pieces from *Jazz Piano Solo Pieces Grades 6-8* (ABRSM) (pp. 14-19). "At Grades 6 to 8, interpreting lead sheets and playing in a jazz trio context gives pianists a chance to develop genuine Jazz performance skills ... directly applicable to a typical jam session" (p. 4). Lists A and B must be accompanied, either with ABRSM minus-one backing tracks (bass and drums, no piano) or a live rhythm section (guitar, bass, drums; no second piano; the candidate plays all solos) (pp. 5-6). Entry requirement: ABRSM Grade 5 in Theory, Practical Musicianship or a Practical Grades jazz instrument (pp. 14, 16, 18).
- **Improvisation (all three grades).** Structure for lists A and B is fixed: head, solo, return of head (p. 5). "In the Solo sections, the chord sequence should be followed and an improvised solo should be played using suitable jazz vocabulary and comping voicing" (p. 7). Solo lengths read from the listed structures: Grade 6, 16 to 54 bars; Grade 7, 16 to 100 bars; Grade 8, 16 to 100 bars (pp. 14-19, read from the structure lines). List C: embellishment encouraged "from a few simple additions or variations to a more extensive reworking" (p. 7).
- **Chord work.** The head is performed "with a clear melody and appropriate accompaniment realised from the chord sequence on the lead sheet"; the melody is embellished "in a way that suits the individual style of the Tune" (p. 7). Which voicings: not specified beyond "comping voicing" (p. 7).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Not specified as a requirement (some tunes carry a feel in the repertoire; for example a "ballad" and a "waltz" section are named for one Grade 6 list-B tune, p. 15).
- **Style-specific technique, aural.** Not specified.
- **Kinds of tunes.** Grade 6: *There Will Never Be Another You* (list A), *Black Orpheus* (list B), key and structure given (pp. 14-15). Grade 7: *Epistrophy* (A), *Stardust* (B) (pp. 16-17). Grade 8: *Confirmation* (A), *'Round Midnight* (B) (pp. 18-19). Some tunes come from *The Latin Real Book* (for example *O Pato*, G6 list A; *Mas Que Nada*, G7 list B; *Loro*, G8 list A) (pp. 14, 17, 18).

# 2. RSL (Rockschool)

## 2a. RSL Keys, 2019 Edition (2a document)

**General.** Graded Examination = three pieces, Technical Exercises (scales, arpeggios/broken chords, chord voicings; from Grade 4 also technical studies), Sight Reading **or** Improvisation & Interpretation, Ear Tests, General Musicianship Questions (pp. 6-7, 31). Free-choice pieces "must be in a modern popular genre such as Pop; Rock; Jazz; Country; Blues; Soul; Reggae, Film and Musical Theatre" (p. 15). Piece titles: not specified in the spec (they are in the grade books). Every General Musicianship exam includes one question on genre (Debut p. 35 onward; grade-by-grade below). Scale and arpeggio tempos below are the recommended exam tempos stated in the spec.

### Debut
- **Improvisation (p. 33).** Key C major, 60 bpm, 4/4. A 4-6 bar chord progression with chord symbols; improvise single-note melodies with either hand (chord voicings allowed but marks only for melodic content); 90 seconds' preparation, then two playthroughs of the backing track (the second assessed).
- **Chord work.** Chord voicings, from memory: C major or C minor (p. 32). Ear test: Chord Recognition, state which of two C-root chords is major and which minor (p. 34). GM: identify major/minor triad chord symbols (p. 35).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Scales in "straight feel" (p. 32). Not specified otherwise.
- **Style-specific technique (p. 32).** C major pentatonic and A minor pentatonic, right hand only, one octave, 52 bpm.
- **Aural/listening.** Melodic Recall (higher or lower) and Chord Recognition (p. 34).
- **GM genre question (p. 35).** "Genre of the piece."

### Grade 1
- **Improvisation (p. 39).** F major or G major, 70 bpm, 4/4, 4-6 bar chord progression, single-note melodies with either hand.
- **Chord work (pp. 38, 40).** Chord voicings from memory: C diminished or C augmented triads. Arpeggio/broken chords of F, G, D minor, E minor; C augmented and C diminished arpeggios (p. 38). Chord Recognition from major, minor, diminished, augmented (p. 40). GM: interval construction of major/minor/augmented/diminished triads (p. 41).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight feel (p. 38). GM rhythm includes eighth-note triplets (p. 41).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 38).** Pentatonics, right hand only, two octaves, 60 bpm: F major, G major, D minor, E minor pentatonic.
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall (three notes) and Chord Recognition (p. 40).
- **GM genre question (p. 41).** Different artists from the same genre as the piece.

### Grade 2
- **Improvisation (p. 45).** B-flat major or D major, 60-95 bpm, 4/4, 4-6 bars, single-note melodies with either hand.
- **Chord work (pp. 44, 46, 47).** Chord voicings from memory: one of major 7th, minor 7th, dominant 7th. Arpeggios include C major 7th and C minor 7th (p. 44). Chord Recognition adds dominant 7 (p. 46). GM: dom7 and sus4 chord construction (p. 47).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight feel (p. 44). GM rhythm: dotted quarters and eighths, eighth-note triplets (p. 47).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 44).** Pentatonics (Bb, D major; G, B minor), right hand; **C blues scale, hands separately** (right hand and left hand), two octaves for the scales, 66 bpm.
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall played back (2-bar melody, range up to a 4th); Chord Recognition (p. 46).
- **GM genre question (p. 47).** Different song titles from the same genre.

### Grade 3
- **Improvisation (p. 51).** E-flat major or A major, 60-95 bpm, 4/4, 4-8 bar chord progression, single-note melodies with either hand.
- **Chord work (pp. 50, 52-53).** Chord voicings from memory: C major harmonised scale in 7ths with root in the bass, or with the 3rd in the bass, to a metronome (80 bpm, half notes) (p. 50). Arpeggios include C dominant seventh and C minor sixth (p. 50). Chord Recognition of two of the chords, from major, minor, diminished, augmented, dominant 7 and major 7th (p. 52). GM: chord degrees of the scale (p. 53).
- **Feel and rhythm.** **Scales and arpeggios "in straight or swung feel"** (p. 50), first appearance. GM: quarter/eighth triplets (p. 53).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 50).** Pentatonics (Eb, A major; C, F# minor), right hand only.
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall (range up to a 5th) and Chord Recognition (p. 52).
- **GM genre question (p. 53).** Different artists and song titles from the same genre.

### Grade 4
- **Improvisation (p. 57).** A-flat major or E major, 60-130 bpm, 3/4 or 4/4, 8-12 bar chord progression; "diatonic single note melodies with right hand and chords in left hand".
- **Chord work (pp. 57, 58, 59).** Chord voicings from memory: major 9, dominant 9 or minor 9 (p. 57). Arpeggios include C minor 7b5 (or half diminished), C diminished 7, and C minor 9 and C dominant 9, each hand separately (p. 56). New ear test **Harmonic Recall**: hear a progression of I, IV, V in C major, play the chords back; voicing not specified, "the diatonic progression needs to be correct" (p. 58). GM: identify intervals relative to a chord symbol (root, 3rd, 5th, 7th) (p. 59).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight or swung feel (p. 56).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 56).** **C dorian and C mixolydian modes**, hands separately; pentatonics (Ab, E major; F, C# minor), right hand only; a technical study of the candidate's choice played to a backing track (style not specified, p. 57).
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall (range up to a 6th) and Harmonic Recall (p. 58).
- **GM genre question (p. 59).** "Melodic / harmonic / rhythmic devices indicative of the genre chosen."

### Grade 5
- **Improvisation (p. 64).** D-flat major or B major, 60-130 bpm, 3/4 or 4/4, 8-16 bar chord progression; diatonic single-note melodies right hand, chords left hand.
- **Chord work (pp. 62-63, 65-66).** Chord voicings from memory: C min11 or C maj9#11 (p. 63). Arpeggios include C minor-major 7, C7add4, C maj9#11, C min11 (p. 62). Harmonic Recall with chords I, II, IV, V, VI and "seventh chords are introduced" (p. 65). GM: intervals relative to a chord symbol up to the 13th (p. 66).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight or swung, three octaves (p. 62).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 62).** **C lydian and C phrygian modes**, hands separately, two octaves; pentatonics right hand only; technical study to backing track.
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall (range up to an octave) and Harmonic Recall (p. 65).
- **GM genre question (p. 66).** Devices indicative of the genre.

### Grade 6
- **Improvisation (p. 71).** G-flat major or F# major, 60-130 bpm, 3/4 or 4/4, 8-16 bar chord progression; "improvise melodies and chordal comping between left hand and right hand, in any way they wish, interpreting the chord progression and backing track".
- **Chord work (pp. 69-70, 72-73).** Chord voicings from memory: C maj13, C 13 or C m13 (p. 70). Arpeggios include C13#11 and C7#5#9 (p. 69). Harmonic Recall in C major with I-VI including seventh chords (p. 72). GM: **appropriate modes to play over maj7, min7, dom7, sus4, 9sus4 and maj9# chords** (p. 73).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight or swung, four octaves (p. 69).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 69).** **C lydian dominant and C locrian**, two octaves; pentatonics (F#/Gb major, D#/Eb minor); technical study to backing track.
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall and Harmonic Recall (p. 72).
- **GM genre question (p. 73).** Devices indicative of the genre.

### Grade 7
- **Improvisation (p. 78).** C-sharp major or C-flat major, 60-130 bpm, 16-20 bar chord progression, melody plus chordal comping in any way chosen.
- **Chord work (pp. 77, 79-80).** Chord voicings from memory: C dominant 7b9, C dominant 7#9#5 or C dominant 13#11 (p. 77). Arpeggios include C9#5 (p. 76). Harmonic Recall of any combination of chords from the C major scale including sevenths (p. 79). GM: fingering for chord tones up to the 13th (p. 80).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight or swung "as directed by the examiner", legato or staccato as directed (p. 76).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 76).** **C altered scale and C phrygian dominant** (two octaves), C major in thirds, chromatic in minor thirds; technical study to backing track.
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall (C major or C minor) and Harmonic Recall (p. 79).
- **GM genre question (p. 80).** Devices indicative of the genre. GM harmony: "Scales / modes which are being played" (p. 80).

### Grade 8
- **Improvisation (p. 85).** Any key, 60-200 bpm, 16-24 bar chord progression, melody and chordal comping in any way chosen.
- **Chord work (pp. 84, 86-87).** Chord voicings from memory: C13b9#11 or C diminished major 7 (p. 84). Arpeggios include C13b9#11 (p. 83). Harmonic Recall from the C major or C minor scale including sevenths (p. 86). GM: fingering to execute voice leading between two consecutive chord voicings (p. 87).
- **Feel and rhythm.** Straight or swung, legato or staccato as directed (p. 83); the examiner can ask for any earlier Group A scale or Group B arpeggio, with feel (straight or swung) and tempo stated by the examiner (p. 83).
- **Style-specific technique (p. 83).** **C half-whole diminished and C whole-half diminished**, two octaves; technical study to backing track.
- **Aural.** Melodic Recall (C major, natural or harmonic minor) and Harmonic Recall (p. 86).
- **GM genre question (p. 87).** Devices indicative of the genre; GM harmony: scales/modes being played.

## 2b. RSL Piano, 2025 Edition (2b document): style-specific items only

**What it does not say.** The grade units do not name any genre, feel or style-specific scale. Technical work is scales, arpeggios and studies (for example Debut p. 28). Piece titles: not specified (in the grade book). Adaptation: candidates marked on "personalised material in a stylistically appropriate way" (p. 18).
- **Improvisation & Interpretation, per grade (the unseen alternative to sight reading).** The examiner gives a chord progression with chord symbols and a backing track; the candidate improvises "either a melodic line or a chordal part to complement the backing track"; 90 seconds' preparation, two playthroughs (pp. 29, 34, 39, 44, 50, 57, 64, 71, 78). - **General Musicianship.** Grades 4 and 5: "Give a brief explanation of the genre and historical context of the chosen piece" (pp. 52, 59); Grades 6-8: stylistic and technical considerations of the chosen piece (pp. 66, 73, 80). Chord-related questions: intervals of a dominant 7th chord from Grade 3 (p. 46), cadence chords from Grade 6 (p. 66).

# 4. ANZCA Modern Pianoforte 2025-27 (4 document)

**General (Modern stream).** Printed pages here. The Modern Pianoforte syllabus (pp. 41-90) is a graded exam from Introductory to Grade 8, with Associate (A.Dip.A.) and Licentiate diplomas. Exam elements: technical work, list pieces, sight reading **or** improvisation (Grades 2-8, 10 marks), aural tests (8 marks), general knowledge (8 marks) (e.g. Grade 2 p. 50; Grade 3 p. 54). List pieces are chosen from six categories: **Pop and Rock, Blues, Boogie** (from Preliminary), **Ragtime** (from Grade 1), **Latin American** and **Jazz and Popular Standards** (from Grade 3) (pp. 45-82). At each grade up to two pieces may use backing tracks; from Grade 3 "at least one piece must contain embellishment (e.g. by adding an introduction, coda, harmony notes, rhythms etc.)" (p. 55); at all levels embellishment/improvisation in Modern pieces is "encouraged, but not mandatory" in Performance exams (p. 37). Several set pieces are marked "(Improvisation required)" (Hunter, *All That Jazz*, from Grade 3: pp. 55, 58, 60; e.g. *Swing Easy*, *F Minor Blues*, *Blues in C*). Aural and general-knowledge items that are style-specific are listed per grade; the general aural items (rhythm clapping, intervals) are not repeated.

### Improvisation test (pp. 25-26): Grade 2 to A.Dip.A.
- **Grade 2 (p. 25).** A four-bar melody with chord symbols; the candidate plays the melody as written with an improvised accompaniment according to the chord symbols; time signatures 4/4 or 3/4; keys C major, G major, A minor; chords I, II (major keys only), IV and V7; progressions I-II-V7-I or I-IV-V7-I, one chord per bar; accompaniment "simple block chords, inverted as required for smooth voice leading"; introduction, coda and melodic improvisation not required. (Improvisation here is accompaniment from chord symbols; melodic embellishment is first asked at Grade 4.)
- **Grade 3 (p. 25).** As Grade 2 but "a simple rhythmic- or arpeggiated-chord accompaniment"; keys up to one sharp or flat.
- **Grade 4 (p. 25).** An eight-bar melody with chord symbols (a repeated four-bar phrase); play the first phrase as written, then "improvise simple melodic embellishments in the second phrase"; chords I, II, IV, V7, VI; "from this level onwards, candidates may freely use other chord extensions, alterations, substitutions etc."; no set progressions; introduction and/or coda optional.
- **Grade 5 (p. 25).** As Grade 4 plus a two-bar introduction (coda optional); keys up to two sharps or flats.
- **Grade 6 (pp. 25-26).** A 12-bar melody (A-A'-B or A-B-A'); two-bar introduction, the first phrase as written, the other eight bars with melodic embellishment, two-bar coda; phrase A has a notated accompaniment "which the candidate will continue in a style consistent with the given part"; chords I-VI, with seventh chords ii7, iii7, vi7 (major keys) and iv7 (minor keys) allowed.
- **Grade 7 (p. 26).** A 16-bar melody (AABA, AABB or ABAB); keys up to three sharps or flats; occasional chords from related keys; major 7th chords and iim7b5 (minor keys) may be included.
- **Grade 8 (p. 26).** A 32-bar melody (four eight-bar phrases); first phrase as written with accompaniment, the remaining 24 bars with melodic embellishment and consistent accompaniment; chords include VII (diminished/half-diminished/diminished 7th) and sus4; introduction and coda up to four bars each.
- **Associate Performer Diploma, Modern only (p. 26; compulsory first section).** A 32-bar lead sheet with chord symbols and no written accompaniment: play a four-bar introduction, play the melody once with an accompaniment style of the candidate's choice (slight embellishment acceptable), repeat "with embellishment or more elaborate improvisation", and play a four-bar coda; keys up to four sharps or flats; "The repeat may be treated as a 'solo' and/or include a change in the accompaniment style." The A.Dip.A. also requires at least one piece with "embellishment or improvisation" (p. 84).

### Per-grade style-specific skills (Modern Pianoforte syllabus)
For all grades the following headings state what the syllabus says for the style skills; chord work other than the above improvisation test is "Not specified" unless shown.
- **Preliminary-Grade 1 (pp. 44-50).** Technical work is general (major and minor scales). Style content only through list categories Pop and Rock, Blues, Boogie (Preliminary) and Ragtime (Grade 1). Examples: Grade 1 ANZCA Series 2 *Slow Walkin' Blues*, *Steam Train Boogie* (p. 39). Improvisation test: not offered (starts at Grade 2). Not specified: feel, chord work, style scales.
- **Grade 2 (pp. 50-53).** Improvisation test above. Style scales: not specified. Kinds of pieces: Pop and Rock, Blues, Boogie, Ragtime categories (pp. 51-53). Examples: *Blues for Beyonce*, *Walking on Blues Street* (ANZCA Series 2 Grade 2, p. 39).
- **Grade 3 (pp. 54-58).** Improvisation test above; embellishment required in at least one piece (p. 55); new categories Latin American and Jazz and Popular Standards (pp. 57-58); Hunter *All That Jazz* pieces "(Improvisation required)" (p. 58). Examples: *Chromatic Boogie*, *Latinesca* (ANZCA Series 2 Grade 3, p. 40). Aural: Grade 3 identifies an arpeggiated triad as major or minor (p. 28). Not specified: feel, style scales.
- **Grade 4 (pp. 59-63).** Style scale: **"Minor blues: E and F"**, hands separately and together, two octaves, minimum speed MM 72 in semiquavers (p. 59). Improvisation test above. Aural: answer a phrase by clapping an improvised rhythm; identify major, minor or dominant 7th chords in root position (p. 28). General knowledge: syncopation and the form (e.g. "12-bar blues") and style of each piece performed; "Describe the style of this piece" (p. 32). Examples: *Friday Groove*, *Ragtime Blues* (ANZCA Series 2 Grade 4, p. 40).
- **Grade 5 (pp. 64-68).** Style scale: **Dorian mode: D and A flat**, two octaves (p. 64). Improvisation test above. Aural: create a melody on black notes (F sharp to F sharp) from a given rhythm; clap an improvised rhythm answer; identify major, minor, dominant 7th or augmented chords (p. 28). GK: more detailed definition of form and style; knowledge of other works by the composer (p. 32). Examples: *Lotus Blossom Blues*, *Maracas* (p. 40).
- **Grade 6 (pp. 69-73).** Style scale: **Mixolydian mode: G and C sharp**, four octaves (p. 69). Improvisation test above. Aural: create a melody in C major from a four-bar rhythm; identify the progressions I-IV-V-I, I-IV-V7-I, I-ii-V-I, I-ii-V7-I, and chords including diminished (p. 29). GK: modulations, another composer in the same genre (p. 32). Examples: *Barnyard Boogie*, *Espagnol* (p. 40).
- **Grade 7 (pp. 74-79).** Style scale: **Lydian mode: F and A flat** (p. 75). Improvisation test above. Aural: create a melody in a major key of the candidate's choice **with a simple accompaniment**; identify minor-key progressions i-iv-V-i, i-ii-V-i; major 7th chords; cadences (p. 29). GK: origins and development of the style of the piece (p. 33). Examples: *Midnight at 'The Blues Club'*, *Orang Utan Rag* (p. 40).
- **Grade 8 (pp. 80-83).** Style scale: **Phrygian mode: E and C; Locrian mode: B and A**, four octaves (p. 80). Improvisation test above. Aural: create a melody in a minor key with a simple accompaniment; identify minor 7th chords and modulations (p. 29). GK: "characteristics, origins and development of the style of this piece, including reference to the greatest exponents" (p. 33). Examples: *Shades of Blue*, *Boogie Etude* (ANZCA Series 2 Grade 8, p. 82).
- **Associate (A.Dip.A.) and Licentiate (pp. 84-90).** Improvisation test above for the Associate; Licentiate list guides include pieces "with improvisation" (e.g. a Funk piece, p. 87). Jazz-standards and blues categories continue (pp. 85-86).

### Modern-stream items in the Theory of Music exam (separate exam, not the practical exam; pp. 13-16)

# Closing note: highest grade at which these syllabuses still add style-specific content

- **Jazz.** To Grade 8 in three sources: ABRSM Performance Grades 6-8 (lead sheets, trio-style backing, improvised solos over the changes, p. 4, p. 7), RSL Keys (altered, phrygian dominant, diminished scales and 7b9/7#9#5/13b9#11 voicings at Grades 7-8, pp. 76-84), and Trinity (jazz as an improvising style at Grade 7, hybrid jazz styles at Grade 8, p. 63). ABRSM's Practical Grades with scales, groove identification and quick-study improvisation stop at Grade 5.
- **Blues.** Blues scales are set at ABRSM Jazz Grades 2-5 (p. 36-39), RSL Keys Grade 2 (C blues, p. 44) and ANZCA Grade 4 (minor blues, p. 59). Trinity names blues only as the Grade 3 improvising style (p. 62). The blues repertoire categories run to Grade 5 (ABRSM lists, pp. 35-39) and to Grade 8 and the diplomas (ANZCA Blues category, pp. 47-86); a named blues or boogie technique after Grade 5 appears only as Trinity's Grade 7 "boogie-woogie" improvising style (p. 63).
- **Chords/pop.** To Grade 8: RSL Keys tests chord voicings every grade Debut-8 (to C13b9#11, p. 84) and harmonic recall to Grade 8 (p. 86); Trinity's improvising chord requirements run from I, IV, V to "any common chords" at Grade 8 (pp. 62-63).
- **Rock.** Trinity names rock only in the improvising styles at Initial-Grade 1 (simple rock, heavy rock), Grade 6 (metal) and Grade 7 (boogie-style rock) (pp. 62-63); ABRSM Jazz asks to identify a "rock" groove at Grades 4-5 (p. 44); ANZCA has a Pop and Rock repertoire category to Grade 8 and the diplomas (pp. 47-87); RSL's specs name rock only as a free-choice genre (Keys p. 15).
- **Improvisation.** RSL Improvisation & Interpretation test at every grade Debut-8 (Keys pp. 33-85; Piano 2025 pp. 29-78); Trinity improvising test Initial-Grade 8 with solos of up to 16 bars in songs at Grade 7 and "any length" at Grade 8 (pp. 57, 62-63); ANZCA improvisation test Grades 2-8 and the Associate diploma (a lead-sheet solo repeat, p. 26), though at Grades 2-5 it is accompaniment and embellishment rather than soloing; ABRSM Practical Grades improvise two to four bars on a set scale at Grades 1-5 and the Performance Grades add solos over chord changes at Grades 6-8 (pp. 40-42, 7).
