# ABRSM Piano Practical Grades: Initial Grade to Grade 8 (extract)

- **Source title:** ABRSM, *Practical Music Grades, Piano, Syllabus 2025 & 2026, Qualification Specification* (official PDF, 63 pages; short name below: **Spec 25-26**). Valid 1 January 2025 to 31 December 2026 (p. 3, "Validity of this specification"). It contains the repertoire lists, the scales and arpeggios, the sight-reading parameters table and the aural test requirements, so one document covers all four areas.
- **Exact URL:** https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20&%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf
- **Edition used and why:** Spec 25-26 is the edition in force on the access date (2026-10-09). A newer edition exists: *Practical Music Grades, Piano, Syllabus 2027 & 2028, Qualification Specification* (**Spec 27-28**; 63 pages; valid 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2028), https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/Piano%20Practical%20Grades%20Qualification%20Specification%202027%20&%202028.pdf . Spec 25-26 states that the scales and arpeggios, sight-reading and aural requirements "stay the same as the preceding syllabus" (p. 11), and Spec 27-28 was compared line by line against Spec 25-26 for those parts: grade-page scale tables, the sight-reading parameters table, the speed table and the aural tests (PDF pages 14-18, 21-45, 46-53) differ only in page headers and one cross-reference ("pages 12-14" for "13-14"), plus where the general scale rules fall on the pages. The sight-reading table notation was read from Spec 25-26 only. Spec 27-28 repertoire lists are not read here: example titles below are from Spec 25-26 and will change in 2027.

**Citation convention.** "p. N" is the page number printed on the page of Spec 25-26, which is the PDF page index minus 1 (for example printed p. 16 is PDF page 17). Sections: general pieces rules pp. 11-13; scales and arpeggios general rules p. 13, speeds p. 14; sight-reading pp. 15-16; aural introduction p. 45. Per grade: Initial pieces pp. 18-19, scales p. 20, aural p. 46; Grade 1 pieces pp. 21-22, scales p. 23, aural p. 46; Grade 2 pieces pp. 24-25, scales p. 26, aural p. 47; Grade 3 pieces pp. 27-28, scales p. 29, aural p. 47; Grade 4 pieces pp. 30-31, scales p. 32, aural p. 48; Grade 5 pieces pp. 33-34, scales p. 35, aural p. 49; Grade 6 pieces pp. 36-37, scales p. 38, aural p. 50; Grade 7 pieces pp. 39-40, scales p. 41, aural p. 51; Grade 8 pieces pp. 42-43, scales p. 44, aural p. 52. In the extracted text a flat sign appears as "-" and a sharp as "+"; flats and sharps were confirmed against page images for Grades 4, 5 and 6 (the other grades were taken from the extracted text) and are written out in words here. Musical notation (note values, rests) in the sight-reading table was read from the page image and is marked "(read from notation)". "(inferred)" marks anything not stated in words. "Not specified" means the syllabus says nothing on the point. Marks, exam procedure and substitution rules are left out.

**General rules that apply to every grade.**
- Scales and arpeggios: all from memory; played in even notes; legato unless the syllabus says staccato; no pedalling; hands together means one octave apart unless stated; any fingering that works; any starting octave provided the range is covered (p. 13). Speeds are "a general guide" (p. 14, table repeated per grade below; all speeds relate to a group of four beamed eighth notes, read from notation).
- Sight-reading: a short unaccompanied piece, not seen before, with half a minute to look through; any fingering printed is guidance only (p. 15). The parameters table (p. 16) shows what is "introduced at each grade" and the parameters are "presented cumulatively, i.e. once introduced they apply for all later grades (gradually progressing in difficulty)". Each grade's heading 7 below lists only what is new at that grade; earlier items still apply. The table gives no clef or note-range column.
- Pieces: one piece from each of lists A, B and C (p. 11). List A "generally faster moving and require technical agility"; List B "more lyrical and invite expressive playing"; List C "reflect a wide variety of musical traditions, styles and characters" (p. 11). Pedalling: examiners take account of "the use and control of pedalling"; printed pedal marks may be adapted or omitted; pieces heavily reliant on pedal should be avoided if appropriate pedalling cannot be managed (p. 12). Duets (one piece, Initial to Grade 3 only, p. 12).

## Initial Grade

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Note range and clefs: not specified. Sight-reading is each hand playing separately in 5-finger position, tonic to dominant (p. 16); this is the only range limit given (sight-reading parameter).
- Aural sung echoes lie within tonic to mediant (p. 46); these are heard, not read.

### 2. Keys
- Scales and arpeggios: C major; D minor (natural or harmonic or melodic, at the candidate's choice) (p. 20).
- Sight-reading: C major, D minor (p. 16). Aural: sung echoes in a major key (p. 46). Repertoire keys: not specified.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Sight-reading (p. 16): 4/4 with 4 bars; 2/4 with 6 bars (the table pairs length and time on two rows; whether the test uses either one is not stated); half note, quarter note, a pair of eighths, whole rest (read from notation).
- Aural clapping: two-bar phrases in three or four time; sung phrases one bar in 4/4 (p. 46).
- Repertoire time signatures and rhythms: not specified.

### 4. Technique
- C major scale, 1 octave, hands separately; D minor scale (natural or harmonic or melodic), 1 octave, hands separately (p. 20).
- Contrary-motion scale: C major, range "a 5th", hands starting on the tonic (unison); pattern shown in notation, not transcribed (p. 20).
- Arpeggios: C major and D minor, range "a 5th", hands separately; pattern shown in notation (p. 20).
- Speed guide: scales quarter = 54; arpeggios quarter = 52 (p. 14).

### 5. Hand coordination
- Scales and arpeggios hands separately; the contrary-motion scale uses both hands (p. 20). Sight-reading: each hand playing separately (p. 16). Pieces: not specified (a duet part may be played for one piece, p. 18).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Sight-reading: legato phrases, staccato; f and p (p. 16). Scales and arpeggios legato, no pedal (pp. 13, 20). Pedal in sight-reading: none at this grade (inferred from the table, first pedal is Grade 6). Pieces: general pedalling rule p. 12; some Initial pieces are marked "pedalling optional" or "una corda optional" (pp. 18-19).

### 7. Sight-reading (p. 16)
- 4 bars (4/4) or 6 bars (2/4); C major, D minor; each hand separately, 5-finger position tonic to dominant; half, quarter, pair of eighths, whole rest (read from notation); legato phrases and staccato; f and p.

### 8. Aural (p. 46)
- A: clap the pulse of a piece played by the examiner. B: clap back the rhythm of two two-bar melody phrases (three or four time). C: sing back two one-bar phrases in a major key, 4/4, within tonic to mediant, after hearing the key chord and tonic. D: answer a question on one named feature, dynamics (loud/quiet) or articulation (smooth/detached).

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three pieces, one from each of A, B, C (p. 18); roles of the lists as in the general rules above. One may be a duet (p. 18).
- Examples: *Gopak* (Haughton), List A (p. 18); *Melody in G*, No. 39 from *École préliminaire de piano*, Op. 101 (Beyer), List B (p. 18); *The Elephant Herd* from *Safari* (June Armstrong), List C (p. 19).

## Grade 1

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified. Sight-reading: any 5-finger position, hands still separate (p. 16). Aural sung echoes within tonic to mediant (p. 46).

### 2. Keys

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Sight-reading new: 3/4; dotted half note, four beamed eighths; half rest, quarter rest (read from notation) (p. 16).
- Aural: pulse in two time or three time (p. 46). Repertoire: not specified.

### 4. Technique

- Speed guide: scales quarter = 60; arpeggios quarter = 58 (p. 14).

### 5. Hand coordination
- Hands together enters for the C major scale, 1 oct., and the contrary-motion scale; other items hands separately (p. 23). Sight-reading still hands separately (p. 16; hands together arrives at Grade 2).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Sight-reading new: occasional accidentals (within minor keys only); slurs, accents; mf and mp; cresc. and dim. hairpins (p. 16). Scales and arpeggios legato, no pedal (pp. 13, 23). Pedal in sight-reading: none (inferred).

### 7. Sight-reading (p. 16)
- 3/4 added; G, F majors, A minor added; any 5-finger position; occasional accidentals in minor keys only; dotted half, four beamed eighths, half and quarter rests (read from notation); slurs, accents; mf, mp; hairpins. Length: no new figure given.

### 8. Aural (p. 46)
- A: clap the pulse and say whether it is two time or three time (louder clap on strong beats). B: sing back three two-bar phrases, major key, tonic to mediant. C: say whether a change of pitch in a two-bar phrase is near the beginning or the end. D: two features: dynamics (loud/quiet, sudden/gradual) then articulation (smooth/detached).

### 9. Repertoire demands
- One piece from each of A, B, C (p. 21 onwards; roles in general rules).
- Examples: *Fireworks Minuet* (Handel, arr. Bullard), List A (p. 21); *A Song of Erin* (Dunhill), List B (p. 21); *Cyberspace Detective* (Amit Anand), List C (p. 22).
