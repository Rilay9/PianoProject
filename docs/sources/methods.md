Scope: Faber *Basic Piano Adventures* (Primer to Level 5) and *Adult Piano Adventures* (Books 1-2); Alfred's *Basic Piano Library* (Levels 1A to 6) and *Basic Adult Piano Course* (Levels 1-3). Only the two publishers' own material (their sites, PDFs, and Hal Leonard's product and concept pages, Hal Leonard being Faber's distributor). Citation style follows `docs/sources/rcm.md`. This file extracts; it proposes no rungs, placements or curriculum changes.

**Date accessed (every document below):** 2026-10-09.

**How it was read.** PDFs were saved and text-extracted, or, where they are scans, read from page images ("read from images"). Ordinary web pages were read through a fetch tool that returns a summary of the page, so wording attributed to a web page is a paraphrase of that page, not verbatim; the "Things to Know", "Q&A", Hal Leonard and Alfred product pages are all in that class. Where a quotation is given in quote marks it is from a PDF (extracted text) unless marked otherwise. "(inferred)" marks anything not stated outright. "Not stated" means the sources read say nothing on the point. "Not publicly available" means no readable contents were found for that book.

# Part 1. Faber Basic Piano Adventures

Contents of the books as published by Faber are available at three depths: concept lists for every level (F1, F3, F4, F5), page-by-page unit lists for Primer to 2B only (F2), and Q&A pages with scattered page numbers (F5). There is no unit or page table of contents for Levels 3A, 3B, 4, 5 in anything read ("not publicly available" for those, below).

Faber's per-level concept list as printed by Faber itself (F1, F3 agree except where noted): see the "Level correlation" section for the F1 table.

## Primer Level (Lesson Book 72 pp.; 48 pieces listed, F6)

Units (F2, Primer, lesson pages): 1 Introduction to Playing pp.4-13; 2 Basic Rhythms pp.14-22; 3 Key Names A B C D E F G pp.24-33; 4 Orientation to the Staff pp.34-41; 5 Middle C D E F G Notes pp.42-45; 6 Middle C B A G F Notes pp.46-50; 7 Skips on the Staff pp.52-55; 8 Bass C D E F G Notes pp.56-63; 9 The Tie pp.64-67; 10 The Quarter Rest pp.68-70.

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Concept list: "Staff notes: Bass C to Treble G" (F1; F3). TTK: grand-staff notes "from low C in the bass to high G in the treble" (F5 TTK, paraphrase).
- Order of introduction (F5 Q&A): pre-reading by tracking up, down, repeat before the staff; three guide notes first, Middle C, Treble G, Bass F; then Treble D E F and Bass B A G fill the gaps (a nine-note range); then Bass C (the term "octave") and Bass D, E, completing the C five-finger scale on the bass staff.
- Page order (F2 Primer): staff and grand staff p.34; bass and treble clef p.35; Middle C p.36; Treble G p.37; Bass F p.40; Middle C-D-E-F-G p.42; Middle C-B-A-G-F p.46; skips p.52; Bass D p.56, Bass C p.57; the octave p.59.
- Note names A B C D E F G taught as "the music alphabet" p.24 before the staff (F2).
- Reading approach: steps and skips and recurring patterns (F5 TTK; F3 "steps and skips on the staff").

### 2. Keys and positions
- Hand position and finger numbers (F1; F3; F2 pp.5-6); "Two Black Ants" p.10 and "Two Blackbirds" p.11 locate fingers 2 and 3 by the two-black-key group (F5 Q&A); "Hopping Hand Position" p.45 (F2).
- C five-finger scale at p.28 ("C 5-Finger Scale; C-D-E-F-G March") (F2). Hal Leonard's description: "five-finger patterns in C" (F6 Primer, paraphrase).
- Other keys and key signatures: not stated.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Note values: quarter, half, dotted half, whole (F1; F3). Order (F2): quarter p.14, half p.18, whole p.21, dotted half p.32.
- Time signatures: 4/4 and 3/4 (F1; F3). Placement (F2): a time signature at p.44 ("The Dance Band") and another at p.47 ("Petite Minuet"); that these are 4/4 then 3/4 is (inferred) from the chart's "4/4 and 3/4" list, the symbols are too small to read in the PDF.
- Rests and ties: "the quarter rest" p.68 (Unit 10) and "the tie" p.64 (Unit 9) (F1; F2). No other rest is listed.
- Teaching note: common errors are rushing half and whole notes; suggested fixes include clapping the beat and teacher duets (F5 Q&A).

### 4. Technique
- Five Technique Secrets: good posture, rounded hand shape, firm fingertips, arm weight, correct thumb position (F5 TTK, Q&A); used as daily warm-ups before the Technique & Artistry exercises, each unit ending in an "Artistry Magic Piece" (F5 Q&A).
- Five-finger patterns: C only (see 2). "C Scale (Bass Clef)" at p.58 (F2) is, by the unit title "Bass C D E F G Notes", the five-note C pattern, (inferred). Scales longer than five notes, chords, crossings: not stated.
- "The Octave" p.59 (F2) is a reading topic; octave playing: not stated.

### 5. Hand coordination
- "The Pecking Rooster (for Left Hand)" p.8 and "The Pecking Hen (for Right Hand)" p.9 (F2) are hand-specific starting pieces. Teacher duets are used for rhythmic independence (F5 TTK, Q&A). Whether any Primer piece asks the hands to play together: not stated.

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Dynamics and "coloristic use of the pedal" (F6 Primer, paraphrase). TTK: using the damper pedal "for expressive, dreamy tone" (F5 TTK). Which dynamic marks and which page pedal first appears: not stated. Articulation (legato, staccato): not stated at this level (first listed at Level 1).

### 7. Theory at the keyboard
- Steps and skips, letter names independent of finger number (F6; F3). Interval names, chords, chord symbols, harmonising: not stated. "Always look for patterns" (analysis) is a stated method (F4 Primer wheel, F5 TTK).

### 8. Other
- Improvisation on black keys only or white keys only is mentioned (F5 Q&A). The Sightreading Book starts after "Middle C March", which the Q&A places at p.40 but F2 lists at p.36 (inconsistent; flagged, not resolved). Ear training, sightreading, improvisation and technique "easy to overlook" (F5 Q&A). Teacher duets; a Player App with orchestrated accompaniments (F5 TTK). Short pieces (about 8 measures) so memorising can start early, from "Two Black Ants" p.10 (F5 Q&A).

## Level 1 (Lesson Book 64 pp.; 51 pieces listed, F6)

Units (F2, Level 1): Primer review p.4; Head Start for Treble and Bass Clef Lines p.6; "Firefly" review piece p.8; 1 Legato and Staccato pp.10-17; 2 Treble F-A-C-E pp.18-21; 3 Treble C-D-E-F-G on the Staff pp.22-25; 4 Intervals (2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th) pp.26-35; 5 Half Rest and Whole Rest pp.36-39; 6 Sharps and Flats pp.40-45; 7 Tonic and Dominant pp.46-47; 8 The C (I) Chord pp.48-51; 9 The V7 Chord pp.52-55; 10 G Five-Finger Scales on the Grand Staff pp.56-61; Adventure Scale and Chord Warm-Ups p.62; certificate p.64.

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- "Reading across the grand staff" (F1); Hal Leonard: the book "introduces all the notes of the grand staff" and interval reading through the 5th (F6, paraphrase).
- Treble line notes E G B D F learned as a set by skips; bass staff anchored by three "Smart Notes", A (top line), D (middle line), G (bottom line) (F5 Q&A). Treble spaces F A C E ("FACE", Unit 2, p.18) and C-D-E-F-G above Middle C (Unit 3, p.22) (F2).
- Intervals 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th (and octave) (F1; F3), Unit 4 pp.26-35 (F2).
- Octave signs and pickup notes are listed among the concepts (F5 TTK, paraphrase).

### 2. Keys and positions
- C and G five-finger scales (F1; F3); Unit 10 "G Five-Finger Scales on the Grand Staff" (F2). A Challenge Section (pp.62-63) has five-finger scales in C, G, D, A and E, each ending in a broken chord, with a I and V7 pattern that transposes (F5 Q&A, paraphrase). Transposition: hands move to another five-finger scale (F5 Q&A). A preview of the G five-finger scale appears at the end of the Primer review (F5 Q&A).
- Key signatures: not stated.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Quarter, half and whole rests (F1; F3), Unit 5 pp.36-39 (F2). Rhythms: quarter, half, dotted-half, whole notes (F5 Q&A for Level 2A says Primer and Level 1 use these); occasional eighth notes appear in PlayTime supplements only (F5 Q&A); eighth notes are formally introduced at 2A (F5 Q&A Level 2A).
- Accents, fermatas, ritardando, pickup notes (F5 TTK, paraphrase). Time signatures: not stated beyond those used in Primer.

### 4. Technique
- Legato and staccato touches (F1; F3), slurs (F5 TTK); Unit 1 pp.10-17 (F2). Four Technique Secrets: round hand shape, relaxed wrist, light hand bounce, finger independence (F5 TTK, Q&A).
- Five-finger scales C and G only in the lessons (see 2). Achievement Skill Sheet #1 "Major 5-finger patterns" is listed as a "challenging" supplement at Level 1 (F4 Level 1 wheel).
- Scales longer than five notes, crossings: not stated. Damper pedal: "used occasionally for an introduction or ending, or held throughout a piece" (F5 Q&A); the first pedal markings appear within pieces, page not stated.

### 5. Hand coordination
- Hands separate or together: not stated as such. A "creative" prompt on a sample page reads "Can you play this piece with a L.H. cross-over to F? Cross-over to A? To C?" (F4 Level 1 wheel, extracted text). Teacher duets (F5 TTK).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Dynamic markings (F5 TTK); legato, slurs, staccato (above); pedal (see 4).

### 7. Theory at the keyboard
- Tonic and dominant notes (F1; F3; Unit 7 pp.46-47); intervals 2nd to 5th (named as steps and skips, Q&A); half steps, sharps and flats (F3 only; absent from F1; Unit 6 pp.40-45 F2). I and V7 chords in C and G (F1; F3): "The C (I) Chord" p.48 and "The V7 Chord" p.52. Level 1 uses a two-note V7 (F5 Q&A: Lesson p.53). Chord symbols: not stated.

### 8. Other
- Improvisation and ear work "from the first lessons" (F5 Q&A); students "feel the meter and improvise on the black or white keys" with the audio accompaniments (F5 Q&A). The Sightreading Book can begin once "Firefly" is learned (F5 Q&A). "Eye Check" prompts to play without looking (F4 Level 1 wheel, extracted text).

## Level 2A (Lesson Book 64 pp.; 34 entries listed, F6)

Units (F2, Level 2A): Level 1 review p.4; Note Reading Guide p.6; "When the Saints" review p.8; 1 Eighth Notes pp.10-17; 2 Transposition pp.18-23; 3 The Phrase pp.24-27; 4 Half Steps and Whole Steps pp.28-33; 5 The D 5-Finger Scale pp.34-40; 6 The A 5-Finger Scale pp.42-47; 7 Minor 5-Finger Scales pp.48-61 (with "Cm, Gm, Dm, and Am Chords" p.56; "What's a Lead Sheet?" p.58, lead sheet p.59); Adventure Scale and Chord Warm-Ups p.62.

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Reading "by intervals" in a "multi-key approach" (F5 level page). Note Reading Guide p.6 (F2): treble line notes E G B D F and space notes F A C E read by name and skips, with four "Mouse Missions" covering all treble notes (p.6); bass staff via Smart Notes G, D, A plus bass B and bass F (p.7), and a drill of 16 bass-staff notes (F5 Q&A, paraphrase). Ledger-line range: not stated.

### 2. Keys and positions
- Five-finger scales and chords in C, D, G, A, major and minor (F5 TTK); F1/F3 list "Cm, Gm, Dm, Am five-finger scales" and "D and A major five-finger scales" as new, C and G major having been met in Level 1. Transposition of five-finger pieces (Unit 2). The Challenge/Adventure Warm-up (p.62) covers the five-finger scale, a broken chord and blocked I and V7 chords, "to play it chromatically through the keys"; a grouping of all 12 major five-finger scales by accidentals is described (F5 Q&A, paraphrase).

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- "8th-note rhythm patterns" (F1; F3); three eighth-note patterns, quarter + two eighths, two eighths + quarter, four beamed eighths, p.10 (F5 Q&A). Counting "ta/ti-ti" or "1 + 2 + 3 + 4 +". Tempo words: andante, moderato, allegro (F1; F3). New time signatures: not stated.

### 4. Technique
- Five Technique Secrets: firm fingertips, light thumb, fast fingers, hands-together coordination, wrist float-off (F5 TTK, Q&A). Finger independence and left-hand melodies, blocked chords, ostinato/boogie bass (F5 Q&A). Five-finger scales and chords "as a framework for finger independence and transposition" (F5 level page). Achievement Skill Sheet #1 "Major 5-Finger Patterns" (F4 2A wheel). Damper pedal: touches of color or held throughout (F5 Q&A).

### 5. Hand coordination
- "Hands-together coordination" is one of the Technique Secrets (F5 TTK); LH development as above. Teacher duets (F5 TTK).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Crescendo and diminuendo (F1; F3); phrase and phrase mark (Unit 3 pp.24-27). Pedal as above.

### 7. Theory at the keyboard
- Whole and half step pattern "W-W-H-W" for a five-finger scale (F1; F3, Unit 4 pp.28-33); minor five-finger scales and chords (Unit 7); first lead sheet (F5 level page; F2 p.58-59); "functional harmony" (F6 2A, paraphrase). Chord symbols: a lead sheet is used for "applied theory" (F5 TTK); symbols themselves not described.

### 8. Other
- Improvisation and composition: "Whirling Leaves" improvisation with major and minor five-finger scales; composing special endings; free improvisation on black or white keys; "Jazz Blast Improvisation" p.55 (F5 Q&A; F2). Ear: imitating teacher patterns; transposition "builds the ear" (F5 Q&A). Sightreading Book from the first song (F5 Q&A). Scale Book 1: five-finger major and minor scales (F5 TTK).

## Level 2B (Lesson Book 72 pp.; 46 entries listed incl. warm-ups, F6)

Units (F2, Level 2B): Level 2A review p.4; 1 The Family of C's pp.6-9; 2 Arpeggios pp.10-13 (cross-hand arpeggios pp.10-11); 3 Sixth (6th) pp.14-19; 4 The C Major Scale pp.20-27 ("I and V7 Chords in the Key of C" p.24, "The Waltz Chord Pattern" p.26); 5 The G Major Scale pp.28-35; 6 More About the Damper Pedal pp.36-41; 7 The Eighth Rest pp.42-43; 8 The Dotted Quarter Note pp.44-49 (lead sheet p.48-49); 9 The Primary Chords pp.50-57; 10 The F Major Scale pp.58-65; Scale and Chord Adventures pp.66-71 (all twelve keys, F6); certificate p.72.

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- "Five C's across the grand staff" (F1; F3): Low C (two ledger lines below the bass staff), Bass C, Middle C, Treble C, High C (two ledger lines above the treble staff); inner ledger-line notes midway through (p.38) (F5 Q&A, paraphrase). Interval of a 6th (F1; F3; Unit 3).

### 2. Keys and positions
- One-octave scales C, G, F major (F1; F3; F5 level page): Units 4, 5, 10. Transposition "extends beyond five-finger position to the full major scale" (F5 TTK; Lesson pp.31, 35 given there). Targets named in Q&A: G minor and D minor, C, G (F5 Q&A).

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- "Eighth rest, dotted quarter note" (F1; F3; Units 7-8); a new rhythm pattern shown as an image (F5 TTK, unreadable); intervals, 1st and 2nd endings, a tempo, D.C. al Fine, accidentals, pianissimo (F5 TTK, paraphrase). New time signatures: not stated.

### 4. Technique
- "Crossing the thumb under allows quick travel up and down the keys" (F5 level page); cross-hand arpeggios on I, V, I (F1; F3; F5 TTK "start lessons with" them); "The Ultimate Scale Warm-up" (seven C scales across the keyboard, G p.29, F p.59, legato, staccato, crescendo, swing variants) (F5 Q&A). Four Technique Secrets: arm weight, slur gesture, light thumb, connected pedaling. Achievement Skill Sheet #2 "Minor 5-Finger Patterns" and #7 "Elementary I-V-I Cadences" (challenging) (F4 2B wheel).

### 5. Hand coordination
- Left-hand work: primary chords (I, IV, V7), broken chords, one-octave passages, waltz bass, ostinato; large gestures across the C's (F5 Q&A). Hands separate or together in scales: not stated.

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- "Connected pedaling" (F1; F3; Unit 6 pp.36-41); sustained pedal for a whole piece or several measures; pianissimo; slur gesture (F5 Q&A, TTK).

### 7. Theory at the keyboard
- I, IV, V7 chords in C, G, F (F1; F3); tonic, dominant, leading tone; musical form AB and ABA (coda) (F1; F3); lead sheets (F2 pp.48-49; F5 TTK); recognising I, IV, V7 in blocked, broken and waltz patterns (F5 Q&A).

### 8. Other
- Improvising with the full major scale; composing an ABA piece; creating harmony from a lead sheet (F5 Q&A). Transposing by ear; imitation of rhythm patterns; ear training in the Technique & Artistry Book (F5 Q&A). Sightreading Book (begins "Moon Walker", F5 Q&A). Duets (F5 Q&A).

## Level 3A (Lesson Book 64 pp.; 23 pieces, F6)

Table of contents or unit/page list: not publicly available (page numbers appear only in the citations below).

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- "Ledger lines" (F1; F3); "expanded use of ledger lines"; the interval of a 7th, introduced p.19 (F5 TTK, Q&A: line-to-line or space-to-space for 3rds, 5ths, 7ths). Clefs: not stated.

### 2. Keys and positions
- One-octave scales and primary chords in C, G, F and D major (F5 TTK); C, G, F scales played hands together, Lesson p.8 (F5 Q&A); the chromatic scale; one-octave arpeggios and extended hand positions (F5 TTK; F1). Pieces transposed to G, F, C, D (F5 Q&A). Key signatures beyond those: not stated.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- 3/8, 6/8, the triplet, swing rhythm, common time and cut time (F1; F3). 3/8 at p.26 and 6/8 at p.28; swing p.40 (F5 Q&A).
