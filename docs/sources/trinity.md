# Trinity College London Piano Syllabus: Initial to Grade 8 (extract)

- **Source title:** Trinity College London, *Piano Syllabus: Digital and face-to-face assessment. Qualification specifications for graded exams from 2023* (official PDF, 131 pages).
- **Edition:** "Online edition, February 2026" (p. 2); copyright 2023. Trinity states it no longer sets an end date for the piano syllabus and that "most repertoire and technical exercises will remain valid indefinitely" (p. 3). The trinitycollege.com Piano page links this same document as "Piano syllabus" (its link text on the Piano page reads "face-to-face"), so it is the current piano syllabus (checked 2026-10-09).
- **Exact URL used:** https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource?id=9079
- **Piano page that links it (trinitycollege.com):** https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/music/grade-exams/piano
- **Second Trinity document, used only for example piece titles (heading 9):** *Piano repertoire list: face-to-face and digital graded exams from 2023*, "Online edition, August 2026", https://www.trinitycollege.com/resource?id=10125 (38 pages; cited as "list p. N").
- **Date accessed:** 2026-10-09

**Citation convention.** "p. N" is the page printed on the page, which equals the PDF page index of the syllabus. Face-to-face requirements are on pp. 69-123 (technical work pp. 79, 97-122; supporting tests pp. 80-89, 99-123). Many requirements are given as notation (time signatures, note values, tempo marks); those were read from the page images and are marked "(read from notation)". "(inferred)" marks anything not stated in words. "Not specified" means the syllabus says nothing on the point; nothing is filled from general knowledge.

**Scope notes.** (a) The syllabus has no separate jazz requirements; "Classical and Jazz" appears only as a column heading in the qualifications table (p. 8). Only the graded face-to-face Piano pathway is extracted. (b) The syllabus gives no reading-range, clef, hand-coordination or dynamics criteria for pieces; headings 1, 3, 5 and 6 therefore draw on the sight-reading parameters (p. 81, a table by grade), the technical-work tables, the aural tasks and the improvisation parameters, and say which. (c) Metronome speeds in technical work are "a minimum pace" (p. 79).

**General rules, all grades.** Scales and arpeggios are played from memory, ascending then descending, similar motion with the right hand an octave above the left, rhythm "even quavers in pairs or fours (except for Grade 1, which requires triplet broken chords)"; fingering is advisory, any logical fingering giving smooth legato is accepted (p. 79). Exercises (three prepared, two played: one chosen by the candidate, one by the examiner; music may be used) are short specially composed pieces, one group per aim: Group 1 tone, balance and voicing; Group 2 coordination; Group 3 finger and wrist strength and flexibility (p. 79, e.g. p. 98). Sight reading: 30 seconds to study (may practise aloud); extract "approximately two grades lower than the exam being taken"; parameter lists are cumulative (p. 80). Supporting tests: Initial-Grade 5 choose two of sight reading, aural, improvisation, musical knowledge; Grades 6-8 sight reading plus one of aural or improvisation (p. 80). Improvisation: three stimulus types (stylistic, motivic, harmonic), candidate chooses; "responses must use both hands"; 30 seconds' preparation (pp. 82-87). Musical knowledge (Initial-Grade 5 only): questions on the candidate's own pieces and notation (p. 88).

## Initial

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified as a range. Musical knowledge asks the treble clef sign and letter names of notes (sample questions, p. 88). Sight-reading parameters (p. 81) give no range or clef. Technique spans one octave (p. 97).

### 2. Keys
- Technique: C major, A minor (harmonic, melodic or natural, candidate's choice) (p. 97). Sight reading: C major (p. 81). Improvisation: C major (pp. 83, 85, 86). Aural: major key (p. 99). Repertoire keys: not specified.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Sight reading: 2/4; crotchet, minim and a long rest (glyph reads as a minim rest, inferred); read from notation (p. 81). Aural melody: 2/4 (read from notation, p. 99). Technique: broken triad pattern in 3/4, quavers then a crotchet, "to 5th" (read from notation, p. 97). Improvisation: 4/4, minims and crotchets (pp. 83, 85). Repertoire: not specified.

### 4. Technique (p. 97)
- Scales, one octave, hands separately, min. crotchet = 60, mf, legato: C major; A minor (harmonic or melodic or natural).
- Broken triads in C major and A minor, "to 5th" (C-E-G-E-C shape, read from notation), same speed, hands separately.
- No arpeggios. Exercises: one from each of three groups of four, two played (pp. 97-98).

### 5. Hand coordination
- Technique is hands separately (p. 97). Exercise Group 2 trains "coordination" (p. 98). Improvisation must use both hands (p. 82). Sight-reading parameters say nothing on hands (p. 81).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Technique: mf, legato (p. 97). Sight reading: p, f, moderato, "simple phrasing" (p. 81). Pedal: not specified.

### 7. Sight-reading (p. 81; level "lower than the exam", p. 99)
- C major; 2/4; crotchet, minim and a long rest; p, f, moderato; simple phrasing. Length in bars: not specified.

### 8. Aural and improvisation
- **Aural (p. 99):** one 4-bar melody, major key, 2/4. Heard three times: clap the pulse on the third playing, stressing the strong beat. Once each: identify forte or piano; legato or staccato; after the first three notes, the highest or lowest note.
- **Improvisation (pp. 82-87):** stylistic: 2-bar introduction by the examiner, then 4 bars improvised, once; 4/4, C major, chords I and V, march or lullaby. Motivic: 2-bar stimulus, response 4-6 bars, 4/4, C major, minims and crotchets, intervals up to a minor 3rd. Harmonic: 4-bar sequence of I and V, C major, played once.
- **Musical knowledge (p. 88):** letter names, note durations, clefs/staves/barlines, key and time signatures, basic terms and signs.

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three pieces from one list, no groups; at most one duet (pp. 76, 97). Example titles from the list (first entries, alphabetical by composer, not chosen for difficulty): *The Sleeping Doll* (Akimov); *Canon* (Badings); *Dialogue* from First Term at the Piano (Bartók) (list p. 2).

## Grade 1

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified as a range. Musical knowledge: notes on ledger lines "up to 2 ledger lines" (p. 88). Sight-reading parameters give no range or clef (p. 81). Technique spans one octave (p. 100).

### 2. Keys
- Technique: scales F, G major; D, E minor (harmonic, melodic or natural); chromatic scale from D; C major contrary motion; broken chords F, G major, D, E minor (pp. 100-101). Sight reading (cumulative): adds G major and A minor (white notes only) (p. 81). Improvisation adds F, G major (stylistic, motivic); harmonic uses C, F, G major (pp. 83-86). Repertoire keys: not specified.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Sight reading: adds 4/4 and semibreve with a long rest (read from notation, p. 81). Aural: 2/4 or 3/4 (read from notation, p. 102). Technique: triplet broken chords (p. 79; pattern shown in F major, bass clef, groups of three quavers, read from notation, p. 101). Improvisation motivic adds quavers (p. 85). Repertoire: not specified.

### 4. Technique (pp. 100-101)
- Scales, one octave, min. crotchet = 70, mf, legato: F, G major; D, E minor, hands separately; chromatic scale in contrary motion starting on D, and C major contrary-motion scale, hands together.
- Broken chords F, G major, D, E minor: one octave, hands separately, min. dotted crotchet = 50, mf, legato.
- No arpeggios. Exercises as the general rule.

### 5. Hand coordination
- Hands separately except two contrary-motion scales, hands together, one octave (p. 100). Exercise Group 2 trains coordination (p. 101). Improvisation both hands (p. 82). Sight-reading parameters: nothing on hands (p. 81).

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Technique: mf, legato (p. 100). Sight reading adds mf; no new articulation (p. 81). Pedal: not specified.

### 7. Sight-reading (p. 81; level "lower than the exam", p. 102)
- Adds to Initial: G major, A minor (white notes only); 4/4; semibreve and long rest; mf. Length: not specified.

### 8. Aural and improvisation
- **Aural (p. 102):** 4-bar melody, major key, 2/4 or 3/4. Heard three times: clap the pulse on the third playing. Identify forte or piano and legato or staccato; after the first two bars, whether the last note is higher or lower than the first; hear twice with a change of rhythm or pitch in the second playing and say where it occurs.
- **Improvisation (pp. 83-86):** stylistic: 2-bar introduction then 4 bars improvised twice (8 bars), F, G major, I and V, fanfare or moderato. Motivic: 2-bar stimulus, response 4-8 bars, quavers, major 3rd. Harmonic: 4-bar sequence, I and V, twice, C, F or G major.
- **Musical knowledge (p. 88):** note values, explain key and time signatures, ledger-line notes (up to 2), terms and signs.

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three pieces from one list; one duet at most (pp. 76, 100). Example titles (list p. 6): *Grumpy Gorilla* and *Hug a Pug* (Arens); *Nine Million Bicycles* (arr. Anand).

## Grade 2

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified as a range. Musical knowledge: notes on up to 3 ledger lines (p. 88). Sight-reading parameters give no range or clef (p. 81). Technique spans two octaves (p. 103).

### 2. Keys
- Technique: scales Bb, D major; G, B minor (harmonic or melodic); chromatic from Bb; C major contrary motion; arpeggios Bb, D major, G, B minor (pp. 103-104). Sight reading adds A minor including G sharp (p. 81). Improvisation adds A minor (pp. 83, 85); harmonic uses C, F, G major (p. 86). Repertoire keys: not specified.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Sight reading: adds 3/4; dotted minim and ties (read from notation, p. 81). Aural: 2/4 or 3/4 (read from notation, p. 105). Improvisation motivic adds dotted notes (p. 85). Repertoire: not specified.

### 4. Technique (pp. 103-104)
- Scales, two octaves, hands together, min. crotchet = 80, f or p, legato: Bb, D major; G, B minor (harmonic or melodic); chromatic in similar motion from Bb; C major contrary-motion scale.
- Arpeggios: Bb, D major, G, B minor; two octaves, hands separately, min. crotchet = 60, mf, legato.

### 5. Hand coordination
- Hands together for scales (two octaves), separately for arpeggios (pp. 103-104). Exercise Group 2 (p. 104). Improvisation both hands (p. 82). Sight-reading parameters: nothing on hands.

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Technique: f or p (scales), mf (arpeggios), legato (pp. 103-104). Sight reading adds allegretto; no new dynamics or articulation (p. 81). Improvisation motivic adds staccato (p. 85). Pedal: not specified.

### 7. Sight-reading (p. 81; "approximately two grades lower", p. 105)
- Adds: A minor with G sharp; 3/4; dotted minim and ties; allegretto. Length: not specified.

### 8. Aural and improvisation
- **Aural (p. 105):** 4-bar melody, major or minor, 2/4 or 3/4. Heard three times: clap the pulse. Describe the dynamics (they vary); legato or staccato; last note higher or lower than the first; hear twice with a change and identify where it occurs and whether rhythm or pitch.
- **Improvisation (pp. 83-86):** stylistic: 2 + 4 bars twice (8 bars), A minor, I, IV, V / i, iv, V, tango or andante. Motivic: response 6-8 bars, dotted notes, staccato, perfect 4th. Harmonic: 4-bar sequence twice, I, IV, V.
- **Musical knowledge (p. 88):** numerical intervals (2nd-5th), metronome marks, grace notes and ornaments, notes on up to 3 ledger lines.

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three pieces from one list; one duet at most (pp. 76, 103). Example titles (list p. 10): *Children at Play*, no. 1 (Bartók); *Minuet in G* (Böhm); *La machine à coudre* (Bonis).

## Grade 6

### 1. Reading range and clefs
- Not specified as a range. Sight-reading parameters give no range or clef (p. 81). Technique spans four octaves (p. 115). The syllabus says Grades 6-8 demonstrate "technical control across the full compass of the instrument" (p. 71).

Improvisation adds F#, C minor (p. 84); harmonic uses C, F, G, Bb, D, Eb, A major and A, D, E, G, B, C, F# minor (p. 87). Repertoire keys: not specified.

### 3. Time signatures and rhythms
- Sight reading: no new time signature or note value listed (cells blank) (p. 81). Aural: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 or 6/8 (read from notation, p. 117). Improvisation: 12/8 (pp. 84, 86). Repertoire: not specified.

### 4. Technique (pp. 115-116)
- Scales, hands together, min. crotchet = 120, f or mf or p, legato or staccato: Bb, D major; Bb, D minor (harmonic and melodic); chromatic similar from Bb and D (four octaves); chromatic contrary from Eb (two octaves).
- C major scale in 3rds: one octave, hands separately, min. crotchet = 60, legato.
- Arpeggios, similar motion, four octaves, hands together, min. crotchet = 100, f or mf or p, legato or staccato: Bb, D major; Bb, D minor; diminished 7ths from Bb and D; dominant 7ths in Bb and D.

### 5. Hand coordination
- Hands together (four octaves) and in 3rds hands separately (p. 115). Pieces: Group A "focus on technique", including "hand coordination and independence (including elements of counterpoint)" (p. 76). Sight reading: nothing on hands.

### 6. Dynamics, articulation and pedal
- Technique: f or mf or p; legato or staccato. Sight reading: "pedalling required but not always marked" (p. 81). Aural: comment on dynamics and articulation (p. 117).

### 7. Sight-reading (p. 81; "approximately two grades lower", p. 117)
- Adds: F# and C minor with limited modulation; pedalling required but not always marked. Nothing else new. Length: not specified.

### 8. Aural and improvisation (aural or improvisation, one of two; p. 117)
- **Aural:** 8-bar harmonised piece, major key, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 or 6/8. Heard twice: identify the time signature, comment on dynamics and articulation; identify and comment on two other characteristics; first four bars once: identify the modulation (subdominant, dominant or relative minor; key names accepted); with a printed copy, two changes to the melody, locate and describe as pitch or rhythm.
- **Improvisation (pp. 82-87):** stylistic: 2-bar introduction, 8 bars twice (16 bars), 12/8, F#, C minor, up to 2 chords per bar, I, ii, IV, V, vi, 7ths, agitato or nocturne. Motivic: 1-bar stimulus, response 12-16 bars, acciaccaturas, augmented 4th and diminished 5th. Harmonic: 8-bar sequence twice, I, ii, IV, V / i, iib5, iv, V, 7ths.

### 9. Repertoire demands
- Three pieces in two groups (A and B); at least one from each (pp. 76, 115). Group A focuses on technique, Group B on expression, colour and tonal balance (pp. 76-77). Example titles (list pp. 26-27): A: *Adagio* from Sonata in B minor (C. P. E. Bach); *Invention no. 6 in E* (J. S. Bach). B: *Stamping Dance* (Bartók, Mikrokosmos vol 5).
