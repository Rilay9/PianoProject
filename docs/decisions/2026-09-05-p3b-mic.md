# 2026-09-05 — P3b decisions: microphone note detection

`docs/05` §11 is a design sketch written before anyone had run it against real
piano audio. Most of it survived contact; the places it did not are recorded
here, along with the measurements that decided them. Every number below came
from `app/tests/fixtures/audio/*.wav`, rendered offline from the *bundled*
soundfont so the detector is measured against the instrument the app itself
plays rather than against sine stacks that would flatter it.

## 1. Where the split between worklet and main thread falls

§11.2 puts the FFT in the `AudioWorklet` and §11.3 puts the decision logic in
`MicSource` on the main thread. The decision logic needs the magnitude
spectrum, and shipping 2048 floats every 11 ms across the port is ~700 kB/s of
copying and garbage on the thread that renders the score.

So the *whole* detector runs in the worklet and posts finished note events.
The split the doc is actually after — pure, testable DSP separate from the
audio plumbing — is kept exactly: `dsp.ts` and `detector.ts` are plain classes
over `Float32Array` that the Vitest suite drives directly, and
`pitchProcessor.ts` is a shell with no judgement in it.

## 2. The inharmonicity formula

§11.2 writes partial *h* of a string at `f0` as `h·f0·(1 + β·h²)`. The
physically correct relation is `h·f0·√(1 + B·h²)`, and the difference is not
academic: at β = 0.0004 and h = 6 the doc's form predicts a partial 1.4 % sharp
where a real string is 0.7 % sharp — about a fifth of a semitone, enough to
walk the search window off a genuine partial in the treble. The square-root
form is used.

## 3. Events are timestamped at the centre of the analysis window

A Hann window weights its edges to nothing, so a note is only fully visible
once it reaches the middle of the window. Stamping events at the window's
trailing edge — the obvious reading — put every onset a consistent ~46 ms late,
which is most of §11.6's 30 ms budget spent on a bookkeeping error.

## 4. The onset window is 150 ms, not 60

§11.3 requires an onset "within the last 60 ms" before a note-on. That assumes
the onset and the spectral evidence arrive together, and they cannot: the onset
is found from spectral flux at the window centre, while the harmonic score only
crosses its threshold once the note has filled the window — tens of
milliseconds later, more for a bass note with a slow attack.

Measured on the fixtures: at 60 ms recall was 56 %; at 150 ms it is 100 %.
Widening to 200 ms changed nothing, which says the gap really is the window's
own latency rather than a loose gate.

## 5. Spectral flux is summed in decibels, so quiet bins had to be gated

A decibel is a ratio. A bin sitting near silence jumping from −100 dB to −90
contributes exactly as much flux as a real partial going from −20 to −10, and
broadband room noise makes thousands of bins do precisely that. Measured at
17 dB SNR, the onset count went from 9 to 0 while the harmonic scores barely
moved (22 → 23 dB).

Bins more than 60 dB below the frame's peak now contribute nothing, and the
onset threshold's delta is expressed **per bin** — as a flat addition to a
2048-bin sum it was numerically negligible, which is why the adaptive threshold
appeared to do nothing at all.

## 6. A re-strike arms on the fall and fires on the rise

§11.3 asks for "onset + re-rise of H(p) by ≥ 3 dB" for a note struck again
under the pedal. Measured trajectory of a repeated C4: each strike dips from
~35 dB to ~10 dB and recovers over ~150 ms. Requiring the score to *recover*
first collapsed recall on that fixture to 20 %, because the next strike lands
before the recovery. Requiring only a rise reported one attack as five, because
a piano attack keeps rising for a couple of hundred milliseconds.

The note is therefore armed once it has fallen 10 dB below its own peak, and
fires on the next 6 dB rise above a short running minimum. 100 % recall, 100 %
precision on the pedal fixture.

## 7. The unexpected-pitch scan

Three changes to §11.3's "88 templates every 4th hop":

- **It runs on onsets only, and half a window after one.** Gating on both a
  strong onset *and* every fourth hop meant the two conditions almost never
  coincided and nothing was ever reported. Running it *on* the onset frame
  reported nothing either: the new note occupies only the tail of the window
  there, so its score is still climbing — measured on the single-notes
  fixture, all nine onsets produced an empty shortlist. Half a window later
  the note fills the window.
- **It stops at C2 (MIDI 36).** A physical limit, not a tuning choice: at C1 a
  semitone is under 2 Hz wide while an 8192-point window at 44.1 kHz resolves
  5.4 Hz, so adjacent bass semitones are unresolvable and the scan happily
  reported a C#1 and a D1 throughout a recording containing neither.
- **It descends to the fundamental.** A struck C4 lights up the C5, G5 and C6
  templates through its own partials, and those templates have narrower
  background bands, so one of them routinely scores *higher* than the note
  actually played: every strike of the pedal fixture's C4 was reported as a C5.
  The scan now walks down the partial intervals for as long as a lower pitch
  has real energy at its *own* fundamental — the one measurement no note above
  it can fake. Where the two cannot be separated the tie goes to the lower
  pitch on consequence: guessing C4 when both sounded misses a note, guessing
  C5 when only C4 sounded names a pitch nobody touched.

Measured across all four fixtures with expectations deliberately set to
pitches the recordings never contain: 12 guesses, 11 of them naming a pitch
the recording actually holds. The one miss is a B5 for a B4 whose own
fundamental is 4 dB over background in that rendered chord against 20 dB for
the B5 it fakes.

## 8. Chord leniency is two thirds, not 70 %

§11.4 completes a chord when "≥ 70 % are satisfied and the loudest onset in the
window was strong". Two notes of a three-note chord is 67 %, so the rule as
written excludes the triad — which is the case it exists for. Two thirds is
used, so it means "all but one" for a triad and still requires three of four.

"The loudest onset was strong" is read as the detector's own confidence, which
is already the evidence margin scaled by onset strength; plumbing a second
signal through the input contract would have said the same thing twice.

## 9. A wrong note only counts when the source is certain

The engine gained `minConfidence` (below it an input is dropped entirely —
neither right nor wrong) and `wrongNoteConfidence` (default 1, so only a
deterministic source can mark you wrong). Everything else is emitted with
`uncertain: true` for the amber colour. This is §11.1's "ambiguity resolves
towards the score" made enforceable rather than left to each screen.

## 10. The metronome click, and the notch

§11.4 asks for a "short high click (≥ 4 kHz) that the detector notches out".
Implemented as a 5 kHz sine burst with a 2 ms attack — a *sine*, because a
notch can only remove a narrow band and the existing woodblock click is a noise
burst, which is by definition everywhere. The notch is applied to the magnitude
spectrum before anything reads it, which deals with both problems at once: the
click no longer contributes to the spectral flux (it would otherwise fire an
onset on every beat, far worse than any effect on the templates) and no
harmonic template can find energy there.

Measured on the pedal fixture with clicks mixed in every 500 ms: 12 note-ons
un-notched, exactly the 5 real strikes notched.

## 11. Calibration measures from recorded audio, on the main thread

The worklet already streams raw audio back for the Diagnostics capture, so the
calibration reuses that path and analyses on the main thread where there is
time to do it properly — and, more usefully, where the whole routine can be run
in Vitest against the rendered fixtures.

One finding: the harmonic template score alone is not selective enough for
calibration. On the pedal fixture, which contains a C4 and nothing else, the
C#4 template scored *higher* than the C4 (37.4 dB against 37.2) by matching the
skirts of C4's partials. A measurement therefore carries the fundamental's
detune in cents, and anything more than a third of a semitone out is "not
heard".

Gain corrections are relative to the median measured pitch rather than to an
absolute level, because how hard the learner happened to play is not a property
of the room. The dozen measured pitches are interpolated across all 88, since
both gain and inharmonicity vary smoothly with pitch.

## 12. Analysis cost is measured on the main thread

`docs/01` §4.7 budgets 3 ms of analysis per 512-sample hop, and the worklet
cannot measure itself: `AudioWorkletGlobalScope` has no `performance` (the
first version of the worklet reported a cost of exactly 0 from Chromium) and
`currentTime` does not advance within a render quantum.
`AudioContext.renderCapacity`, which would have given the audio thread's real
load, is not in Chrome 153 either.

So `audio/pitch/benchmark.ts` runs the same detector over synthesised strikes
and times it. That leaves out the audio thread's scheduling overhead, which is
why the warm-up pass and the percentiles are there — and it has the advantage
of running from the Diagnostics screen on the phone, which is where the number
actually matters. Under a ×4 CPU slowdown in headless Chromium: mean 0.90 ms,
median 0.90, p95 1.60.

## 13. Accuracy on the rendered fixtures

Expectations are published from the score, as they are in a real run.

| fixture | recall | precision | mean onset error |
|---|---|---|---|
| single notes, C2–C6 | 100 % | 90 % | 18 ms |
| three-note chords | 100 % | 90 % | 15 ms |
| repeated note under the pedal | 100 % | 100 % | 23 ms |
| scale, 16ths at 120 bpm | 100 % | 89 % | 36 ms |

With broadband noise mixed in:

| fixture | 23–25 dB SNR | 17–19 dB SNR |
|---|---|---|
| single notes | 100 % / 90 % | 89 % / 100 % |
| chords | 100 % / 90 % | 100 % / 82 % |
| repeated pedal | 100 % / 100 % | 100 % / 100 % |
| scale | 100 % / 80 % | 88 % / 88 % |

§11.6 asks for ≥ 95 % recall and ≥ 90 % precision monophonic and ≥ 85 % recall
for chords, with mean onset error under 30 ms. Everything meets that except the
fast scale, which measures 89 % precision and 36 ms — notes 125 ms apart
against a 93 ms analysis window. The window cannot be shortened without losing
the frequency resolution the bass staff needs, so that one is recorded in the
test rather than tuned away.

## 14. Known limits

- **The bass below C2 cannot be identified without the score.** Expected
  pitches are still tested across the whole keyboard, because there the score
  says which note it is; only the open-ended scan is restricted.
- **The `?worker&url` worklet import** is bundled to a single self-contained
  file by `vite build`, which is what the e2e suite and the deployed app use.
  In `vite dev` it is served as a module with imports, which Chromium's
  AudioWorklet supports but older browsers may not. Only the builder sees dev
  mode.
- **The measured input latency folds in the learner's own timing**, because
  §11.5 defines it as onset time minus click time while the learner plays along
  with the metronome. It is a median and it is clamped to 150 ms, so a
  mis-measurement degrades to "no correction" rather than to a systematic
  offset.
