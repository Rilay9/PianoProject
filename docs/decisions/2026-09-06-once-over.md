## 1. What the project actually is

PianoPath is a piano-teaching app for one person, one phone and one piano: the owner, a
Samsung Galaxy S25, and a Roland HP-130 over a USB MIDI cable. It is a web app (Vite,
TypeScript, no framework) that installs from Chrome and will be wrapped as a Trusted Web
Activity APK so that Android treats it as a real app while Chrome keeps supplying Web MIDI.
Everything it needs is on the phone after the first launch: the app, the whole bundled
library, the piano samples. Nothing has an account, a server or a network dependency except
the teaching-video links, which say so before you tap them.

**What it does at the piano.** It draws real sheet music — a grand staff, a window of a few
bars at a time — and moves through the piece as you play. It hears you three ways: the MIDI
cable (exact), the microphone (score-informed, honest about uncertainty, amber when unsure),
or nothing, in which case a clock drives the cursor. It scores a run, keeps the result, and
brings a passed piece back for review at 1, 3, 7 and 21 days. It plays pieces back, has a
metronome that works on its own and over the music, and nineteen kinds of drill — reading,
chords, ear, rhythm, pedal, dynamics, harmony — each with the same four-section advice a
lesson ends with and one coaching sentence when a rule fires.

**What it teaches.** A curriculum of 93 lessons across ten stages: a core path from first
notes to Grade 1 (Stages 0–4, plus a memorisation rung), then sixteen tracks — classical,
chords and pop, blues, jazz, ragtime, theory and ear, improvisation, a technique rung per
stage, a five-lesson "how to practise" module, and mini-modules for hymns, holiday, latin,
rock and metal, jamming with a guitarist, and the shelf of beautiful pieces. Every track now
reaches Stage 9. Nothing is locked: every lesson opens, "I already know this" is one tap, and
strict prerequisites exist only as an opt-in badge with a confirmation, never a disabled
card.

**Where the music comes from.** Three pipelines feed one catalog of 1,527 items: 934
generated exercises (scales, arpeggios, Hanon-style cells, harmony families, rhythm rows —
levelled from a table, at every level 1–8), 530 songs and 63 runtime drills. The songs are
hand-authored folk tunes checked against a reference, the MuseTrainer library, Craig Sapp's
Humdrum editions, the Chopin Institute's first editions, and 216 scores quarried from PDMX
after a human review. A difficulty model fitted on the judged songs estimates a level for
anything that arrives without one, in Python at build time and in TypeScript on the phone,
and the app prints an estimate as `≈` until the owner overwrites it.

**Where the rest of the music comes from.** The owner. Every rung and every concept carries
a finder — a search line and a chatbot prompt, generated from data and validated — that says
what music would train it. A found file reaches the right rung in two taps from Android's
share sheet, is levelled from its notes, and becomes an option of that rung. The 37,261
usable PDMX files sit as a folder on the phone with a manifest beside them; the app browses
the folder and imports what is wanted. Books he owns on paper are registered on a shelf,
page by page, and practised against with a metronome that measures the one thing it can
measure without a score — steadiness — and says in words that it cannot measure accuracy.

**What keeps it honest.** `validate.py` refuses a build with a thin rung, an orphan
exercise, a stale ladder report, a missing finder, a missing tips file, a section that runs
past the last bar, or a track the data does not define. The render check opens every score
in a real Chromium through the app's own loader, compares the cursor's step count to the
model's, captures the console, and remembers what it measured so the next build engraves
only what changed. The licence gate is code. Two builds come from one table: the owner's
`--personal` build carries everything; the `--strict-license` build carries only what could
be redistributed.

**What it is not.** It is not multi-user, it does not scan sheet music into notes, it does
not judge a paper piece it has never read, and it does not pretend a generated exercise
teaches tone.

## 3. Does it meet the vision?

The vision, in the owner's words across `00` A1–A6 and the notes: he reads basic notation,
plays a few chords, had a few lessons, plateaued; the app must never assume a level and must
make moving forward or back one tap; classical first and beautiful pieces, blues and jazz
for a friend with a guitar, rock and metal via his own scores; short weekday sessions and
long weekend ones; everything offline; the metronome over the sheet music.

**Where it is met, and measurably.** Nothing is locked (D17, kept through P18's opt-in
gating, which was designed as a badge and a confirmation precisely so it could not become a
corridor). "I already know this" and "Mark done" exist on every lesson. Session templates
match A3. The metronome runs on the Score screen, the PDF viewer and the paper screen. The
whole bundled library is precached and the e2e suite proves it every run. The plateau is
addressed twice — the coaching rule that links to `practice.5` after three flat runs, and
the practice module itself.

**Where it drifts, and the drift is a bug rather than a judgement.** On a fresh phone,
**Today does not know which tracks are on.** `planStore` starts with `trackOrder: ['core']`;
Plan falls back to the data's `defaultActive` set when the order has one entry, but Today
passes `plan.trackOrder` straight to `nextRecommended` (§6 C2). So until he opens Plan and
touches a track chip, Today recommends core only, and after 4.7 recommends nothing — the
"New" row falls back to "anything at this level". For a learner who plateaued once, an app
that quietly stops recommending after the core path is the failure mode to avoid. One
helper, used by both screens, fixes it.

**Where it drifts toward the diligent student.** Three things ask for bookkeeping the owner
did not ask for:

- **The shelf** is typed in by hand — title, page, rung — "twenty seconds a piece". Honest,
  and the right design given no OMR, but it is a chore, and the lesson pages' `paperHint`
  lines on 29 rungs will read as nagging if he never registers a book. Judgement: keep it,
  but the hint should be one muted line, not a section heading — and the prompt makes the
  "From your own books" block collapse to nothing when the shelf is empty.
- **The PDMX review.** 368 candidates passed the machine gates; 216 were kept. The remaining
  152 sit in `review.csv` undecided. That is his ears' job, and it is fine to leave it
  undone; it is not fine for the docs to imply the quarry is finished. The ladder report
  shows no rung under the floor, so nothing depends on it.
- **The finders** want him to go and search. That *is* the design (§9 of the replan), but
  it works only if the "what this rung wants" line is true, and today it is not once he has
  acted on it (§6 C3): the line keeps asking for a song he already added.

**Where the library still does not match his stated tastes.** Blues and jazz for the friend:
the rungs exist to Stage 9 but every jazz and blues rung is `songOptional` because no
public-domain standard is bundled — the 1918–1930 standards were never authored and the
quarry's jazz bucket found 16 files. Rock and metal: of the seven target songs, one (*Final
Masquerade*) surfaced in PDMX; the others are still import-only. Both are honest states and
both are what the finders are for. Neither is a bug.

**What should be deleted.** Nothing in the app. In the repository: `pages.yml`, the moment
the repo is private (it will fail on a private repo without a paid plan, and its only job was
a public test URL); the stray fixture score in `content/scores/pdmx/` (§6 C8); and the
`--allow-nc` flag from every document, keeping it in `build.py` as a silent alias so an old
command still works. `tools/content/pdmx/index.py`'s static browse page duplicates the app's
folder screen on a laptop; keep it — it is the reviewer's tool — but say so in its docstring.

3. **The generator is the slow step of a no-change build** — five minutes of music21 for 934
   exercises nothing changed. Caching it by (generator source hash, parameters) is the same
   trick as the conversion cache. (P11 §1.)
4. **Ragtime stops at Stage 8, technique at 8, rock-metal at 5.** (P12b.)
5. **152 PDMX candidates are undecided** in `build/pdmx/review/review.csv`. (P14.)
6. **Everything that needs the phone**, gathered in §7.

**Still open, and does not matter much:** the render manifest never prunes dead entries;
`fetchedAt` is stamped at build time; failed conversions are retried every build; the e2e

table will be wrong about somebody; band 7–9's eight converter failures; the "quick check"

## 5. Test rigour, judged

The counts — 481, 1,310, 236 — are real; all three suites are green on this Windows machine
as well as in CI. The question is what they would fail to catch. Species by species:

**A test that agrees with the implementation rather than the requirement.** The needs line
(`finder.spec.ts`) asserts only that the line contains the word "rung"; it would pass with
the wrong number in it, and it does — see §6 C3. The folder screen's "Added" state has no
test at all beyond `folderLibrary.test.ts`'s unit coverage of the store; the screen's
title-matching (§6 C4) is unpinned either way.

**A rule enforced only by a check that could silently stop running.** `stale_ladder_report`
returns no error when `docs/generated/ladder.md` is absent — by design, so a fresh checkout
builds — which means deleting the file turns the rule off with no message. The tips check is
the opposite and the model to copy: `runtime_drill_kinds()` returns an *error* when it cannot
parse the TypeScript, rather than an empty list. The ladder check should say "no report
committed" as a warning line every run, the way the estimated-level count is printed.

**A port kept honest by one test.** `difficulty.test.ts` runs Python's numbers and the
TypeScript estimator over 41 fixtures at 0.2 of a stage, and P15 records that it caught three
real bugs. It still covers what the two do: the fixture set is regenerated by
`export_levelling_fixture.py`, the model file is read by both, and `ornaments` and
`handCrossings` carry zero weight so their absence in TypeScript is harmless — until a refit
gives them weight, which the same test would then report. Checked and fine.

**An allow-list that drops what is new.** `coerceSettings` copies keys explicitly and
dropped `strictPrerequisites` once. It has every key today (read), but nothing stops the next
one being dropped: there is no test that every key of `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` survives a
round-trip. One test, in the prompt.

**A check that runs against a different build from the one under test.** P11 fixed the
worst of this (the e2e web server rebuilding content). One remains by design: CI builds
without `--personal`, so the 153 personal-build items are placeholders in every automated
test and the "file present, tag present, strict build refuses it" path is exercised only by
the Python fixture (`test_pdmx.py`). That is adequate; noting it so nobody assumes the e2e
suite saw the owner's build.

**Where a screenshot is the only guard.** The window layout (bars-per-window, landscape and
portrait) is held by 15 Linux-only snapshots. On a Windows machine those tests write a
baseline and pass. Fine while CI runs; it is the reason not to delete `ci.yml` when Pages
goes.

**Sound, and checked:** the validator's `--strict-license` path does refuse a bundled
`personal-build` file (read, and the CI/pages build proves the placeholder path); the DB
upgrade is keyed on `oldVersion` and tested from a version-3 database; the backup round-trips
`books` and `levelOverrides` and deliberately omits `folderLibraries`; the offline test asserts
the tips, the level model and a PDMX file are precached; `LESSON_ID_PATTERN` has a regression
test naming every id shape; the section picker is gated on the parsed score; the router
drops an unrecognised `?for=`; `share-target.js` redirects to an absolute URL; render-check
cursor parity runs on every new file.

## 6. The oversights

**C** = confirmed by running or by reading the exact lines; **S** = suspected, with what
would settle it. Costs are to the owner, not to the code.

**C1 — The public repository contains 153 transcriptions whose compositions are not public
domain.** `content/scores/pdmx/` holds 216 committed scores; `content/sources/pdmx.json`
marks 134 `unknown` and 19 `in-copyright`; and the GitHub API reports `Rilay9/PianoProject`
as `private: false` (queried today). D23 guarded the *Pages deploy* — `--strict-license`
placeholders them, and the validator refuses them, both confirmed — but git itself is a
publication, and the files went in with P14's commits. Cost: the one thing D18 and D23 were
careful about is happening anyway, on GitHub rather than on Pages. Fix: **make the repository
private now**, before any other step in §7. Rewriting history is unnecessary once it is
private; if he ever wants it public again, those files come out first.

**C2 — Today and Plan disagree about which tracks are active on a fresh phone.**
`app/src/ui/screens/PlanScreen.ts:258–261` falls back to the data's `defaultActive` set when
`trackOrder` has at most one entry; `app/src/ui/screens/TodayScreen.ts:289` and `:300` pass
`plan.trackOrder` — `['core']` from `planStore.ts:13` — straight through. Confirmed by
reading both. Cost: §3 — Today recommends core only until he touches a chip, then nothing
after 4.7, and the practice and technique modules the data marks active are never suggested.
Fix: one `activeTracksFor(plan, curriculum)` in `curriculum/` used by both screens, with a
unit test on a fresh plan and an e2e on a fresh profile's Today.

**C3 — The "what this rung wants" line ignores what the owner added.** `validate.py` writes
`needs` at build time; `LessonScreen.ts:137–156` prints it verbatim; `curriculum/load.ts`
overlays imported and shelf pieces onto `songOptions` and `paperOptions` at runtime and
nothing recomputes the shortfall. Confirmed by reading. Cost: he does exactly what the finder
asked, and the rung keeps asking. Fix: keep `needs.floor` and the song-optional rule from the
build, count the overlaid options at runtime; test with one assigned import.

**C4 — The folder screen marks a score "Added" by title.** `FolderScreen.ts` `restore()`
builds `alreadyAdded` from the folded titles of every import, then disables Add on any folder
row with the same title. Confirmed by reading. PDMX has six files titled *The Entertainer*
and dozens of *Minuet in G*; adding one greys out the rest. Cost: the second edition of
anything cannot be added from the folder. Fix: record the origin on the import
(`origin: { folder, file }`) when it comes from a folder and match on that; fall back to
title only for imports with no origin; a unit test with two same-titled rows.

**C5 — `showDirectoryPicker` exists on Chrome for Android.** D24, `04` §4b, `01` §4.5, the
owner guide and `folderLibrary.ts` all state that it does not, and build the "pick the folder
again every time" limitation on that. MDN's compatibility data (`api/Window.json`, fetched
today) gives `chrome_android: 132` for `showDirectoryPicker`, the same release that made
`webkitdirectory` usable. **Confirmed against the data; not tried on the S25**, which is the
only thing that settles it — the API may exist and still refuse a persisted handle. Cost if
the data is right: on every Add he re-picks a folder and Chrome re-enumerates 37,261 files,
when a stored handle plus one "Allow" tap would do; and four documents state a platform fact
that is false. Fix: feature-detect; if present, keep the handle in `folderLibraries` and
`requestPermission` on Add; otherwise the current path. Owner checklist item: open
`chrome://version` on the S25 and try it.

**C6 — The owner guide's build command produces the wrong build.** See §2, first wrong row.
Confirmed by reading `build.py`. Fix: `--personal`, everywhere the guide and `06` say
`--allow-nc`.

**C7 — No P12a decision note.** The phase that rebuilt the level table and added a dozen
technique families has no record of what it decided; `02` Part E's amendments and `01` §7
carry fragments. Seam, not a bug. Fix: a short note written from the merge.

**C8 — A fixture score is committed as content.** `content/scores/pdmx/QmFixtureBachMinuet.mxl`
is tracked, is not in `pdmx.json`, and is referenced only by `test_pdmx.py`; P13 said P14
would replace it. Harmless (the build copies only listed files) and confusing. Fix: move it
under `tools/content/tests/fixtures/`, point the test at it, delete the `.gitkeep`.

**C9 — `compositionStatus` is a field in the docs and not in the catalog.** See §2. Fix:
emit it on the item (schema: optional enum) so the Library can show *in copyright* on a row
and the docs become true.

**C10 — Twelve stale numbers and names across six documents.** Listed in §2. None causes a
wrong action except the README's "what is next" and the owner guide's flag; all are edits.

**C11 — A `--personal` build cannot pass validation.** Found by running it after this
review was drafted: `build.py --offline --personal` fails at `validate.py` with
"`docs/generated/ladder.md` is stale". The report was written from the strict build
(1,527 items); the personal build has 1,533, because `import_musetrainer.py` *excludes* the
six composition-refused files in a strict build and *admits* them in a personal one, where
`import_pdmx.py` emits placeholders in both so its ids never move. Two builds, two catalogs,
one committed report: whichever flavour wrote it, the other fails. Cost: the owner's own
build has failed validation since P14 and nobody ran it. Fix: MuseTrainer's
composition-refused files become strict-build placeholders exactly as PDMX's do, so both
builds carry the same ids; a test builds the fixture both ways and asserts the id sets are
equal. (The app for the phone was built anyway by regenerating the report, validating, and
restoring the committed copy; that is the workaround, not the fix.)

**S1 — 37,261 files through `webkitdirectory` on a phone.** Unmeasured. Chrome for Android
hands the files over through the document-tree picker; on desktop 37k `File` objects are
instant, and the note says the folder was built in 27 s on the laptop. Whether the S25
enumerates a 37,261-file tree in seconds or minutes, and whether a 6 MB `library.json` parse
plus a ~10 MB `folderLibraries` row write feel fine, only the phone knows. Settled by: pick the
folder once and time it. If it is slow, C5's stored handle is also the fix.

**S2 — A pedalled chord on the paper screen.** P16 says so itself; the 60 ms chord window has
never met the HP-130. Owner checklist.

**S3 — The five coaching thresholds, the 120 ms chord boundary, the staccato/legato fractions,
the 1.4 voicing ratio, the half-pedal range.** All chosen on a laptop, all named in the owner
guide §8, none met a piano. Owner checklist; each is one constant.

**Checked and fine, so nobody checks again:** the validator's strict path; the precache
globs after four content phases (json, mxl, md all covered; `offline.spec.ts` asserts tips,
level model and PDMX); `coerceSettings` today; `share-target.js`; the DB migrations; the
router patterns; the render check's parity and console capture; the ladder report is fresh;
the truncation scan finds nothing; Node 24 and Python 3.11 run every suite here.
