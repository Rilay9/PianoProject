Date: 2026-09-06 · Branch: `docs/p11-replan` · Input: `prompts/_FABLE-BRIEF-replan.md`

This is the document the P11–P17 prompts are held to. It rules on the eight open problems
in the brief (§1), designs the six things the owner asked for (§2–§7), reports what the

well-rated easy pop and film arrangements at Stages 2–5, not the Chopin. That reorders the

## 1. Rulings on the known problems (brief §5)

### 1.1 Thirteen Chopin scores that OpenSheetMusicDisplay refuses (`Invalid note initialization object: {}`)

**Root-cause it, with a timebox, using a bisector rather than a debugger.** P11 builds
`tools/content/bisect_render.py`: given a MusicXML file that fails the render check, it
slices the score by measure ranges with music21 (`score.measures(a, b)`), writes each slice
through the normal converter, and renders each in the dev route until it isolates one
measure; then it prints that measure's XML. The same tool serves every future "OSMD will not
draw it" case, which is why it is worth two hours of a session and a hand-run debugger is
not. Timebox: **half a session.** If the cause is a normalisable pattern (a `<type>`-less
note in a tuplet, a zero-duration note, a `<cue>`, a rest inside a chord — the candidates,
since grace notes and beams are ruled out) it is fixed in `convert.py` like `clean_beams()`
was. If it is not found in the timebox, the thirteen stay excluded, the minimal failing
measure is committed as `app/tests/fixtures/scores/edge/known-issues/osmd-empty-note.musicxml`
with a test that asserts the failure (the P2 pattern for the grace-16th defect), and an
upstream issue is opened from that fixture. **No fallback renderer**: a second engraver would
double every rendering test and split the note→SVG map, for thirteen pieces of which the
library already holds the important ones (the Op. 9 no. 2 nocturne exists in the MuseTrainer
edition).

### 1.2 Étude Op. 25 no. 7

**Stays excluded**, with an import placeholder rather than silence: a catalog item with no
file, the reason in `importHint` ("MusicXML cannot express the 2048th-note tuplet in bar 27;
import an edition that notates the cadenza differently"), and `alternatives` pointing at the
other Op. 25 études. Quantising would change the music; the pipeline does not do that.

### 1.3 Seven-minute content build and ten-minute render check

**Worth a phase, and it is in P11.** Two mechanisms:

- **Conversion cache.** `convert_file()` is wrapped by `cached_convert()`, keyed on the
  sha256 of the source bytes, the sha256 of `convert.py` + `abc_tools.py`, and the music21
  version. Hits are copied from `build/cache/convert/<key>.mxl`. CI restores that directory
  with `actions/cache` keyed on the same three things. A cache never changes an answer,
  only when it is computed, so it needs no correctness argument beyond the key.
- **Incremental render check.** `build/render-manifest.json` records, per output file
  sha256, the result of the last render (ok, steps, measures, duration, cursor-step parity).
  The check renders only files whose sha256 is not in the manifest, and reuses the recorded
  numbers for the rest. `--full` ignores the manifest; a weekly `workflow_dispatch` job runs
  `--full` so a renderer upgrade cannot hide behind the cache. `apply_durations` reads from
  the merged result, so the catalog still carries every measured number.

Expected: a no-change build in about a minute, and a full render only when OSMD or the
converter changes. Measured numbers go into `01` §7 by P11.

### 1.4 The `level-banded` tag

**It stops being a tag and becomes a field the app reads.** Catalog schema gains
`levelSource: "judged" | "estimated"` (required from P11; the build migrates the tag: items
carrying `level-banded` become `estimated`, everything else `judged`). Rules:

- The app shows an estimated level as `≈ 7.1` everywhere a level is printed, and the item
  detail sheet says "estimated from the opus/its features — move it if it feels wrong".
- **"Re-level"** on the detail sheet writes `{ itemId, level }` to a new `levelOverrides`
  store; `allItems()` applies overrides, so the session builder, swaps and Library all see the
  owner's number. Overrides are in the backup. Re-levelled items are shown as judged.
- Swap and session-builder tiers prefer judged over estimated at equal distance.
- `validate.py` prints the estimated count per stage every run, and P14's levelling model
  (§2.4) sets `levelSource: "estimated"` on every PDMX item until the owner touches it.

### 1.5 Pieces the ladders name that no reachable source has

**The ladders stop naming them as options and start naming them as things to find.** In
P14, `docs/02` Part D's repertoire columns are rewritten from the catalog (§2.6): a rung
lists what is bundled, and pieces that are wanted and absent move into that rung's *finder*
(§4) as examples — *Frog Legs Rag* becomes a line in the ragtime Stage 8 search prompt, which
is where a piece the owner has to fetch himself belongs. Concretely for the six in the brief:
PDMX holds one *Frog Legs Rag* (checked by title; P14 verifies it is Scott's and not a
different tune of the same name), 17 files titled "Invention" and 10 "Prelude and Fugue"
whose composer strings match Bach (P14 checks BWV numbers), and 11 Scarlatti sonatas whose
K numbers P14 reads from the titles. *Euphonic Sounds* and *Ragtime Nightingale* are absent
and go to the finder.

### 1.6 The ragtime Stage 5 rung offering the complete *Entertainer*

**Ruling: an "easy arrangement" rung is filled first from PDMX, then by authoring.** PDMX has
six titles containing "Entertainer", one of them 37 bars at MuseScore complexity 1 — an easy
arrangement by the look of it, to be verified by P14. If none survives review, P14 authors
`song.ragtime.joplin-entertainer.easy` in ABC: the A strain, 16 bars, melody plus an oom-pah
left hand in C — an arrangement of a public-domain piece is `[AUTH]` material and always was.
The general rule, added to `03` §2: **an "easy arr." entry in a ladder is satisfied by an
authored simplification when no source has one**, and the authored file names the edition it
was checked against.

### 1.7 `level` versus rung number

**Stands, and gets written down where the learner sees it.** `level` is global difficulty,
used for swaps, sorting and the session builder; a rung is an order within a track. The
lesson object gains `levelBand: [min, max]` (schema, validated: every song option's level must
fall inside the band, or the build fails naming the item). The lesson page prints the band
("Pieces on this rung run level ≈ 6.8–7.3") and `02`'s terminology section says why a Stage 6
rung can hold level-7 pieces: the easiest complete Joplin rag is a Grade 6 piece and the
number is honest about that.

### 1.8 Two sources of truth for the track list

**One source: `content/curriculum/00-tracks.json`.** The `tracks` enum comes out of
`catalog.schema.json` (it becomes `array of string, minItems 1`); `validate.py` checks every
catalog `tracks[]` entry and every unit's `track` against the ids in that file;
`common.TRACKS` is read from it. Done in P11 with a test that a made-up track fails.

## 2. PDMX becomes the library (brief §6.1)

### 2.1 Shape: quarry once on the owner's machine, commit what was chosen

The archive never enters the repository or CI. Two separate programs, with a hard line
between them:

```
tools/content/pdmx/
  select.py     CSV → build/pdmx/candidates.json           (owner's machine; needs the CSV)
  extract.py    candidates → build/pdmx/raw/<cid>.mxl       (owner's machine; streams the tar once)
  quarry.py     raw → converted + features + level + render (owner's machine; music21, Chromium)
  review.py     writes build/pdmx/review/index.html + review.csv; reads the owner's decisions
  commit.py     accepted rows → content/scores/pdmx/<id>.mxl + content/sources/pdmx.json
tools/content/import_pdmx.py   the build step: reads the committed files, verifies checksums,
                               writes build/catalog.pdmx.json. Needs nothing but the repo.
```

- `select.py`, `extract.py`, `quarry.py` and `review.py` take `--pdmx-dir` (default env
  `PIANOPATH_PDMX_DIR`). They **refuse to start** with a one-paragraph message if `PDMX.csv`
  is missing, and treat `mxl/` (unpacked) and `mxl.tar.gz` (not unpacked) as equivalent —
  `extract.py` streams the tarball once (`tarfile` in streaming mode, `r|gz`) and writes only
  the wanted members, so nobody has to unpack 250,000 files. A missing archive is a message
  naming the file, never a traceback. The 42 rows whose `mxl` is `N/A` are skipped by
  `select.py` before anything touches the disk.
- **Key off the `mxl` column.** The `path` column points into `data/`, which is not on disk.
  The MuseScore id, for `source.url`, is the number in the `metadata` column's filename
  (`./metadata/5/5740212.json` → `https://musescore.com/score/5740212`); nothing in
  `data.tar.gz` is needed for it.
- **What is committed is the converted, normalised `.mxl`**, not the raw upload: the build
  then does no music21 work for PDMX at all. `content/sources/pdmx.json` records, per item,
  the CID, the raw file's sha256, the converted file's sha256, the CSV facts used, the
  features, the level estimate and its inputs, the review decision and note. `import_pdmx.py`
  fails the build if a listed file is missing or its checksum differs — the deterministic,
  archive-free build the brief asks for.
- `.gitignore` gains nothing: `content/scores/pdmx/` is tracked, like `authored/`;
  `build/pdmx/` is under the already-ignored `build/`. `content/scores/imported/` stays as it
  is. `01` §2's layout table gains the line.
- Provenance fingerprint: `pdmx.json`'s header carries the CSV sha256 and row count, the
  archive sizes, and the Zenodo record id the owner names (§10 Q1).

### 2.2 Selection: a haystack becomes a shortlist

`select.py` is a pure function of the CSV and two committed tables, and prints why every
rejected row was rejected (aggregated: counts per reason, plus the 200 most frequent
unmatched composer strings, which is how `composers.json` grows).

Gates, in order (a row must pass all):

1. `mxl` present.
2. `n_tracks` ∈ {1, 2} and every entry of `tracks` is a MIDI program in 0–7 (the piano family;
   a two-track file is a grand staff written as two parts, which `convert.py` already merges).
3. `subset:no_license_conflict` (the authors' own recommendation) and `subset:deduplicated`.
4. Not `is_draft`, not `has_paywall`.
5. 8 ≤ `song_length.bars` ≤ 400 and `n_notes` ≥ 30.
6. **Composition status — a label, not a gate, for the personal build (D23):**
   - `composer_name` is normalised (NFKD, lower, punctuation stripped, "composed by",
     "arr.", years in brackets removed) and matched against `content/sources/composers.json`:
     `{ canonical, aliases[], born, died }` for every composer the ladders and Part F name plus
     the usual method-book names (Türk, Beyer, Gurlitt, Duvernoy, Czerny, Clementi, Kuhlau,
     Diabelli, Heller, Bertini, Lemoine, Streabbog, Köhler, Burgmüller, Schytte, Gedike,
     Maykapar…). A match gives `composer_died`, and the existing `composition_verdict()` rules
     with its 1930 bar → `compositionStatus: "pd"`. A year in brackets in the composer string
     is parsed and must agree.
   - A string matching the traditional aliases (`trad`, `traditional`, `anon`, `folk`,
     `unattributed`, and the Irish/Scottish collection markers) → `"pd"` with
     `traditional: true`.
   - A composer the table knows to be in copyright (the decoys and anyone dying after 1930)
     → `"in-copyright"`; no match → `"unknown"`. **Neither rejects.** They are carried on the
     item, tagged `personal-build` in the catalog, admitted by `build.py --personal` (a
     superset of `--allow-nc`, which it replaces as the owner's flag), and refused by
     `--strict-license`, which is what the public Pages deploy runs. `validate.py` counts
     them out loud on every build, as it does for NC items.
   - The catalog item's `composer` is the matched canonical name when there is one and the
     raw string otherwise; `source.name` says "PDMX (MuseScore upload, uploader's
     public-domain claim)" so the provenance is honest about what was claimed by whom.
7. `has_lyrics` does not reject (a song sheet with a piano part is fine; lyrics are stripped
   by the converter) but marks `lyrics: true`, which the reviewer sees and the ranking
   penalises.

With 36,000 candidates instead of 4,000, **ranking does the selecting**: a Bayesian rating
(prior 4.0, weight 5) times log(1 + `n_views`), with `is_official` up-weighted and `lyrics`
down-weighted. The paper's finding — that ratings track quality — is used to order each
band's queue so the best-rated files fill the quota first and the owner reviews the best
first. It is still never used to *admit* a file: the machine gates in §2.3 and the human
review decide that.

Quotas (per level band and per genre bucket, after the machine gates in §2.3; more than
this is not a library):

| Band | Target songs | Split by bucket (classical / folk-hymn-carol / pop-film-game / jazz-latin) | Why |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Stages 1–2 | 40 | 10 / 20 / 10 / — | folk melodies with a written simple LH or single line; the Part F verification set; the easiest pop arrangements |
| Stage 3 | 50 | 20 / 15 / 15 / — | easy dances, Türk, Beyer, Gurlitt, hymn settings, easy pop |
| Stage 4 | 70 | 30 / 10 / 25 / 5 | Grade-1 pieces: Czerny 599, Duvernoy 176, Clementi easy, Bach AMB; the well-rated easy pop and film arrangements |
| Stage 5 | 70 | 30 / 5 / 25 / 10 | sonatinas, Burgmüller 100, Bach little preludes, Tchaikovsky 39; pop and film at intermediate |
| Stage 6 | 40 | 15 / — / 20 / 5 | top-up where the current 21 are thin in a style; the *Beautiful* import suggestions (Einaudi, Yiruma, Tiersen, Zimmer, Sakamoto, Hisaishi) if PDMX has them |
| Stages 7–9 | 30 | 20 / — / 10 / — | named gaps (Inventions, WTC, Scarlatti, Frog Legs); advanced pop and film |
| **Named wants, any band** | as found | — | the seven rock-module songs (`02` Part D8), searched by title and artist; each found one replaces its import placeholder in the personal build and keeps the placeholder in the strict build |

The genre bucket comes from the CSV's `genres` column with a title/composer fallback (a
`composers.json` match is classical or folk; `NA` genre with an unmatched composer is
pop-film-game). Roughly 300 files, a few megabytes. Over-quota candidates stay in
`candidates.json` with their scores, so a later run can take the next fifty without
re-selecting. A bucket that cannot fill its share hands the remainder to the others in the
same band.

### 2.3 Quality: machine gates before a human sees anything

`quarry.py` runs on every extracted candidate, in this order, and records the first failure:

1. **Parse and normalise** through `convert_file()` (one part, two staves, tempo, lyrics
   stripped). A converter exception rejects.
2. **Round trip**: the converted file re-parsed must have the same multiset of (bar, staff,
   MIDI pitch) as the raw file — the P10 listen-check, generalised. Mismatch rejects.
3. **Structure**: ≥ 8 bars; a time signature; tempo 30–220 (a missing one gets the default
   and a `tempoDefaulted` flag for the reviewer); no pitch outside A0–C8; fewer than 20 % of
   bars empty in both hands; both staves present unless the row is a single-line melody, in
   which case the item is marked `hands: right` and `singleLine: true` (these are the
   sight-reading corpus and the Part F reference set, §9.2, not repertoire).
4. **The P2 defect scan**: a measure whose summed duration is under half its time signature
   next to a short-type grace note is the OSMD truncation bug; rejected with that reason.
5. **Render** through the app's own loader (the render check's spec, pointed at
   `build/pdmx/converted/`), with `lastError`, step count ≥ 1, and **cursor-step parity**
   (`cursorStepCount()` equals the model's step count — the P2 invariant, never yet run on
   content). Failure rejects.
6. **Duplicates against the catalog**: title+composer folded and compared; a match marks
   `duplicateOf` and the reviewer decides whether the PDMX edition is an *alternative*
   (kept, id suffixed `.pdmx`, listed in `alternatives` both ways) or redundant. At most two
   editions of one work are bundled.

**Rejection-rate rule.** The machine rejection rate per band is printed. If human review
(§2.5) then drops more than **40 %** of what the machine passed in a band, the selector's
thresholds for that band are wrong and P14 stops, adjusts `select.py`, and re-runs that band
before committing anything from it. The rate is written into `pdmx.json`'s header so the
next run knows what the last one saw.

### 2.4 Levelling: one formula, computed the same way for every score

The CSV's `complexity`, `notes_per_bar` and MusPy metrics exist only for PDMX, so they are
used **only for ranking the review queue**. The level estimate comes from features the
pipeline computes itself, identically for the calibration set and the candidates:

`tools/content/difficulty.py` → `features(score) -> dict`: bars; notes per bar; notes per
second at the file's tempo; max simultaneous notes per hand; max hand span (semitones) per
hand; largest melodic leap per hand; pitch range per hand; proportion of black-key notes;
key-signature accidentals; shortest common note value; voices per staff; hand-crossing count;
ornament count; ledger-line note ratio; distinct rhythmic values.

`content/sources/level-model.json` holds the model: a linear function of log-scaled features
with a bias, clamped to [1.0, 9.0], plus the feature means used to scale. **P14 fits it** on
the calibration set — every *song* in the catalog whose `levelSource` is `judged` (about 200:
the 59 MuseTrainer items, the authored tunes, the eight hand-levelled Joplin rags and the
Chopin overrides) — by least squares with monotone signs enforced (no feature may lower the
level as it grows). Acceptance for using the model at all: Spearman correlation ≥ 0.8 and
median absolute error ≤ 0.7 stages on leave-one-out. Below that, P14 falls back to the coarse
table (`complexity` × notes-per-bar bins, written in the prompt) and says so. Either way every
PDMX item is `levelSource: "estimated"` (§1.4). The model file is also what P15's runtime
estimator reads (§4.4), so an imported score and a quarried one are levelled by the same
arithmetic.

### 2.5 Human review, designed as a spreadsheet

`review.py` writes `build/pdmx/review/index.html` — one row per machine-passed candidate:
the 2-bar preview PNG the render check already produces, title, composer as matched, band,
estimated level with the three features that drove it, flags (`reviewRequired`, `lyrics`,
`tempoDefaulted`, `duplicateOf`), and the MuseScore link — and `review.csv` with columns
`cid, decision (keep|drop|later), level_override, note`. The owner (or the Opus session on
his machine, with him looking) fills the CSV; `review.py --check` reports what is undecided;
`commit.py` refuses a row with no decision. The page is static, so it opens from the file
system without a server. Nothing is committed that a person has not marked `keep`.

### 2.6 The curriculum reorients around what is found

**The catalog is the truth about repertoire; `docs/02` Part D is a report of it.** P14 adds
`tools/content/ladder_report.py`, which prints, per track and rung, the bundled options with
levels, the option counts against the floor, the level band, and the finder's wanted list.
Its output replaces the hand-written repertoire columns in Part D (kept as prose about
*focus*, which is pedagogy and stays authored). `02` §D says that the table is generated and
how to regenerate it; a validate check fails the build if the committed report is stale
(same mechanism as a formatted-file check). Who wins when the ladder and the library disagree:
the library, every time, and the ladder's wish goes into the finder.

What P14 does with the found pieces, stage by stage: every Stage 1–5 lesson's `songOptions`
is rebuilt from the quarry plus what exists, honouring the level band; Part F tunes that P5
skipped for want of a source are **authored now, checked against the PDMX file** (that is the
verification P5 lacked, and it is the biggest single gain at the owner's level); the
`classical.5` lie is corrected with real sonatinas and Burgmüller; jazz, blues, chords-pop,
theory and improvisation get Stage 6–9 units in P12b, not from PDMX (PDMX has 75 rows tagged
jazz and they are mostly copyrighted).

### 2.7 Failure modes, named

- *The composer table is wrong about a death year* → the build's licence test suite gains a
  fixture per composer with a known-in-copyright decoy (Bartók 1945, Kabalevsky 1987,
  Shostakovich 1975) and a test that the table labels them `in-copyright`, so the strict
  build cannot ship them.
- *A `personal-build` item reaches the public deploy* → `pages.yml` runs `--strict-license`,
  which refuses `compositionStatus` other than `pd`; `validate.py` prints the count of
  personal-build items on every build; and the P9 rule stands: whoever makes `--personal`
  the default must delete or repoint the Pages workflow in the same commit.
- *A "traditional" row is a modern song* → it is still admitted (D23), but the label is
  wrong, so the reviewer sees the MuseScore link and can correct `compositionStatus` in the
  review CSV; the strict build trusts the corrected label.
- *The archive is a different PDMX version from the one the table was built against* →
  `pdmx.json`'s header fingerprint (CSV sha256, rows, archive bytes) is compared by
  `select.py` and it refuses to mix versions without `--new-version`.
- *The owner's machine converts differently from CI* → the committed converted file is the
  artefact, and its checksum is verified by the build; conversion never re-runs in CI.
- *The review is half done* → `commit.py` commits only `keep` rows and lists the rest; a
  second run picks up where the CSV left off.
- *A quarried file breaks on the phone at full length* (a 300-bar sonata) → the render check's
  per-item timing is recorded in the manifest and anything over 20 s to lay out is flagged
  `longScore`, which the Score screen treats as "window layout only, no scroll layout".

## 3. Generated material at every level (brief §6.2)

### 3.1 The level-4 bulge is a parameter table, so it is fixed in a parameter table

The 225 exercises at level 4 are there because `default_plan()` hands every scale variant a
literal `4.1`. P12a replaces the literals with one table, `LEVELS` in
`generate_exercises.py`, keyed by (family, mode, hands, octaves, motion, rhythm), derived from
`02` Part E:

| Family and parameters | Level |

| five-finger HS / HT | 1.1 / 2.1 |
| major scale HS 1 oct: C G F / D A / others | 2.5 / 3.1 / 4.2 |

| major HT 2 oct: sharp side / flat side / rest | 4.1 / 4.2 / 5.1 |
| harmonic + melodic minor HS 1 oct: A E D / others | 3.3 / 4.2 |
| minors HT 2 oct to 3 accidentals / rest | 5.1 / 5.2 |
| any scale HT 3 oct, 4 oct | 6.1, 6.2 |
| chromatic 1 oct HS / HT / 2 oct HT | 4.4 / 4.4 / 6.1 |

| octave scales | 7.2 |
| 4 oct in 16ths at ♩=120 | 8.1 |

The item ids do not change (they carry no level), so nothing in the curriculum breaks; the
distribution does, and `validate.py` prints the exercise count per level so the shape is

### 3.2 Families that do not exist, by the concept that needs them

Walked through `02` Parts C, D and E concept by concept against the generator's families and
the drill kinds. What has nothing, grouped by the phase that builds it:

**P12a — technique (notation, the generator):** scales 3–4 octaves; scales in 3rds and
6ths; octave scales and broken octaves; chromatic 2 octaves; maj7/min7/half-diminished
arpeggios and broken-7th patterns; **Hanon-style cells 21–60 as families rather than as
Hanon** — repeated notes (3 and 4 to a note), double thirds, double sixths, trills and
mordents (measured and unmeasured, with the rule for how many notes), tremolo octaves, wrist
rotation (Alberti at speed) — because no reachable edition of 21–60 exists and encoding sixty
exercises from memory is exactly what P4 refused to do; articulation pairs (the same phrase
staccato then legato, judged by note-off timing, which the engine records but nothing yet
scores); hand independence beyond unit 2.1 (LH quarters under RH eighths, 3:1, then
polyrhythm 2-against-3 in both directions); dynamics shaping (crescendo over a scale:
velocity slope, not p/f ratio); voicing (a chord whose top note must be louder — measurable
by per-note velocity, the skill the *Beautiful* shelf lesson names and nothing tests); ties
across the bar line and syncopation at 16th level; 5/4 and 7/8 rhythm rows.

**P12b — harmony, ear, reading (drills in `catalog.static.json`, runtime kinds, and the
sight-reading generator):** seventh-chord voicings in all keys and the shell-voicing drill
made real (it currently borrows the `chord` kind); rootless A/B voicings; ii–V–I in twelve
keys; the four-chord loop in twelve keys with inversions (the chords-pop 4 lesson demands it
in five keys and offers it in none); slash-chord stepwise bass lines; walking bass over a
blues and over a ii–V–I; comping rhythms; stride left hand (bass–chord–tenth–chord);
turnarounds; tritone substitutions; quartal voicings; sus2/sus4/add9 open voicings; boogie
pattern variants (Pinetop, Yancey); ear: modes (the theory.5 lesson teaches four and no drill
plays them), secondary dominants, modulation to V and relative, harmonic dictation (a
progression back as *chords*, which needs the chord-boundary timing rule the P8 doc left
open), extended chords 9/11/13, chord-scale relationships, transposition (play the given
four bars in the named key — judged against the transposed expectation), Roman-numeral
reading (a printed progression, played); **sight-reading levels 5–7**, rule-based (keys to
four accidentals, two octaves, syncopation and triplets, a left hand drawn from the
accompaniment families, chord-tone targeting on strong beats) — the Markov-table idea in
`05` §8 is dropped: it needs a corpus at build time and adds nothing a contour rule does not,
and determinism from a seed is the property that matters.

**P12b also writes the missing rungs**: jazz 6–9, blues 6–9, chords-pop 6–9, theory 6–9,
improv 6–9, each with its three-plus options built from the new families, and lesson text.
Those tracks then exist above Stage 5, which today they do not.

**Individual element training** (the owner's phrase) is a view, not new content: the Skills
review screen already lists every concept with one drill; P12b makes it list *every*
exercise and drill for the concept, at every level, so any single skill can be practised at
whatever level the owner is at — the "always something to work on for one skill" requirement
is met by the data plus that one screen change.

### 3.3 What generated material cannot honestly cover

Ear-training against real music, phrasing, rubato, pedalling beyond the clean-change rule,
and tone. Those stay in lesson text, repertoire and the teaching-video links. The plan does
not pretend a generator teaches them.

## 4. Every rung gets a finder (brief §6.3)

### 4.1 Data, not a text field

The lesson object gains a required (from P15) `finder` block, validated by the curriculum
schema:

```jsonc
"finder": {
  "skill": "hands together with a held left hand",         // what the rung trains, in words
  "levelWords": "very easy, beginner, first pieces hands together, C position",
  "constraints": ["C major", "no black keys", "left hand whole notes", "8–16 bars"],
  "avoid": ["arrangements with chords in the right hand", "6/8", "anything over 40 bars"],
  "examples": [ { "title": "Ode to Joy", "composer": "Beethoven", "note": "bundled" },
                { "title": "Frog Legs Rag", "composer": "James Scott", "note": "wanted" } ],
  "formats": "MusicXML or .mxl preferred; PDF works but cannot be scored"
}
```

The **prompt text is generated at build time** by `tools/content/finder.py` from those
fields, in two forms written into `curriculum.json`: `searchQuery` (short, for a search
engine: "easy piano sheet music C major hands together whole note left hand musicxml") and
`chatPrompt` (a paragraph for a chatbot: the skill, the level in plain words, the constraints,
what to avoid, "music still in copyright is fine, I am finding it for myself", "prefer
sources that offer MusicXML; MuseScore, IMSLP, sheet-music retailers with MusicXML export",
and "list ten, with composer and where to get each"). Generating rather than hand-writing
means one wording change fixes 66 prompts, and `validate.py` can check length (≤ 900
characters), that every constraint appears in the prompt, and that no prompt asks for a
transcription of a copyrighted song to be *downloaded from a website* — the `00` D18 rule,
which the prompt states rather than violates.

Concepts get finders too: `content/curriculum/concepts.json` (new; also the home of the
concept display names the Skills screen currently derives from ids) carries a finder per
concept, so "find more for *legato pedalling*" exists independently of any rung.

### 4.2 What a rung still needs

`validate.py` already knows the option counts; it now writes `needs` into each built lesson:
`{ songs: n, exercises: n, paper: n }` short of the floor plus the level band's coverage (how
many judged items sit inside the band). The lesson page shows one line — "This rung has 2
songs; the floor is 3. Find one, or play the paper option." — and the finder sheet opens
from it. Diagnostics lists the thin rungs, as it does now.

### 4.3 Two taps from a download to the right rung

Today: share → PianoPath → Library row → Edit → level/tags → Save → Plan → lesson. Target:

1. Share (or open) the file → the service worker parks it → Library imports it and, if the
   import came from a share or from the lesson page's "Import for this rung" button, the
   **assign sheet** opens by itself: the rung the owner came from (carried in the hash as
   `#/library?for=<lessonId>`) is pre-selected, the level estimate (§4.4) is filled in, the
   concepts are the lesson's. One tap on **Save** — two taps total from the share sheet.
2. `ImportRow` gains `lessonIds: string[]`, `concepts: string[]`, `levelSource`;
   `importToCatalogItem` maps them; `load.allItems()` already merges imports, and a new
   overlay in `curriculum/load.ts` appends imports carrying a `lessonId` to that lesson's
   `songOptions` at runtime — so the imported piece is an *option of the rung*, counts toward
   completion, appears in swaps, and never "sits outside the curriculum". Backup carries it.

### 4.4 Levelling an import

`app/src/score/difficulty.ts` ports `difficulty.py`'s feature set to the ScoreModel (notes
per beat, per-hand density, span, range, leap, accidentals, tempo) and reads
`content/level-model.json` (the same coefficients, copied into `app/public/content/` by the
build). The estimate is shown as `≈` and editable in the assign sheet. A unit test feeds the
same fixture scores through both implementations and asserts they agree within 0.2 — the
test that keeps the two estimators one formula.

## 5. The shelf: books the owner already owns (brief §6.4)

### 5.1 Data model

A new IndexedDB store `books` (in the backup, `DB_VERSION` 2 with a migration):

```ts
interface BookRow {
  id: string;                 // 'book.<slug>'
  title: string; author?: string;
  kind: 'method' | 'repertoire' | 'other';
  pdfImportId?: string;       // the owner's own PDF of it, if he has one
  barsPerSystem?: number;     // for the PDF viewer's timed mode
  pieces: { id: string; title: string; page?: number; bars?: [number, number];
            lessonIds: string[]; concepts: string[]; level?: number; levelSource: 'estimated' | 'judged';
            itemId?: string /* a MusicXML twin, bundled or imported */ }[];
}
```

A piece is registered once, by hand, from Library → Shelf → book → "Add a piece" (title,
page, which rung), or by tapping "I have this on paper" from a lesson page, which
pre-fills the rung. Nothing is scanned; the owner is looking at paper and types a page
number. That is the honest input.

### 5.2 A rung can ask for paper

Lessons gain `paperHint` (string, optional): *"or bars 1–16 of whatever your method book gives
for hands together with a held left hand"*. When a book piece is registered against a rung, it
becomes a `paperOption` of that rung at runtime, the same overlay mechanism as §4.3.
Completing a lesson with a paper piece is allowed where the lesson's `mastery.custom` does not
demand a measured accuracy (repertoire rungs; not the pedal drill), and the pass is recorded
`selfAssessed`, with the badge the self-pass already has.

### 5.3 Practising against paper, honestly

A `#/paper/<bookId>/<pieceId>` screen: metronome with count-in, tempo, the keyboard strip,
MIDI and microphone capture, a timer, and at the end the "How did it go?" three-button
self-report `04` §5 already specifies for no-MIDI runs. What it records in `sessions`: mode
`paper`, duration, tempo, notes played, and **tempo steadiness** — when the metronome is on
and MIDI is present, the standard deviation of onset offset from the nearest click, which is
a real measurement of the one thing the app *can* hear without a score. What it never
records: accuracy. The summary says "the app cannot see the notes; it heard N notes over M
minutes at ♩=T, steadiness ±S ms" and the self-report is what the progress row gets.

Two things make paper practice *scorable* without pretending:

- If the piece has a MusicXML twin (`itemId`: an import, or a bundled item the owner links by
  search from the piece's row), "Practise with the score" opens the Score screen in Wait
  mode and the run is a normal measured run credited to the book piece too.
- For technique the app already knows — the scale or arpeggio the book page asks for — the
  piece row can point at the generated exercise, and the Score screen gains **blind mode**
  (score hidden, keyboard strip only; a `hideScore` engine option that P16 adds and the
  memorisation rung in §8 also uses). Playing the book's C major scale then *is* judged,
  because the expectation is known even though the page is paper.

### 5.4 A PDF of a book he owns

The existing import handles the file; the shelf links it (`pdfImportId`) and each piece's
`page` opens the viewer at that page (the PDF route gains `?page=`). The viewer's
`barsPerSystem` is stored per book rather than per open. No OMR, as before.

## 6. Tips for every drill (brief §6.5)

**Where:** `content/tips/<drill-kind>.md`, one per kind (twelve), same front-matter style as
lessons, body fixed to the four lesson sections — **What it's for · How to practise it ·
Common mistake · How you'll know you've got it** — and validated by `validate.py`: every
runtime kind in `RUNTIME_DRILL_KINDS` has a file, every file has exactly those four headings
in that order, ≤ 250 words. Optional variants `content/tips/<kind>.<variant>.md` with a
front-matter `when:` block matched against `drill.params` (e.g. `when: { clef: bass }`,
`when: { feel: shuffle }`) — static per kind by default, parameter-aware where a parameter
changes the advice.

**How it reaches the screen:** fetched from `content/tips/` when the drill screen opens (the
precache globs already include `content/**/*.md`, so it is offline and costs the entry bundle
nothing), rendered with the existing `ui/markdown`, shown as a collapsible "Tips" block under
the prompt and, in full, on the result sheet.

**History-aware:** a small rule table in `engine/drills/coaching.ts`, pure and unit-tested,
takes the last three `sessions` rows for the item and the current result and returns at most
one sentence: slow-but-accurate (`meanReactionMs` high, accuracy ≥ 0.9 → "right, now faster:
the next set has fewer cards"), fast-and-wrong, a pedal drill whose lifts are consistently
early or late (the sign of `reactionMs`), a dynamics ratio stuck under target, three runs
without improvement (→ the lesson's plateau text, §8). Rules, not a model.

## 7. Robustness (brief §6.6)

Every phase's prompt carries the same clause: a feature arrives with its validator rule, its
unit tests, and — where it produces notation — a render-check entry; the render check and
`validate.py` are the definition of "content works". P11 also goes looking for the blind
spots the brief predicts. Found by reading, to be fixed in P11:

1. The render check has never run the **cursor-step parity** invariant on content, only on
   fixtures. It runs it on every new file (manifest-gated, so it is cheap).
2. It does not read the browser console; an OSMD *warning* about a dropped element is
   invisible. It captures `console.error`/`warn` per item into the report.
3. A file that renders but whose **duration is absurd** (a tempo of 8, or 40 minutes) passes.
   `validate.py` already bounds durations; the render check now also flags
   `durationSec / measures` outside 0.5–12 s per bar.
4. **Hands mislabelled**: an item that says `both` whose model has one hand present, or the
   reverse. Flagged from `modelSummary().hands`.
5. **Orphans**: exercises reachable from no lesson and no concept. Reported, and after P12b,
   failed.
6. The catalog schema's `tracks` enum drift (§1.8).
7. **The precache's blind spot is the globs**; `content/scores/pdmx/**` matches the existing
   pattern, and `offline.spec.ts`'s manifest test covers it, but P13 adds a one-line test that
   a file under `pdmx/` appears in the precache manifest, so the third scores directory cannot
   repeat the soundfont and worker mistakes.
8. A render run that crashes mid-way loses everything; with the manifest it resumes.
9. `build.py --if-missing` skips a rebuild when the catalog exists, so a changed source is
   silently stale on `npm run build`. With the cache, `--if-missing` goes away: the build is
   cheap enough to always run.

## 9. Is the shape right?

The six ideas are right. Two things about their *order* and one about their *weight* are
changed here, and one thing not in the brief matters more than one that is.

1. **Generated before quarried.** The owner is at Stages 1–4. PDMX's yield there is folk
   melodies and a few dozen easy classical pieces; the generator's yield there is unlimited
   and licence-free, and its level-4 bulge is what makes the bottom of the ladder look thin
   today. So P12a/b run before the quarry run, and the quarry *tooling* (P13) is built in the
   container in parallel — it needs no archive, only a fixture.
2. **PDMX is worth most as a verification source.** P5 skipped forty Part F tunes because
   nothing could be checked against. PDMX's folk corpus is that check. Authoring those tunes
   in ABC against a PDMX reference, at Stages 1–3, is a bigger gain than any Chopin, and it
   is the reason the Stage 1–2 quota in §2.2 is small: the pipeline needs the reference, not
   the file.
3. **Weight: the finder and the shelf are the same feature.** Both answer "this rung needs
   music the app does not have"; both end in a piece attached to a rung, levelled and tagged.
   They share the assign sheet, the overlay that makes an owner-supplied option count, and the
   `needs` line. P15 builds the mechanism once; P16 adds books on top of it.
4. **What the brief does not ask for and this plan adds:** the missing upper rungs for five
   tracks (§8), a practice-method module, and blind mode. None of the six ideas fixes a
   library where the jazz track has three items; §3.2 does.

Nothing in the brief is dropped.

**Carry-overs (added 2026-09-06 at the owner's request).** The "not done" lists in the P6,
P7 and P8 decision notes were never picked up: a count-in and metronome for the rhythm
drill and drills listening to the microphone (both into P12b, whose new drills need them);
named loop sections, the mic's amber state, drag-to-reorder tracks, the chord chart's
bass-and-drums loop, strict prerequisites and the tablet side panel (P18, one session,
independent of the rest). What stays on the owner's own checklist is everything that needs
the phone: the APK, Web MIDI inside the TWA, a real Android share, a bought PDF through
adjust-cuts, airplane mode from the second launch.

## 11. Rejected alternatives, briefly

- *Unpack PDMX into the repo or CI* — 1.9 GB, not reachable from CI, and the point of a
  library is selection.
- *Trust the CSV's licence column as a statement about the composition* — §0 shows it is
  the uploader's claim about the edition. The owner chose to accept that for his own phone
  (D23); the plan still labels every item so the public deploy can refuse what it must.
- *Use ratings as an admission gate* — they order the queue; the quality gates and a person
  admit.
- *A second renderer for the thirteen Chopin scores* — §1.1.
- *A Markov sight-reading model* — §3.2.
- *OMR for the shelf* — ruled out in P4 and nothing has changed.
- *Hardcoding the finder prompts in the lesson screen* — 66 copies of one sentence, with no
  validation; the brief already says why.
- *A per-rung "difficulty" separate from `level`* — one number, two uses, explained; a second
  number would be a third source of truth.
