# An audit of the 2026-09-18 work, and whether it is worth keeping

The owner asked for an honest look rather than more fixes: *"tell me, is this fixable or is
this just a garbled, complicated mess… that I should just give up on and wait until I can
get [a reviewer] to organize things better."*

Nothing here was fixed. Everything found is written down.

---

## The verdict first

**It is fixable, and it is not garbled — but the two halves of it are not the same risk and
should not be judged together.**

| | What it is | Confidence | If you threw it away |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tooling and checks** | 6 files of pipeline code, 4 new test files, 2 schema fields | **High** | You would lose two genuine pre-existing bug fixes and every guard that now stops this class of mistake |
| **Content** | 9 new rungs, 9 new lessons, ~15 option additions | **Low to medium** | You would lose a day of content and nothing else would break |

**They are cleanly separable.** The content is nine units in `content/curriculum/stage-*.json`
and nine files in `content/lessons/`. Reverting it is a listable operation, and the tooling
does not depend on it. The reverse is not true: the checks are what found the content faults.

**The strongest argument for keeping the tooling** is that it fixed two things that were
broken before any of this started, both of which had been shipping for weeks:

- The Library's **Latin and Rock & metal filters contained no songs at all**, because a
  song's genre came from its import bucket. 87 rows were mis-tagged.
- The build was computing 19 measurements per score and **throwing all of them away**, so
  every downstream decision was made from a title and a number.

**The honest argument against the content** is below, and it is not "the music is wrong" —
that was measured separately. It is that several rungs are thin and repetitive in ways a
real player would notice within a minute.

---

## What was measured, and how

**Claims about music: 20 checkable, 1 wrong.** Every factual statement the nine lessons make
about a specific piece — key, metre, staves, bars, chord symbols — checked against notation
read from the file. The one wrong claim (*Bella Ciao* "is in E minor"; it has three key
signatures and ends in F♯ minor) was found, removed, and is now a regression test.

**What that number does not cover:** whether a lesson is any *good*. No test can tell you
whether `rock.4` teaches the power chord well. That is the part only a musician reading it
can judge, and it has not been judged.

---

## Findings, by severity

### HIGH — three rungs are thinner than they look

**1. `rock.5` offers one exercise four times.** Its four "options" are
`open-voicing.c.sus2`, `.c.sus4`, `.c.add9` and `.f.sus4` — one generated family with the
parameter changed. A learner opening that rung sees four cards and has one thing to do.
*Fix:* add a second family, or drop to two options and mark it song-optional honestly.

**2. `rock.4` owns none of its material.** Every one of its six options also appears on
another rung, and worse, the four exercises are ones this same session placed on core rungs
`2.1`, `2.3` and `3.3`. A learner who did the core path arrives at `rock.4` and is offered
the same four exercises again. *Fix:* the generated rock families should be on the rock rung
**or** the core rungs, not both — probably core, with `rock.4` naming them as revision and
adding its own material.

**3. `hymns.2` owns none of its material either.** Its four songs come off the `hymns` list
rung and its three exercises off core rungs. It is a re-presentation, which is defensible as
a *placement fix* (those four songs are Stage 2 music that was sitting on a Stage 3 rung)
but is not a new rung's worth of content.

### MEDIUM — seven

- **`rock.6`'s band spans four levels (3.4–7.4)** and `rock.7`'s spans 3.2. The lesson page
  prints the band, so a learner reads "this rung is somewhere between Grade 1 and Grade 7".
  `rock.6`'s lesson argues for it in a paragraph; `rock.7`'s does not.
- **Five rungs draw their exercises from only two families each** (`rock.7`, `jam.5`,
  `jam.6`, `latin.3`, `holiday.3`). Less severe than `rock.5` but the same shape.

### LOW — two

- **`rock.6` and `jam.6` each have a lab button the prose never mentions.** A control with
  no explanation is not wrong, but the "Tools for this rung" paragraph is where the *why*
  lives and it is silent on both.

### Not a finding, checked and cleared

- **`content/sources/pdmx.json` shows 8,186 changed lines to alter three rows.** A
  `JSON.stringify` rewrite reformatted the whole 533-row table. Verified semantically: 533
  rows before and after, **none removed, none added, exactly three changed, each exactly as
  intended**. It is diff noise, not data loss — but it makes that file unreviewable and
  should be restored and re-edited surgically.
- The five generated families are one shape per family across their keys, which is what a
  family is.

---

## What is genuinely unverified

1. **Whether the nine lessons are pedagogically sound.** They were written to be accurate
   and were checked for accuracy. Nobody has judged whether they teach.
2. **Whether the generated music sounds like what it claims.** Five faults were found by
   rendering and looking, but the previews are **crops** — which is exactly how the one
   wrong claim got through. No generated family has been played.
3. **Whether the new rungs are in the right place in each track's order.** Validation checks
   bands and options; nothing checks that `rock.5` should come after `rock.4`.
4. **Everything above Stage 7.** The upper holes were left alone deliberately and are
   untouched by any of this.

---

## If you keep it, the order to fix in

1. `rock.4`'s duplication with the core rungs — the one a learner would actually hit.
2. `rock.5`'s four-of-one-family.
3. Restore `pdmx.json`'s formatting so the three-row change is reviewable.
4. The two unexplained lab buttons.
5. `rock.7`'s band, or a paragraph justifying it as `rock.6` has.

None of these needs new music. All five are edits to files that already exist.

## If you throw the content away

`git checkout -- content/curriculum/ content/lessons/` and delete the nine new lesson files
and the nine units. The tooling, the tests, the schema fields, the two pre-existing bug
fixes and the archive search all survive untouched, because none of them depends on the
content existing.
