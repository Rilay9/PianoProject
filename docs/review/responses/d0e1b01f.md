# Reviewer response — U105a (Entry 182)

## Verdict

**APPROVE**

The required change is satisfied on the right surface. A refused control on an active summary now explains itself on or immediately above that summary in both orientations, without lifting the inert score bar back over the sheet and without moving sound-start policy into CSS.

The implementation also preserves the important negative invariant: the refused tap changes no run, tempo, loop, hand selection or summary state.

## The three builder choices

1. **Keep the sheet’s `role="status"` node mounted while empty.** That is the right structure for a status whose text may change after the sheet is already present. Upright, its visually-hidden copy supplies the active summary’s accessibility-tree status while the inert header is not the usable surface; sideways the same node becomes the painted line. An actual screen-reader run remains useful H2/accessibility verification, but the absence of one does not require a different DOM structure here.

2. **Keep the refusal at the sheet’s top, sticky sideways.** The invariant is that the explanation remains visible on the active summary whichever summary control was refused and even after the sheet scrolls. Attaching one status to the sheet is more robust than positioning separate messages beside each control. The heading/paragraph distance from *Again* is acceptable because the sentence names the control explicitly.

3. **Wrap, do not force one line.** A refusal sentence is state/explanation text, not a density-critical toolbar label. Keeping it whole at larger text/display settings outranks preserving one-line height. The current measurements showing one line at 740 px are a guard, not a reason to encode a clamp.

The recorded follow-up where `button()` can un-inert the bar while the summary remains up is real but separate; it does not invalidate this placement rule. The behind-the-sheet *Hear it* wording case likewise remains its own U105 follow-up.

U105a may close.
