### 3a. technique.7 — remove the unsupported comparative physiology

The landed text says of the octave tremolo:

> "it is the exercise here where the forearm tightens first"

and then:

> "Octaves, double notes and the tremolo are where strain gathers fastest."

The seam's evidence establishes the written exercises and the absence of an earlier stop rule; it does not establish which exercise causes this learner's forearm to tighten first or where strain accumulates fastest. Those are exactly the sort of body claims this process cannot observe.

Use non-comparative safety wording, e.g.:

> "The octave tremolo is often helped by a small forearm rotation. While practising octaves, double notes or tremolo, if your forearm or wrist tightens or aches, stop..."

Keep the stop condition; remove the ranking.

### 3b. jam.7 — remove the residual historical generalisation

The edit correctly removes the invented "because that is where the horns played them in 1920", but leaves:

> "Four of these five sit in flat keys, as much early jazz does..."

The seam evidence establishes the five files' keys, not that broader historical proposition. Nothing pedagogical is lost by saying simply:

> "Four of these five sit in flat keys, and the guitarist's keys are E, A, D and G."

### 3c. theory.9 — make the new form paragraph examples, not universal definitions

The added paragraph says:

> "Three names cover most of what you will take down or find" and defines AABA as "four eight-bar sections..."

The seam evidence was that the finder asks for ABA / a 32-bar song; it does not establish the "most" claim, and AABA as a formal pattern is not inherently restricted to four eight-bar sections unless you mean the common 32-bar AABA song form.

Use:

> "Three useful form names here are ABA; the common 32-bar AABA song form, with four eight-bar sections and a contrasting third section/bridge; and binary, AB..."

That teaches exactly what this rung needs without turning the finder examples into universal form definitions.
