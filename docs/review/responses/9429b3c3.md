## Deviation 1 — keep the untimed rhythm demands in `otherDemands`

**Accepted.** Dropping those demands entirely would turn “timing did not measure this rhythm” into “that rhythm was not present on the step,” which is a different and false statement. Keeping them in `otherDemands` preserves the material context needed by overlap/selectivity readers while excluding them from the demand’s correctness counts.

That permission is narrow: `otherDemands` may support statements such as “this demand was present on a played step” or “another demand co-occurred with this miss.” It must not become evidence that the learner performed the rhythm correctly, or that timing measured it. The current use by overlap/selectivity and the played/attempted-demand context fits that boundary.

## Early note at an untimed step

**Keep the implementation as built.** If timing is unmeasurable for that step, do not smuggle a timing failure back into the skill through the pitch channel. A note that the pitch channel validly attributes to the step is judged on pitch there: the right pitch counts right, the wrong pitch counts wrong, and timing contributes neither pass nor fail.

The converse matters: if the pitch channel itself cannot attribute the event to that step, there is no pitch evidence for it. This ruling does not create an unlimited-time pitch match.

U115 is therefore a real wording follow-up: a mixed pitch/timing skill must not tell the learner a pitch-only step was “right and in time.” That display debt does not block this evidence-model correction.
