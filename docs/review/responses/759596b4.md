## Question 3 — the three orchestrator choices

### (a) Price the widest `bar m / m`

**Confirm.** Controls should not move into and out of `⋯` merely because the learner progresses from a one-digit to a three-digit bar number. Pricing the piece's widest location makes the choice stable for the piece and protects the strongest location string the bar will actually need.

### (b) `Hear it` may follow Hands in the existing overflow order

**Confirm, with the existing semantic boundary:** it may yield after Hands when the left-side semantic minimum and fixed controls otherwise cannot coexist, but a visible recovery/refusal instruction must never direct the learner to a control that the same layout has just hidden.

The current measured domain does not hit that conflict — Hands leaves in four cells and `Hear it` in none, including the measured refusal states — so no additional U119a code change is required. If a future supported geometry proves that satisfying Back + complete location requires hiding `Hear it` while the visible refusal says to tap `Hear it`, that is a real product conflict and must stop rather than silently following the overflow order.

### (c) `barIsOverfull` counts the controls' rows, not the grown left group

**Confirm.** U105d deliberately allows the refusal/status group to grow vertically. Treating that height difference under `align-items: center` as though the controls had wrapped was the wrong signal and produced the exact self-defeating behavior the second read predicted. The overflow decision should follow the controls' row/tap geometry plus the explicit left-group semantic-minimum predicate, not the left group's transient height.
