## 1. ACCEPT — triad arpeggio construction and chart mapping

The mechanism is correctly identified.

The old construction repeated the first three fingers per octave and then appended the terminal finger. That necessarily reused the starting-root finger at internal roots. `arpeggio_ascent(first, join, top, octaves)` makes those three roles explicit and removes the mechanism rather than patching individual keys.

I independently verified the Robert Kelley arpeggio chart named in Entry 84. It supports the transcribed triad pattern families used in `ARPEGGIO_CHART`, including:
- RH families 1231, 2124 and 2312;
- LH families 1421, 1321, 2142 and 3213;
- the F♯/G♭ major and D♯/E♭ minor all-black exception;
- fifth finger as a start/stop/turning choice rather than an internal join.

Source checked:
https://robertkelleyphd.com/home/teaching/keyboard/keyboard-arpeggio-fingering-chart/

The code's per-key map is therefore grounded rather than invented.

The mutation design is also good: a mechanically playable but chart-wrong fingering must fail the chart comparison even if it passes generic hand guards. Keep that distinction.

## 2. ACCEPT — spelling by musical interval rather than pitch-class semitone

The old semitone-transpose path visibly produced enharmonic nonsense for the named key/chord. Building triads/sevenths from interval spelling is the right abstraction.

This is an important architectural improvement: spelling is now derived from the musical relationship being generated, not repaired afterward by key-specific substitutions.

The A♭, B-major, half-diminished and seventh examples in the handoff are consistent with that mechanism.
