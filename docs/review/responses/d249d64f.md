## 1. Push / closure

**Yes, push it.**

Do not manufacture a deliberately failed-fetch Pages deployment merely to observe the refusal on GitHub's runner. The failure semantics are already structurally pinned:

- the guard step is after `npm run build`;
- it is before `configure-pages` and `upload-pages-artifact`;
- it has no `continue-on-error` or permissive `if`;
- the deploy job needs the build job;
- a guard failure therefore prevents artifact upload and prevents deploy-pages from running.

The local strict full/unfetched pair plus the workflow mutants are sufficient evidence for the refusal path.

After the push, read the normal healthy Pages run once to confirm that the new **Guard the deploy** step actually executes successfully in the runner environment and that the artifact/deploy proceeds. The *failed-fetch runner path* may remain explicitly unobserved until it occurs naturally.

That unobserved failure path does **not** keep Q88 open.

Q88 closes after the landed/pushed change and the ordinary healthy runner read-back. If the healthy runner itself exposes an environment/path problem, fix forward from that concrete failure.

## 2. Input directory

That is better than guarding `app/public/content` because the product question is: *is the artifact we are about to publish complete?*

The guard should inspect the bytes under the upload root. The test pinning the guard directory to `upload-pages-artifact`'s path plus `/content` is therefore correct.

Do not add a second comparison against `app/public/content` unless a future Vite/config change gives reason to suspect the published copy diverges.

## Workflow-speed note

This seam is a good example of where the process should **not** become more conservative than the risk warrants. The architecture was already reviewed, the complete workflow diff is present, the local refusal/pass pair and six workflow mutants cover the meaningful failure modes, and the remaining uncertainty is runner-environment execution. That uncertainty is best resolved by pushing the approved change and reading the real run, not by inventing another pre-push simulation gate.

Use that principle generally: serialize or add a gate when a wrong decision would corrupt shared truth, user data, architecture, or deployment safety; otherwise prefer bounded parallel work plus targeted verification and post-build review.
