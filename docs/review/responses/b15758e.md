## Findings

1. **BLOCKING — BLOCKS NEXT BRIEF.** Version 2 does not preserve its hard layer when the bounded search finds no valid candidate. In `compose`, `rightBars` and `leftBars` are overwritten on every draw. Only promise-keeping draws enter `pool`, and only hard-valid pool entries can be selected. But if `chooseCandidate` returns `undefined`, the code deliberately leaves the last generated draw in `rightBars`/`leftBars` and returns it: “None valid within the budget: the last draw stands, as in version 1.” That last draw may have failed a requested promise, and even a promise-keeping last draw may have failed `hardViolations`. The sampled 23 configurations finding a valid candidate does not establish this for every options combination the reader can construct. This contradicts the approved D1 contract that the scorer never rescues or emits a candidate violating requested promises, taught material, range, metre, or tie constraints.

   **Required mechanism:** make the no-valid-candidate outcome explicit and safe. Version 2 must return only a candidate that passed both the promise predicate and `hardViolations`. If the bounded candidate window contains none, either continue deterministically within the existing overall promise budget until one is found, construct a valid fallback that is checked by the same predicates, or refuse generation with a concrete reason. Never fall through to an arbitrary last draw. Add a focused regression that forces the zero-valid-candidate path and proves no invalid phrase is returned, plus a test that a valid candidate found after the scoring window is handled according to the chosen policy. Keep version 1's note-for-note behavior unchanged.

2. **ACCEPT — CONSTRAINS NEXT BRIEF.** The optional `SessionRow.generator` record field is the right next step, with no `DB_VERSION` bump. Missing data must mean sight-reading generator version 1; newly recorded phrases must store family, version, and seed; phrase-seen comparisons and evidence reconstruction must compare version as well as seed. Flip `SIGHT_READING_IN_FORCE` to 2 only after those writers and readers, the seven named tests, and Finding 1 are complete.

3. **ACCEPT — CONSTRAINS NEXT BRIEF.** Keep the capped chord-tone move at levels 5–7. It preserves the level's hard leap cap while retaining harmonic predictability on strong beats where possible; the remaining unsnapped cases are exposed to the harmony score rather than allowed to violate the cap. The distribution report shows 100% strong-beat chord tones for the sampled level 5–7 configurations without material density drift. This is an acceptable, bounded deviation from scoring alone.

4. **ACCEPT — CONSTRAINS NEXT BRIEF.** Keep the stricter tie rule: an off-beat tie is allowed only where the generated phrase intentionally carries syncopation. Once syncopation has been taught, that does not require every later phrase to introduce it accidentally. The rule is represented explicitly in `phraseRules`, tested in simple and compound metre, and leaves version 1 untouched.

5. **ACCEPT — CONSTRAINS NEXT BRIEF.** The generator identity boundary is handled correctly in D1 itself. `SightReadingResult.generator` binds family, version, and seed, while `SIGHT_READING_IN_FORCE` remains 1 because encounter history cannot yet preserve the new identity. Do not let the version-2 distribution results justify changing learner history before the record work in Finding 2 lands.

6. **ACCEPT — LATER WAVE.** The distribution evidence supports the claimed improvement but does not settle musical quality. Arrival improves across all 23 configurations and contour improves materially, while levels 5–7 remain mostly walks and level 1 near-duplicates worsen. Keep S34 and S35 open; do not raise their observed sample shares into universal product guarantees. Nothing has been heard, and the record states that correctly.

7. **ACCEPT — PRUNE/MERGE.** The named implementation, scorer tests, distribution suite, helper, preserved promise suite, documentation, red runs, tables, and status lines support this seam. The unrelated CRLF failures and isolated load timeouts do not belong in D1. D2 and E0a remain independent.
