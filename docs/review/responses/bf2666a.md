# E1 brief review — bf2666a

**Verdict: APPROVE WITH ONE REQUIRED CHANGE**

The first-class excerpt design is the right abstraction. A cut score with its own catalogue row, local measurement, provenance, review identity, eligibility, and evidence reference preserves the distinctions in Part 24; reusing `teaching.sections` or attaching a range to the parent would not. Cutting approved definitions from the parent's built notation before the existing score, demand, provenance, and render stages is the smallest domain-complete mechanism. The proposer remains advisory, and E2 retains chooser ownership.

## Required change

- **BLOCKS NEXT BRIEF — remove the owner and unsupported “teacher” judgment as gates.** The brief says placement is “F's and the owner's,” requires the builder to approve the first excerpts “as a teacher,” and asks the orchestrator to read renders “as a teacher.” Yali has explicitly said they do not know how to make these musical judgments and do not want human-required work assigned to them. Revise E1 so Yali supplies neither musical-quality review nor rung placement. The builder may inspect notation and record what the defined signals and source-backed rules establish; the reviewer may audit that evidence and the implementation. Neither may silently claim teacher or musical-ear authority. If a boundary, teaching-use decision, or placement genuinely requires expertise beyond substantiated rules, keep it undecided and out of learner assignments until an appropriate reviewer is available. This should also replace “placement is F's and the owner's” with a non-owner gate and state who owns it. The first handful may be built into the Library as unplaced candidates, with uncertainty explicit; E1 does not need to fabricate placement or quality to finish.

With that correction, dispatch is appropriate when the brief's other gates are met.

## Architectural findings

- **ACCEPT — first-class object and cut mechanism.** `type: excerpt`, `excerptOf`, a dedicated score under `scores/excerpts/`, excerpt-local level and demands, inherited licensing, and no change to `teaching.sections` match Part 24. Running the cut through the ordinary downstream build avoids a parallel truth path.
- **ACCEPT — identity.** The derived id is appropriate here: moving a boundary changes the material presented and therefore creates a new content identity, while changing the excerpt's display label does not. The provenance key over parent bytes, printed range, selection, and cut version gives Parts 26–27 a stable versioned fingerprint without prematurely designing encounter migration. A future UI may group versions by parent and intent; it should not make them one evidence identity.
- **ACCEPT — one-hand score.** Dropping the unselected staff is the honest representation of what the learner is asked to read and measure. The workbench still shows the parent with surrounding context, so boundary review is not deprived of the other staff. The cut must retain enough initial clef, key, meter, tempo, and hand labeling to read independently, as the brief already tests.
- **ACCEPT — proposal by positions.** Scoring candidates from detector locations is consistent with “mining proposes, never creates truth,” because only the approved cut's own detector pass becomes catalogue truth. The deviation clause is necessary: any demand whose detector locations do not compose into window-local density must be scored on a cut or excluded from that proposal claim.
- **ACCEPT — refuse repeat/volta crossings in E1.** Unrolling or interpreting alternate endings changes the transformation contract. Refusal is a safer first seam; a later cutter version can add an explicit, tested interpretation.
- **CONSTRAINS NEXT BRIEF — type consumers must be semantically reviewed, not merely made exhaustive.** The current Library filter union only names song, exercise, and drill, while session helpers use `item.type === 'song'` to define repertoire behavior. E1 correctly keeps excerpts out of learned-piece retention and “performed,” but the implementation review must verify each type reader's intended treatment. Passing TypeScript is not sufficient if an `else` branch silently treats excerpts as exercises or songs.
- **CONSTRAINS NEXT BRIEF — attribution must survive the normalized score header.** Removing parent credits from the cut file may make byte identity stable, but the catalogue/provenance and every export or display path must still preserve the parent edition's required attribution and licence. Normalization cannot erase legal provenance from the user-facing artifact.
- **LATER WAVE — density hypotheses.** `minInWindow` may be a useful proposal and establishment rule, but each value remains a hypothesis. Report refused candidates and avoid treating the threshold as proof of teaching quality.
- **PRUNE/MERGE — no new general-purpose excerpt chooser.** Candidate-rung reporting belongs here; selection, cross-source ranking, novelty, and experience choice remain E2/Parts 25–27 as the brief states.

This review does not approve any particular excerpt, boundary, musical-quality decision, or rung placement. It approves the architecture subject to the required ownership correction.
