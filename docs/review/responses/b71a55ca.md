## Verdict

**APPROVE WITH ONE REQUIRED CHANGE**

The import sheet now tells the truth about the file: it reads the opening bar's `<sound tempo>` as quarter notes per minute, keeps the printed metronome mark's own beat unit beside it, identifies a text-only mark as the app's interpretation, does not call a later change the opening, keeps authored and file facts separate, and handles fractional values without silently rounding the file's number. The store remains the sole writer.

### BLOCKS NEXT BRIEF — make the Score screen play and label the same canonical tempo the sheet and control state

X3c's own probe establishes a live learner-facing contradiction:

- a half-note mark at 60 with `<sound tempo="120">` is truthfully shown on the sheet as 120 quarter notes per minute, but the Score model opens at 60;
- a dotted-quarter mark at 60 with `<sound tempo="90">` opens at 60;
- a later quarter-note mark can replace an explicit opening `<sound tempo>`;
- after the learner states 100 or 72 through E48, marked files can still play at 50 or 132.

This means the tempo correction control does not reliably control the music, and the app can teach or practise at a tempo different from the one it states. X24 is therefore not closed merely by truthful sheet copy.

Fix the canonical score-model tempo path before another import door opens. The owner is the score model/tempo-map adapter, not an import-store rewrite: the same defect affects bundled scores and ordinary MusicXML, including at least the two probed bundled pieces. Normalize metronome `beat-unit` and dots to quarter-note BPM, honor event position, and define precedence with `<sound tempo>` so the Score label, playback clock, count-in, measurement, import sheet and E48 statement consume one tempo map. Do not patch only imported XML or reproduce OSMD's misreading in the sheet.

Required adversaries:

1. half note = 60 plus sound 120 opens, labels and plays at 120;
2. dotted quarter = 60 plus sound 90 opens at 90;
3. an opening sound 100 is not displaced by a mark appearing only in bar 2;
4. E48's stated 100 and 72 override those two marked shapes in the actual Score path;
5. a genuine later tempo change remains later;
6. quarter-note and fractional controls remain unchanged.

The import sheet's accepted words should consume the same canonical result after this change, rather than maintaining a second tempo interpretation.
