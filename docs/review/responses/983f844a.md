## 1. U68 — one staff for a left-hand-only item

**A genuinely one-hand-only item may use one staff whether it is a drill or a piece. Do not make “piece of music” versus “exercise” the boundary.**

The correct boundary is semantic:

- the item is genuinely for one hand only;
- the hand role is explicit data, not inferred from clef, staff number, silence, pitch or register;
- the clef remains a notation fact chosen for the written range, independently of hand ownership;
- every consumer that currently needs hand truth reads that explicit role.

So a left-hand scale, five-finger pattern, Hanon-style exercise or tumbao may all be one-staff when the other staff communicates nothing. A two-staff representation may still be kept where the second staff deliberately communicates a pedagogical relationship, but not merely because the extractor otherwise guesses `R`.

U68 therefore remains its **own lane**. The 214-item census is the right scope input; do not bulk-convert those rows until the explicit hand-role contract, extractor read and continuity/version consequences are pre-reviewed. The current CL15 choice to leave them on two staves is correct.

## 2. `transferPolicy.newSeedOf` across a family version bump

**This is a fault, not intended behavior. Record it as its own evidence/transfer-policy row.**

`newSeedOf` currently defines “same generator material, new seed” as the same family **and exact version** and recipe. A family-wide version bump can therefore turn an otherwise unchanged recipe into apparent new transfer material merely because another shape in the family changed. CL15's per-item former-generator relation preserves contact/history for an unchanged exact item, but it does not prove that a different seed under the bumped version should escape the anti-loop rule.

Do **not** fix this by simply ignoring generator version globally: when the generator rule actually changed, version is meaningful and a new seed may genuinely be different material. The follow-up should establish a relation-aware rule for “same seed family/recipe across a version transition whose relevant recipe semantics did not change,” then pin both adversaries:

1. an unchanged recipe across a coarse family bump still counts as a new seed of established material and cannot demonstrate transfer merely because the version number changed;
2. a recipe whose generation semantics did change is not silently collapsed across the version boundary.

This belongs to the transfer/evidence owner, not CL15's generator implementation. Treat it as P2 unless the row's reach check finds a live current transfer-intended route through one of these bumped families; if it does, raise it to the owning current-priority seam.

## 3. Walking-bass closing bar

**Confirm the notation fact and the detector reason.**

For the blues forms, bars 1–12 remain the twelve-bar form and the added bar is a closing tonic tag after the form; for ii–V–I the final I bar itself now closes on the tonic octave. Keeping the closing bar as root–third–fifth–octave is internally consistent with the current whole-piece `left-hand-pattern` / `walking-bass` detector contract: a held whole-note tonic would make the item's own declared walking-pattern evidence disappear from the rows the handoff names.

That is a correctness statement about the written exercise and detector contract, **not** a judgement that the walking close is musically preferable to a held tonic. The latter remains unverified as music. The titles “over a 12-bar blues” remain truthful because the harmonic form itself is still the first twelve bars; the extra bar is the finite ending after that form, not a thirteenth bar inserted into `TWELVE_BAR`.
