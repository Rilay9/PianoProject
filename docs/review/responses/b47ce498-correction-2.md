# Correction 2 to `responses/b47ce498.md`

This correction refines the **procedure amendment** in `responses/b47ce498-correction-1.md`. The U122 product correction remains: redesign the whole landscape Score chrome rather than merely optimizing the bottom bar. What changes here is the process rule intended to prevent recurrence.

## Procedure amendment — continuous holistic reassessment, not a narrower gate

The previous correction said to challenge the container and named a specific alternative-decomposition exercise. That is useful for U122, but as a standing rule it risks becoming exactly the failure mode it is meant to prevent: another guardrail that can be satisfied mechanically and then used as permission to stop thinking.

The standing doctrine is now `docs/review/holistic-reassessment.md`.

Its governing principle is:

> Do not treat an accepted premise, boundary, brief, abstraction, or prior ruling as permission to stop thinking about whether the overall approach is still right.

This is a **working posture**, not a one-time gate and not a checklist with a pass state. Builder, orchestrator, and reviewer should step back whenever the accumulated evidence, complexity, learner experience, or newly visible alternative could materially change the approach. The reassessment may confirm the current approach; it may also reopen scope, surface ownership, abstraction, architecture, task framing, or a prior local implementation assumption.

There is deliberately no fixed lane count, no mandatory number of alternatives, and no requirement to brainstorm on every small bug. The question is whether further thought could materially improve the decision. Increasingly elaborate machinery to preserve an inherited boundary is one strong signal, but not the only one.

For U122 specifically, comparing materially different landscape Score decompositions remains required because the exact failure was that the design space had been constrained to one surface. That comparison is evidence needed for this lane, not a universal ritual to impose on every future lane.

The reviewer must challenge the shape of the question as well as the correctness of the answer. The builder/orchestrator must not use reviewer approval as a shield against later evidence that the product shape should change. Approval records the best judgement from the evidence at that time; it does not make the premise sacred.

This correction supersedes only the **standing-process formulation** in `b47ce498-correction-1.md`. Its U122 landscape-Score redesign requirement remains in force.
