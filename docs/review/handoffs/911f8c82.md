**The screen, in product terms, first (the question before the mechanism).** On a short landscape phone screen, the Score screen's chrome is in question: at the top, nothing today, except under the recommended model below; at the bottom, one row that today holds everything — Back, the piece's name, `bar n / m`, the mode, Hands, the tempo, ▶, ⋯ and the ordinary status line; and, only while it is needed, a sound refusal's sentence. The recommendation below (c6) moves two of those items to the top: a thin line carrying the piece's name and `bar n / m`, drawn inside the same band a run already reserves at the top of the stage for `bar n / m`, so during a run it costs nothing beyond what the run already prices; and when the chrome folds away mid-run, the small chip that takes the bar's place already says `bar n / m` in that same corner. Everything a learner's hands reach for while playing stays at the bottom, in one row: Back, ▶, `Hear it`, the mode, Hands, the tempo and ⋯, with the ordinary status line (the paused line, a practice ladder's pass line) sharing whatever width is left. A sound refusal is the one thing that is ever transient: its sentence, naming the control it refers to, appears on a line of its own directly above the bottom row, and only for as long as the refusal stands. Why this split: chrome drawn inside the bottom bar is paid for only while the screen is at rest, because a run gives the bar's entire row back to the music at the run's start; and a run already reserves a band at the top of the stage for `bar n / m`, so a second line of orientation placed inside that same band is free during a run and costs the at-rest screen only one line's height. Controls stay where the hand already is; the refusal, being exceptional, is the only thing allowed to take extra room, and only while it is actually shown.

**The recommendation (c6), as a trade, not a choice already made.** Gains: the piece's name and `bar n / m` are drawn whole in every one of the 86 sideways cells measured, at rest and while paused alike — today's one row only manages that at rest, and only in 39 of 86; the music no longer jumps 22 px when the chrome folds away mid-run or comes back, because the top line sits below the reserved band from the run's start rather than moving into it at the fold; and Hands stays on the row, rather than behind ⋯, in 3 cells where today's layout already hides it. Losses: at rest, the stave is smaller in 16 of the 86 sideways cells measured — a few percent at one size, 7–10% at two others — and every one of those 16 is a cell where the screen's own height, not the look-ahead rule, was already deciding the size; the smallest affected stave is still about 29 px, and every other affected cell is above 50 px. A run's size is unchanged in every cell, and the next bar stays visible everywhere, under every candidate. While a sound refusal is shown over a paused run, its sentence covers the foot of the music in 36 of 86 cells under c6, against 13 under today's layout, because the music sits a little lower whenever the top line is drawn. If this trade is declined, today's one-row layout (c1) is the zero-cost choice: it costs the music nothing anywhere, at the price of losing the piece's name whenever the screen is paused, and keeping the 22-px jump at the fold.

**Why c6 and not one of the others.** The owner asked, on 2026-10-01, why everything sits on one line, and why not put the piece's name and the bar location at the top of the screen — c6 is exactly that shape: the name and `bar n / m` move to the top, and the bottom row keeps every control a learner's hand uses, rather than hiding some of them behind ⋯ the way the other role-based candidates (c4, c5) do. c4, the most literal reading of putting Back and the name at the top, turns out to be the most expensive candidate measured: restoring a Back button to a top zone sets that zone's own height at 36–46 px, which is why it costs the stave in 46 of 86 cells, by as much as a fifth, and sends the selected mode and the ladder's tempo off the screen in every cell, reversing an existing acceptance (U121). c3 (a corner overlay) is ruled out on its own terms: it sits over fingerings and other ink in more than half the cells measured, and the correction already rules out chrome over the score unless the region is measured clear. c6 was derived only after measuring the others, specifically to recover what c4 and c5 give up: every control stays on the bottom row, readable and reachable, while the top line still costs nothing during a run.

**CL07's reading objective, in one line.** A chrome's height only ever costs music size on the wide, short phone screens at rest, where the screen's own height, not the look-ahead rule, decides the size; every other cell, upright or tablet, is unaffected by any candidate measured — the same stage, stave and number of systems in all 52 upright cells and both tablet cells under today's layout and under the most expensive candidate alike. U5's own tablet case is reproduced at the two tablet sizes, 1024 × 768 and 1366 × 1024: Bars 2, two systems of one bar each, no third bar inked, the stave many times the readable floor, unchanged by the chrome decision either way. So the phone's readable-floor question and the tablet's look-ahead question are each untouched by this decision; they stay with the window rule as it already stands.
