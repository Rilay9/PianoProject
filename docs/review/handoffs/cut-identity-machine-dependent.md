## 2. Two solution classes; the orchestrator's lean and the question

- **(b) Deterministic archive bytes.** The cutter writes stored entries (no compression) with the pinned headers, so the `.mxl` bytes are a pure function of the inner entries on any machine. Identity stays "the built file's sha256" as `content/review/README.md` defines it; every cut's identity moves once; the Bizet fact row is re-bound by `passages.py --verify` on the new identity (the row's identity field, itemised), the E50b relation re-recorded, and Entry 253's decision is stale by the README's rule (a file whose bytes change) and needs your re-issue on the new identity, which this handoff asks for in §3. A test asserts no compressed entry in a cut and recomputes the archive from the inner bytes to the same sha. Cost: six cuts grow from about 2 KB to about 22 KB each. Risk: any future header change moves identities again.
- **(c) Identity by notation.** A cut's identity becomes the sha256 of its inner entries in a canonical order, machine-independent by construction; the archive bytes stop mattering. It changes the README's definition for cuts, the catalogue's `identityOf`, E0's cache key for cuts, the app-side identity reader and its tests; the same one-time move of every cut's identity and the same re-binding as (b).

The orchestrator's lean: **(b)**, because it keeps one identity definition for every notated file and the smallest blast radius, and the deflate variance is the only known source. Say (b), (c) or a third shape.
