## What is asked

The engine carries its own copy of the tick interval — `ScoreSession.ts` is read-only here, so it cannot import the engine's constant, and a test pins the two equal. A loop's wrap behaves the same way as the last step: the next lap's downbeat moves later by the wait, because the wrap rebases the lap from the clock at the tick that completes it — the lap test pins this, the base's downbeat position reading early by exactly the wait, though the stall itself already moved that downbeat before this change and the hold adds at most a tick interval and a tick. **What changes for a learner, unverified on a device** (nothing here was heard or run on a phone; observed in the engine on its injected clock and in Chromium on real timers).

- A MIDI note played in time during a stall that outlasts the note's remaining tolerance: before, the step was painted red as missed and the key flashed red as an extra wrong note — two faults for one note played in time; now it is a hit, judged green with the delta its stamp gives, and no miss or extra is counted. Observed live-stamped, on the replay path on the engine clock, and in Chromium with the page busy 400 ms (B1, red 3/3 on the base, green 1/1 alone then 5/5); also at the window's inclusive edge and on a rhythm-only run.
- The last note of a run: a stall across the run's end no longer loses it. The end waits for the hold — the finish sheet and the lap's score come at most one tick interval and one tick after the stalled tick — and the note is counted. While the end waits, a note stamped after the last window, one stamped after the run's end, and a wrong key are each dropped, as the finished run always dropped them; none becomes a wrong note.
- A loop's wrap behaves the same way: the next lap's downbeat moves later by the wait, because the wrap rebases the lap from the clock at the tick that completes it (pinned by the lap test, the base's downbeat position reading early by exactly the wait); the stall itself already moved that downbeat before this change, and the hold adds at most a tick interval and a tick.
- When a miss mark lands: on an unstalled run, on the tick it always did; after a stall, a window nothing arrived for is marked missed on the first tick at least one tick interval after the stalled tick — later than before by at most one tick interval and one tick. A jittered interval tick more than 25 ms after the previous one also counts as a stall, at the same bounded cost.
- An on-screen tap is unchanged: stamped when its handler runs, after the stall, and judged as before — an extra, with the window missed.
- The stated limits are unchanged: a note that reaches the engine more than a second after its stamp, or more than one tick interval after the stalled tick, is lost the same way it always was.

**The mechanism and the discriminating test.** `tick()` (`PracticeEngine.ts`) closed windows by comparing their end with the tick's own music time; after a long task, the tick due first came off the queue before the MIDI message that had waited behind the same task, and deleted the slot before the stamped note could arrive — the stamp half of the row's framing was already true, the defect was the slot's removal. The rule chosen is (b), stall-aware: a tick whose gap since the previous tick exceeds `TICK_BUDGET_MS` (the session's `TICK_INTERVAL_MS`, pinned equal by a test) follows a stall; each window that tick finds ended is held in `heldUntil`, not closed, until a tick at least one tick interval after the stalled tick, or closed at once once it has been over longer than `STAMP_TRUST_MS` (the existing 1 s trust bound), since no stamp inside it could then be trusted; an unstalled tick never starts a hold. The reproduction on the base (`melody`, 60 bpm, tolerance 150; `start()`, `advance(90)`, `clock.set(400)`, `tick()`, `play(60, {atMs: 100})`) gave `hits 0, missedTotal 1, wrongNotesTotal 1`, the `missed` event's `tMs` at 400 — the stalled tick, before the note was fed — confirming the hypothesis. Two mutants prove both halves necessary: M2 (the bound removed, close up to the tick) and M1 (judge by the clock instead of the stamp) each return the base's red result; neither alone makes the note a hit.

**The last step and the lap wrap.** `holdsTheEnd` holds every window still open when a stalled tick reaches the end, and the deferral holds back `completeLap` itself, so `running` stays true and `feed` still accepts notes; during the deferral, only a note stamped before the run's end that matches a window still open is judged, anything else (including a note stamped after the last window) is dropped, never counted wrong; a deferred wrap rebases the next lap from `clock.now()` at the completing tick, so that lap's downbeat moves by the wait. `resumeStep` and `recountFrom` treat a held window as over, so it is never where the learner picks up, and a rewound clock cannot make a closed window playable again. `stop()` closes held windows as the misses they were.
