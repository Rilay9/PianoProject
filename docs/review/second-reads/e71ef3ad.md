## 4. X42: "agrees" at the scale of number-writing noise. AGREE, with a note
- How the reader parses: `<sound tempo>` is read with `Number(raw)` and kept as written (tempoFromXml.ts:134–139). `<per-minute>` accepts an optional "c." and then `\d+(\.\d+)?` (:153–158). `quartersPerMinute` normalises it as perMinute × beat-unit length × (2 − 0.5^dots) (:82–87). That product is exact in floating point for a whole-number per-minute, because the lengths are powers of two and the dot factors are 1.5, 1.75 and 1.875.
- Corpus scan (built scores, committed PDMX and MuseTrainer sources; 2,039 files; Python copy of the reader): 2,439 positions where a sound and a mark stand together, 2,322 of them exact. The nonzero differences:
  - 2 at 1.4e-14 (PDMX: a dotted-quarter 67 is 100.5, written as 100.49999999999999);
  - 35 at exactly 0.0002 (MuseScore: 76.0002, 79.9998 ×11, 70.0002 ×11, 64.9998 and others);
  - nothing else below 0.1. The smallest real differences are in Bella Ciao (PDMX): 68.1 against 68, 74.1 against 74, and so on.
  - No `<per-minute>` value in the corpus has a fractional part.
- The 0.0002 comes from MuseScore storing beats per second to 5 decimals and multiplying by 60, which bounds the error at 0.0003. A tolerance derived from that (at least 0.0003, for example 0.001) falls in a 500× gap below the smallest real difference. So the tolerance is well-defined.
- The 95-vs-100 guard falls on the right side. At a ratio of 1.053 the two values count as agreeing under R = 1.1. At a difference of 5 they disagree under any tolerance inside the gap.
- Note: the guard kills R but does not pin the boundary; a tolerance of 1 or 4 would pass it too. Add the corpus's real 0.1 case (sound 100.1 against mark 100 → no agreement) next to Satie's 76.0002 and a 79.9998-against-80 case, so the tolerance is pinned from both sides.
- My copied census gives the same result at 0.001 and at R = 1.1: only Satie (0, 0) and Maple Leaf (1, 0.25) and (51, 0) move, which is the set the reviewer expects. The builder's census through the app's own reader is the one that counts.
