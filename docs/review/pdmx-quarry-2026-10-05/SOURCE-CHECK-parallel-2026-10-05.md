## Jazz — Berklee Jazz Piano

**Map use:** minor-key ii-V-i, bossa, repertoire practice, arranging, intros/endings.

- Source read: Berklee Online, `Jazz Piano`, OPIAN-315, current public syllabus page.
- Public syllabus sequence includes: walking bass and comping; Lesson 7 `Playing a Bossa Nova` with `Comping Patterns for Bossa Nova`; Lesson 8 `Setting the Mood in a Minor Key` with relative minor, harmonic and jazz melodic minor, and `Using Minor Key II V I in A/B Form`; Lesson 9 repertoire mastery with harmony, melody, memorization, chord-tone soloing; Lesson 11 solo-piano arranging including harmonizing melody and drop-2; Lesson 12 introductions, endings and tag endings.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED** for the dossier-level claim that a coherent jazz-piano sequence includes minor ii-V-i after basic comping/walking-bass work and later integrates repertoire mastery, arranging, and intro/ending design.
- Correction/boundary: this syllabus does **not** prove the exact voicing, key list, repetition count, or rung number PianoProject should use. Those remain local design choices.
- Source: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/jazz-piano

## Blues / rock — Berklee Blues and Rock Keyboard Techniques

**Map use:** stylistic blues/rock keyboard vocabulary should progress from accompaniment/time feel to licks/improvisation and solo/group application.

- Source read: Berklee Online, `Blues and Rock Keyboard Techniques`, current public course page.
- Course description says the course develops fundamental blues/rock keyboard skills in solo and group settings through a progressive series of techniques, building theory, accompaniment ability and phrasing before shuffles, New Orleans piano, rock 'n' roll, modern rock piano and solo blues piano.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED** for the broad sequence `accompaniment/time feel + phrasing foundation -> idiomatic patterns/licks -> improvisation/application`.
- Boundary: the public page does not establish PianoProject's exact turnaround formula or six-step blues ladder. Those need notation/source-specific evidence locally.
- Source: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/blues-and-rock-keyboard-techniques

## Latin — Berklee Latin Piano Styles

**Map use:** distinguish Cuban guajeo/montuno/tumbao from Brazilian bossa/samba and from tango/habanera-derived patterns; teach rhythmic frameworks on piano in context.

- Source read: Berklee Online, `Latin Piano Styles`, OPIAN-300, current public syllabus.
- Lesson 3 explicitly separates `Guajeo and Montuno`, `Tumbao`, `Montuno and Tumbao`, `Tumbao Stride`, and cha-cha-cha under Cuban clave-based styles.
- Lesson 6 separately covers samba and bossa nova, including `Bossa Nova (Tom Jobim, João Gilberto)`.
- Lesson 10 separately covers tango and `Tango Patterns Derived from Habanera`.
- Course outcomes include executing core rhythmic frameworks and applying them to original piano parts/accompaniments.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED**, and it directly supports the terminology correction already made in PianoProject: these labels should not be flattened into one generic `Latin accompaniment` detector/family.
- Boundary: the public syllabus names categories and sequence; it does not provide the exact event-level contract. G14/G15 therefore still need the concrete pattern sources recorded in `PARALLEL-UNBLOCKS-2026-10-05.md`.
- Source: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/latin-piano-styles

## Improvisation — Berklee Basic Improvisation

**Map use:** improvisation should include transcription/internalization, motives/variation, harmony-aware choices and cohesive solo construction rather than scale-running alone.

- Source read: Berklee Online, `Basic Improvisation`, OPERF-110, current public syllabus page.
- Public outcomes include transcribing and internalizing phrasing/techniques of master improvisers, integrating chord tones/chord scales in real time, and performing cohesive improvised solos using motivic development and variation. Later lessons explicitly build and vary chord-tone/chord-scale motifs and chromatic approaches.

- Source: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/basic-improvisation

## Practice / memory — Chaffin & Imreh 2002

**Map use:** structural memory, retrieval cues, deliberate retrieval practice, multiple restart points rather than `blind mode = memory`.

- Source read: Psychological Science / SAGE abstract for Roger Chaffin & Gabriela Imreh, `Practicing Perfection: Piano Performance as Expert Memory`, 2002; PubMed record cross-check.
- Study: one concert pianist recorded practice while learning Bach's Italian Concerto and described formal structure, basic/interpretive features, and performance cues. The abstract reports effects of formal structure and performance cues on practice, initial-memory hesitations and recall two years later; authors interpret formal structure as a retrieval scheme and performance cues as retrieval cues, with extended retrieval practice.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED** for the narrow claims `formal structure can organize memory`, `performance/retrieval cues matter`, and `retrieval practice is distinct from merely removing the page`.
- Boundary: single-expert case study; do not turn it into a universal quantitative prescription (e.g. exact number of starts, exact spacing interval) without additional evidence.
- Sources: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.0956-7976.2002.00462.x ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12137137/

## Practice / memory — Williamon & Valentine 2002

**Map use:** structural restart points and retrieval structures across skill levels.

- Source read: `The Role of Retrieval Structures in Memorizing Music`, Cognitive Psychology 44(1), 2002; PubMed/ScienceDirect abstract.
- Study: 22 pianists at four skill levels learned/memorized Bach pieces; practice starts/stops were classified as structural, difficult or other. Across learning, pianists increasingly started/stopped at structural bars and less at difficult bars; use of structural starts/stops increased with skill.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED** for the app's structural-restart / multiple-start-point concept and for treating structure as part of memory training rather than relying on motor continuity from bar 1.
- Boundary: this does not establish that every beginner should use expert-level formal analysis or one fixed restart schedule.
- Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11814308/ ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010028501907595

## Practice quality — Williamon & Valentine 2000

**Map use:** hedge `more minutes = better`; distinguish practice content/quality from raw quantity.

- Source read: `Quantity and quality of musical practice as predictors of performance quality`, British Journal of Psychology 91, 2000; PubMed abstract.
- Study: 22 pianists, four skill levels. Quantity of practice was not significantly related to performance quality in the reported analyses; pianists using longer practice segments by the middle stage produced better musical, communicative and technical performances.
- Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH CAUTION** for the narrow claim that raw practice time alone is not a sufficient quality metric.
- Correction/boundary: do **not** convert `longer segments` into a simplistic app rule that blocked repetition is bad or that longer chunks are always better. The study is not an interleaving-vs-blocked trial.
- Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10958579/
