## Technique — endpoint breadth

### Source: ABRSM Piano Practical Grades 2025 & 2026, Grade 8 technical requirements
Official PDF: `https://www.abrsm.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/Piano%202025%20%26%202026%20Prac%20syllabus%2020240524_access.pdf`

Relevant published requirements at Grade 8 include:
- four-octave major and harmonic/melodic minor scales hands together;
- scales a sixth apart, four octaves, hands together;
- contrary-motion major/minor scales from unison;
- a legato scale in thirds and staccato scale in sixths (hands separately for the named examples);
- chromatic scale a major sixth apart;
- whole-tone scales;
- four-octave major/minor arpeggios;
- four-octave dominant-seventh and diminished-seventh arpeggios.

Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION.** The older dossier/third-party summary is directionally right about advanced breadth, but do not paraphrase ABRSM as requiring every major/minor key for every advanced form. The official Grade 8 requirement uses named key subsets for several patterns. It does directly support the proposition that an advanced piano technique endpoint includes multi-octave major/minor scales, separated-hand/interval scale work, chromatic/whole-tone material and dominant/diminished seventh arpeggios.

### Source: RCM Piano Syllabus 2022, Level 10 Technical Tests
Official syllabus page: `https://teacherportal.rcmusic.com/Resources/Syllabus-Piano`
Official PDF: `https://teacherportal.rcmusic.com/getattachment/57f3734d-97e5-4777-b67e-4b1111ee31a3/piano-syllabus-2022-edition.pdf`

Level 10 explicitly lists:
- four-octave major and harmonic/melodic minor scales in named upper-cycle keys;
- scales separated by a 3rd, four octaves, hands together;
- scales separated by a 6th, four octaves, hands together;
- octave and chromatic-octave work.

Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTION.** RCM strongly supports advanced multi-octave scale breadth and interval-separated scale work, but again the exact key inventory and tempo are syllabus-specific and should not become PianoProject requirements by copying.

### Consequence for the ability map
Keep the advanced breadth SHOULD/NICE rows if desired, but phrase them as a PianoProject musical endpoint rather than an exam-equivalence claim. The source support is for breadth, not for a level crosswalk.

## Rock / blues keyboard progression

### Source: Berklee Online — Blues and Rock Keyboard Techniques (OPIAN-220)
`https://online.berklee.edu/courses/blues-and-rock-keyboard-techniques`

The official course sequence explicitly moves through:
- blue notes, bass lines and basic vocabulary;
- Mixolydian theory and shuffle bass;
- comping;
- blues technique;
- phrasing/vocabulary, including intros, turnarounds and endings;
- rock-and-roll piano with walking/boogie bass;
- New Orleans comping;
- modern-rock comping;
- solo-blues left-hand patterns, melody and improvisation;
- final performance with accompaniment choices.

Verdict: **CONFIRMED.** The broad PianoProject proposition — accompaniment/time/harmonic vocabulary before fuller stylistic improvisation, and a later move toward independent keyboard-part choices — matches the official sequence. This does not authorize any one PianoProject pattern merely because Berklee teaches the style.

### Source: Berklee Online — Pop/Rock Keyboard
`https://online.berklee.edu/courses/pop-rock-keyboard`

The official course describes triads, arpeggiated keyboard parts, voice-leading and comping as bedrock principles, then dominant sevenths, blues licks/call-and-response, gospel-influenced vocabulary, improvisation, groove, country/country-pop and performance.

Verdict: **CONFIRMED.** This supports keeping pop/rock accompaniment, voice-leading and arpeggiated/repeated patterns as foundational keyboard jobs before advanced stylistic soloing. It also supports using country material by musical job rather than inventing a separate country track.

## Hymns / gospel

### Source: Berklee Online — Gospel Music for Keyboard (OPIAN-410)
`https://online.berklee.edu/courses/gospel-music-for-keyboard`

The official course states that students learn by playing hymns of the past and present and builds toward functioning as a gospel keyboardist. Its syllabus includes:
- call-and-response and testimony-service music;
- traditional and contemporary hymns;
- traditional church cadences and chants;
- traditional/contemporary gospel repertoire;
- reharmonization techniques;
- listening/ear-based backing of a preacher or gospel singer;
- repertoire building and live-context decision making.

The course prerequisites include scales, chords, jazz piano and blues/rock keyboard or equivalent experience.

Verdict: **CONFIRMED WITH IMPORTANT SEQUENCING BOUNDARY.** It supports the PianoProject idea that hymn/gospel competence grows beyond melody-plus-block-chords into cadences, accompaniment, reharmonization, ear-led response and live support. It also supports treating advanced gospel vocabulary as later material built on chord/scale/blues/jazz foundations, not as beginner hymn notation rules.

### Consequence for current hymn rows
- Keep the early hymn lane focused on truthful basic melody/chord accompaniment and voice-leading.
- Do not use the Berklee advanced gospel course to justify overloading early rungs with shout/talk-music/reharmonization vocabulary.
- Later hymn/gospel SHOULD rows for cadences, reharmonization, call-response and ear-led accompaniment have direct source support.
