The question is not whether each song closes a blocker, but whether it is musically usable, familiar enough to aid motivation/ear-checking, and worth surfacing in the curriculum or optional repertoire.

## Recommended actual additions

### 1. Stand By Me — strongest add

CID: `Qmf7ENREU9UngoF2LgRY1ZT9hAGDnCVBBYq8k3NeJUWBNt`

Why it is unusually useful:
- compact complete form, 37 bars;
- separate melody/voice and real two-staff piano;
- 23 chord symbols with an extremely legible C–Am–F–G harmonic loop;
- RH accompaniment is a persistent off-beat chord-stab pattern;
- LH supplies a simple rooted bass figure;
- Verse/Chorus labels plus repeat/ending/D.S./Fine navigation;
- the same pattern survives almost the whole score, so the learner can hear/feel consistency rather than decoding a different accompaniment every bar.

Disposition: **ADD TO CURRICULUM as familiar optional/alternate transfer material.**

Best job: early-to-middle chords-pop accompaniment transfer after basic I/vi/IV/V or inversion/chord-change work. It can also support form/navigation later, but do not require all navigation notation on first use.

Do not use the fuller A-major edition unless a specific harder-version need appears; the C-major edition is cleaner and sufficient.

### 2. Your Song — strong approachable full-piece add

CID: `QmcZnZByDzCDxRpXJfSGsPqDP9reRxzqpwmcxs3HR82kHc`

Observed:
- 38-bar two-staff piano arrangement;
- melody is plainly exposed in RH;
- LH is mostly slow half-note block harmony/chords, becoming whole-note support later;
- no need to infer accompaniment from a full band reduction;
- texture is considerably simpler than Someone Like You or A Thousand Miles;
- short enough to be a real learner piece rather than a huge capstone.

Disposition: **ADD TO CURRICULUM OR FEATURE PROMINENTLY AS OPTIONAL FAMILIAR REPERTOIRE.**

Best job: melody-over-chordal-accompaniment full-piece transfer; later ask learner to simplify/revoice the LH rather than merely copy it.

### 3. Someone Like You (easy piano) — strong later accompaniment model

CID: `QmXPbpPc6BkSripAS5CUxqoLiXoaaSrG7w3D3s9Zwzfcm2`

Observed:
- 73 bars, A major, 101 chord symbols;
- persistent LH sixteenth-note arpeggio pattern under RH melody/chords;
- opening makes the repeating pattern extremely explicit: A/C#/E/C# then equivalent chord-specific shapes bar after bar;
- later texture expands, so it can show how a persistent accompaniment engine survives while the arrangement gets denser;
- despite the title `easy piano`, the constant sixteenths and 3-sharp key make it a later task than Your Song.

Disposition: **ADD AS A LATER OPTIONAL/TRANSFER PIECE**, not an early easy song.

Best job: arpeggiated pop accompaniment continuity and texture building. This is more educational than merely adding another famous ballad because the accompaniment job is exceptionally clear.

## Good optional/fun material, not necessary curriculum spine

### Take Me Home, Country Roads

CID: `QmTmhiFsKppNMNycpcjsq2dzzDRMMFGME4zjky17GLerzb`

Observed:
- three separate single-staff parts rather than a normal grand staff;
- P1 is melody;
- P2 is a persistent accompaniment pattern: root/bass pickup into off-beat triad attacks;
- P3 is a very simple bass line, mostly root/fifth half notes;
- G major, simple harmonic vocabulary, familiar melody.

Disposition: **OPTIONAL / FUN REPERTOIRE; HIGH-VALUE arrangement exercise if the three parts are deliberately recombined.**

Best use: learner chooses which of P2 and P3 to combine beneath the melody, or reduces the three-part texture into two hands. That is pedagogically better than mechanically merging all three parts into a dense piano score.

Best job: maintaining a characteristic riff while other material enters, or reducing a signature piano texture. Do not place it early merely because it is familiar.

## Reject / deprioritize

- `Karma Police` retained edition: reject for curriculum use; alto-clef RH / placeholder-loop and numbering weirdness make this edition more trouble than its familiarity is worth.
- `Chasing Cars`: the retained keyboard line is a one-staff part in an 11-part band score. Keep only if a future arrangement/reduction project specifically wants it; otherwise lower priority.
- No useful current PDMX edition: Let It Be, Ring of Fire, I Walk the Line, Tennessee Whiskey. Do not spend more time on these unless a new source appears.

## Suggested product use

Do not make all of these required rung pieces. Use a light hierarchy:

1. **Required/alternate transfer:** Stand By Me; possibly Your Song.
2. **Later optional learning piece:** Someone Like You.
3. **Fun / project shelf:** Country Roads, A Thousand Miles, Creep, Mad World, Jolene, Iris.

The strongest three additions are deliberately different:
- Stand By Me = four-chord groove + bass/accompaniment pattern;
- Your Song = accessible melody over slow chordal LH;
- Someone Like You = persistent arpeggiated sixteenth accompaniment.

That gives familiarity without filling the curriculum with redundant pop arrangements.
